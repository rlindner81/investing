# Grab Holdings Limited (NASDAQ: GRAB) — Management Team

## Executive Leadership

Grab's senior team is unusually stable for a company of its age: the CEO is a
founder, the CTO was the company's first technical lead in 2012, and the CFO has
been in seat since before the SPAC listing. The most substantive recent change
is the elevation of Alex Hungate to the board in May 2026 as part of a stated
annual rotation of management directors.

---

### Anthony Tan Ping Yeow — Founder, Chairman & Group Chief Executive Officer

Co-founded Grab in 2012 and has served as Group CEO since inception. Conceived
the business at Harvard Business School as MyTeksi, a Malaysian taxi-booking
app, before relocating the company to Singapore and expanding across the region.

- **Prior to Grab:** family automotive business (Tan Chong Motor lineage);
  earlier work in supply chain and marketing roles
- **Education:** MBA, Harvard Business School (2011); B.A. with honors in
  economics and public policy, University of Chicago (2004)
- **Recognition:** Fortune 40 Under 40 (2016, 2018), The Bloomberg 50 (2017),
  Fast Company 100 Most Creative People (2018), Fortune World's 50 Greatest
  Leaders (2021), Nikkei Asia Prize (2020)
- **Control:** holds \~59.9% of total voting power through Class B shares while
  owning \~3.2% economically — the single most important governance fact about
  the company
- Leads the ACE Team Foundation, a Singapore family philanthropic foundation
  established with his wife Chloe Tong in 2021

**Note on the co-founder:** Tan Hooi Ling, who co-founded Grab alongside Anthony
Tan, stepped back from operations and **no longer holds any position at Grab**.

### Alex Hungate — President & Chief Operating Officer

Joined January 2022 and runs the operating business end-to-end: Mobility,
Deliveries and Financial Services, plus country operations, marketing and
commercial/support functions. Joined the board effective May 1, 2026, replacing
Peter Oey under the planned annual rotation of management directors.

- **Prior to Grab:** President & CEO of SATS (SGX: S58) from January 2014;
  joined the SATS board as independent director in 2011, executive director 2013
- **Banking:** CEO of HSBC Singapore (Aug 2010–Jul 2013); joined HSBC in 2007 as
  Group Managing Director, Personal Financial Services and Marketing (London)
- **Earlier:** Reuters — Managing Director Asia Pacific (Hong Kong, 2005–2007),
  and before that Co-CEO Americas and Global CMO in New York (1994–2005);
  Booz, Allen & Hamilton, London (1989–1991)
- **Public service:** member of Singapore's Future Economy Advisory Panel;
  formerly on the Singapore Economic Development Board (six years, to Jan 2024)
  and a non-executive director of UOB Group
- **Education:** engineering, economics and management, Oxford (1989); MBA,
  Harvard Business School, Baker Scholar (1993)

The combination of a Singapore banking CEO background with regional operating
experience is directly relevant to Grab's digibank strategy.

### Peter Oey — Chief Financial Officer

CFO since April 2020 — hired roughly 18 months before the SPAC listing and
responsible for taking Grab public. Owns financial operations, accounting and
reporting, treasury, FP&A, investor relations, tax, SOX compliance and
procurement, and additionally oversees corporate finance and legal. Served as a
director from May 1, 2025 until the May 2026 rotation handed that seat to
Hungate.

- **Prior to Grab:** CFO of LegalZoom.com (Dec 2014–Apr 2020); CFO of
  MyLife.com (Mar 2012–Nov 2014)
- **Activision Blizzard (1996–2012):** a 15-year run including VP & Corporate
  Controller (2008–2012), Senior Director of Global Financial Systems &
  Processes, Senior Director of Finance Europe, and Director of Finance Asia
  Pacific
- **Education:** B.Ec. with accounting major, University of Sydney (1991);
  Fellow CPA (Australia); member, Institute of Singapore Chartered Accountants

Oey is the voice on guidance — the FY revenue and Adjusted EBITDA outlook is
delivered in his quote in each quarterly release.

CPO since February 1, 2023, leading Product, Design and Analytics, plus Grab's

- **Prior to Grab:** co-founder and CTO of Skobbler (mapping/navigation),

- **Education:** MBA in Global e-Management, University of Cologne; alumnus of

Kandal's Geo/mapping background matters more than it might appear: routing and
ETA accuracy are the direct inputs to both delivery unit economics and driver
utilization.

## Corporate Governance Notes

**Dual-class share structure — the dominant governance consideration.** Class B
ordinary shares carry supervoting rights, giving Anthony Tan \~59.9% of voting
power against \~3.2% economic ownership. Grab is effectively a controlled
company: public Class A holders cannot determine board composition, approve or
block transactions, or force strategic change. Any investment thesis has to
accept founder control as a fixed condition rather than a risk that resolves.

**Foreign private issuer status.** As a Cayman-incorporated, Singapore-based FPI,
Grab files an annual 20-F and furnishes quarterly results on 6-K rather than
10-K/10-Q. Practical consequences for research:
- Financial statements are **IFRS**, not US GAAP
- Quarterly figures are *furnished*, not *filed*, and are explicitly unaudited
  and derived from management accounts
- Grab is exempt from several NASDAQ corporate governance standards applicable
  to domestic issuers, and from US proxy rules
- Insider transactions are not reported on the Section 16 cadence US investors
  expect

**Board rotation of management directors.** Grab runs a stated annual rotation
of the management seat on the board (Ong Chin Yin 2024–2025, Peter Oey
2025–2026, Alex Hungate from May 2026). This gives senior executives board
exposure in turn, though it does not alter voting control.

**Compensation.** Executive equity is heavily weighted to Class B ordinary
shares via service- and performance-based RSUs and options — Anthony Tan alone
held over 8.1M service-based restricted Class B shares plus 4.2M performance
RSUs and 6.2M performance options (\$4.59 strike, granted March 2025). Non-founder
executives (Oey, Hungate) each own well under 1% of ordinary shares, so their
alignment is through equity grants rather than accumulated stakes.

**Auditor:** KPMG (Singapore).
