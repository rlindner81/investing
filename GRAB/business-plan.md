# Grab Holdings Limited (NASDAQ: GRAB) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

Grab is Southeast Asia's leading "superapp" — a single consumer application
bundling ride-hailing, food and grocery delivery, package delivery, digital
payments, lending, insurance and digital banking. The strategic thesis is that
one app, one wallet and one driver/merchant network across eight fragmented
national markets produces cross-segment economics no single-vertical competitor
can match: a Mobility rider becomes a Deliveries customer becomes a lending
customer, each acquired at near-zero incremental cost.

- **Founded:** 2012 (as MyTeksi in Malaysia; later Grab, HQ moved to Singapore)
- **Listed:** December 1, 2021 (NASDAQ: GRAB), via SPAC merger with Altimeter
  Growth Corp — at the time the largest-ever SPAC business combination
- **Headquarters:** Singapore (incorporated in the Cayman Islands)
- **Reporting:** Foreign private issuer — annual **20-F**, quarterly **6-K**;
  accounts under **IFRS**, presented in **USD**
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year)
- **FY2025 Revenue:** \$3,370M (+20% YoY)
- **Footprint:** 900+ cities across eight countries — Cambodia, Indonesia,
  Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam
- **Scale (Q2 2026):** 53.9M Group Monthly Transacting Users (MTUs),
  \$6.5B quarterly On-Demand GMV

## Revenue Model

Grab reports three revenue segments plus a residual "Others". Revenue is
recognized net of driver- and merchant-partner incentives, so reported revenue
is materially smaller than GMV (gross transaction value flowing through the
platform).

**Deliveries (\~53% of FY2025 revenue) — \$1,800M FY2025, +21% YoY**
- Food delivery (GrabFood), groceries (GrabMart), and package/courier
  (GrabExpress). Commission take on merchant orders plus delivery fees.
- Includes **GrabAds**, the high-margin advertising business selling merchant
  sponsored listings inside the app — a key driver of segment margin expansion.
- Includes owned offline grocery: **Jaya Grocer** and **Everrise** supermarkets
  in Malaysia, which put physical inventory on the balance sheet.

**Mobility (\~36% of FY2025 revenue) — \$1,219M FY2025, +16% YoY**
- Ride-hailing (car, taxi, motorbike/GrabBike), plus **GrabRentals** vehicle
  leasing to driver-partners. The oldest and structurally most profitable
  segment (FY2025 Segment Adjusted EBITDA \$690M, \~57% of segment revenue).

**Financial Services (\~10% of FY2025 revenue) — \$347M FY2025, +37% YoY**
- Payments (GrabPay, OVO in Indonesia), lending (GrabFin), insurance.
- **Digital banks:** GXS Bank (Singapore), GXBank (Malaysia), **Superbank**
  (Indonesia — consolidated from June 2026). Customer deposits reached \$2.5B
  in Q2 2026; gross loan portfolio \$2.3B (+197% YoY).
- The only structurally loss-making segment (FY2025 Segment Adj. EBITDA −\$110M),
  run as an investment in the lending flywheel off platform transaction data.

### Segment economics (FY2025)

| Segment | Revenue | Segment Adj. EBITDA | Margin on revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deliveries | \$1,800M | \$287M | \~16% |
| Mobility | \$1,219M | \$690M | \~57% |
| Financial Services | \$347M | −\$110M | n/m |
| **Group Adjusted EBITDA** | **\$3,370M** | **\$500M** | **\~15%** |

Group Adjusted EBITDA sits well below the sum of segment EBITDA because
**regional corporate costs** (\~\$217M in H1 2026 alone) are held above the
segment line.

## Products & Brand Portfolio

### Consumer superapp
One app spanning Mobility, Deliveries, payments and financial products, with
**GrabUnlimited** as the paid subscription tier driving frequency and retention.

### Driver- and merchant-partner side
GrabRentals (vehicle leasing), partner lending and insurance, merchant SaaS
tooling and GrabAds self-serve advertising. Grab monetizes both sides of the
marketplace — partner-facing services are booked into Deliveries/Mobility GMV.

### Digital banking
Three licensed digibanks (Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia) originating loans
underwritten on Grab's own platform behavioural data — the core argument that
Grab can underwrite thin-file Southeast Asian borrowers better than incumbents.

### AI / "Grab intelligence layer"
Management's stated 2026 strategic priority: an AI layer embedded across
allocation, pricing, fraud, driver earnings and merchant tooling, pitched as
both a user-engagement and an operating-efficiency lever.

## Technology & Competitive Moat

**Network density.** In ride-hailing and delivery, the moat is local liquidity —
the densest driver network wins on wait time and price simultaneously. Grab's
scale advantage is per-city, not regional, which is why it defends leadership
city by city.

**Cross-segment data.** Transaction, location and repayment history from
Mobility and Deliveries feeds Financial Services underwriting. This is Grab's
strongest structural claim: a lending book acquired at effectively zero
customer-acquisition cost with proprietary risk data.

**Regulatory capital.** Digital banking licences in three countries are scarce
and slow to obtain — a genuine barrier for would-be entrants into the
financial-services flywheel.

**Geographic focus.** Eight markets with distinct languages, regulators,
payment rails and road networks. This complexity has repelled global
competitors (Uber exited Southeast Asia in 2018, selling its business to Grab
alongside a non-compete agreement).

## Growth Strategy

1. **Affordability to expand the user base.** Deliberately pushing lower-priced
   product tiers (saver options, group orders) to convert the large
   price-sensitive middle of the Southeast Asian market — MTUs +17% YoY in
   Q2 2026 against GMV per MTU +3%, i.e. growth is coming from users, not basket.
2. **Advertising attach.** GrabAds is the highest-incremental-margin revenue
   line; scaling merchant ad adoption lifts Deliveries margin without GMV growth.
3. **Lending flywheel.** Grow the loan book off platform data, with the digibanks
   supplying low-cost deposit funding. Gross loan portfolio nearly tripled YoY.
4. **M&A and consolidation.** Superbank consolidation (June 2026) and the Stash
   Financial acquisition (July 2026) extend the financial-services stack.
5. **Capital return.** \$1.75B cumulative buyback authorization since 2024,
   including an incremental \$750M announced with Q2 2026 — a notable posture
   shift for a company that was cash-burning at IPO.

## Key Risks

### Financial Services drag and credit risk
The lending book is scaling fast (gross portfolio +197% YoY) into consumer
segments with limited credit history, funded increasingly by retail deposits.
Net impairment losses on financial assets more than doubled YoY in Q2 2026
(\$72M vs \$33M). A regional credit cycle would hit revenue, impairments and
deposit stability simultaneously.

### Reported profit is not operating profit
Q2 2026 profit of \$235M was driven overwhelmingly by a **\$307M one-time
remeasurement gain on consolidating Superbank**; operating profit was only
\$19M. Convertible-note fair-value swings add further volatility. Headline
IFRS net income is a poor read on the underlying business — Adjusted EBITDA and
operating profit are the fairer gauges.

### Cash flow optics
Grab's headline "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" excludes digibank loan and deposit
flows. On a plain OCF-minus-capex basis the group has been roughly breakeven to
negative (FY2025: OCF \$79M less capex). The faster the lending book grows, the
wider the gap between Grab's Adjusted FCF and actual cash generation.

### Regulatory exposure
Gig-economy worker classification, fare and commission caps, foreign ownership
limits, data localization and banking regulation — across eight jurisdictions
with independent regulators. Any single market imposing driver-employment status
or commission caps would compress segment economics.

### Competition and incentive intensity
Partner incentives (+32% YoY) and consumer incentives (+27% YoY) both grew
faster than revenue (+22%) in Q2 2026. Growth still requires subsidy; a
competitive escalation in Indonesia would show up immediately in margin.

### Governance — dual-class control
Anthony Tan holds \~59.9% of voting power via Class B shares against \~3.2%
economic ownership. Public shareholders have effectively no ability to
influence board composition or strategy.

### FX translation
Revenue is earned in SGD, IDR, MYR, THB, VND and PHP but reported in USD.
Reported growth has repeatedly diverged from constant-currency growth in both
directions (Q2 2026: +22% actual vs +21% cc).
