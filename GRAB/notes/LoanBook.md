# The Loan Book and Why OCF Is Misleading

**Read this before getting excited about a positive OCF print.**

## Why our FCF looks broken

`check-valuation` shows TTM FCF at −\$160M (company) / −\$426M (strict) and `n/m`
for P/FCF. That is **not** operational deterioration. Under IFRS, loan
origination is an *operating* outflow for a lender, and Grab is funding a loan
book off its own balance sheet: \~\$369M of loan-receivable growth in H1-2026
sits inside the −\$3M H1 operating line.

Grab's answer is "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" (\$450M TTM as of 2026-06-30), which
adds the lending swing back. Structurally that is the normal treatment for a
hybrid industrial/finance company — but it assumes every loan is money-good,
which is the entire open question. We keep the unadjusted view; `n/m` is the
honest output. See [../reviews/2026-09-10-review.md](../reviews/2026-09-10-review.md).

Note they added a *new* exclusion ("net changes in treasury liquidity positions",
−\$95M) starting Q2-2026 — a non-IFRS definition changed in the quarter it helps.

## The trap: OCF inflection is ambiguous

Interest received **is** already in operating CF (\$41M in Q2-2026, \$87M H1), so
we are not blind to it. The problem is that origination outflow swamps it on the
same line. When OCF turns positive, at least three different causes look
identical:

1. Book maturing, interest compounding — good
2. Underwriting tightened deliberately — fine
3. Demand collapsed, or they braked because losses scared them — bad

**The real trap:** tightening underwriting improves OCF *immediately* (less
origination) while the loans already on the book default *later*. The good signal
and the deferred bad news run on different clocks. Worse, slowing origination
also shrinks the IFRS 9 impairment charge, since losses are front-loaded at
origination — so both indicators improve together for reasons unrelated to
whether the existing book is any good.

## What to check when OCF inflects

- **Is the loan book still growing?** OCF up *with* the book still growing =
  healthy seasoning. OCF up *because* origination stopped = ask why.
- **Impairment vs. FS revenue.** Q2-2026 baseline: \$72M impairment against \$134M
  Financial Services revenue, +118% Y/Y vs +59% revenue growth. Segment still
  EBITDA-negative at −\$15M. (\$15M of that was a one-off day-1 ECL on the acquired
  Superbank book.)
- **The gap between the two.** Impairment is *their model's opinion*, fast but
  unfalsifiable; OCF is factual but lags defaults by \~a year. If impairment stays
  tame while OCF keeps disappointing, the credit models are wrong.
- **Vintage/cohort loss curves** — losses by origination quarter. Grab does
  **not** publish these. Without them the existing book cannot be verified.

## What they don't disclose

No net interest income, no NIM, no yield on loans. Financial Services revenue
(\$134M in Q2-2026) blends lending, payments, Superbank and Stash. "Interest
income received" in the cash flow statement is group-wide treasury interest — it
*fell* Y/Y while the book doubled, so it tracks the \~\$7.4B cash pile, not loans.
Management asserts risk-adjusted returns are "comfortably above cost of capital";
that is unverifiable from the filings.

\~⅓ of borrowers had no prior credit-bureau record. The models are young and have
not seen a downturn.

## Bottom line

A positive OCF print is a **necessary but not sufficient** signal — a prompt to
investigate, not a verdict. It says nothing about future defaults on loans
already written.
