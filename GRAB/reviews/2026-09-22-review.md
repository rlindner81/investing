**Revenue.** Growth is intact and mildly accelerating: TTM revenue of \$3,731M with Q2-2026 at \$997M (+21.7% Y/Y) and Q1-2026 at +23.5%, against FY-2025's +20.5% and FY-2024's +18.5%. Four straight quarters above 18%, and the FY-2026 guide was raised to \$4,100–4,150M from \$4,040–4,100M. The delivery record against guide is clean — +0.2% to +2.6% beats in FY-2024/2025, with a single −0.6% miss at Q4-2025.

**Cash flow — read with the loan book in mind.** Headline FCF looks broken: TTM company FCF of −\$160M and −\$426M strict ex-SBC, versus +\$775M in FY-2024. P/FCF prints `n/m` on every recent column. Per [notes/LoanBook.md](../notes/LoanBook.md) this is largely an IFRS artefact — loan origination is an operating outflow for a lender, and \~\$369M of loan-receivable growth in H1-2026 sits inside the operating line. Grab's own Adjusted FCF was \~\$450M TTM at 2026-06-30. But the adjustment assumes every loan is money-good, and the company *added a new exclusion* ("net changes in treasury liquidity positions", −\$95M) in Q2-2026 — a non-IFRS definition changed in the quarter it helps. SBC is real and sizeable: \~\$240M/yr, roughly 6.4% of revenue, the gap between the two FCF rows.

**Valuation.** At \$2.91 the market cap is \$11.87B — 3.2x TTM sales and 2.9x forward FY guidance, versus 5.1x at FY-2025 and 7.3x a year ago. That is a severe de-rating against *improving* revenue growth. Net cash is \~\$0.83B (\$2.86B cash vs \$2.03B debt) on the balance-sheet lines here, though group gross liquidity is \~\$7.4B once time deposits and marketable securities are counted.

**The new overhang.** The \$1.49B Atome cash outlay (announced 2026-09-15, closing \~Q3-2027) plus a Phase-2 obligation floored at \$2.0B commits \$3.5B+ of that liquidity to a consumer-credit book — before the \$900M of remaining buyback. Short interest has climbed relentlessly: 1.5% of shares out in Q2-2024 to 6.2% at Q2-2026.

**Verdict.** Genuinely cheap on sales for a 22% grower, but the only multiple that computes is P/S, and the cash pile that made it safe is now being spent on credit risk.

**Rating:** 0.3 — HOLD

## 3. News

Two findings this week resolve the open question from the 2026-09-10 review and change the picture materially.

**The September 9 mystery is solved — Toyota exited entirely. Substantiated.** The last review flagged a \~150M-share day on 2026-09-09 with no disclosed cause, and declined to guess. The cause is now in the SEC record: Toyota Motor Corporation filed a **Schedule 13G/A on 2026-09-14** (accession 0001193125-26-389761) reporting **0 shares and 0.0% of class, effective as of the close of business on September 9, 2026** — amending its original December 2021 filing. Toyota's position dated to its 2018 \~\$1B investment. That is the large holder repositioning the volume implied, confirmed by filing rather than inferred. Importantly it is a **strategic exit by a corporate partner, not a fundamental verdict**, and it is now *done* — a finished overhang rather than a live one.

**The \$1.49B Atome Financial acquisition — substantiated, and the real story.** On 2026-09-15 Grab filed three 6-Ks. Per the EX-99.1 (which I read in full), Grab will acquire a controlling **60% of Neuroncredit Pte. Ltd. (Atome Financial)** for **\$1.49B in cash**, of which \$0.26B is primary growth capital, funded entirely from existing cash, closing **by Q3-2027**. Phase 2 buys the remaining 40% \~two years later at 13.0x annualized adjusted EBITDA / 2.5x revenue (75/25 weighted), **floored at \$2.0B and capped at \$4.5B** — so the committed total is at least \$3.5B. Atome brings a **\$1B gross loan portfolio**, 25M cumulative users and 30,000+ brand partners across five markets. Grab upgraded 2028 targets to **\$1.7B adjusted EBITDA** and **30%+ revenue CAGR 2025–2028**, with Financial Services at \$500M segment adjusted EBITDA and a **\$6B** gross loan book.

**The deeper read: the targets are the attention, the credit is the substance.** The transaction and its terms are hard fact. The *justification* is not. The press release discloses **no Atome revenue, no EBITDA, no loss rate** — only that delinquency rates are "improving or stable" on figures explicitly footnoted as **"management accounts, which have not been reviewed or audited."** This lands squarely on the [LoanBook](../notes/LoanBook.md) thesis: the open question was always whether Grab's own young credit models are any good, and the answer is to buy a second, larger, equally unverifiable book. S&P (BB/Positive) flagged heightened consumer-credit and regulatory exposure and forecasts 3x–4x debt/EBITDA, noting the \$6B 2028 target against **\$2.3B at 2026-06-30**. Grab's own net impairment losses rose **81% to \$120M in H1-2026** from \$66M. A 2028 target is an uncommitted forward claim; the \$3.5B is committed.

**Insiders reversed hard — substantiated, and the strongest signal here.** The prior review noted Tan and Hungate *selling*. That flipped. Form 4s filed 2026-09-21 show **CEO Anthony Tan purchased 10,350,000 Class A shares at \$2.8866 (\~\$29.9M)**, transaction code **P** (open market), **with no 10b5-1 plan**, lifting his direct stake from 428,498 to **10,778,498 shares — a \~25x increase**. President/COO Alexander Hungate bought 299,571 shares at \$2.8936 (\~\$867K) the same day. These dwarf the routine 10b5-1 sales by CFO Oey (50,000 at \$2.9038) and CPO Kandal (30,000 at \$3.08) on Sep 15. Several aggregators still report "no insider buying in 12 months" — that is stale; I verified the XML directly. Unplanned open-market buying of this size, by the CEO, days after announcing the deal the market disliked, is the most credible available statement that management thinks the selloff is wrong. Separately, Grab announced \~**\$900M remaining buyback** to be completed over 12 months, explicitly unaffected by Atome.

**Sentiment and catalysts.** Sell-side stays constructive with targets chasing price down (consensus \~\$5.86–6.11; StoneX Buy/\$7, Macquarie \$5.55), but **HSBC cut to Hold on 2026-09-17** and Weiss to Sell. Q2-2026 13Fs were net-negative (337 decreasers vs 223 adders; Tiger Global exited \~93M shares). Short interest keeps building — 6.2–6.4% of shares out, 7.6% of float, 6.8 days to cover. Watch: **Taiwan FTC ruling on Foodpanda Taiwan (\$600M) due October 27**, extended specifically to study Uber's \~13% Grab stake; and **Q3-2026 earnings in early November**.

**Risks.** \$3.5B+ committed to unaudited consumer credit into a book that has never seen a downturn; Uber's stake as both overhang and regulatory obstacle; Indonesia's 8% commission cap as replicable precedent; and profitability that still leans on the Q2 \$307M one-off Superbank gain.

**Rating:** 0.4 — HOLD

## Verdict

Both questions left open by the 2026-09-10 review are now answered, and they answer in opposite directions. The unexplained \~150M-share day was **Toyota liquidating its entire 2018-vintage stake** (13G/A, effective 2026-09-09) — a finished strategic exit rather than an informed verdict, which removes an overhang rather than confirming one; and the reason the selling continued is the **\$1.49B Atome acquisition**, which commits at least \$3.5B including the Phase-2 floor to a \$1B consumer loan book whose credit quality Grab discloses only via unaudited management accounts. That is the crux: a 22%-grower at 3.2x TTM sales with \~\$900M of buyback left is cheap, but the market is repricing Grab from a superapp into a lender, and the [LoanBook](../notes/LoanBook.md) problem — young models, \~⅓ of borrowers with no bureau record, no vintage loss curves published — just doubled in size while impairments rose 81% Y/Y. Against that, **CEO Tan's unplanned \$29.9M open-market purchase on 2026-09-21**, a 25x increase in his direct stake alongside the COO's, is the most substantive counter-signal available and coincides with the first session of falling-volume absorption at the lows. I would not chase a tape still stacked `↓↓` on every SMA against cleanly bullish benchmarks, but the asymmetry has improved enough that this is no longer a sell. What would change it: Q3-2026 in early November showing operating cash flow inflecting *while the loan book still grows* (the healthy-seasoning case, not the braking case), or any Atome loss-curve disclosure at closing — either would convert the valuation from a falling knife into support; conversely, impairments accelerating or a Taiwan FTC rejection on October 27 would re-break it.

**Rating:** 0.0 — HOLD
