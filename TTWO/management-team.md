# Take-Two Interactive — Management Team

## Strauss Zelnick — Chairman & Chief Executive Officer

Strauss Zelnick is the face of Take-Two, serving as Chairman and CEO. He came to
the company through **ZelnickMedia** (now **ZMC**), the private-equity/media-advisory
firm he co-founded in 2001; ZelnickMedia took a controlling interest in Take-Two in
2007 amid a management shake-up and options-backdating scandal, and Zelnick installed
himself as Chairman, later adding the CEO role (2011). He is widely credited with
stabilizing the company, restoring Rockstar's autonomy, and building the disciplined
"most creative, most innovative, most efficient" operating philosophy he repeats on
every call.

Before Take-Two, Zelnick ran a string of media businesses: he was **President & CEO
of BMG Entertainment** (Bertelsmann's music arm), **President & CEO of Crystal
Dynamics** (a game developer), and **President & COO of 20th Century Fox**. This blend
of music, film, and games gives him an entertainment-portfolio lens on the business.
On capital allocation he is explicit and consistent: fund organic growth first,
pursue **only accretive** M&A (Zynga in 2022, Gearbox more recently), and buy back
stock opportunistically "at deep value" (noting a prior buyback at ~\$158/share). He
is also a well-known fitness enthusiast whose personal-discipline ethos is part of
the company's public persona.

## Karl Slatoff — President

Karl Slatoff is President of Take-Two, the operating lieutenant who runs the label
and pipeline detail. He joined the company in 2008 (arriving alongside the
ZelnickMedia era) and rose through executive vice president roles before being named
President in 2013. On earnings calls he owns the **product pipeline** narrative —
title counts and release timing across Rockstar, 2K, and Zynga (e.g., 29 titles
through fiscal 2029, six additional FY2027 releases beyond GTA VI). His prior career
was in media and private equity, including time at ZelnickMedia, aligning him closely
with Zelnick's operating discipline.

## Lainie Goldstein — Chief Financial Officer

Lainie Goldstein is Take-Two's Chief Financial Officer. She is a long-tenured
Take-Two finance executive, having joined the company in the early 2000s and served
in senior finance roles (including Senior Vice President, Finance / deputy CFO) for
many years before being elevated to CFO. On calls she delivers the quantitative
outlook — Net Bookings and GAAP net-revenue guidance, recurrent-consumer-spending
mix, operating cash flow, and capex — and frames the FY2027 story around GTA VI (net
bookings \$8.0–8.2B, operating cash flow in excess of \$1B, and an expected return to
a **net cash position** by year-end).

## Nicole Shevins — SVP, Investor Relations & Corporate Communications

Nicole Shevins is Senior Vice President of Investor Relations and Corporate
Communications, and hosts the earnings calls (opening remarks, forward-looking
disclaimer, and Q&A logistics).

## Corporate Governance Notes

- **Single class of common stock** (no dual-class structure), unusual among
  founder-influenced media names — voting rights track economic ownership. Control is
  a product of the ZelnickMedia relationship and board composition rather than
  super-voting shares.
- **Founder-operator through PE.** Zelnick's control originates from ZelnickMedia's
  2007 stake and management agreement; the same PE-discipline lens (accretive-only
  M&A, opportunistic buybacks) governs capital allocation.
- **Rockstar autonomy.** Rockstar Games (Sam Houser's studio) operates with notable
  creative independence; management repeatedly defers GTA-specific detail to Rockstar
  ("Rockstar will provide more details when they're ready"), underscoring that the
  crown-jewel franchise is run at the studio level.
- **Key-person / franchise concentration.** Governance risk is concentrated in the
  dependence on Zelnick's leadership and on the GTA and NBA 2K franchises; SEC filings
  list dependence on key management and on those two franchises as explicit risk
  factors.
- **Compensation scrutiny.** Take-Two's executive pay (particularly Zelnick's
  performance-based equity awards tied to stock/TSR targets) has periodically drawn
  say-on-pay attention from proxy advisors — a recurring governance watch item.
