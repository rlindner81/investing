# Neurocrine Biosciences, Inc. (Nasdaq: NBIX) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

Neurocrine Biosciences is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on
neuropsychiatric, neurological, endocrine and immunological disorders — conditions
it describes as "under-addressed." The investment case rests on a single fact about
its shape: for most of its history Neurocrine was a **one-product company**, with
INGREZZA (tardive dyskinesia) supplying essentially all revenue. Since December 2024
it has been converting that concentrated cash flow into a three-product commercial
portfolio and an unusually deep late-stage neuroscience pipeline.

- **Founded:** 1992
- **IPO:** May 1996 (Nasdaq: NBIX)
- **Headquarters:** San Diego, California
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year)
- **FY2025 Revenue:** \$2,860.5M (+21.4% YoY)
- **FY2025 Net Product Sales:** \$2,833.9M (+22% YoY)

The company is profitable and self-funding: FY2025 operating cash flow was \$782.7M
against \$34.0M of capex, and it carries **no debt**. That combination — a
cash-generative base drug funding a broad pipeline — is the structural feature that
distinguishes Neurocrine from most mid-cap biotech.

## Revenue Model

Neurocrine sells small-molecule prescription drugs in the U.S. through specialty and
retail pharmacy channels, and collects royalties/milestones from partners abroad.
Revenue splits into two lines on the income statement:

**Net product sales (\~99% of revenue):** \$2,833.9M in FY2025. Three marketed
products, all U.S.-focused.

**Collaboration revenue (\~1%):** \$26.6M in FY2025 — milestones and royalties from
partners, principally Mitsubishi Tanabe (East Asia rights to valbenazine) and AbbVie
(ORILISSA / ORIAHNN, which Neurocrine discovered and out-licensed). This line is
lumpy and immaterial to the thesis.

**Revenue concentration is the defining risk and is now falling:**

| CRENESSITY | \$14M (launch) | \$301.2M (\~11%) | \$184M (\~19%) |

## Product Portfolio

### INGREZZA (valbenazine) — the cash engine
A selective VMAT2 inhibitor, FDA-approved April 2017 for **tardive dyskinesia (TD)**
and August 2023 for **chorea associated with Huntington's disease**. Also available as
INGREZZA SPRINKLE (approved April 2024) for patients who cannot swallow capsules.

- FY2025 net sales \$2.51B, +9% YoY — growth has decelerated sharply from the 20–30%
  of 2022–2024 as the franchise matures
- The growth equation has inverted: **double-digit prescription volume growth offset
  by lower net price** from expanded (largely Medicaid/Medicare) access. Volume is
  healthy; realized price per script is the drag
- Key competitive claim: selective VMAT2 targeting with once-daily dosing and no
  required titration. Head-to-head data presented in late 2025 showed nearly two-fold
  higher VMAT2 target occupancy vs. AUSTEDO XR after a single dose
- **Under-diagnosis is the long-run volume story** — Neurocrine has consistently said
  roughly 7 of 10 U.S. TD patients are undiagnosed, which is what its
  direct-to-consumer advertising spend targets

### CRENESSITY (crinecerfont) — the launch
A CRF1 receptor antagonist, FDA-approved **December 2024** for classic congenital
adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) — the first new mechanism for CAH in decades, allowing
reduction of chronic supraphysiologic glucocorticoid dosing.

- Scaled from \$14M (FY2024 stub) to \$301.2M in FY2025, then \$184M in Q2-2026 alone
- 2,048 new patient enrollment start forms in FY2025; >80% reimbursement coverage on
  dispensed scripts by Q4-2025
- By Q2-2026 management said \~15% of the estimated diagnosed patient population had
  been prescribed it — i.e. the launch is early relative to its own addressable base
- This is the single most important validation that Neurocrine can commercialize a
  second product, not just harvest INGREZZA

### VYKAT XR (diazoxide choline) — the acquisition
Acquired via the **Soleno Therapeutics acquisition, closed May 18, 2026** for
\~\$2.36B net of cash. Treats hyperphagia in Prader-Willi syndrome (PWS).

- \$94M pro forma net sales in Q2-2026; \$54M recognized by Neurocrine post-close
- \~\$2.2B of the purchase price was booked as intellectual property, amortized over
  an expected 16-year life — so GAAP earnings now carry a large non-cash amortization
  charge that non-GAAP figures exclude (\$16.4M in Q2-2026 alone)
- Management flagged that new patient demand was consistent with Q1 while
  discontinuation tracked expectations after the initial 2025 launch bolus —
  i.e. this asset needs market development, not just distribution

### Partnered / legacy
ORILISSA and ORIAHNN (endometriosis, uterine fibroids) are marketed by **AbbVie** from
Neurocrine-discovered chemistry; Neurocrine receives royalties only.

## Pipeline

The pipeline is the reason the stock trades on more than INGREZZA's annuity. Two
Phase 3 programs anchor it:

- **Osavampator (NBI-1065845)** — AMPA positive allosteric modulator for **major
  depressive disorder**. Potential first-in-class; positive Phase 2 top-line in 2024,
  Phase 3 underway
- **Direclidine (NBI-1117568)** — M4-preferring muscarinic agonist for
  **schizophrenia**. The muscarinic class was validated commercially by
  Bristol Myers Squibb's COBENFY; Neurocrine's bet is a differentiated selectivity
  profile within it
- **NBI-1065890** — next-generation selective VMAT2 inhibitor, Phase 2 initiated in TD.
  An explicit attempt to extend the VMAT2 franchise past INGREZZA's patent life
- Broader muscarinic franchise plus preclinical work across neuropsychiatry

R&D spend reflects this: GAAP R&D rose from \$565.0M (FY2023) to \$731.1M (FY2024) to
\$1,015.7M (FY2025), guided to \$1.20–1.25B for FY2026. Neurocrine is deliberately
spending its INGREZZA margin on the pipeline rather than banking it.

## Competitive Position

**Within TD (INGREZZA's market):** a two-drug market. INGREZZA competes with
Teva's AUSTEDO / AUSTEDO XR (deutetrabenazine). Both are VMAT2 inhibitors; Neurocrine
differentiates on selectivity, once-daily dosing without required titration, and
target-occupancy data. Competition here is commercial (access, DTC, prescriber reach)
more than scientific.

**Within CAH:** CRENESSITY is first-in-mechanism with no direct competitor, though
Crinetics Pharmaceuticals is developing a rival CRF1 antagonist.

**Within PWS:** VYKAT XR is the only approved hyperphagia treatment.

**The durable moat is narrower than the product count suggests.** It rests on
(1) twenty years of accumulated VMAT2 and neuroendocrine expertise, (2) a specialty
neuroscience commercial organization that has now launched three products, and
(3) orphan/rare-disease exclusivity on the newer assets. It does **not** rest on
network effects or switching costs — these are patent-protected small molecules facing
eventual generic entry.

## Growth Strategy

1. **Defend and extend INGREZZA volume** — attack TD under-diagnosis through DTC and
   prescriber expansion, accepting net-price erosion as the cost of broader access
2. **Scale CRENESSITY** — penetrate the \~85% of diagnosed CAH patients not yet
   prescribed; this is the nearest-term revenue lever
3. **Develop the PWS market for VYKAT XR** — management frames this as market
   development, with sequential growth expected exiting 2026
4. **Convert the pipeline** — osavampator in MDD and direclidine in schizophrenia are
   both large-indication shots; either would transform the revenue base
5. **Acquire and in-license** — Soleno set the template. With no debt, large securities
   balances and a revolver, Neurocrine has capacity for further deals, and IPR&D
   expense lines show a steady stream of smaller early-stage licenses
6. **Return capital opportunistically** — \$300M repurchased in FY2024, \$167.7M in
   FY2025, \$66.0M in H1-2026, alongside the much larger acquisition spend

## Key Risks

### Concentration and the INGREZZA patent cliff
Even after diversification, INGREZZA was \~75% of Q2-2026 revenue. Its decelerating
growth (+9% FY2025) against a maturing TD market, combined with eventual loss of
exclusivity, is the central bear case. Everything else in the portfolio is being built
against that clock.

### Net price erosion
The recurring pattern in every recent report: volume grows double digits, net price
falls. Expanded access into government channels mechanically lowers realized revenue
per script. If volume growth slows while price continues to compress, INGREZZA revenue
can flatten or decline even with a healthy prescription base.

### Pipeline binary risk
Neuroscience has a notoriously high Phase 3 failure rate, and MDD and schizophrenia
trials are especially prone to placebo response. Osavampator and direclidine are
funded by INGREZZA cash flow; failure of both would leave the company dependent on
CRENESSITY and VYKAT XR to replace INGREZZA.

### Soleno integration and valuation
\~\$2.36B in cash for an asset generating \~\$94M/quarter of pro forma sales is a
demanding price. \~\$2.2B of it sits on the balance sheet as intangible IP amortizing
over 16 years; if PWS market development disappoints, that carrying value is an
impairment candidate. It also consumed most of the securities portfolio — total cash
and AFS securities fell from \~\$2.54B at 12/31/25 to \~\$482M at 6/30/26.

### Reimbursement and policy
All three products are U.S.-centric and exposed to Medicaid/Medicare pricing, IRA
drug-price negotiation, PBM rebate pressure, and 340B dynamics. Neurocrine has minimal
ex-U.S. revenue to offset U.S. policy risk.

### Competition in muscarinics
Bristol Myers Squibb's COBENFY validated the M4 mechanism commercially but also
established an entrenched first mover. Direclidine enters a class that will be
contested by a much larger company by the time it could launch.

### Expense growth outpacing revenue
R&D nearly doubled across FY2023→FY2025 and is guided higher again. FCF margin is
volatile quarter to quarter (from +9.4% in q1-2025 to +48.5% in q4-2025) and the
strict ex-SBC view is materially thinner than the headline — SBC ran \$217.9M in FY2025.
