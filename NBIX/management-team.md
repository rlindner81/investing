# Neurocrine Biosciences — Management Team

Neurocrine completed a **generational leadership handover in October 2024**, when
co-founder-era CEO Kevin Gorman retired after 32 years with the company and 13 as CEO.
The successor, Kyle Gano, was an internal promotion from the business-development side
rather than a clinical or commercial one — a meaningful signal, since the strategy
since has leaned heavily on M&A and in-licensing (the Soleno acquisition, a steady
stream of early-stage licenses). The rest of the executive team has turned over
substantially in 2025–2026, so the group running the company today is materially
different from the one that built INGREZZA.

---

## Kyle W. Gano, Ph.D. — Chief Executive Officer

Appointed **President and CEO in October 2024**, succeeding Kevin Gorman; joined the
Board of Directors at the same time.

**Background:** A 25-year Neurocrine insider. Joined in 2001 and moved through roles
spanning marketing analytics to business development. Named Chief Business Development
Officer in 2011, then **Chief Business Development and Strategy Officer in 2020** — the
role he held until becoming CEO.

**Education:** B.S. in Chemistry (University of Oregon); B.S. in Biochemistry
(University of Washington); Ph.D. in Organic Chemistry and M.B.A. (both UCLA).

**Notable:** Serves on the Board of Directors of PhRMA, the industry trade group.

**What his appointment implies:** Gano's entire executive background is deal-making and
corporate strategy, not drug development or commercial operations. The strategic
posture since he took over is consistent with that — the \~\$2.9B Soleno acquisition
(\$2,358.5M net of cash acquired per the cash flow statement) closed May 2026 under his
first full year, alongside recurring IPR&D charges for smaller in-licensing deals. An
investor underwriting NBIX today is underwriting a capital-allocation CEO deploying
INGREZZA's cash flow into external assets.

---

## Matt Abernethy — Chief Financial Officer

CFO since **November 2017** — now the longest-tenured member of the senior team and
the continuity anchor through the CEO transition. His remit is unusually broad for a
biotech CFO: corporate finance plus information technology, investor relations,
facilities, and European operations.

**Background:** Came from **Zimmer Biomet** (Feb 2009 – Nov 2017), where he held roles
including VP of Investor Relations and Treasurer, and VP of Finance for the Americas
and Global Product Engines. Began his career at **KPMG LLP** as a CPA (inactive).

**Board service:** The Jacobs & Cushman San Diego Food Bank (since 2021); California
Life Sciences and **Fate Therapeutics** (both since 2025).

**Notable:** Abernethy delivers the guidance on every earnings call, and the guidance
convention is his: Neurocrine issues a **full-year INGREZZA net-product-sales range
only** — never consolidated revenue, never next-quarter guidance. He updates that
single range through the year (raised twice in 2024, narrowed mid-2025, raised again
in Q2-2026). Anyone modelling the company has to build CRENESSITY, VYKAT XR and
collaboration revenue independently, because guidance deliberately does not cover them.

## Eric Benevich — Chief Commercial Officer

Leads all commercial operations across the three marketed products. He is the executive
most directly accountable for the two things that drive the revenue line: INGREZZA's
volume-versus-net-price dynamic, and the CRENESSITY and VYKAT XR launch trajectories.

**Notable:** On the Q2-2026 call, Benevich framed the transformation explicitly —
five years earlier Neurocrine was a single-product company celebrating INGREZZA's first
\$1B year; by mid-2026 the portfolio was three first-in-class medicines with combined
quarterly sales approaching \$1B, annualizing toward \~\$4B. He also owns the
direct-to-consumer strategy that targets the roughly 7-in-10 U.S. tardive dyskinesia
patients the company believes remain undiagnosed.

## Sanjay Keswani, M.D. — Chief Medical Officer

Appointed **effective June 2, 2025** (announced April 4, 2025), succeeding
**Eiry W. Roberts, M.D.**, who had served seven years as CMO and stayed on in a
strategic advisory role. Leads clinical development and medical affairs.

**Background:** A physician-scientist with 20+ years in pharma. Immediately prior he was
**President and CEO of ImmunoBrain**, a clinical-stage neuroimmunology biotech
(he remains on its board). Before that, **SVP and R&D Head for Neuroscience,
Ophthalmology and Rare Diseases at Hoffmann-La Roche**, and earlier **VP of Exploratory
and Clinical Translational Research at Bristol-Myers Squibb**, spanning immunology,
neuroscience, rare disease, fibrosis and virology.

**Notable:** Gano's stated rationale for hiring him was to extend Neurocrine's reach
"beyond small molecule medicines" — i.e. new modalities. That is a deliberate widening
of a company whose entire commercial history is small-molecule chemistry. Keswani
inherits the two Phase 3 readouts (osavampator in MDD, direclidine in schizophrenia)
that carry the most valuation weight.

## Samir Siddhanti — Chief Business Officer

Appointed **July 21, 2026**, joining the executive Management Committee. Leads
enterprise growth strategy: business development, corporate strategy, alliance
management and program management.

**Background:** Joined Neurocrine in **2017** and rose through business development,
alliance management and corporate strategy, most recently as **VP, Business
Development** — in which role he led the Soleno Therapeutics acquisition. Prior to
Neurocrine he was on the **biotechnology equity research team at Goldman Sachs**.

**Education:** B.S. and M.S. in Management Science and Engineering (Stanford).

**Notable:** Also serves as **Program Team Leader for direclidine**, steering its
development in schizophrenia and bipolar disorder and shaping the broader muscarinic
portfolio strategy — an unusual dual mandate combining deal-making with hands-on
program leadership on the company's highest-profile pipeline asset. His promotion
effectively recreates, one level down, the business-development role Gano vacated.

## Corporate Governance

**Board chair:** **William H. Rastetter, Ph.D.** — director since February 2010,
Chairman since May 2011. He also chairs the boards of Fate Therapeutics and
Daré Bioscience. Note the overlap: CFO Abernethy joined the Fate Therapeutics board in
2025, where Rastetter is chairman — a shared-board relationship worth tracking, though
neither role is at Neurocrine itself.

**Founder on the board:** **Kevin C. Gorman, Ph.D.** remained a Neurocrine director
after retiring as CEO in October 2024, preserving institutional memory but also keeping
the former CEO in the boardroom during his successor's first years.

**Share structure:** A **single class of common stock** (\$0.001 par value, 220.0M
authorized) with 5.0M authorized preferred shares, none issued. One share, one vote —
no dual-class structure and no founder super-voting control, which distinguishes NBIX
from many of the founder-led names in this repository. Shareholders retain full voting
recourse on strategic decisions.

**Capital allocation record:** The board authorized and executed \$300.0M of repurchases
in FY2024, \$167.7M in FY2025, and \$66.0M in H1-2026, while the company remains
debt-free. Cash compensation is supplemented by substantial equity — stock-based
compensation was \$217.9M in FY2025 against \$478.6M of GAAP net income, so dilution
from equity awards is a real, recurring cost that the buybacks partly offset. Share
count has nonetheless crept up (99.4M at end-2024 → 101.6M at 6/30/26), meaning
repurchases have not fully absorbed issuance.

**Reporting convention to be aware of:** Neurocrine shifted its earnings release timing
between q3-2024 and q4-2024, from pre-market (\~07:00 ET, 8:00 AM ET call) to
after-close (\~16:02 ET, 4:30 PM ET call). This matters only for reading price
reactions, but it means the reaction bar moves from the announcement day to the
following session partway through the history.
