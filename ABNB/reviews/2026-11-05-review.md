**Revenue is accelerating.** Q3-2026 revenue was \$4,869M, up 18.9% Y/Y. Growth has gone from +12.0% in Q4-2025 to +17.9%, +16.5% and now +18.9% in the three 2026 quarters, the fastest in more than three years. TTM revenue is \$13.9B. Q4 guidance of \$3,220–3,280M (+16–18%) implies FY2026 growth of \~18%, against +10.3% in FY2025.

**FCF is strong and growing in dollars, but the margin is shrinking.** TTM company FCF is \$4,904M (35% margin) and Q3 FCF was \$1,426M, +5.7% Y/Y. The Q3 figure was held back by an \$82M New York property purchase and by Reserve Now, Pay Later (RNPL), which lets guests pay closer to the stay. RNPL pulls unearned fees down (now \$1.5B vs. \$1.7B at year end), so cash arrives later than it used to. The gap to strict ex-SBC FCF is material: SBC runs at \$1.77B TTM, so strict FCF is \$3.1B (22.5% margin). The buyback more than offsets the dilution: shares fell from 619M to 589M over six quarters (\~5%).

**Valuation is fair, not cheap.** At the \$170.13 pre-report close, ABNB trades at 7.2x TTM sales, 20.4x company FCF and 32.0x strict FCF. Forward P/S on Q4 guidance is 6.9–7.0x. After hours the stock traded near \$188–190, which reprices it to roughly 8.0x sales and \~22.7x FCF. That's reasonable for a high-teens grower with a 50%+ adjusted EBITDA margin in its peak quarter, but well above the 15–16x P/FCF trough of late 2025.

**Guidance credibility is excellent.** Every quarter in the table beat the midpoint of its next-quarter guide, by +0.9% to +3.3% (Q3: +2.9% above the top of the range). The qualitative full-year guide moved from "at least low double digits" (Feb) to "at least mid teens" (Aug) to "high teens" (Nov), three straight raises.

**The balance sheet is a fortress.** It holds \$12.6B of cash and short-term investments against \$2.37B of debt, so net cash is \~\$10B. A new \$12B buyback adds to the \$2.7B remaining on the old one, together \~15% of market cap.

**Verdict:** a reaccelerating, cash-rich compounder at a fair multiple. Most of the re-rating happened after hours, so the remaining upside depends on growth staying in the high teens into 2027.

**Rating:** +0.8 — BUY

## 3. News

**The surface narrative.** The story is Thursday's Q3 report: revenue of \$4.87B vs. \~\$4.75B consensus, diluted EPS of \$3.16 vs. \$2.85, adjusted EBITDA of \$2.51B vs. \~\$2.36B, the FY2026 growth guide raised to "high teens", and a new \$12B buyback. Shares rose \~11–12% after hours, and the CEO says Airbnb is "back in growth mode". Before the print, October news flow was a mix:
- **Analyst moves:** KeyBanc upgraded to Overweight (\$191 target), Oppenheimer raised its target to \$210, Bernstein reiterated \$217, Wells Fargo trimmed to \$185.
- **Partnership:** an American Express Travel deal (Oct 22) bringing 1M+ listings onto Amex Travel.
- **AI fears:** a recurring worry that AI travel agents disintermediate online travel platforms, which hit the whole group early in October.

**The deeper read.**
- **Q3 beat and raise: substantiated.** The 8-K letter and 10-Q confirm everything: revenue +19% (+16% ex-FX), nights and seats booked +16% (155.0M), GBV +21% to \$27.7B, net income \$1.9B, adjusted EBITDA margin 52%. Growth is broad: North America nights grew at a low-double-digit rate, the best in four years, EMEA mid-teens, Latin America and APAC 20%+. First-time bookers grew 13%, the highest since 2022. Q4 guidance of \$3.22–3.28B is a hard dollar range.
- **\$12B buyback: substantiated as an authorization, not as a commitment.** Actual buybacks ran \$2.85B over nine months. The program is a signal of intent; its real effect depends on execution pace. One outlet put it at "17% of market cap" using a \$70.7B cap, which is stale. At \~\$100B it is \~12%.
- **Analyst target changes: mostly attention.** They move with the price and carry no new data.
- **Amex partnership: real but small.** It adds distribution, but no economics were disclosed.
- **AI-agent fear: narrative.** No measurable impact shows in bookings. Airbnb's own data (40% of support issues resolved by AI, 66% of nights booked in-app) points the other way, and the in-app share makes Airbnb less exposed to agents booking through third parties.

**Price moves since Q2.** The stock rose from \~\$152 (Aug 6) to an August high near \$183 on the Q2 beat, then fell 12% in September with the whole travel group (BKNG -16.6%, EXPE -13.4%). That drop came from sector-wide de-rating and AI-agent worries, not from company data. The post-Q3 gap rests on reported numbers.

**Catalysts.** The Q4-2026 report is due in mid-February 2027 and should bring the first 2027 framing. Also: the 2027 rollout of the "AI-native" agent product, and the pace of buybacks under the new authorization.

**Sentiment.** Short interest is modest: 3.5% of float, 3.2 days to cover, 15.2M shares (2.6% of shares out), down from 21.2M a year ago. Insider selling is small, routine Form 144 activity. Institutional posture is constructive, with targets clustered at \$180–217.

**Key risks.**
- **EMEA:** management's outlook says it is "closely monitoring ongoing developments in EMEA". That is unspecified and worth watching.
- **Cash timing:** RNPL's expansion compresses take rate (17.6%, down Y/Y) and delays cash collection.
- **Q4 margin:** Q4 adjusted EBITDA margin is guided slightly down Y/Y on marketing timing.
- **Regulation:** short-term rental rules in big cities.
- **FX:** a \~2-point FX tailwind is baked into Q4 guidance and could reverse.

**Rating:** +1.2 — BUY

## Verdict

ABNB has reaccelerated to high-teens revenue growth, has raised its full-year outlook three times in 2026, and beat its own guide again in Q3. It throws off \~\$4.9B of TTM FCF and holds \~\$10B of net cash, and the new \$12B buyback authorization should keep shrinking the share count. The main risk is valuation after the gap: at \~\$189 the stock prices in sustained high-teens growth at \~23x FCF. That leaves little room for any EMEA slowdown, a reversal of the FX tailwind, or RNPL-driven FCF timing showing up as decelerating cash flow. A Q4 print that keeps nights growth at low double digits and guides 2027 to mid-teens or better would push this toward STRONG BUY. Growth falling back toward \~12% or the post-earnings gap failing to hold above the \$173 POC would push it to HOLD.

**Rating:** +0.9 — BUY
