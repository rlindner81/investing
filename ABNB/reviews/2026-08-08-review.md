Revenue is **re-accelerating**, which is the single most important thing in this table. Q2-2026 revenue of \$3,608M grew +16.5% Y/Y, on top of Q1's +17.9% — both well above the +9.7% to +12.7% band that ran through all of 2025. FY-2025 grew just +10.3%; the current fiscal year is tracking several points faster. That is a genuine inflection, not a comp artifact — Q2-2025 was already a +12.7% quarter.

FCF quality is excellent and improving. TTM operating cash flow is \$4,860M against zero reported PP&E capex (ABNB is asset-light and nets capex into other investing), so company FCF equals OCF at a **+36.9% margin**. Q2-2026 FCF of \$1,270M was +30.3% Y/Y, reversing the -4.5% and -7.2% prints of the prior two comparable quarters. The SBC gap is real but not alarming for a platform business: \$1,707M TTM SBC cuts FCF to \$3,153M strict ex-SBC, a **+24.0%** margin — still a genuinely cash-generative profile, and SBC is growing (+8.1% Y/Y) slower than revenue.

Valuation has re-rated hard on the print. TTM P/S of **8.0x** and P/FCF of **21.6x** sit at the top of the two-year range — compare 5.7x P/S and 15.0x P/FCF at the FY-2025 mark just six months ago. Forward P/S on Q3 guidance is 7.6x. So the multiple is no longer cheap; it is back to early-2024 levels (9.8x in q1-2024). The offsetting argument is that growth is materially better now than it was then and the guide implies +15–17%.

Guidance execution is metronomic: ABNB has beaten its own next-quarter guide **every single quarter** shown, by +0.9% to +3.3%. Balance sheet is clean — \$6.82B cash vs \$2.48B debt, ~\$4.3B net cash — and the share count is steadily shrinking (636M → 590M, -7.2% over two years) on consistent buybacks.

Verdict: a genuinely improving business, but the market repriced most of the good news in a single session.

**Rating:** +0.6 — BUY

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** One story dominates: the Q2-2026 print on Aug 6 (after close) and the ~18% two-session repricing that followed, taking ABNB to a four-year high. Headlines frame it as a beat-and-raise on "strong travel demand across all regions."

**Depth pass — this one is substantiated.** Unusually for a big gap, the move rests almost entirely on hard, disclosed numbers rather than narrative. Revenue \$3.61B (+17%) beat the \$3.58B consensus; EPS \$1.37 vs \$1.25 expected; net income \$816M vs \$642M. More important than the beat is the **volume acceleration**: Nights and Seats Booked reached **148.3M, +10% Y/Y, accelerating from Q1** — that is a unit metric, not a pricing or FX artifact, and it is the number that had been the bear case (ABNB's growth had been increasingly ADR-driven). Supporting disclosures are equally concrete: nights booked on the app +23% (now 64% of nights, from 59%), first-time booker growth +11% (highest in four years), and **origin-market growth accelerating in the US, France, UK and Australia** — i.e. the reacceleration is in the mature core, not just expansion markets. Q3 guidance of \$4.69–4.77B versus ~\$4.61B consensus is a committed number, and the FY revenue-growth outlook was raised to "at least mid-teens" from a lower prior framing.

Two further items are real but softer. The **AI cost story** — customer-support cost per booking down ~16%, with AI resolving ~45% of issues without a human agent — is disclosed and shows up in the 35% adjusted EBITDA margin, so it is substantiated, though "AI efficiency" is also the kind of claim every platform now makes. The **\$1.1B of Q2 buybacks** is verifiable and consistent with the share count falling 636M → 590M over two years.

**Price move vs. driver.** The +18% is running somewhat ahead of the fundamental delta — a ~5pp growth improvement and a modest guidance raise do not mechanically justify an 18% re-rate from 5.7x to 8.0x TTM sales. The gap is multiple expansion on restored confidence, which is the part that can be given back.

**Catalysts / sentiment / risk.** Next earnings ~early Nov 2026. Watch whether Services/Experiences (the 2025 relaunch) starts contributing disclosed revenue. Sentiment has flipped decisively bullish, and the stock now embeds premium expectations (~37x P/E) while sell-side models only ~11% forward revenue growth — a visible gap between price and consensus forecasts. Risks: consumer travel is cyclical and this is a late-cycle print; regulatory pressure on short-term rentals in European cities is persistent; and the Q3 guide implies deceleration from +16.5% toward +15–17% with FX help.

**Rating:** +1.2 — BUY

## Verdict

The business genuinely inflected this quarter: nights and seats booked accelerated to +10% Y/Y with the reacceleration showing up in mature core markets, revenue growth stepped up to +16.5%, and the FCF margin held at ~37% with an unbroken record of beating its own guidance. The problem is entirely price — an 18% single-session gap took TTM P/S from 5.7x to 8.0x, leaving the stock ~19% above its 20-day SMA with a volume-profile air pocket down to \$170 and a mean-reversion flag lit. The main risk is that this is a late-cycle consumer-travel print being awarded a premium multiple just as growth is guided to decelerate. A pullback into the \$160–170 shelf that holds would make this a clear buy; conversely, a Q3 report showing nights growth slipping back toward the mid-single digits would confirm the reacceleration was a one-quarter comp effect and undercut the re-rate.

**Rating:** +0.7 — BUY
