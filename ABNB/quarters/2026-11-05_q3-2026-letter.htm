Building a new kind of AI agent We don’t believe people will use one AI agent for everything. We believe the best agents will be specialized, built by companies that deeply understand the people they serve, have earned their trust, and can help them take action—not just answer questions in a chatbot. We’re building our own AI agent to help guests plan and book trips. Today’s AI agents are good at simple, routine tasks, like making a restaurant reservation or scheduling an appointment. But planning a trip on Airbnb is different. Travel is aspirational, and discovering where to go and where to stay is part of the experience. In fact, 80% of travelers enjoy spending time researching trips.3 People don’t want to delegate the choice of where to stay. They want to browse unique homes, compare options and decide for themselves, often with the people they’re traveling with. We see this in how guests use Airbnb. On average, more than 75% of guests use Airbnb maps when deciding where to stay, over 90% browse multiple listing photos, and 70% read reviews before booking. And with an average of 3 guests per booking, choosing a place to stay is often a shared decision. We believe Airbnb has three distinct advantages in building an agent for this experience. First, we have something that other agents don’t: nearly 20 years of experience bringing millions of hosts and guests together. We’ve learned how people search, book, and host, giving us a deep understanding of both sides of our marketplace. For example, more than 90% of stays involve direct communication between guests and hosts, with verified identities and reviews helping build trust. Everything we’ve learned about our marketplace gives us an advantage in building a personalized travel search and booking experience that outside agents can’t replicate. Second, we’re part of the reservation itself. We know that travel doesn’t end with a booking. Because Airbnb handles reservations, payments, and customer support, our agent will be able to help guests throughout their trip—from changing a reservation or resolving a payment issue to getting help with refunds, rebooking, or 24-hour safety support. These are things an outside agent can’t easily do on its own. Third, and perhaps most importantly, we’re building a new interface specifically for travel. We don’t think a traditional chatbot is the right way to plan a trip. We’re developing a custom-designed, AI-native experience that builds the interface around your question. Rather than a chat window, our agent draws on a library of hundreds of travel-specific interface elements—maps, photos, comparisons, calendars, itineraries—and composes them in real time to match what you’re looking for. Our agent will help guests explore options, make decisions with the people they’re traveling with, and take action directly in Airbnb. Over time, we want our agent to help guests plan their entire trip in one place, across homes, hotels, services, experiences, and more. We plan to start rolling it out to guests in the first half of 2027. 3 Skift and McKinsey’s seventh annual research collaboration, based on a study of 1,000 consumers and traveler diaries, as cited in Skift, “How Travel Search, Discovery and Booking Are Being Rewritten”.

Business and Financial Performance Q3 2026 155.0M Nights and Seats Booked 16% Y/Y $27.7B Gross Booking Value 21% Y/Y 21% Y/Y (ex-FX) In Q3 2026, Nights and Seats Booked grew 16% year-over-year—accelerating significantly compared to the year-over-year growth rate during Q2 2026—primarily driven by strong demand across all regions and continued momentum behind our product roadmap, including search and merchandising, pricing and tools, flexible payments, and sign-up and login. We’ve continued to see strong growth across market types and travel corridors, as well as an extension of lead times, in part due to Reserve Now, Pay Later (“RNPL”). Additionally, growth continued to be driven in part by our app strategy, with nights booked on our app in Q3 2026 increasing 29% year-over-year and accounting for 66% of total nights booked—up from 62% in the prior-year period. In addition, we saw year-over-year growth of first-time bookers accelerate to 13% in Q3 2026—the highest since early 2022—with acceleration seen in many of our largest markets. In Q3 2026, GBV was $27.7 billion and increased 21% year-over-year—a five percentage point sequential acceleration—driven by strong growth of Nights and Seats Booked, as well as a moderate increase in ADR. ADR was $179 in Q3 2026, increasing 4% compared to Q3 2025. On an ex-FX basis, ADR in Q3 2026 increased 5% year-over-year and was up across all regions—particularly North America and EMEA—primarily due to price appreciation. Geographic Performance In Q3 2026, we continued expanding globally by making Airbnb more locally relevant in each market. Our initiative to improve product-market fit and build brand preference is gaining traction and helping drive traffic and nights booked on our platform. In Q3 2026, net nights booked on an origin basis across our expansion markets increased at the fastest pace in almost three years, representing a meaningful acceleration compared to Q2 2026. • North America: In North America, we saw low-double digit growth of Nights and Seats Booked during Q3 2026 compared to Q3 2025—the highest growth we’ve seen in four years—driven by strong demand across market types and travel corridors, and an underlying improvement of inbound travel to the U.S. ADR in North America increased 6% in Q3 2026 compared to Q3 2025, driven by both price appreciation and mix shift. Growth in short-term stays and entire homes, particularly listings with four or more bedrooms, continued to outpace monthly stays (trips of 28 days or more) and private rooms, extending a mix-shift trend we have now seen for over a year.

• EMEA: In EMEA, we saw mid-teens growth in Nights and Seats Booked during Q3 2026 compared to Q3 2025, which represented an acceleration relative to the year-over-year growth rate seen in Q2 2026. We’ve observed a widespread improvement in demand across Europe, including a sequential acceleration of origin net nights across the UK, France, Germany, Spain, and Italy. ADR in EMEA increased 5% in Q3 2026 compared to Q3 2025. On an ex-FX basis, ADR increased 7% compared to Q3 2025, primarily driven by price appreciation and mix. • Latin America: In Latin America, we saw over 20% growth in Nights and Seats Booked during Q3 2026 compared to Q3 2025. ADR in Latin America increased 8% in Q3 2026 compared to Q3 2025, primarily driven by FX. On an ex-FX basis, ADR increased 4% compared to Q3 2025. Brazil continued to be a standout performer in Q3 2026, with year-over-year origin net nights booked growth sustaining over 30% and the number of first-time bookers increasing over 30%. And for the rest of Latin America (“ROLA”)—Chile, Argentina, Colombia and Peru—we saw origin net nights accelerate to growth of over 20%. • Asia Pacific: In Asia Pacific, we saw over 20% growth in Nights and Seats Booked during Q3 2026 compared to Q3 2025. ADR in Asia Pacific increased 1% in Q3 2026 compared to Q3 2025. Our hyper-local approach yielded positive results in countries like Japan, where in Q3 2026, origin net nights booked growth accelerated to the low-20s year-over-year, with the number of first-time bookers increasing approximately 35%. Additionally, in Q3 2026, we saw year-over-year origin net nights booked in India accelerate to over 60%—the second consecutive quarter of acceleration—with the number of first-time bookers increasing over 80%. Supply Active listings grew at a high-single-digit rate year-over-year in Q3 2026, consistent with recent quarters. Our supply acquisition strategy is disciplined and targeted, prioritizing the right homes, in the right place, at the right time. We’re emphasizing markets where demand is strongest, encouraging existing hosts to open more of their calendars where possible, and leveraging tools to help hosts more seamlessly onboard as well as price more competitively. This has resulted in growth in nights available increasing faster than total supply. Together with efforts to drive demand, this targeted supply approach is helping drive a higher fill rate, and ultimately more nights and earnings to our hosts.

Adjusted EBITDA is defined as net income or loss adjusted for (i) provision for (benefit from) income taxes; (ii) other income (expense), net; (iii) interest income; (iv) interest expense; (v) depreciation and

amortization; (vi) stock-based compensation expense; (vii) acquisition-related impacts consisting of gains (losses) recognized on changes in the fair value of contingent consideration arrangements; (viii) settlements and reserves for lodging, withholding, transactional and other non-income taxes where significant uncertainty exists as to how these taxes apply to users of our platform and Airbnb; and (ix) stock-settlement obligations, which represent employer and related taxes associated with our Initial Public Offering (“IPO”). The above items are excluded from our Adjusted EBITDA measure because these items are non-cash in nature, or because the amount and timing of these items is unpredictable, not driven by core results of operations and renders comparisons with prior periods and competitors less meaningful. Reconciliations of expected Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA Margins to corresponding net income (loss) and net income (loss) margins have not been provided because of the unpredictability of certain of the items excluded from Adjusted EBITDA and because we cannot determine their probable significance. We believe Adjusted EBITDA provides useful information to investors and others in understanding and evaluating our results of operations, as well as provides a useful measure for period-to-period comparisons of our business performance. Moreover, we have included Adjusted EBITDA in this letter because it is a key measurement used by our management internally to make operating decisions, including those related to operating expenses, evaluating performance, and performing strategic planning and annual budgeting. Adjusted EBITDA has limitations as a financial measure, should be considered as supplemental in nature, and is not meant as a substitute for the related financial information prepared in accordance with GAAP. These limitations include the following: • Adjusted EBITDA excludes provision for income taxes; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect interest income, interest expense, and other income (expense), net, which include unrealized and realized gains and losses on foreign currency exchange, investments, and financial instruments; • Adjusted EBITDA excludes certain recurring, non-cash charges, such as depreciation of property and equipment and amortization of intangible assets, and although these are non-cash charges, the assets being depreciated and amortized may have to be replaced in the future, and Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect all cash requirements for such replacements or for new capital expenditure requirements; • Adjusted EBITDA excludes stock-based compensation expense, which has been, and will continue to be for the foreseeable future, a significant recurring expense in our business and an important part of our compensation strategy; • Adjusted EBITDA excludes acquisition-related impacts consisting of gains (losses) recognized on changes in the fair value of contingent consideration arrangements. The contingent consideration, which was in the form of equity, was valued as of the acquisition date and is marked-to-market at each reporting period based on factors including our stock price; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect settlements and reserves for lodging, withholding, transactional and other non-income taxes where significant uncertainty exists as to how these taxes apply to users of our platform and Airbnb; and • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect stock-settlement obligations, which represent employer and related taxes related to our IPO.

Adjusted EBITDA Margin is defined as Adjusted EBITDA divided by revenue. Because of these limitations, you should consider Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA Margin alongside other financial performance measures, including net income (loss) and our other GAAP results. Free Cash Flow represents net cash provided by operating activities less purchases of property and equipment. We believe Free Cash Flow is a meaningful indicator of liquidity that provides information to management and investors about the amount of cash generated from operations that, after purchases of property and equipment, can be used for strategic initiatives, including continuous investment in our business, growth through acquisitions, and strengthening our balance sheet. Our Free Cash Flow is impacted by the timing of GBV because we collect our service fees at the time of booking, which is generally before a stay or experience occurs. Funds held on behalf of our hosts and guests and amounts payable to our hosts and guests do not impact Free Cash Flow, except interest earned on these funds. Free Cash Flow Margin is defined as Free Cash Flow divided by revenue. We track our TTM Free Cash Flow to account for the timing difference in when we receive cash from service fees, which is at the time of booking. Free Cash Flow has limitations as an analytical tool and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for analysis of other GAAP financial measures, such as net cash provided by operating activities. Free Cash Flow does not reflect our ability to meet future contractual commitments and may be calculated differently by other companies in our industry, limiting its usefulness as a comparative measure. TTM Free Cash Flow Margin is defined as TTM Free Cash Flow divided by TTM revenue. In addition to the actual amount or percentage change, we disclose the amount or percentage change in our current period results for revenue, GBV, and ADR from the corresponding prior period results by comparing results using constant currencies. We present constant currency amounts and change rate information to provide a framework for assessing how our revenue, GBV, and ADR performed excluding the effect of changes in foreign exchange rates. We use the amounts and percentage change in constant currency revenues, GBV, and ADR for financial and operational decision-making and as a means to evaluate period-to-period comparisons. We believe the presentation of results on a constant currency basis in addition to the U.S. GAAP presentation helps improve the ability to understand our performance because it excludes the effects of foreign currency volatility that are not indicative of our core operating results. We calculate the percentage change in constant currency by determining the change in the current period over the prior comparable period where current period foreign currency amounts are translated using the exchange rates of the comparative period.
