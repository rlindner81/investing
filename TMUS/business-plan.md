# T-Mobile US, Inc. (NASDAQ: TMUS) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

T-Mobile US is the second-largest US wireless carrier by postpaid customers and the self-styled "Un-carrier". It sells postpaid and prepaid wireless service, devices, and (more and more) home broadband through 5G fixed wireless and fiber, to consumers and businesses across the United States. The 2020 Sprint merger gave T-Mobile the 2.5 GHz mid-band spectrum that became its multi-year 5G network lead, and since then the strategy has been to turn that network lead plus a value position into industry-leading customer growth, service-revenue growth and free cash flow.

- **Founded:** 1994 as VoiceStream Wireless (a Western Wireless spin-off); acquired by Deutsche Telekom in 2001 and rebranded T-Mobile USA
- **Public listing:** May 2013 via the reverse merger with MetroPCS (NYSE: TMUS, later moved to NASDAQ)
- **Sprint merger:** closed April 1, 2020
- **Controlling shareholder:** Deutsche Telekom AG (majority of voting power)
- **Headquarters:** Bellevue, Washington (second HQ in Overland Park, Kansas)
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year)
- **FY2025 Revenue:** \$88,309M (+8.5% YoY); service revenue \$71.3B (+8%)

## Revenue Model

Revenue is reported as **service revenues** (\~80%) and **equipment revenues** (\~20%, device sales and leases), plus a small "other" line.

**Postpaid service (\~65% of total revenue, the core profit engine):**
- Monthly plans for phones, tablets, wearables and other connected devices, billed per account
- FY2025 postpaid service revenue \$57.9B (+11%); Q2-2026 \$15.9B (+13% YoY, including UScellular)
- From 2026 the company has reframed its headline KPIs around **accounts**: postpaid net account additions, account churn and **ARPA** (Q2-2026 ARPA \$152.91, +2% YoY). Phone-level net adds are no longer the guided metric.

**Prepaid service:**
- Metro by T-Mobile, Mint Mobile (acquired 2024) and other prepaid brands; low single-digit customer growth, churn \~2.7%

**Broadband (fastest-growing line):**
- **5G fixed wireless home internet:** 8.5M customers at end-2025; 1.9M net adds in 2025. It uses spare network capacity, so incremental margins are high.
- **Fiber:** built through joint ventures, **Lumos** (closed Q2-2025) and **Metronet** (closed 2025); 136K fiber net adds in 2025, scaling up
- Total broadband net customer adds of 2.0M in 2025

**Equipment revenue:**
- Handset sales, mostly on equipment installment plans (EIP). Low margin and volume-driven, and the main reason total revenue moves around from quarter to quarter (e.g. Q2-2026 total revenue fell 1.4% sequentially while service revenue grew).

**Other growth vectors:**
- **T-Mobile for Business / T-Priority:** enterprise and government share gains, network slicing on 5G standalone
- **Advertising:** Vistar Media and Blis (ad-tech acquisitions in 2025)
- **T-Satellite:** direct-to-cell service with SpaceX Starlink

## Network & Spectrum

- **5G lead:** first nationwide 5G standalone network; mid-band 2.5 GHz (from Sprint) plus 600 MHz low-band and C-band/3.45 GHz. Third-party awards in 2025–2026 include Ookla Best Mobile Network, Opensignal category sweeps, and J.D. Power network quality (5 of 6 regions in 2026).
- **Spectrum purchases:** lumpy cash outlays outside PP&E capex (Auction 108 in Q3-2024, about \$2.4B; \~\$1.6B in Q3-2025). They are tracked as `ytd_capex_spectrum` in FINANCIALS.yml.
- **Capex:** about \$10.0B of cash PP&E in 2025 and guided \~\$10.0B for 2026, higher than the \$8.8B in 2024 because of greenfield site builds and the UScellular network integration.

## Technology & Competitive Position

### Network + Value Combination
- T-Mobile's core claim is that it offers the best network *and* the best value. Historically carriers had to choose one: Verizon was the network premium, T-Mobile/Sprint the value players.
- Wireless NPS hit a record 46 in Q2-2026 (HarrisX), which management calls the highest ever for a big-three carrier.

### Scale and Cost Structure
- Sprint merger synergies (completed) structurally lowered cost per customer
- Digital and AI-driven customer care (T-Life app) plus a 2025 workforce transformation are expanding margins. Core Adjusted EBITDA is up roughly 7–12% YoY.

### Free Cash Flow Machine
- Adjusted FCF of \$13.6B (2023), \$17.0B (2024), \$18.0B (2025), guided \$18.4–18.8B for 2026
- Cumulative shareholder returns of \$54.6B from Q3-2022 through June 2026 (\$44.2B buybacks + \$10.4B dividends); the share count fell from 1,234M (Dec-2022) to 1,075M (Jun-2026)

## Growth Strategy

1. **Share gains in "network seeker" households:** management cites 20M+ families and businesses that still choose a rival for its network reputation, which T-Mobile now argues it has overtaken
2. **Account-level monetization:** move up ARPA with premium plans (Experience lineup), more lines per account, and bundling of broadband with wireless
3. **Broadband expansion:** grow FWA toward the Capital Markets Day targets and scale fiber through JVs
4. **UScellular integration:** synergy capture and migration of customers onto the T-Mobile network through 2026–2027
5. **Business segment and new revenue streams:** enterprise, government, advertising (Vistar/Blis), satellite
6. **Capital returns:** \$18.2B shareholder return authorization through end-2026; the dividend, first introduced in late 2023, is growing

## Key Risks

### Competitive Intensity
- Verizon and AT&T have responded with aggressive device promotions, price locks and their own FWA products. Cable MVNOs (Comcast, Charter) take a large share of industry phone net adds at low prices.
- The stock fell from \~\$255 (Apr 2025) to \~\$146 (Oct 2026) as the market questioned T-Mobile's ability to keep its customer-growth premium

### Saturated Wireless Market
- US postpaid phone industry growth is slowing (population, immigration, penetration), and the switch to account-based KPIs in 2026 was read by some as a move away from slowing phone net adds

### Leverage
- About \$85B of financial debt (ex. leases) against \$2–3B of cash; net debt is roughly 2.5x Core Adj. EBITDA. Spectrum auctions and M&A (UScellular, fiber JVs) compete with buybacks for cash.

### Integration
- UScellular, Metronet, Lumos, Mint, Vistar and Blis are all being integrated at once, and merger-related costs weigh on 2026 OCF/FCF

### Governance / Control
- Deutsche Telekom controls the majority of votes; minority holders have limited influence, and DT's own capital needs can affect TMUS capital allocation. SoftBank sold down most of its stake in 2025.

### Regulatory
- FCC spectrum policy and auction timing, merger reviews, and data-breach liability (T-Mobile settled class actions over its 2021 cyberattack)
