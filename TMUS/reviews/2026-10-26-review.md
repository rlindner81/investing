**Revenue** keeps compounding at a steady telecom clip with an acquisition boost. Q2-2026 revenue of \$22.8B was +7.9% Y/Y after +10.6% in Q1 and +11.3% in Q4-2025. Growth stepped up from \~3–7% in 2024 to \~8.5% in FY-2025, but much of that came from deals (UScellular, plus the fiber JVs) rather than organic acceleration. TTM revenue is \$92.2B. T-Mobile gives no revenue guidance, so every forward P/S row is `n/m`. The useful guidance is Core Adj. EBITDA and adj. FCF. Both were raised at almost every report from 2024 to 2026. The FY2026 adj. FCF range moved \$18.0–18.7B → \$18.1–18.7B → \$18.4–18.8B, and EBITDA sits at \$37.1–37.5B.

**FCF** is the core of the case. TTM company FCF (OCF − PP&E capex) is \$18.4B, a \~20% margin, and it lines up with management's own adj. FCF guide. One caveat: for 2023–2024 the company's figure included securitization proceeds, so the FY-2024 column (\$13.5B vs. the company's \$17.0B) understates their number. Y/Y FCF growth has slowed sharply, from +34% in FY-2025 to just +4–5% in Q1/Q2-2026. After spectrum purchases and SBC (\$866M TTM, a modest \~5% of FCF), strict FCF is \$15.4B. Spectrum spend comes in lumps (\$2.6B in FY-2025) and is a real, recurring cost of doing business.

**Valuation** has compressed hard. At \$145.58 the market cap is \$156.5B, about half of the \$290B peak at Q1-2025. That puts the stock at 1.7x TTM sales, 8.5x company FCF and 10.2x strict FCF, down from 18–19x a year ago. An \~11.8% FCF yield is very cheap for the industry's growth leader, and it comes alongside a 6% drop in share count over two years from buybacks.

**Balance sheet:** debt is \$84.6B against \$2.8B of cash, so net debt is \~\$81.8B before lease liabilities, or \~2.2x the guided EBITDA. That is normal leverage for a carrier, but it amplifies equity moves if growth stalls.

**Verdict:** on the reported numbers TMUS is the cheapest it has been in years. The market is pricing in that a fourth carrier will erode its growth, not the current cash flow.

**Rating:** +1.2 — BUY

## 3. News

**Surface narrative:** SpaceX as a fourth US carrier is the only story in town. On Oct 8, Grain Management said SpaceX will buy its entire nationwide 800 MHz portfolio (up to 14 MHz paired). The WSJ reported the price at \~\$8B in cash. On Oct 9 TMUS fell \~13% to \$148.58, its worst day since 2013. AT&T fell \~10% and Verizon \~9%, together losing about \$39B of market value. On Oct 22, Bloomberg reported that SpaceX is in talks to access at least 80,000 US cell sites, with Crown Castle named. TMUS dropped another 7.7% on Oct 23 to \$146.54, a one-year low, while CCI and AMT rallied. YTD the stock is down \~20%.

**Deeper read, item by item:**
- **800 MHz deal: substantiated.** Grain announced it on the record and there is a signed agreement, subject to FCC approval, which the trade press expects to be a formality under Chairman Carr. It follows SpaceX's \~\$17B and \$2.6B EchoStar spectrum buys and FCC approval of 15,000 direct-to-cell satellites plus a waiver to sell service without a terrestrial partner. The irony is that this is spectrum T-Mobile itself sold to Grain (closed Aug 2026). The threat to the long-run industry structure is real. What is still missing is any timeline, pricing, capacity plan or capex commitment from SpaceX for a terrestrial network. Owning coverage spectrum is not the same as having a carrier's capacity.
- **80,000 tower sites: mostly attention for now.** It is single-source "people familiar" reporting with no signed deal and no disclosed value, and American Tower's involvement is unconfirmed. Crown Castle's CEO said only "we're talking to all parties." It is plausible and consistent with the spectrum buy, but a 7.7% one-day move is running ahead of anything concrete.
- **Analyst actions: attention.** Bernstein cut its target from \$220 to \$180 (Neutral), while Raymond James upgraded to Outperform. The consensus target of \~\$245 is far above the price and mostly stale.
- **Rate-plan migration: substantiated, and self-inflicted.** On the Q2 call, management guided Q3 to only \~250K postpaid account net adds because of a deliberate legacy-plan migration that temporarily raises churn. Q3 guidance is also service revenue of \~\$19.3B (+6%) and Core Adj. EBITDA of \~\$9.4B (+8%). FY2026 guidance (EBITDA \$37.1–37.5B, adj. FCF \$18.4–18.8B) was reaffirmed and raised.

**Catalysts:** Q3-2026 results come **Wed Oct 28 after the close** (release \~4:05 pm ET, call 4:30 pm). Consensus is EPS \~\$2.86 and revenue \~\$23.3B. Investors will focus on account adds vs. the 250K guide, ARPA, churn and any commentary on SpaceX. AT&T's Q3 (379K postpaid phone adds, raised EPS outlook) shows demand holding up across the industry. Other pending items are the FCC decision on the Grain transfer and any signed SpaceX tower deal.

**Sentiment:** retail sentiment is dominated by the "Starlink kills telcos" narrative. Short interest is low (\~2% of shares, 3.0% of float, 2.8 days to cover), so this is long-holder liquidation, not a crowded short. The quarterly dividend was reported raised to \$1.17 (\~3.2% yield), and the buyback is ongoing.

**Risks:** a well-funded new entrant pressuring pricing and ARPA; churn from the rate migration running above plan; \~\$82B net debt plus leases; and multiple compression for the whole sector regardless of how T-Mobile executes.

**Rating:** -0.6 — SELL

## Verdict

The numbers and the narrative disagree. T-Mobile is still growing revenue \~8%, keeps nudging its EBITDA/FCF guidance higher, and now trades at \~8.5x FCF, half its multiple of a year ago. The tape, meanwhile, is in a clear distribution downtrend driven by SpaceX's credible but still unbuilt move toward becoming a fourth carrier. The main risk is that SpaceX turns signed spectrum and tower access into real price competition, which would justify a structurally lower multiple. On a shorter horizon, Q3 churn from the rate-plan migration could overshoot. The rating would move up if the Oct 28 print holds the \~250K account-add guide and the FCF range and the stock reclaims the 20-day SMA (\~158). It would move down on a guidance cut or a signed SpaceX tower deal with a launch timeline.

**Rating:** -0.2 — HOLD
