# Ero Copper Corp. — Management Team

Ero is run from **Vancouver** (Suite 1050, 625 Howe Street), while operations,
mining leadership, and the bulk of the workforce sit in **Brazil**. The executive
team reflects that split: corporate finance and strategy in Canada, operating
leadership in-country.

## Makko DeFilippo — President and Chief Executive Officer

DeFilippo became **CEO effective January 2025**, succeeding co-founder David
Strang in a planned, well-signposted succession. He had previously served as
**President and Chief Operating Officer**, and before that led corporate
development — so he took the top job having already run the Tucumã build and the
operating portfolio rather than arriving from outside.

His first full year in the seat (FY2025) was the company's inflection: revenue up
**+67%** to \$785.8M, a swing from a \$67.8M net loss to \$266.9M of net income,
and free cash flow positive for the first time after two years of construction
spend. The agenda he set out publicly in early 2025 — **deleverage the balance
sheet, ramp Tucumã, take cost out** — is the frame he has reported against since.
On the Q2-2026 call he tied the quarter back to exactly those "core commitments
we made to our shareholders in early 2025."

He chairs the quarterly earnings calls and signs the financial statements as a
director.

## Wayne Drier — Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer

Drier is the continuity figure across the CEO transition: he was CFO under Strang
and remained CFO under DeFilippo, and has presented the financial section of
every earnings call through the period covered here. His title was elevated to
**Executive Vice President and CFO** alongside the 2025 leadership change.

The CFO agenda in this cycle has been balance-sheet repair and FX management:

- **Deleveraging.** Net debt down to **\$452.7M** at Q2-2026, a **0.8x** net
  debt/TTM adjusted EBITDA ratio, with \$60M of revolver repayments in 2026
  including \$25M after quarter end.
- **FX hedging.** A program covering \~**70%** of consolidated full-year operating
  and capital costs at an average USD/BRL floor of **5.54** — \$19.9M realized
  gains in H1-2026, \$40–45M expected for the full year. This matters
  disproportionately at Ero, where costs are BRL and revenue is USD.

## Gelson Batista — Executive Vice President and Chief Operating Officer

Batista leads operations across all three Brazilian mines — Caraíba, Tucumã, and
Xavantina. He is the in-country operating voice on earnings calls, handling the
throughput, grade, recovery, and cost questions that drive the quarter.

The operating story under his tenure has been the **Tucumã ramp** (sequential
throughput gains, the Q2-2026 tailings filtration expansion adding \~8% capacity
with more modular filters due in H2), the **Pilar shaft** project extending
Caraíba at depth, and the **Xavantina turnaround** — where ventilation and cooling
infrastructure tied in during H1-2026 opened access to higher-grade stopes and
lifted gold output 170% quarter-over-quarter.

## Courtney Lynn — Executive Vice President, External Affairs and Strategy

Lynn covers strategy, external affairs, and capital markets, and appears on
earnings calls alongside the CEO, CFO, and COO. The remit spans government and
community relations in Brazil — material for a single-jurisdiction miner whose
permits, royalties, and social licence all sit with one country — as well as
corporate strategy and the **Furnas Copper-Gold Project** development pathway.

## Farooq Hamed — Vice President, Investor Relations

Hamed opens and moderates the quarterly earnings calls, handles the
forward-looking-statements disclaimer, and manages the analyst relationship. He
is the practical point of contact for the sell-side coverage.

## David Strang — Co-Founder and former Chief Executive Officer

Strang **co-founded** Ero and led it as CEO from its 2016 incorporation through
the **TSX listing (2017)**, the **NYSE listing (2021)**, and the Tucumã
construction decision. He handed the CEO role to DeFilippo effective **January
2025**; the **Q3-2024 call (November 5, 2024) was his last as CEO**, where he
closed by thanking shareholders "for the last eight years" and handing the next
quarterly to DeFilippo.

His tenure defined the company's shape — the MCSA acquisition, the Brazilian
asset base, and the decision to build Tucumã through a heavy negative-FCF period
(FY2023 company FCF −\$284.1M, FY2024 −\$183.5M) that only paid off in FY2025.

## Corporate Governance Notes

- **Dual listing, single symbol.** Ero trades as **ERO** on both the **TSX** and
  the **NYSE**. The TSX is the home listing — relevant in practice because
  StockAnalysis.com keys transcripts under `quote/tsx/ERO/`, not the US path.
- **MJDS filer.** As a Canadian issuer using the Multijurisdictional Disclosure
  System, Ero files an annual **40-F** and furnishes quarterly results on **Form
  6-K** — there are no 10-Qs or 10-Ks. Each earnings 6-K carries three exhibits:
  financial statements, MD&A, and the press release. Canadian continuous-disclosure
  documents (AIF, management information circular) are filed to **SEDAR+** and
  furnished to EDGAR on 6-K.
- **Board sign-off.** The FY2025 financial statements were approved on behalf of
  the board by **Makko DeFilippo** (President, CEO and Director) and **Jill
  Angevine** (Director).
- **Non-controlling interests.** Ero does **not** own its subsidiaries outright:
  **99.6%** of Mineração Caraíba S.A. and **97.6%** of NX Gold S.A. The minority
  stakes are small but real, so consolidated figures slightly overstate the
  economics attributable to Ero shareholders (FY2025 net income \$266.9M total vs.
  **\$263.7M** attributable to owners of the company).
- **Cash-settled compensation.** A significant portion of executive equity awards
  (PSUs, RSUs, DSUs) is **cash-settled and remeasured to the share price each
  period**. This makes reported share-based compensation volatile and occasionally
  negative — FY2024 SBC of \$10.0M was *below* the nine-month figure of \$17.5M,
  implying a **−\$7.5M** Q4 reversal as the share price fell. Expect comp expense
  to move inversely with the stock.
- **Non-IFRS measures.** C1 cash cost, AISC, adjusted EBITDA, and net debt
  leverage are all non-IFRS and reconciled at the back of each press release.
  They are the metrics management guides and is judged on — but they are not
  standardized and are not comparable across issuers.
