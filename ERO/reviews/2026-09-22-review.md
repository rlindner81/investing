**Revenue trend.** ERO has gone through a genuine step-change, not a drift. TTM revenue is \$1,044.7M — the first time above \$1B — against FY2025 \$785.8M and FY2024 \$470.3M. Growth has been extraordinary but is decelerating off the Tucumã ramp base: +161.3% in q4-2025, +110.4% in q1-2026, +73.9% in q2-2026. That deceleration is arithmetic, not deterioration — the comparison base is now a fully ramped Tucumã. Absolute sequential revenue still climbed, \$263.2M → \$284.3M.

**FCF profile.** The more important story. FCF was deeply negative through the Tucumã construction years (FY2024 −\$183.5M, every quarter red) and flipped positive in FY2025 at \$132.4M. TTM company FCF is now \$193.5M at an 18.5% margin, with q2-2026 alone at \$57.3M (20.2% margin). Capex has stopped growing (TTM \$276.5M vs FY2024 \$329.0M) while operating cash flow more than tripled — that is the whole deleveraging thesis in two lines. The gap to strict ex-SBC FCF (\$152.2M TTM) is moderate, \~\$41M, made up of exploration capex (\$21.9M) and SBC (\$19.4M). SBC is small relative to FCF, but note it is largely cash-settled and remeasured to the share price, so it swings with the stock (a −\$7.5M reversal in q4-2024) and will run hotter after the 2026 rally.

**Valuation.** TTM P/S 3.5x and P/FCF 18.7x company / 23.8x strict. For a copper producer, 3.5x sales is not cheap — it sits at the top of ERO's own two-year range (2.6–3.8x) and reflects the re-rating that followed the ramp. P/FCF near 19x is defensible only if FCF holds, and FCF here is a leveraged function of the copper price, not a contracted stream. Forward P/S is undefined: ERO guides production, cost and capex, never revenue (`prod-guided` on every column), so there is no forward multiple to anchor on — you are underwriting a metal price.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt \~\$452M (\$102M cash vs \$554M debt), down steadily from \$642M gross / \$561M net a year ago — real, visible deleveraging funded by FCF rather than equity. Shares out are flat at 104.3M; no dilution. Short interest eased to 3.4% from a 5.1% peak in q3-2025.

**Verdict:** a genuinely transformed cash-generating business, but the market has already paid for the transformation — the multiple now leans on copper staying where it is.


**Rating:** 0.4 — HOLD

## 3. News

**The surface narrative.** Three things are moving ERO right now: a Capital Markets Day on Sep 14 in São Paulo, a cluster of analyst downgrades immediately preceding it, and — the dominant factor nobody is putting in the headline — copper at record highs. The stock ran +35.7% in August, spiked to \$38.25 on Sep 9, then gave back to \$34.70.

**Copper is the real driver, and it is substantiated.** Copper is \~\$6.75/lb as of Sep 22, up \~47% year-over-year and \~2% in the past month. This rests on hard data, not narrative: global mined copper output fell 1.1% y/y in H1-2026 and may decline for the first time since 2017, with disruptions in Indonesia and the DRC removing an estimated 600,000 tonnes; the Yangshan premium hit \$121/t, its highest since November 2022. For a price-taking producer with no revenue guidance, this *is* the thesis — ERO's August outperformance (+35.7% vs COPX +17.0%) is operating leverage on a rising metal price, which is real. The important counterweight is equally concrete: Macquarie models a 262kt refined surplus in 2026 and \>700kt annually in 2027–28, and the ICSG reported a 98kt H1-2026 surplus. The physical tightness driving spot and the projected refined surplus are pointing opposite directions, and LME inventories have been reported at multi-year highs. Proposed US copper tariffs (15% from January 2027, 30% from 2028, pending a presidential decision) are distorting trade flows and flattering price — a policy artifact, not demand.

**Capital Markets Day — substantiated but long-dated.** The Sep 14 event disclosed a 2026–2028 production outlook (Caraíba 35–50kt Cu, Tucumã 32.5–40kt Cu, Xavantina 40–60koz Au) and positioned Furnas as the growth cornerstone. Furnas is backed by a filed technical report (PEA filed Mar 30, 2026): after-tax NPV(8%) \$2.04B, 27.0% IRR, 3.1-year payback, \~108kt CuEq annually over 15 years at first-quartile \~\$0.24/lb, 24-year mine life, 200,000m drilled with 11 rigs turning. That is a real document with real drill holes behind it. But the substantiation is about the *resource*, not the *cash flow*: PFS in 2027, FID targeted 2029, and \$1.28B initial capex against a \$3.6B market cap and \$452M of existing net debt. The PEA also uses a \$4.60/lb copper deck — conservative versus spot, which is genuinely favorable — while the 8% discount rate sits at the aggressive end. Furnas supports the multiple; it does not yet fund anything.

**Q2-2026 was substantiated.** EPS \$0.83 vs \$0.82 expected, revenue \$284.3M (+8% sequential), operating cash flow +50% to \$138M, net debt down \$38M to \$452.7M, leverage 0.8x from a 2.6x peak in early 2025. Copper guidance maintained at 67,500–77,500t. The tailings filtration expansion (+8% capacity, three modular filters commissioning in Q4) is explicitly *excluded* from 2026 guidance — a rare case of unguided upside with hardware attached.

**Mostly attention.** The downgrade cluster — BTG Pactual to Neutral (Sep 1), Bradesco BBI to Neutral (Sep 2), Freedom Broker to Hold (Sep 3) — carries no new information. All are explicitly valuation calls after the run, and Bradesco reversed its own July upgrade after six weeks; BofA had raised its target to \$44 from \$34 on Aug 26. Consensus targets rose 29.7% over three months, so the analyst complex is chasing price, not leading it. Note also the negatives in guidance the rally has ignored: gold production is tracking the *low end* of 40–50koz, gold C1 was raised to \$1,100–1,350/oz and AISC to \$2,200–2,700/oz, and total 2026 capex was raised to \$285–330M.

**Catalysts and risk.** Next earnings Nov 10, 2026 — the first quarter to test the H2-weighted production promise, with Q4 filter commissioning behind it. Furnas PFS in 2027. Short interest is 3.4% of shares out (5.6% of float, 4.0 days to cover), down from a 5.1% peak — no squeeze setup. Key risks: 100% single-country Brazilian exposure; a stronger BRL raising USD costs against a \~70% hedge at a 5.54 floor that rolls off; copper reversing toward the modeled surplus; and \$1.28B of eventual Furnas capex needing a funding answer.

## Verdict

ERO has completed the hard part: Tucumã is ramped, TTM revenue crossed \$1B, free cash flow swung from −\$183.5M in FY2024 to +\$193.5M trailing, and net debt fell to \$452.7M at 0.8x EBITDA from a 2.6x peak — all funded internally with shares flat at 104.3M. The driver from here is almost entirely the copper price, which is at record highs on genuinely tight mine supply, and the main risk is that the same lever works in reverse: at 3.5x sales and 18.7x FCF the stock sits at the top of its own two-year multiple range, so a move back toward the refined surplus most forecasters model for 2027–28 would compress both earnings and multiple at once. Technically the setup is constructive but not urgent — a stacked monthly uptrend with late-day accumulation, capped by the 35.2 POC and supported at 33.3. The rating would go higher on Nov 10 if H2-weighted production lands and the unguided filtration upside shows in throughput, or if management pairs Furnas with a funding structure that does not lean on equity; it would go lower on a copper rollover, a materially stronger BRL as the 5.54 hedge floor rolls off, or a second consecutive quarter of gold guidance slipping.


**Rating:** 0.7 — BUY
