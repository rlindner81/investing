# Ero Copper Corp. (NYSE/TSX: ERO) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

**Ero Copper Corp.** is a Vancouver-headquartered base-metals producer whose
operating assets are all in **Brazil**. It was incorporated on **May 16, 2016**
under the *Business Corporations Act* (British Columbia), listed on the **Toronto
Stock Exchange** in 2017, and added a **New York Stock Exchange** listing in 2021
(symbol **ERO** on both). Head office: Suite 1050, 625 Howe Street, Vancouver,
BC, V6C 2T6, Canada.

- **Fiscal year:** calendar year (ends December 31).
- **Reporting:** IFRS, presentation currency **US dollars** — notable, because
  the company is Canadian and its operating subsidiaries are Brazilian with the
  **BRL as functional currency**. No FX translation is needed to value the stock.
- **SEC status:** Canadian **MJDS filer** — an annual **40-F** plus quarterly
  results furnished on **Form 6-K**; there are no 10-Qs or 10-Ks.
- **Latest fiscal year (FY2025):** revenue **\$785.8M**, net income **\$266.9M**,
  operating cash flow **\$395.1M**.
- **Trailing twelve months (through Q2-2026):** revenue **\$1,044.7M** — ERO
  crossed the \$1B revenue mark for the first time.

Ero holds its assets through two Brazilian subsidiaries:

| Subsidiary | Ownership | Assets |
|---|---|---|
| Mineração Caraíba S.A. (MCSA), via Ero Brasil Participações Ltda | **99.6%** | Caraíba Operations (Bahia); Tucumã Operation (Pará) |
| NX Gold S.A., via Ero Gold Corp. | **97.6%** | Xavantina Operations (Mato Grosso) |

## Revenue Model

Ero sells **copper concentrate** (with gold and silver as by-products) and
**gold** (doré and concentrate, with silver as a by-product). Revenue is
essentially **production volume × prevailing metal price**, net of treatment and
refining charges levied by smelters. There is no subscription, backlog, or
contracted-price component to smooth results — realized price moves with the LME
copper and spot gold markets, so revenue is inherently cyclical and outside
management's control.

This is why **Ero issues no revenue guidance** (see *Guidance Cadence* below).

## Product / Asset Portfolio

**Caraíba Operations (Bahia)** — the legacy asset and historical core of MCSA.
Underground copper mine feeding a mill that produces copper concentrate. Q2-2026
production: **8,351 tonnes** of copper in concentrate at a C1 cash cost of
**\$2.76/lb** — the company's highest-cost operation. Growth capital is going into
a **new Pilar Mine shaft** and ancillary infrastructure (\~\$80M of 2026 capex) to
extend mine life and lower haulage costs at depth.

**Tucumã Operation (Pará)** — the company's transformational growth project.
Construction completed and first production achieved in **H2 2024**; it ramped
through 2025. Q2-2026 production: **8,964 tonnes** of copper in concentrate at a
C1 cash cost of **\$2.10/lb**. An open-pit operation with materially better unit
economics than Caraíba. A tailings filtration expansion completed at the end of
Q2-2026 lifted capacity \~8%, with further modular filters due in H2 2026 — and
those benefits are **not** in 2026 guidance.

**Xavantina Operations (Mato Grosso)** — a high-grade underground gold mine held
through NX Gold, producing gold doré and gold in concentrate. Q2-2026 gold output
**20,553 oz** (+170% q/q), of which **8,693 oz** from mine production and
**11,860 oz** recovered from **historic concentrate stockpiles** at a strikingly
low C1 of \$633/oz. Strongly seasonal: the rainy season slows concentrate drying
and depresses Q1 sales.

**Furnas Copper-Gold Project** — the principal development-stage asset, advancing
through exploration, engineering and permitting (\$30–40M of 2026 capex alongside
other exploration).

## Competitive Position and Moat

Ero's advantage is **cost position and jurisdictional concentration**, not
technology:

- **Low-cost new supply.** Tucumã's \~\$2.10/lb C1 sits well below Caraíba's, and
  new, permitted, producing copper supply is scarce globally. Copper demand is
  structurally supported by electrification and grid build-out.
- **Brazilian operating depth.** MCSA has operated in Bahia for decades; Ero has
  the permits, workforce, and relationships that are slow and expensive to
  replicate.
- **BRL cost base against USD revenue.** Costs are incurred in Brazilian reais
  while metal sells in dollars — a natural margin tailwind when the BRL weakens,
  and a headwind when it strengthens. Management runs an explicit **FX hedge
  program** covering \~70% of consolidated full-year operating and capital costs
  at an average USD/BRL floor of **5.54**, which generated **\$19.9M** of realized
  gains in H1-2026 with **\$40–45M** expected for the full year.
- **OneEro program.** A company-wide efficiency initiative running \~18 months as
  of mid-2026: \~**\$10–15M** of annualized savings on renegotiated supply and
  third-party contracts, plus improved smelting/refining terms worth **\>\$20M**
  in 2026.

The moat is real but shallow in the usual sense — this is a price-taking
commodity producer. The durable edge is being a **low-cost producer with a young,
ramping asset** and a deleveraging balance sheet.

## Markets and Channel Mix

Copper concentrate and gold are sold into global commodity markets through
offtake and smelter arrangements; there is no retail or direct channel. All
production is **Brazilian**, so the entire asset base carries a single country's
political, tax, permitting, currency, and infrastructure risk. Copper is the
dominant exposure (\~77% of Q2-2026 revenue), with gold a meaningful high-margin
minority (\~23%).

## Growth Strategy

1. **Ramp Tucumã to sustained nameplate.** Sequential throughput improvements and
   incremental tailings filtration capacity, with upside not yet in guidance.
2. **Extend and deepen Caraíba.** The new Pilar shaft is the largest single growth
   capital item, aimed at mine life and cost at depth.
3. **Lift Xavantina gold.** Ventilation and cooling infrastructure tied in during
   H1-2026 unlocked higher-grade stopes; a mobile filter press and industrial
   dryer commissioned at the end of Q2-2026 shorten concentrate drying and free
   the historic stockpiles.
4. **Advance Furnas.** Exploration, engineering, and permitting toward a future
   development decision.
5. **Deleverage.** Net debt fell to **\$452.7M** at Q2-2026 (net debt/TTM adjusted
   EBITDA of **0.8x**), with a further \$25M repaid after quarter end — \$60M of
   2026 revolver repayments in total.
6. **OneEro cost-out**, as above.

## Guidance Cadence — an important modelling note

**Ero never guides revenue.** Every quarterly release guides **annual production
(copper tonnes, gold ounces), C1 cash cost, AISC, and capital expenditures**.
Because revenue is production × an unforecast metal price, there is no revenue
guidance to model against, and forward P/S is undefined. `FINANCIALS.yml` records
this with `guidance_fy_hint: prod-guided` on every quarter rather than inventing
numbers — the same convention used for [KGC](../KGC/FINANCIALS.yml).

## Key Risks

- **Copper and gold price risk.** The dominant driver of results, entirely
  outside management's control. Revenue swings with the cycle.
- **Single-country concentration.** 100% of production is Brazilian: tax,
  permitting, royalty, labour, infrastructure, and political risk are undiversified.
- **BRL/USD exchange rate.** A stronger BRL raises USD-denominated operating and
  capital costs. Hedged \~70% for 2026 at a 5.54 floor — but the hedge is partial
  and rolls off.
- **Execution and ramp risk at Tucumã.** The FY2025 revenue step-up depends on the
  ramp holding; throughput, grade, and tailings capacity are the constraints.
- **Grade and reserve risk.** 2026 guidance explicitly reflects **lower planned
  copper grades** at both copper mines; guidance assumes reserve estimates and
  metallurgical recovery hold.
- **Leverage.** Net debt \$452.7M. Deleveraging fast at 0.8x EBITDA, but a
  sustained copper price fall would re-tighten it quickly.
- **Seasonality.** Xavantina's rainy season depresses Q1 concentrate sales, making
  quarters non-comparable sequentially.
- **Capital intensity.** Even post-Tucumã, sustaining plus growth capex runs
  \$285–330M/yr against \$395M of FY2025 operating cash flow — FCF is thin and
  price-dependent.
- **Cash-settled share comp.** A large share of awards are cash-settled and
  remeasured to the share price, so SBC expense swings with the stock and can go
  negative (Q4-2024 booked a **−\$7.5M** reversal).

**\$95.7M gain** in FY2025 — a reminder of how much reported earnings move on FX
rather than operations.
