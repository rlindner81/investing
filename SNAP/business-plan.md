# Snap Inc. (NYSE: SNAP) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

Snap Inc. is a camera and communication company whose primary product is Snapchat, one of the most widely used social media applications globally among Gen Z and Millennial users. Snap's core thesis: the camera is the most human way to communicate, and augmented reality (AR) will become the defining computing interface for the next generation. The company generates nearly all revenue from digital advertising sold inside Snapchat, supplemented by a growing subscription tier (Snapchat+).

- **Founded:** 2011 (as Snapchat Inc.); rebranded Snap Inc. in September 2016
- **IPO:** March 2, 2017 (NYSE: SNAP), raised ~$3.4B at $17/share (~$24B market cap at open)
- **Headquarters:** Santa Monica, California
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year)
- **FY2024 Revenue:** $5,357.8M (+14.3% YoY)

---

## Revenue Model

Snap generates revenue primarily through digital advertising sold to brands and direct-response (DR) advertisers within the Snapchat app. Two revenue streams:

**Advertising (>95% of revenue):**
- **Direct Response Ads:** Performance-based ads optimized for app installs, website visits, and purchases. The fastest-growing segment as Snap improved its measurement and targeting infrastructure.
- **Brand / Awareness Ads:** Snap Ads (full-screen video), Sponsored Lenses (AR-powered brand experiences), Sponsored Filters
- **Dynamic Ads / Catalog Ads:** Product feed-based ads for e-commerce advertisers

**Snapchat+ Subscription (~16% of revenue, growing):**
- Launched June 2022 at $3.99/month (later $4.99/month in some markets)
- Exclusive features: custom app icons, friend solar system, AI-powered tools (Memories, Lens+), early access to experimental features
- Reached 24M subscribers in Q4 2025 (+71% YoY); "Other Revenue" (primarily Snapchat+) was $232M in Q4 2025 (+62% YoY)
- Growing faster than ad revenue and carries high incremental gross margins; key strategic lever to diversify from advertising cycle volatility

**Geographic revenue segments (Q4 2025):**

|---|---|---|---|

## Products

### Snapchat (Core App)
The flagship product, used by 453M daily active users as of Q4 2024. Core surfaces:
- **Camera / Snaps:** Ephemeral photo/video messages; the founding product feature
- **Stories:** 24-hour photo/video content shared with friends or publicly
- **Snap Map:** Real-time friend location sharing; became a core daily engagement driver
- **Spotlight:** TikTok-style short-form video feed; primarily for content discovery
- **Discover:** Curated media and publisher content; primary ad revenue surface
- **Chat:** Direct messaging with ephemeral-first defaults
- **My AI:** Integrated AI chatbot (powered by external LLM), launched March 2023 — used by 750M+ Snapchatters in the first year; enables text and image interactions within the chat interface

### Augmented Reality / Lenses
Snap pioneered mainstream AR lenses (face filters) and continues to be the leading consumer AR platform:
- **Lens Studio:** Open developer platform for building AR lenses; 350,000+ community creators; 3M+ lenses published
- **Sponsored Lenses:** High-value ad unit; brand-specific AR experiences (e.g., try-on for beauty/fashion, virtual product demos)
- AR is core to Snap's long-term platform bet — lenses are used by 250M+ daily

### Spectacles (AR Smart Glasses)
Snap's hardware product line, representing its bet on AR as the next computing platform:
- Multiple generations since 2016; early generations were primarily camera glasses
- **5th Generation Spectacles (announced September 2024):** Full augmented reality overlays in the physical world; waveguide optics; built on Snap OS. Developer-focused at $99/month subscription. Not yet commercially available to consumers.
- Spectacles have never been a significant revenue contributor; treated as a long-term R&D investment in the AR ecosystem

### SnapML / Infrastructure
Snap has developed significant ML infrastructure for ad targeting, content ranking, and AR:
- On-device ML for AR lens experiences
- Ad auction and measurement tools for advertisers (Snap Conversions API, Snap Pixel)
- Improved advertiser tooling has been central to DR ad revenue growth since 2023

## Technology & Competitive Position

### Augmented Reality Moat
- Snap has the most advanced consumer AR platform in the world by active daily usage
- The Lens Studio ecosystem creates a moat: 350,000+ developers building free content that drives daily engagement
- AR try-on for beauty and retail is an emerging high-value use case; Snap has established brand relationships in this category
- Spectacles positions Snap as the default AR OS platform if wearable AR achieves mass adoption

### Gen Z Demographics
- Snapchat reaches more than 90% of 13–24 year olds in the U.S., UK, France, and Australia
- Brand advertisers pay a premium to reach this demographic, which is underrepresented on competing platforms
- The daily active habit (especially Snap Map and streaks) creates strong retention among core users

### Privacy Positioning
- Snapchat's ephemeral-by-default design is structurally different from Facebook/Instagram's permanent record; appeals to younger users concerned about digital permanence
- GDPR and CCPA compliance is more straightforward given ephemeral data model

### Advertiser Infrastructure (Improving)
- Snap rebuilt its ad infrastructure post-iOS 14.5 (2021) which devastated its DR business
- Snap Conversions API and improved off-platform signal reconstruction have restored more of its targeting accuracy
- Still significantly behind Meta in DR ad ROAS (return on ad spend) for most advertiser categories

## Growth Strategy

1. **DR advertising growth:** Continue improving ad infrastructure (CAPI, ML models, measurement) to compete with Meta for performance advertisers; DR is higher-margin and more scalable than brand advertising
2. **Snapchat+ subscriber growth:** Expand subscription revenue as a percentage of total; reduce dependence on advertising cycle volatility; target 20M+ subscribers
3. **Monetizing Rest of World:** 258M+ daily users in markets where average revenue per user is ~$1.10 vs. ~$9.77 in North America; improving ad infrastructure in these markets is a long-term growth lever
4. **AR commerce:** Grow Sponsored Lens and AR try-on advertising as a premium-priced ad unit that leverages Snap's unique capability
5. **Spectacles platform:** Establish Snap OS / Spectacles as the developer-preferred AR platform ahead of mass consumer AR adoption; currently a long-horizon bet
6. **My AI and creator tools:** Drive engagement through AI-powered features; My AI has shown strong adoption and may become a monetizable surface

## Key Risks

### Platform and Measurement Risk
- iOS 14.5 (2021 Apple privacy changes) severely damaged Snap's ad targeting; the company spent 2022–2024 rebuilding measurement infrastructure; still recovering ROAS credibility with advertisers
- Meta remains dominant in social advertising with superior targeting, measurement, and scale; Snap competes for a subset of advertising budgets

### User Growth Deceleration
- North American and European DAU growth has slowed significantly; future DAU growth will come primarily from Rest of World, which monetizes at a fraction of the rate
- TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, and BeReal all compete for the same young audience's time

### Financial Losses
- Despite meaningful EBITDA improvement in 2024, Snap continues to generate large GAAP net losses driven by ~$1.4B annually in stock-based compensation
- Convertible note debt (~$3.75B face value) creates financial leverage risk; near-term maturities will require refinancing or cash outflows

### Governance
- Dual-class shares (Class C) give Evan Spiegel and Bobby Murphy majority voting control regardless of economic ownership — shareholders have limited recourse on strategic decisions
- Evan Spiegel's close control over product direction means the company's fate is heavily tied to his vision

### Macro / Advertising Cycle
- Digital advertising is cyclical and was severely impacted in 2022; Snap was disproportionately affected as a smaller, less-proven DR platform vs. Meta and Google
- Macro downturns cause brand advertisers to cut budgets first, which disproportionately hurts Snap's higher-margin brand ad category
