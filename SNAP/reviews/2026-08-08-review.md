SNAP's revenue is **accelerating meaningfully**. Q2-2026 revenue of \$1,599M grew **+18.9% Y/Y**, up from +12.1% in Q1 and a clear break from the +8.7% to +10.2% band that ran through all of 2025. FY-2025 grew +10.6%. An 8-10pp acceleration is the strongest fundamental development in this table — though part of Q2 was World Cup advertising spend, which management says normalizes in Q3, and the Q3 guide of \$1,700–1,740M implies deceleration back toward ~+13%.

The FCF picture is where SNAP demands care. Company FCF is genuinely and rapidly improving: TTM **\$705.5M at a +11.1% margin**, versus FY-2025's \$437.2M and FY-2024's \$218.7M — a tripling in two years, with eight straight positive quarters. On that basis TTM P/FCF of **12.7x** looks cheap, and it has compressed dramatically from 92.2x at FY-2024.

But strict ex-SBC FCF is **-\$325.3M** and P/FCF is `n/m`. SBC of **\$1,030.8M TTM is 16.2% of revenue and 146% of company FCF** — the company does not generate cash once the dilutive comp is charged. This is the central valuation question for SNAP and it is not improving on an absolute basis: SBC has been essentially flat at ~\$1.02–1.04B for three years. What *is* improving is SBC as a share of revenue (19.4% in FY-2024 → 16.2% TTM) and the share count, which finally turned down (1,737.9M → 1,682.3M, -3.2%). Management has flagged a multi-year dilution program for 2027.

Valuation on sales is undemanding: TTM P/S **1.4x**, forward P/S 1.4x, down from 3.8x at FY-2024 — the stock has de-rated ~60% while revenue grew. Guidance execution is reliable (beats of +0.9% to +4.2% every quarter shown, with Q1's +4.2% the largest). The balance sheet is the other weak point: **\$959M cash against \$3.53B debt — roughly \$2.6B net debt**, the only meaningfully levered name in this group.

Verdict: real top-line acceleration at a cheap sales multiple, undermined by SBC that consumes all the free cash flow.

**Rating:** +0.5 — BUY

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** Two stories are competing. The first is a clean Q2 beat on Aug 3 — revenue +19%, adjusted EBITDA up 6x, eighth straight quarter of positive FCF. The second, louder one is **Specs**, the \$2,195 AR glasses, with a public launch event set for **Sept 16, 2026** in Los Angeles.

**Depth pass — the earnings are substantiated; the glasses are not yet.** The Q2 numbers rest entirely on disclosed figures and they are good. Revenue \$1,599M (+18.9%) beat the ~\$1.54B consensus; adjusted EBITDA \$250M versus \$41M a year ago; gross margin **58%, +7pp Y/Y**; FCF \$121M. The operating detail is more persuasive than the headline: **Dynamic Product Ads revenue +43% with cost-per-purchase down 18%** is a direct-response performance metric advertisers act on, and **"Other Revenue" (Snapchat+ subscriptions) +85% to \$316M** is now ~20% of revenue and structurally higher-quality than advertising. DAUs reached 493M (+5%), beating the 487M expected. Q3 guidance of \$1.70–1.74B is a committed number above consensus.

Two caveats sit inside the good print, both disclosed: **North America DAU was flat at 92M**, with growth only in the 35+ cohort — the most monetizable region is not growing users. And management attributed part of the acceleration to **World Cup ad spend that normalizes in Q3**, which is why the guide implies deceleration to ~13%. The **~1,000 headcount reduction (~16% of staff)**, cutting headcount to 4,723 from 5,381, is a verified cost action underpinning the margin expansion.

**Specs is the attention story, and the market has been skeptical, not credulous.** At the June unveiling the stock **fell ~9.7%** on the \$2,195 price. Snap has sunk **over \$3.5B** into the project. Rosenblatt stayed Neutral (\$6.40 target), arguing the business case is unproven and "at a minimum there could be patent value." Meta holds ~76% of smart-glasses shipments at roughly a third of Snap's price, and reviewers flag ~4-hour battery life and bulk. Activist **Irenic Capital is pushing for the unit to be funded separately** — a governance signal that at least one institutional holder views Specs as value-destructive to the core. No unit forecasts or committed revenue exist; this is pure forward claim.

**Price move vs. driver.** The +5.8% week is backed by real numbers, but Tuesday's spike to \$5.79 was sold back to \$5.33 by Wednesday on 75M shares — the market took the beat and faded it, which is a reasonable response to a quarter with a World Cup tailwind and a September cash-burn event ahead.

**Catalysts / sentiment / risk.** **Sept 16 Specs launch** is the near-term binary. Management guided a **\$50M increase in FY infrastructure costs (to \$1.65–1.7B)** and promised sustained positive net income from 2027 plus a dilution program — forward commitments to hold them to. Sentiment: SNAP is -41% in 2026 and -94% from its 2021 high; retail participation is at its lowest since Q3 2024. Risks: ~\$2.6B net debt, SBC at 146% of company FCF, flat NA users, competition from TikTok/Reels/Meta, and Specs consuming capital with no demonstrated demand.

**Rating:** +0.4 — HOLD

## Verdict

SNAP delivered the cleanest fundamental acceleration in this group — revenue +18.9% versus a ~10% run-rate, gross margin +7pp, subscriptions +85%, and direct-response ad metrics (DPA +43%, cost-per-purchase -18%) that suggest the ad platform is genuinely working better — at only 1.4x sales after a ~60% de-rate. The problem is that none of it reaches shareholders as cash: \$1.03B of TTM SBC is 146% of company FCF, so strict ex-SBC FCF is -\$325M, and the balance sheet carries ~\$2.6B of net debt. Part of the Q2 acceleration was World Cup spend that management says normalizes, North America users are flat, and the Sept 16 Specs launch is a binary event on a project that has consumed \$3.5B with an activist arguing it should be funded separately. The \$4.72–4.78 volume shelf gives the stock real support beneath it. Evidence that the 2027 dilution program is actually shrinking the share count — or a Q3 print holding low-teens growth without the World Cup — would turn this into a clear buy; a Specs flop combined with decelerating ad revenue would send it back to the lows.

**Rating:** +0.4 — HOLD
