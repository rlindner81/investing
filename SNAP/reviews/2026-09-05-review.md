Revenue is **accelerating**: +18.9% Y/Y in q2-2026 to \$1,599M, up from +12.1% in q1-2026 and a +8.7% trough in q2-2025. TTM revenue is \$6,351M. But the acceleration is lower-quality than the headline implies — advertising grew only **+9%** while Other Revenue (Snapchat+, Memories, Lens+) grew **+85%** to \$316M, and management conceded a World Cup benefit it declined to size. Q3 guidance of \$1,700–1,740M implies deceleration back to roughly +13–16%.

The FCF profile has genuinely inflected. TTM company FCF is **\$705.5M** (11.1% margin) against \$392M a year ago, with eight consecutive positive quarters. Operating leverage is real: costs grew 4% against 19% revenue growth, gross margin expanded 700bps to 58%, and Adjusted EBITDA rose \$208M Y/Y to \$250M. Guidance execution is consistent — every quarter since q1-2025 beat its own next-quarter guide, q2-2026 by +4.2%.

The critical caveat is **SBC**. TTM stock-based comp is \$1,030.8M — larger than all of TTM FCF. Strict ex-SBC FCF is **negative \$325.3M** (-5.1% margin), and q2-2026 alone was -\$142.7M. The company's \$705M FCF is entirely a function of paying employees in stock; on a fully-loaded basis Snap still does not generate cash. Guided FY2026 SBC of ~\$1.05B means this does not resolve soon.

Valuation looks cheap on headline numbers: TTM P/S of **1.4x** and P/FCF of **13.0x**, versus 2.8x and 40–52x a year ago — a dramatic de-rating. Forward P/S on Q3 guidance is 1.4x. But P/FCF strict ex-SBC is **n/m** (negative), which is the honest read. The balance sheet carries **net debt of ~\$2.58B** (\$959M cash vs. \$3,535M debt), though ~\$2.7B including marketable securities largely offsets it, and near-term maturities have been retired.

**Verdict:** cheap on reported FCF and re-accelerating, but the multiple is only low because SBC-funded FCF flatters it and growth quality is mixed.

**Rating:** +0.6 — BUY

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** Three stories dominate: the Q2 beat (Aug 3), the **Pennsylvania AG lawsuit** (Aug 25), and the **September 16 Specs launch event**. The stock rose +14.9% on the Q2 print, sold off roughly 9% on the lawsuit, and bounced ~4% on Sept 2–3 on a WSJ feature about Spiegel's hardware strategy plus above-normal bullish call volume.

**Depth pass — what is substantiated:**

- **Q2 results — fully substantiated.** Revenue \$1,599M (+19%), Adjusted EBITDA \$250M (+505%), FCF \$121M, all verifiable in the 10-Q and 8-K. Worth flagging that several outlets (Timothy Sykes, StocksToTrade, ts2.tech) repeat a **"\$311 million net loss"** figure that is simply **wrong** — the 10-Q reports a \$164.0M net loss for Q2 and \$252.9M for H1. Also unverified in the filings: the widely-repeated claim that Barclays raised its target to \$16, which sits far outside the \$7.38–7.47 consensus and looks like a misread of the target *range*.
- **Pennsylvania AG lawsuit — substantiated but bounded.** Filed Aug 25 in Philadelphia County Court of Common Pleas by AG Dave Sunday, alleging Unfair Trade Practices violations over SnapStreaks, disappearing messages, and app-store age ratings. Critically, it is a **Complaint for Declaratory and Injunctive Relief** — the demand is product changes and disclosure, not damages. PA joins a dozen-plus states. The 9% one-day drop looks like an overreaction to a filing whose direct financial exposure is limited; the real risk is engagement-damaging product mandates, not a cash judgment.
- **Broader litigation — substantiated and material.** The CFO himself flagged "several trials scheduled in the United States later this year." MDL 3047 opened against Meta on Aug 18; the next JCCP personal-injury bellwether is October 2026; federal school-district bellwethers are set for February 2027. **Snap's revealed pattern is to settle confidentially before facing a jury** (Jan 22 2026 pre-KGM; July 20 2026 pre-R.K.C.), which caps headline risk but implies recurring undisclosed cash outflows. The March KGM verdict (\$6M against Meta/Google) sets a modest per-plaintiff benchmark.
- **Specs — mostly attention.** Real and verifiable: \$2,195 price, \$200 refundable deposit, Sept 16 LA event, ~132–136g, Snapdragon silicon, US/UK/France shipping. **Not substantiated:** demand. Spiegel was asked directly by Rich Greenfield about pre-orders and gave zero numbers. The reported ~100,000-unit initial run traces to Alex Heath's Oct 2025 newsletter and predates shipping. Even at full sell-through, ~100k × \$2,195 ≈ \$220M — immaterial against \$6.4B revenue. Spiegel's own admission that mass-market adoption is **"towards the end of the decade"** undercuts the near-term narrative that is currently moving the stock.

**Sentiment.** Institutional posture is cautious: Truist and BofA cut targets to \$7, Bernstein to \$5, Mizuho raised to only \$6; consensus ~\$7.40 has been revised **down ~4% over three months**. Freedom Broker's upgrade is the outlier. Fidelity (FMR) fell below the 5% threshold to 4.8% as of 6/30. Retail narrative is fixated on Specs as an AR call option; that enthusiasm is running well ahead of any disclosed demand data.

**Catalysts:** Sept 16 Specs launch (with ship date), Q3 earnings ~Nov 4, October JCCP bellwether.

**Risks:** advertising growing only 9% ex-subscriptions; World Cup comp reversal in Q3; North America DAU -7% Y/Y; age-assurance and teen social-media bans threatening the user base; raised infrastructure spend; Specs capital drain against an end-of-decade payoff.

**Rating:** -0.4 — HOLD

## Verdict

Snap's Q2 delivered genuine operating leverage — 19% revenue growth against 4% cost growth, 700bps of gross margin expansion, and TTM FCF of \$705M — and at 1.4x sales the stock is priced for far less. But the quality of that improvement is thinner than the headline: advertising grew only 9%, subscriptions and an unquantified World Cup benefit carried the rest, Q3 guidance implies deceleration, and TTM SBC of \$1,031M exceeds all of TTM FCF, leaving strict ex-SBC FCF at negative \$325M. The main risk is a two-sided squeeze — litigation and age-assurance rules pressuring the North American user base (already -7% Y/Y) while Specs consumes capital for a payoff Spiegel himself places at "the end of the decade." Technically the bounce is fading under the \$5.84 200-day on declining volume, with elevated IV into the September 16 event. What would change the rating: advertising revenue reaccelerating into double digits without a sports comp, North America DAU turning positive, SBC declining as a share of revenue, or disclosed Specs demand that is material rather than symbolic; conversely, an injunctive ruling forcing engagement-reducing product changes would turn this decisively negative.

**Rating:** -0.1 — HOLD
