### vs. Consensus Estimates (Q2 2025)

Revenue of $1,345M came in slightly below consensus of ~$1,350M. EPS (net loss per share) of -$0.16 missed consensus of -$0.15. Global ARPU of $2.87 fell short of analyst estimates of ~$2.90. The EBITDA miss was driven primarily by an ad-tech auction pricing error in April that compressed advertising revenue growth to ~1% that month before recovering. Adjusted EBITDA of $41M was well below the prior year's $55M as revenue growth decelerated and costs were sustained.

### Key Themes
- North America DAU declined for the second consecutive quarter (to 98M from ~100M in Q2 2024), reflecting reduced paid user acquisition spending. North America ARPU also fell -9% YoY to $8.33 — a combination of fewer high-value users and an ad-tech error that suppressed auction clearing prices in April.
- An ad-tech system error in April caused "some campaigns to clear the auction at substantially reduced prices." The change was quickly reverted; ad revenue growth recovered to approximately 3–4% by May/June, but the April impact weighed on the full quarter result.
- Snapchat+ subscribers approached 16 million (+42% YoY), with an annualized subscription run rate near $700M. A new Lens+ tier was introduced offering exclusive AI-powered lenses as an additional premium layer above the base Snapchat+ subscription.
- Sponsored Snaps (full-screen interstitial ad units in the Chat inbox) demonstrated strong early performance: 2x lift in conversions, 5x improvement in click-to-convert ratios, and 2x increase in website dwell time versus other inventory formats. However, early inventory expansion initially depressed platform-wide eCPMs.
- Adjusted gross margin slipped 100 bps YoY to 52%, reflecting the mix effect of lower-margin advertising (whose growth lagged subscription revenue) and ongoing investment in content and infrastructure.
- Snap repurchased 30 million shares for $243 million in Q2, reducing share dilution to 1.6% YoY net of buybacks — demonstrating commitment to managing dilution even as the company pursues profitability improvement.
- Spotlight content engagement grew strongly: time spent on Spotlight rose 23% YoY, with Spotlight now representing more than 40% of total content time spent. The platform is increasingly a short-form video destination in addition to its messaging roots.

## Stock Reaction

Shares fell approximately **-15%** in after-hours trading on August 5, 2025, dropping from a pre-announcement close near $9.38 to as low as $7.84. The sharp decline reflected: (1) a slight top-line miss versus consensus expectations (~$1.345B vs. ~$1.350B expected); (2) global ARPU of $2.87 missing estimates of ~$2.90; (3) North America DAU declining for a second consecutive quarter to 98M; and (4) the ad-tech auction error which raised concerns about platform execution and reliability. Despite Q3 guidance coming in ahead of Wall Street consensus, the combination of a revenue miss and ARPU shortfall drove a strong negative market reaction.

## Conference Call Highlights

**Participants:** Evan Spiegel (CEO), Derek Andersen (CFO)

---

### CEO Evan Spiegel — Key Statements

- On community growth: "Our global community continued to grow in Q2, reaching 932 million Monthly Active Users as we continued to invest in AI and augmented reality." Highlighted that DAU growth of 9% YoY to 469M and MAU growth of 7% YoY demonstrates continued global platform relevance, even as North America headwinds persist.

- On advertising recovery post ad-tech error: Acknowledged the April auction pricing issue but emphasized that the revert was rapid and that the underlying advertising platform is performing well. Framed Sponsored Snaps as "a significant new pool of inventory" with strong early advertiser outcomes — users exhibited "a 2x increase in conversion, a 5x increase in click to convert ratios, and a 2x increase in website dwell times" compared to other Snap ad formats.

- On Snapchat+: Highlighted that the subscription service "approached 16 million subscribers" with annualized run rate near $700M. Announced Lens+, a new premium tier offering exclusive AI-generated lenses and early feature access, as a way to expand the subscriber monetization opportunity beyond the base plan.

- On AR and Spectacles: Reaffirmed the 2026 consumer launch for Spectacles. Noted "we're quickly moving to a world where AR experiences can be generated on the fly," and that the developer subscription program is driving compelling use cases in education and utilities that validate a consumer launch.

- On engineering efficiency: Highlighted continued focus on realigning engineering teams to support core app improvements and monetization priorities, reducing friction and improving advertiser return on ad spend.

---

### CFO Derek Andersen — Key Statements

- On the ad-tech auction error: "We shipped a change that caused some campaigns to clear the auction at substantially reduced prices." He confirmed the team reverted the change and that "ad revenue growth declined to approximately 1% before largely recovering as we moved through May" and settled at approximately 3–4% post-correction for the remainder of Q2.

- On Q2 EBITDA and margins: Adjusted EBITDA of $41M was below the prior year's $55M, reflecting the revenue headwinds from the ad-tech issue and seasonal cost patterns. Adjusted gross margin of 52% was modestly below 53% in Q2 2024, though subscription revenue mix was a partial offset.

- On free cash flow trajectory: Highlighted that free cash flow of $24M in Q2 and $392M on a trailing twelve-month basis demonstrates the business generating real cash even as profitability is still ramping. Operating cash flow turned sharply positive to $88M in Q2 from $(21)M a year ago.

- On share buybacks: Confirmed Snap repurchased 30 million shares for $243M in Q2, keeping net dilution to approximately 1.6% YoY — "a meaningful step toward managing dilution efficiently."

- On Q3 guidance rationale: Infrastructure cost per DAU guidance of $0.82–$0.87 (with actuals expected toward the top half) reflects continued investment in AI/ML for ad performance while calibrating infrastructure spend geographically toward higher-monetization markets.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Ad-tech auction pricing error: impact and recurrence risk**
Ross Sandler (Barclays) pressed on the nature and magnitude of the April auction error. CFO Andersen quantified the impact — April ad revenue growth fell to ~1% during the error period, recovering to 3–4% once reverted. Spiegel emphasized this was an isolated engineering incident and not reflective of underlying demand trends, pointing to May/June recovery as evidence.

**2. Sponsored Snaps ramp and eCPM dynamics**
Multiple analysts asked about the path from early Sponsored Snaps success metrics to meaningful revenue contribution. Spiegel highlighted the 2x conversion and 5x click-to-convert improvements as indicative of high advertiser demand building. Andersen noted that near-term eCPM compression is expected as new supply enters the auction, but that this should normalize as advertiser demand catches up with inventory growth.

**3. North America DAU and monetization**
Analysts flagged the second consecutive quarter of North America DAU decline. Management acknowledged the trend but reframed it as a deliberate shift away from low-quality paid user acquisition. The focus is on monetizable engagement — and North America ARPU of $8.33, even with its YoY decline, reflects a user base that generates significant ad and subscription revenue per user relative to other geographies.

**4. Snapchat+ pricing and Lens+ expansion**
Mark Smolik (Bernstein) asked about the pricing strategy for Snapchat+ and how Lens+ fits in. Spiegel outlined a tiered approach: base Snapchat+ at the existing price point, with Lens+ as a premium add-on for users who want exclusive AI-generated lenses and early feature access. Pricing experiments are ongoing, with the focus on building value before optimizing for price.

**5. SMB advertiser growth and automation tools**
Analysts highlighted the continued strength of small and medium business (SMB) advertisers, driven by automated tools (Smart Audience, Smart Budget, Smart Placement). Management confirmed SMBs remain a primary growth driver, with active advertiser counts growing YoY, and noted that SMB revenue growth is helping offset continued caution among large brands.
