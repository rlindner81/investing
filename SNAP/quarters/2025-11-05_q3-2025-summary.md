### vs. Consensus Estimates (Q3 2025)

Revenue of $1,507M beat consensus of ~$1,490M by approximately $17M. Adjusted EPS of -$0.06 significantly beat the consensus estimate of -$0.12 — a 50% positive EPS surprise. Revenue growth of +10% YoY exceeded the Q2 guidance midpoint of ~$1.490B and surpassed analyst expectations. Adjusted EBITDA of $182M also came in ahead of consensus, as gross margin expansion to 55% drove better-than-expected flow-through. The results were characterized as a broad beat after the Q2 miss.

### Key Themes
- North America DAU declined for the third consecutive quarter (to 98M from ~101M in Q3 2024), reflecting deliberate pullback from low-return paid user acquisition. Australia's new online minimum age legislation (Social Media Minimum Age Bill, taking effect in December) was flagged as an upcoming headwind — management anticipated it would adversely affect engagement metrics as implementation progressed into Q4.
- North America ARPU recovered sharply to $9.20 (+8% YoY), rebounding from the -9% decline in Q2 that was partly attributable to the ad-tech auction error. This recovery demonstrates the underlying monetization strength of the North America user base when the ad platform functions normally.
- Adjusted gross margin expanded to 55% — up 300 bps from 52% in Q2 2025 and up 100 bps YoY — driven by the increasing mix of high-margin Snapchat+ subscription revenue and a favorable shift in advertising mix toward Sponsored Snaps and Spotlight.
- Snapchat+ subscribers grew to approximately 17 million (+35% YoY), generating an annualized Other Revenue run rate exceeding $750M. Other Revenue now represents approximately 13% of total quarterly revenue, continuing the structural diversification away from pure advertising dependence.
- The Perplexity AI partnership was announced concurrent with Q3 results: Perplexity will pay Snap $400 million over one year (in a combination of cash and equity) to integrate its AI answer engine into the Snapchat chat inbox. Revenue recognition is expected to begin in early 2026 upon global rollout.
- Direct response advertising grew 8% YoY, with purchase-related ad revenue growing over 30% YoY — indicating that lower-funnel, performance-focused advertisers are getting meaningful return on Snap's platform. The SMB segment in North America grew over 25% YoY.
- Large client solutions (LCS / brand advertising) declined modestly in Q3, representing approximately 43% of global revenue — down roughly 10 percentage points from two years prior as the revenue mix has diversified toward performance advertising and subscriptions.
- The board of directors authorized a new $500M Class A share repurchase program during Q3, signaling confidence in the company's financial trajectory and commitment to managing dilution.

## Stock Reaction

Shares exhibited extreme volatility around the Q3 2025 report. The initial release drove shares up as much as **+25%** in after-hours trading on November 5, 2025, as investors reacted positively to the revenue beat ($1.507B vs. ~$1.490B expected), the strong EPS surprise (-$0.06 vs. -$0.12 estimated), the Perplexity AI deal ($400M over one year), and Q4 EBITDA guidance of $280–$310M significantly ahead of consensus (~$255M). However, shares pared the after-hours gains sharply during the earnings call as CFO Andersen detailed headwinds: Q4 DAU expected to potentially decline, North America large-account advertising remaining soft (+1% YoY in Q3), and the Perplexity revenue not beginning until 2026. The stock ultimately settled near $7.42 in after-hours trading (essentially flat to the pre-announcement close), reflecting the tug-of-war between a strong beat and cautious Q4 commentary.

## Conference Call Highlights

**Participants:** Evan Spiegel (CEO), Derek Andersen (CFO)

---

### CEO Evan Spiegel — Key Statements

- On AR strategy and competitive positioning: "We believe Snap is uniquely positioned to win the next wave of AR computing." Spiegel used the Q3 call to reemphasize Snap's 11-year, $3B+ investment in AR technology, noting 350 million daily AR users and 4 million lenses created by 400,000 creators globally as evidence of the platform's depth.

- On the Perplexity AI partnership: Framed the deal as foundational to Snap's vision for AI distribution through messaging: "We see Snapchat as the ideal platform to distribute AI agents to the next generation." Perplexity's answer engine will be integrated into the Snapchat chat inbox in early 2026, with revenue to Snap beginning upon global rollout. Spiegel emphasized that Snap is uniquely positioned because messaging is the primary daily use case for its core young adult demographic.

- On monetization trade-offs: Acknowledged that new features like Sponsored Snaps in the chat inbox, Lens+ (premium AI lenses), and memory storage plans deliberately introduce "trade-offs with engagement" in exchange for monetization. Spiegel characterized this as a conscious choice: "We're focused on improving average revenue per user by more directly monetizing our core product."

- On DAU headwinds in Q4: "We expect overall DAU may decline in Q4 given these internal and external factors" — citing age verification regulatory compliance (particularly Australia's new minimum age legislation), new monetization features that may affect retention at the margins, and reduced investment in low-ROI community growth marketing in certain geographies.

- On Spectacles 5th generation: Confirmed Snap is establishing Spectacles as a standalone subsidiary and exploring manufacturing and distribution partnerships to accelerate the path to consumer launch in 2026. Framed the hardware-software combination as Snap's long-term platform bet, with AR glasses eventually serving as the primary Snapchat device.

---

### CFO Derek Andersen — Key Statements

- On margin expansion: "Adjusted gross margin reached 55% in Q3," up 300 bps sequentially from Q2's 52% and up 100 bps YoY. Driven by the mix shift toward higher-margin Snapchat+ subscription revenue and a favorable shift in advertising inventory mix toward Sponsored Snaps and Spotlight. Andersen identified "several dimensions to improving gross margins" including optimizing content spend and recalibrating infrastructure investment geographically.

- On infrastructure cost efficiency: Infrastructure costs per DAU held flat at $0.85 despite 8% DAU growth — demonstrating that efficiency gains are more than offsetting the incremental compute required by AI/ML model improvements for ad performance. Andersen reiterated the goal of holding infrastructure costs flat in 2026 even as DAU and ad complexity grow.

- On North America LCS (large client solutions): Acknowledged that LCS advertising, which now accounts for approximately 43% of global revenue, declined modestly in Q3 — down roughly 10 percentage points of revenue share from two years prior. This is primarily a structural mix shift toward performance advertising and subscriptions rather than a loss of large advertiser relationships.

- On Perplexity deal economics: Confirmed the $400M total value is "a combination of cash and equity" paid by Perplexity to Snap over approximately one year. Revenue will begin contributing to Snap's reported financials in early 2026 upon global rollout. Andersen noted the deal is a model for how Snap can monetize distribution to its young adult audience beyond traditional advertising.

- On Q4 guidance and EBITDA flow-through: Q4 guidance of $280–$310M in adjusted EBITDA on $1.68–$1.71B revenue (midpoint ~17–18% margin) reflects seasonal advertising strength and continued Snapchat+ growth. Infrastructure costs of $420–$435M in Q4 are consistent with the annual run rate and support the full-year cost discipline target.

- On share repurchase authorization: The new $500M buyback program "reflects our confidence in the long-term value of the business" and commitment to reducing share dilution as the company approaches sustained profitability.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Perplexity AI deal monetization mechanics**
Rich Greenfield (LightShed) pressed on how Snap monetizes the Perplexity integration beyond the $400M distribution fee. Spiegel confirmed Snap will not sell advertising inside Perplexity's answer results — instead, the distribution fee is the primary direct compensation. Future optionality around branded conversational commerce via Sponsored Snaps in adjacent chat surfaces was mentioned but not committed to. Greenfield and other analysts noted that $400M annualized would represent approximately 5–6% of Snap's current revenue run rate.

**2. Direct response advertising recovery path**
Michael Morris (Guggenheim) asked whether the 8% DR advertising growth in Q3 represents a sustainable recovery or a one-time bounce from Q2's ad-tech error. Andersen attributed the Q3 DR growth to genuine platform improvements — pixel optimization, app event conversion, and SMB automation adoption — rather than purely a normalization from the Q2 anomaly. Purchase-related ad revenue growing 30%+ YoY was highlighted as evidence that lower-funnel performance advertisers are seeing real return.

**3. SMB go-to-market expansion**
Analysts (including from JPMorgan) asked about Snap's approach to expanding the mid-market advertiser base. Management discussed faster onboarding workflows, simplified campaign setup, and improved API partner integrations. SMB growth of 25%+ in North America was called out as a key growth engine, with the mid-market segment described as "underpenetrated" despite strong product-market fit for direct response use cases.

**4. Spectacles commercialization and capital structure**
Ross Sandler (Barclays) asked about the capital structure for scaling Spectacles to a consumer product. Spiegel confirmed Spectacles is being established as a standalone subsidiary — a structure that provides flexibility to raise external capital, form manufacturing partnerships, or pursue joint ventures without diluting the core Snap equity holders at the parent level. He characterized the goal as "leveraging our software advantages while maintaining optionality for partnership acceleration."

**5. Regulatory age verification risk**
Multiple analysts asked about the impact of Australia's age verification legislation and the risk of similar regulations in the U.S. and UK. Spiegel acknowledged Australia's bill resulted in approximately 400,000 account removals and is expected to have a modest impact on Q4 DAU. He noted Snap's historical leadership in building age-appropriate experiences (Family Center parental controls, My AI age filtering) and expressed openness to working with regulators — while acknowledging that blanket platform-level age bans carry inherent engagement risk regardless of Snap's cooperative posture.
