### Key Themes
- **Return to sequential DAU growth:** Global DAU grew +9M sequentially from Q4 2025's 474M to 483M — the company described this as a "return to growth" after deliberately cutting paid user acquisition spending. Rest of World continues to drive the large majority of incremental DAU.
- **North America and Europe DAU remain pressured:** NA DAU fell 7% YoY (92M vs. ~99M); Europe DAU fell 2% YoY. These declines reflect both the deliberate reduction in paid community growth marketing and the impact of age verification regulations. However, North America revenue grew +2% on 7% fewer users because ARPU improved +10% YoY — monetizable engagement per user is rising.
- **Europe ARPU surge (+48% YoY):** Europe ARPU reached $3.34, up from ~$2.26 in Q1 2025 — a dramatic improvement driven by better advertiser tooling, increased Snapchat+ subscriber penetration, and improved DR ad performance in European markets.
- **Snapchat+ at ~$285M quarterly run-rate:** Other Revenue growing 87% YoY reflects Snapchat+ subscriber growth from ~13M in Q1 2025 to approximately 24–25M in Q1 2026, plus some pricing and mix improvement.
- **AI tools now dominant in ad buying:** ~70% of Snap ad spend uses at least one AI automation tool (Smart Audience, Smart Budget, Smart Placement). Pixel Purchase Campaign efficiency improved 23%+ YoY. LLM-based Dynamic Product Ads generated 2%+ purchase conversion lift.
- **Middle East headwind:** Geopolitical conditions in the Middle East created a $20–25M advertising revenue drag in March 2026. Q2 2026 guidance assumes the headwind persists at similar levels.
- **Perplexity AI partnership ended:** Snap's integration with Perplexity (AI search) was ended amicably in Q1 2026. Q2 guidance excludes any contribution from this relationship.
- **April 2026 restructuring:** After the quarter closed, Snap announced a restructuring targeting $500M+ in annualized cost reductions effective H2 2026, with $95–130M in pre-tax charges expected mostly in Q2 2026. This is framed as part of the path to GAAP profitability.

## Stock Reaction

Shares moved **+1.96%** in after-hours trading to ~$6.23 (from a pre-announcement close of $6.11). The reaction was mixed:
- Beat on revenue and significantly beat on EBITDA was well-received
- Q2 2026 guidance was cautious (implying deceleration) and included Middle East and Perplexity headwinds that were not in prior estimates
- Restructuring announcement was seen as both a signal of financial discipline and evidence that the current cost structure was unsustainable
- Ending the Perplexity deal removed a widely-anticipated AI revenue catalyst

## Conference Call Highlights

**Participants:** Evan Spiegel (CEO), Derek Andersen (CFO)

---

### CEO Evan Spiegel — Key Statements

- On the quarter: "Our return to user growth and the acceleration of subscription services are key indicators of our strategic progress." Noted the 9M sequential DAU rebound as evidence that user declines from paid marketing cuts were stabilizing.

- On the restructuring: Framed the April restructuring as making Snap "a faster, more focused, and more efficient company." The $500M+ annualized savings are intended to accelerate the path to GAAP profitability.

- On Spectacles: "All hands on deck" for the consumer launch of Spectacles, expected in late 2026. Highlighted an expanded Qualcomm collaboration (Snapdragon chip powering the device). Described an upcoming demo at AWS Expo on June 16, 2026 as a key milestone.

- On Snapchat+: Emphasized Memories (cloud storage) and Lens+ (exclusive AR lenses for subscribers) as the primary features driving subscriber retention and growth. Described a "tiering strategy" to create different price points for different levels of access, expanding the ARPU ceiling beyond the base $3.99–$4.99 subscription.

- On the advertising business: Highlighted AI-driven ad tools as the key driver — "nearly 70% of ad spend now uses at least one AI automation tool." Direct Product Ads revenue grew 30%+ YoY; App Purchases revenue grew 87% YoY.

- On Promoted Places (Snap Map ads): Early results showing 20M+ incremental venue visits from a small test — this is described as a novel high-intent, location-based ad product that leverages Snap Map's 450M+ monthly active users.

---

### CFO Derek Andersen — Key Statements

- On EBITDA improvement: "The significant improvement in adjusted EBITDA demonstrates our commitment to operational efficiency and profitability." EBITDA of $233M was more than double Q1 2025's $108M; FCF of $286M was up 150%.

- On the Europe ARPU surge: Europe ARPU grew 48% YoY to $3.34 driven by better DR ad tooling adoption, increased Snapchat+ penetration in European markets, and improved targeting through the Snap Conversions API.

- On share repurchases: $350M was repurchased in Q1 2026 alone — representing roughly 6% of the market cap at current prices. "We view these as highly accretive at current valuations."

- On constant-currency growth: Headline +12% revenue growth includes ~$29M in favorable FX tailwinds; constant-currency growth was +10%.

- On the restructuring economics: The $95–130M in one-time charges will be reported in Q2; the $500M+ annualized run-rate savings will begin flowing through in H2 2026. CFO framed this as accelerating the timeline to GAAP profitability by "at least two years."

- On Middle East: The $20–25M revenue drag in March was primarily from advertisers pausing campaigns in geographically exposed categories; Q2 guidance assumes this persists throughout the quarter.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. North America ad recovery without DAU growth (Rich Greenfield, LightShed)**
Greenfield challenged whether North America advertising could sustainably grow with DAU declining 7% YoY. Spiegel and Andersen argued the relevant metric is monetizable DAU (users engaging with ad-supported surfaces) — which actually grew even as total DAU declined, because the users leaving were primarily inactive accounts that generated zero ad impressions. ARPU growing +10% YoY on a shrinking user base validates this.

**2. DSP strategy — opening inventory to third parties (Michael Nathanson, MoffettNathanson)**
Nathanson asked about Snap's positioning relative to third-party demand-side platforms (DSPs). Spiegel acknowledged growing interest from DSPs wanting to buy Snap inventory, especially for upper-funnel video; said Snap was "evaluating" expanded DSP access but wanted to maintain the quality of advertiser relationships as a priority.

**3. Subscription tiering and ARPU ceiling (multiple analysts)**
Analysts explored whether there was headroom to raise Snapchat+ pricing or introduce premium tiers above the current $3.99–$4.99 level. Spiegel confirmed a tiering strategy is in development — lower tiers for lighter users, higher tiers (with Memories storage, exclusive Lenses, early AI features) for power users. No launch date given.

**4. Spectacles launch execution (Eric Sheridan, Goldman Sachs)**
Sheridan asked for specificity on the consumer Spectacles launch: distribution model, pricing strategy, and how Snap would avoid the commercial failure of prior Spectacles generations. Spiegel said "all hands on deck" but declined to give pricing or distribution specifics ahead of the launch; the June 16 AWS Expo was positioned as a major showcase moment.

**5. Restructuring scope and GAAP profitability timeline**
Analysts asked what specifically would be cut in the $500M+ restructuring. Management said cuts would span real estate, headcount in non-core areas, and third-party vendor consolidation — while protecting R&D, sales, and Spectacles development. Multiple analysts pressed on a specific GAAP profit timeline; management declined to give one but said H2 2026 results would "demonstrate clear progress."

**6. Snap Map as an ad product (Promoted Places)**
The 20M+ incremental venue visits from early Promoted Places tests generated significant analyst interest. Analysts asked about TAM and pricing. Spiegel said the product is in early testing with a limited set of advertisers and was not yet in Q1 revenue numbers; called Snap Map's 450M+ MAU an "enormous asset" for location-based advertising that is "dramatically undermonetized."
