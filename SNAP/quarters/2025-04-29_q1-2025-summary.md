### vs. Consensus Estimates (Q1 2025)

Revenue of $1,363M came in at the very high end of guidance (guidance range was provided for Q1). Adjusted EBITDA of $108M significantly exceeded the prior guidance range and consensus expectations. Net loss of $(140)M improved 54% year-over-year, driven by a $63M improvement in adjusted EBITDA, a $70M improvement from fewer non-recurring restructuring items versus Q1 2024, and a $49M improvement from financing/investment activity (including gains on repurchasing convertible notes at below par). DR advertising revenue grew 14% year-over-year, contributing 75% of total advertising revenue for the first time.

### Key Themes
- Global DAU reached 460M (+9% YoY), with robust Rest of World growth (+16%) offsetting a slight North America decline (-1% to 99M) and modest Europe growth (+3% to 99M). MAU surpassed 900 million, putting Snap on track toward its goal of 1 billion MAU.
- DR advertising revenue grew 14% YoY and for the first time contributed 75% of total advertising revenue, reflecting the ongoing mix shift away from brand/upper-funnel advertising (brand ad revenue was -3% YoY) toward performance-oriented DR solutions.
- Snapchat+ subscribers grew to nearly 15 million (+59% YoY), generating Other Revenue of $152M — a 75% YoY increase — and surpassing a $600M annualized run rate. This was the primary driver of revenue mix diversification.
- Active advertisers grew 60% YoY, even as management noted lapping the prior-year Q1 Snap Promote launch. The SMB (small and medium business) segment was the largest contributor to ad revenue growth in Q1.
- Adjusted EBITDA of $108M — up from $46M in Q1 2024 — produced 37% flow-through of incremental revenue growth to EBITDA, demonstrating improving operating leverage. Adjusted gross margin expanded 100 bps YoY to 53%.
- Snap executed a major balance sheet refinancing in Q1: issued $1.5B of 6.875% senior unsecured notes due 2033 and used proceeds to repurchase $1.44B of shorter-dated convertible notes (2026, 2027, 2028), reducing near-term maturities to just $36M by year-end 2025. Total debt declined ~$0.2B QoQ.
- The Simple Snapchat (three-tab) redesign test was sunset in favor of an evolved five-tab design incorporating the best learnings — management noted the five-tab design avoids the monetization disruption that would have come from deprecating the tile ad unit.
- Management declined to give formal Q2 revenue guidance due to macro uncertainty (tariff-related headwinds, de minimis exemption changes), instead updating full-year cost structure guidance: adjusted operating expenses guided down ~$50M at the midpoint to $2.65–$2.70B; SBC guided down ~$30M at the midpoint to $1.13–$1.16B.

## Stock Reaction

Shares reacted positively to the Q1 2025 results on April 29, 2025. The strong beat on Adjusted EBITDA ($108M versus prior consensus expectations anchored near the $50–60M range), 14% revenue growth at the high end of guidance, and the substantial improvement in net loss all contributed to a positive initial reaction. However, the decision to withhold Q2 guidance and management commentary acknowledging early-quarter headwinds (citing de minimis exemption impacts on a subset of advertisers and lingering macro uncertainty) tempered enthusiasm. The stock moved moderately in after-hours trading as investors balanced an operationally strong quarter against uncertain forward visibility.

## Conference Call Highlights

**Participants:** Evan Spiegel (CEO), Derek Andersen (CFO)

---

### CEO Evan Spiegel — Key Statements

- On Q1 milestone: "Q1 marked an important milestone for Snap as we reached more than 900 million monthly active users on the way to our goal of 1 billion monthly active users." Framed the result as validation of Snap's focus on visual communication between friends and family as a strategic differentiator.

- On the advertising platform: Highlighted that improvements to ML model freshness and training data scale "increased the rate of model learning by six times and grew the volume of historical interaction data used for training by over five times." Noted SK Ad Network reported app purchases grew more than 30% YoY in Q1 as a tangible output.

- On the five-tab redesign: Acknowledged that the Simple Snapchat three-tab test was retired after learning that power users struggled with its navigation. The new five-tab evolution brings Spotlight directly adjacent to the camera and integrates more friend Stories into the chat experience, delivering "more broad-based gains" while preserving the tile ad unit.

- On Sponsored Snaps: Stated the initial rollout in Q1 was limited to biddable testing; revenue impact was "less than $10 million, given the limited scale of this testing." Committed to expanding Sponsored Snaps to additional regions and bidding goals in the months ahead, citing "encouraging results" from early performance data.

- On Spectacles: Reaffirmed plans to make Spectacles available to the public in 2026. Highlighted six months of developer progress since the Q4 2024 launch, including GPS integration, advanced hand tracking, an improved keyboard, and in-game leaderboards as key feature additions.

- On macro uncertainty: Acknowledged early Q2 headwinds, noting that some advertisers' spending had been impacted by de minimis exemption changes, but cautioned that it was "difficult to parse the drivers between the various potential factors" given the evolving macro environment.

---

### CFO Derek Andersen — Key Statements

- On revenue growth: "Total revenue was $1.363 billion, up 14% year-over-year and up 15% year-over-year on a constant currency basis." DR advertising revenue grew 14% YoY and reached 75% of total advertising revenue for the first time, reflecting the structural shift toward performance-oriented solutions.

- On EBITDA flow-through: "Flow-through, or the percentage of year-over-year revenue growth that flowed through to adjusted EBITDA, was 37% in Q1." EBITDA of $108M exceeded the top of the prior guidance range, driven by revenue outperformance and operating expense discipline.

- On the balance sheet refinancing: Confirmed the issuance of $1.5B in 6.875% senior unsecured notes due 2033 and the repurchase of $1.44B in existing convertible notes at a total cash repurchase price $198.1M below par value, contributing to a ~$0.2B reduction in total debt outstanding QoQ. Near-term maturities reduced to just $36M due in 2025.

- On share buybacks: "We repurchased 27 million shares at a cost of $257 million in Q1," keeping net dilution (YoY growth in share count) to 1.9%.

- On infrastructure costs: Infrastructure cost per DAU of $0.82 in Q1 came in at the low end of the full-year guidance range of $0.82–$0.87, as anticipated.

- On Q2 guidance decision: Cited the rapidly changing macro environment and visibility challenges ("We've learned from some of the big past macro events...to be thoughtful about how this can impact the operating environment") as the rationale for withholding formal Q2 guidance. Confirmed that the business was still growing in Q2 but that top-line growth had encountered some headwinds.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. North America revenue acceleration and Simple Snapchat update**
Tom Champion (Piper Sandler) asked about the 4-point acceleration in North America revenue growth. Spiegel attributed the improvement to DR platform investments and SMB momentum, noting that long-term advertising investments were "beginning to pay off." On Simple Snapchat, he explained that the three-tab design was retired because power users struggled to find map and stories features; the new five-tab approach takes learnings from both approaches and avoids the monetization disruption of deprecating the tile ad unit.

**2. Macro environment and Q2 visibility**
Ross Sandler (Barclays) asked about early Q2 trends across brand and DR. Andersen acknowledged early headwinds from de minimis exemption changes (citing a "subset of advertisers" impacted) but declined to parse specific drivers, noting the business was still growing. On costs, Andersen characterized the $50M midpoint reduction in adjusted opex guidance as reflecting the company's ongoing discipline to calibrate investment to realized revenue growth.

**3. Path to 20%+ DR advertising growth**
Rich Greenfield (LightShed) asked what it would take to accelerate DR from ~14% to 20%+. Spiegel outlined a multi-factor path: continued ad platform ML improvements, better Goal-Based Bidding objectives (including on Sponsored Snaps), Dynamic Product Ad roadmap enhancements, and the consolidated go-to-market leadership under Ajit Mohan as a "multiplier" on foundational platform work. Andersen noted that China-based advertiser revenue is not broken out, and characterized the early-quarter trends as "just a few weeks in."

**4. SMB advertiser growth and Snap Promote**
Dan Salmon (New Street Research) asked about the sustainability of 60% YoY active advertiser growth given the Snap Promote launch lap. Spiegel noted that Snap Promote product improvements and international expansion remain a focus, and that medium-sized customers are the priority for dollar-volume growth, with a simplified Ads Manager transition playing a key supporting role.

**5. My AI differentiation and Spotlight engagement**
Mark Shmulik (Bernstein) asked about My AI's competitive positioning and domestic engagement trends. Spiegel highlighted multimodal AI as a key differentiator (sending Snaps and receiving visual responses through Gemini), with My AI DAU growing 55%+ YoY in the US. On Spotlight, he noted more than 500 million MAU and view time growing ~25% YoY in Q1, driven by the team's focus on content freshness and timely, original content.

**6. Headcount and cost structure**
Mark Mahaney (Evercore) asked whether the lowered SBC guidance implied slower hiring following Q1's headcount additions. Spiegel confirmed that hiring in Q1 was focused on SMB go-to-market, advertiser measurement support, and ML/AI engineering, and that the pace would be calibrated carefully to maintain the 37% EBITDA flow-through discipline demonstrated in Q1.
