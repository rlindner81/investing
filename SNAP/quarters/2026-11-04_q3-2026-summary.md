### Key Themes

- **Ad revenue decelerated to +4% from +9%.** This is the quarter's central problem. Direct response
  grew strongly (app-purchase revenue +54%, web-purchase revenue +11%), but **upper-funnel brand
  advertising declined YoY** on lower CPG spend (US macro, Middle East), and Q2's World Cup boost and
  easy comps are gone. Spiegel: "4% ad growth is below our ambitions." Brand isn't expected to
  "regain ground" until **over the course of 2027**.
- **Other Revenue +84% carries the headline.** More than 30M paid subscribers across Snapchat+,
  Memories Storage and Lens+, with Lens+ "exceeding expectations." Other Revenue is now \~20% of
  revenue, and roughly three quarters of incremental YoY revenue came from it.
- **Operating leverage is exceptional.** Total cost structure grew +1% on +14% revenue, so \~93% of
  incremental revenue flowed to Adjusted EBITDA. Headcount is down 13% YoY after the April
  restructuring. EBITDA beat the guide top by \$33M; GAAP net income turned positive.
- **Reporting change: DAU → MAU from Q1 2027.** Global DAU and regional DAU/ARPU will be
  discontinued; MAU (restated to a last-30-days-of-quarter basis) and MAU-based ARPU become the
  primary metrics alongside FCF/share. Management frames it as not optimizing for daily frequency.
  The market will likely read it as **hiding NA DAU**, which is -6% YoY and has gone 3 quarters
  without growth. Regional revenue stays.
- **Q4 guide implies reacceleration.** \$1,950–1,985M = +13.6% to +15.6% YoY vs. \$1,716M; the
  midpoint (\~\$1,968M) sits slightly above the \~\$1.95B consensus. Spiegel is "cautiously
  optimistic… seeing a bit of acceleration on the ad side" in Q4.
- **Infrastructure guide lowered** to \$1.65–1.66B (from \$1.65–1.70B). Last quarter's raise was
  optionality that wasn't needed: AI/ML investment grew inside a flat infra budget.
- **Specs launched;** shipping "later this fall." 10,000+ demos at the Westfield Century City LA
  pop-up. No unit or pre-order numbers.
- **Dilution:** fully diluted count +3.8% YoY to 1.93B, with awards outstanding up to 214M from 150M.
  The current buyback completes in Q4; a multi-year program targets a **stable fully diluted share
  count in 2027**. It is still unsized.
- **Legal/regulatory overhang** on youth safety was flagged again in the same language as Q2.

## Stock Reaction

SNAP ran **+9.8% into the print**, from \$5.39 (Nov 2) to \$5.92 at the Nov 4 close on 79M shares
(\~2x normal volume). It then fell **\~10–11% after hours** to \~\$5.24–5.30, erasing the run-up,
against options pricing a \~13% move. Day +1 (Nov 5) had not traded when this was written; re-run
`check-reaction SNAP` for the reaction bar.

Why it sold off:
- Revenue was \$1.722B vs. \~\$1.73B LSEG consensus, the first print below the top of Snap's own guide in six quarters
- Ad revenue only +4%, and Spiegel conceded it is "below our ambitions"
- DAU of 495M vs. \~499M expected, alongside the announcement that DAU reporting will stop. The market read the timing as hiding a weak metric
- The EBITDA beat (\$383M vs. \~\$337M) and first GAAP profit were already priced in by the +9.8% pre-print run

Setup per the [Q3 scenario plan](../earnings/2026-q3-scenario-plan.md): the print maps to the
**Base (mixed)** scenario. Other Revenue drove the result, ads were soft (below the plan's 5% bear
threshold), and the Q4 guide was around consensus. A bull-case EBITDA beat and positive net income
offset part of that.

## Conference Call Highlights

**Participants:** Evan Spiegel (CEO, Co-Founder), Ronan Harris (Chief Commercial Officer — first call),
Doug Hott (CFO), David Ometer (Head of IR)

### CEO Evan Spiegel — Key Statements

- **On the quarter:** "Q3 demonstrated the growing earnings power of Snap's business." Gross margin
  +\~7pp, net income and EBITDA margins each +\~10pp.
- **Priorities (from his September 15-year letter):** keep the community safe, grow advertising
  faster, strengthen relationships. The letter's stated ambition is to move ad growth toward the
  **mid-teens**.
- **Engagement:** daily messages +8% YoY, two-way communicators +6%. Spotlight posters **+130%** and
  daily active viewers **+40%** globally, helped by removing AI-generated and reposted content.
- **On AI internally:** merged pull requests more than doubled YoY and defect rate per merge fell
  71%+. AI search resolves 92%+ of advertiser help sessions and support cases are down 35%.
- **On ads:** "4% ad growth is below our ambitions, and I'm cautiously optimistic about the progress
  we're seeing here in Q4 as we're seeing a bit of acceleration on the ad side."
- **On not optimizing DAU:** he gave two examples of things Snap is deliberately not doing: user
  acquisition in geographies it can't monetize, and notifications or features that create an app
  open without long-term value.

### CCO Ronan Harris — Key Statements

- **Brand weakness:** "declined mainly because of lower CPG spending driven by macro pressures in the
  U.S. and also the Middle East." Seeing traction in diversified verticals such as US financial
  services. Expects brand to "regain ground over the course of 2027."
- **DR now \~77% of revenue,** a substantial increase vs. last year.
- **Sponsored Snaps revenue +56% QoQ;** Chat's share of total revenue up \~8pp YoY. Auction-based
  Sponsored Snaps launched in Europe; DPA now in Chat. Spotlight eCPM +22% YoY. Promoted Places
  revenue and advertisers more than doubled QoQ and it now runs in 14 countries.
- **Holiday tooling:** Commerce Power Pack, Third-Party Web Optimization (open beta), and Unified
  Attribution (GA with AppsFlyer/Adjust, covering 75%+ of app ad revenue).

### CFO Doug Hott — Key Statements

- **On leverage:** "revenue increased 14%, while our total cost structure grew just 1%." Cautioned
  that the 93% flow-through "is a measure of this quarter's year-over-year operating leverage" and
  is shaped by mix, seasonality and expense timing. **Don't extrapolate it.**
- **On cash:** TTM OCF \$983M (+59%), TTM FCF \$777M (+88%).
- **On the MAU switch:** follows the 2025 pivot to profitable growth, with less community-growth
  marketing and less emphasis on DAU. Regional revenue reporting continues.
- **On dilution:** multi-year approach after the current buyback completes in Q4, aiming for a stable
  fully diluted share count in 2027.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Brand weakness and Q4 quarter-to-date (Ross Sandler, Barclays)**
Called brand "once again the culprit." Harris blamed CPG/macro plus the World Cup comp and pointed
to 2027 for a brand recovery. Spiegel gave a Specs update (10K+ demos, shipping later this fall) but
did not quantify Q4 quarter-to-date trends beyond the guide.

**2. MAU metric shift and Sponsored Snaps (Eric Sheridan, Goldman Sachs)**
Hott justified the MAU switch as the continuation of the profitable-growth pivot and said it gives
teams "flexibility" on decisions that may hurt DAU near term. Harris: Sponsored Snaps +56% QoQ,
expanding to Europe.

**3. What accelerates ads to mid-teens (Doug Anmuth, JPMorgan)**
Spiegel: better AI-driven performance, easier measurement with advertisers' own tools, and new
placements (Chat, Spotlight, Map). He gave no timeline for mid-teens.

**4. DAU-vs-value tension; size of brand (Rich Greenfield, LightShed)**
Greenfield asked when DR becomes big enough to overwhelm brand pressure. Harris: DR \~77% of revenue
and rising. Brand product and go-to-market improvements kick in into 2027. Neither speaker said
when total ad growth reaccelerates meaningfully.

**5. Spotlight engagement not showing in US/EU DAU (Justin Post, BofA)**
Post asked why the engagement gains show up in MAU but not in daily users. Spiegel answered on
Spotlight content strategy and ranking-architecture work, **without addressing the DAU question**.

**6. Sustainability of DR / advertiser count (Dan Salmon, New Street)**
Harris focused on ease of buying and measurement for SMBs and dependable performance for large
advertisers. **No advertiser-count metric was given.**

**7. Margin runway into 2027 (Alice Duska for Rob Sanderson, Loop Capital)**
Hott tempered the 93% flow-through and said the objective is sustained FCF/share, not single-quarter
margin. He gave no 2027 margin framework.
