### Key Themes

- **Revenue reaccelerated to +19%, the fastest growth in years.** The headline beat both guidance
  (\$1,520–1,550M) and consensus. But the composition matters: advertising revenue grew only **+9%**
  while Other Revenue grew **+85%**. Roughly half the incremental revenue growth came from
  subscriptions, not the core ad business.
- **World Cup was a real but unquantified tailwind.** CFO Doug Hott confirmed the World Cup
  "provided a benefit during the quarter" and that Q3 guidance "reflects the expected normalization
  of World Cup-related spending." Management declined to size it when pressed directly by Rich
  Greenfield. Q2 also benefited from easy comps — the ad platform only stabilized at the end of
  Q2 2025.
- **North America DAU stabilized at 92M sequentially** (flat vs. Q1 2026's 92M) after several
  quarters of decline, though still -7% YoY. NA revenue grew +15% on 7% fewer users because NA
  ARPU jumped +23% to \$10.26 — the monetization-over-users thesis continues to hold.
- **Cost discipline is the story.** Total adjusted cost structure grew just **4%** against 19%
  revenue growth. Gross margin expanded 700 bps to 58%. Adjusted EBITDA rose \$208M YoY to \$250M
  (16% margin). The April 2026 restructuring's personnel savings are only partially reflected —
  management expects fuller flow-through in Q3 and beyond.
- **New primary financial objective: Free Cash Flow per share.** New CFO Doug Hott reframed the
  company's north star around FCF/share, connecting operating performance, capital allocation
  discipline, and dilution management. Eight consecutive quarters of positive FCF; TTM FCF \$706M.
- **Specs (Spectacles) priced at \$2,195 with a September 16 launch event.** Unveiled at AWE in
  June; pre-orders open ~7 weeks at the time of the call. Spiegel declined to disclose pre-order
  volumes. Notably, he conceded mass-market adoption is **"towards the end of the decade"** —
  weight and cost must come down first. Specs investment sits inside the existing opex outlook.
- **Infrastructure cost guidance raised** to \$1.65–1.70B for FY2026 from \$1.60–1.65B, to fund AI
  and ML infrastructure supporting revenue growth.
- **Sustained positive net income guided to begin in 2027** — the first explicit GAAP profitability
  timeline management has committed to.
- **Legal and regulatory overhang flagged prominently.** The CFO called out increased regulatory
  scrutiny on youth-related issues and "several trials scheduled in the United States later this
  year," warning of possible product changes, compliance costs, judgments/settlements, and negative
  user growth impacts.
- **CFO transition.** Doug Hott has replaced Derek Andersen as Chief Financial Officer; this was his
  first full quarter presenting results.

## Stock Reaction

Shares rose **+14.9%** the session after the release, closing at **\$5.79** on August 4 (from a
pre-news close of \$5.04) on 88.7M shares. The move faded quickly: **+5.8%** by day +2 (\$5.33) and
**+3.6%** by day +3 (\$5.22).

Drivers of the initial pop:
- Revenue and Adjusted EBITDA both materially beat, with EBITDA up 505% YoY
- Q3 EBITDA guidance of \$300–350M came in well above expectations
- The 4% cost growth vs. 19% revenue growth demonstrated genuine operating leverage
- First explicit commitment to sustained positive net income beginning in 2027

Why the fade:
- Advertising revenue grew only +9%; the +19% headline leaned heavily on subscriptions and an
  unquantified World Cup benefit
- Q3 revenue guidance implies deceleration back to the low-to-mid teens
- Infrastructure cost guidance was raised
- Spiegel's "end of the decade" comment on mass-market Specs adoption pushed out the timeline for
  the company's biggest long-term bet, while spending continues now

## Conference Call Highlights

**Participants:** Evan Spiegel (CEO, Co-Founder), Doug Hott (CFO), David Ometer (Head of IR)

---

### CEO Evan Spiegel — Key Statements

- **On the strategic frame:** "Free cash flow per share will be our primary financial objective
  going forward." Positioned Snapchat as "approaching 1 billion people using our service every
  month" and the objective as turning scale into "durable growth and stronger cash generation."

- **On the three priorities set last fall** (strengthen community/engagement, accelerate and
  diversify revenue, build a profitable cash-generative core): called Q2 "a meaningful step forward
  across all three."

- **On Spotlight as an engagement flywheel:** in the United States, people posting to Spotlight grew
  **+115% YoY** and Spotlight DAU grew **+20%**, supported by creator investment and AI-powered
  recommendations.

- **On advertising performance gains:** cost per install -8% YoY, cost per purchase -18%, app
  purchase volume +128%, Dynamic Product Ads revenue +43%. Roughly one-third of Snapchatters reached
  by Sponsored Snaps were incremental to other Snapchat surfaces. An independent Measured study
  found Snapchat delivered ~19.3% higher incremental ROAS than the brands' blended social average.

- **On audience broadening:** the US audience "continues to broaden quarter-over-quarter, led by
  people aged 35 and older," increasing relevance in automotive, healthcare, home goods, financial
  services, insurance, and B2B.

- **On AI inside the company:** code commits per engineer +75% YoY, major reliability issues -57%,
  internal AI code reviewer covering 90% of pull requests (~30,000 hours of review time saved), AI
  support agent answering ~3.9M questions monthly and cutting support tickets ~62% since the start
  of the year, and first-pass ad image review automation up from 40% (Q2 2025) to nearly 90%.

- **On Specs:** "a new kind of computer built into see-through glasses, more capable than today's
  limited AI glasses and more wearable than bulky VR headsets." Priced at **\$2,195** — "obviously a
  high consideration purchase." Launch event in Los Angeles on **September 16**.

### CFO Doug Hott — Key Statements

- **On the operating leverage:** "Our total adjusted cost structure increased just 4%
  year-over-year, as operating efficiencies offset investments in long-term revenue drivers."
  Gross margin +7pp to 58%; net loss improved \$99M to \$164M; Adjusted EBITDA +\$208M to \$250M.

- **On cash generation:** Q2 OCF \$176M and FCF \$121M; TTM OCF \$919M and FCF \$706M. "We have now
  generated positive Free Cash Flow for eight consecutive quarters, while limiting fully diluted
  share-count growth to approximately 2% over the past five years."

- **On the balance sheet:** ended Q2 with ~\$2.7B in cash and marketable securities; repaid the \$47M
  of convertible notes due August 2026 on top of \$2B+ of 2027/2028 notes retired earlier.

- **On the FCF-per-share objective:** "it connects operating performance, disciplined capital
  allocation, and long-term shareholder value creation." Goal is to generate enough FCF to invest in
  Snap's long-term potential, offset SBC dilution, and strengthen the balance sheet.

- **On the infrastructure guidance raise:** the increase "primarily reflects additional investment in
  the AI and machine learning infrastructure needed to support revenue growth."

- **On Q3 deceleration:** guidance "reflects the expected normalization of World Cup-related spending
  and a more difficult year-over-year comparison in Q3 following the easier comps we had in Q2 as our
  ad platform stabilized last year at the end of Q2 2025."

- **On 2027:** expects direct revenue to keep growing "materially faster than the overall business,"
  with continued gross margin improvement supporting further EBITDA margin expansion and "sustained
  positive net income beginning in 2027."

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Durability of the Q2 improvement (Doug Anmuth, JP Morgan)**
Asked what gives confidence the growth is durable. Hott pointed to progress across both the ad
platform and go-to-market execution, stronger performance for app/e-commerce/lower-funnel
advertisers, and a **56% YoY increase in conversions** across the platform. "The breadth of the
improvement here is what gives us confidence that it's being supported by stronger fundamentals."

**2. Specs investment pacing and structure (Ross Sandler, Barclays)**
Asked about H2 and 2027 Specs investment and whether spinning Specs out as a separate company is off
the table. Hott sidestepped the structural question, framing the answer around the "real inflection
in free cash flow generation" allowing Snap to invest in Specs, offset dilution, and strengthen the
balance sheet simultaneously. Current focus is product quality, customer experience, and ecosystem
development.

**3. North America DAU trajectory (Dan Salmon, New Street Research)**
Asked about NA DAU after stabilizing at 92M and for color on under-35 users. Spiegel said Snap is
"encouraged by the stabilization in North America DAU on a quarter-over-quarter basis," credited
Spotlight/Snap Map/AR, and pointed to Spotlight posting +115% and viewers +20%. He did **not** break
out under-35 trends, and flagged that Snap is "closely monitoring the regulatory environment,
including age assurance, privacy, and online safety requirements which may affect the product
experiences or our user growth and engagement over time."

**4. Specs pre-orders and World Cup sizing (Rich Greenfield, LightShed)**
Two-part. On pre-orders, Spiegel said Snap has "been overwhelmed at the extent to which our
announcement really broke through" but gave **no pre-order numbers**, emphasizing the \$2,195 price
makes it a high-consideration purchase requiring hands-on trial. On the World Cup, Greenfield pressed
for a size and asked whether the Q3 guide-down was World Cup absence or something else; Hott confirmed
the benefit was real but **declined to quantify it**, attributing the Q3 deceleration to World Cup
normalization plus tougher comps.

**5. Specs competitive and financial rationale (Michael Nathanson, MoffettNathanson)**
Pressed hardest on why Specs makes financial sense for a company Snap's size versus partnering, and
what gives confidence against Apple, Meta, and Alphabet. Spiegel argued the next computing platform
opportunity is "absolutely enormous, even at single-digit percentages of smartphone volumes," and
that Snap's advantage is full-stack integration (developer platform and tools, LensCore rendering
engine, operating system, optical engine) plus first-mover position — contrasting it with Snap's late
entry into social.

**6. Infrastructure cost raise and ROI (James Heaney, Jefferies)**
Asked what drove the raise and how to think about return, particularly for DR/lower-funnel. Hott
framed it as **optionality rather than committed spend**: "It doesn't mean we'll have to do that, but
we wanted to make sure that we gave full-year guidance to reflect the opportunity that we could make
those investments."

**7. FCF-per-share philosophy under the new CFO (Mark Shmulik, Bernstein)**
Asked how Hott's philosophy is evolving as he ramps into the seat. He laid out three pillars —
improving operating performance, investing capital with discipline, and managing dilution — and cited
Q2's 19% revenue growth against 4% cost growth as the proof point, crediting the early-quarter
restructuring.

**8. Revenue mix diversification (Eric Sheridan, Goldman Sachs)**
Asked about the evolving revenue mix, Snapchat+ signals, and Specs as a hardware plus subscription
opportunity. Spiegel called it "exciting that we're becoming a multi-engine revenue business,"
highlighting Other Revenue +85% driven by Snapchat+, Memories Storage, and the higher-priced Lens+
tied to AI creative tools.

**9. Subscription penetration runway (Shweta Khajuria, Wolfe Research)**
Asked where the sub-3% of MAU paying-subscriber penetration can go. Spiegel said it is "still early
days" and hard to be specific, but cited industry benchmarks: app-based subscription products
typically reach **7%–12% penetration** over the long term, implying meaningful headroom. Lens+ is the
named next growth driver.

**10. Specs investment guardrails and Sponsored Snaps mix (Lloyd Walmsley, Mizuho)**
Asked for guardrails on Specs spend and when mass-market product-market fit arrives. Spiegel gave the
call's most consequential admission: **"I think it will be towards the end of the decade before we
see mass market consumer adoption,"** citing weight and cost as the blockers. He did not answer the
Sponsored Snaps ad-mix sizing question.
