### Key Themes
- North America DAU declined from ~99M (Q4 2024) to ~94M: Snap deliberately reduced "community growth marketing" (paid user acquisition spending) to shift toward profitable growth rather than volume growth. Management noted that **monetizable DAU** (more engaged users generating ad impressions) grew even as overall DAU declined.
- Snapchat+ subscriber growth (+71% YoY to 24M) is transforming the revenue mix — Other Revenue now represents ~14% of total. Higher-margin subscription revenue helps structurally improve gross margins.
- Adjusted gross margin reached 59%, up 4pp sequentially from Q3 2025 (55%) and 2pp YoY — driven by subscription revenue mix and infrastructure efficiency gains.
- Active advertisers grew 28% YoY — primarily SMBs adopting Snap's automated ad tools (Smart Audience, Smart Budget, Smart Placement).
- North America ARPU reaching $10.88 demonstrates continued improvement in ad monetization efficiency even against a smaller DAU base.
- eCPM declined 8% YoY, offset by 14% growth in global impression volume — volume/efficiency trade-off continues.

## Stock Reaction

Shares fell **-3.4%** in after-hours trading to ~$6.10 (from a pre-announcement close near $6.31), despite beating estimates on both revenue and EBITDA. The market focused on:
1. North America DAU declining 5% YoY — a structural concern even if partially explained by deliberate paid acquisition cuts
2. Q1 2026 revenue guidance ($1.5–1.53B) implying deceleration in growth rate from Q4's +10%
3. Broader investor skepticism about Snap's long-term competitive position given stock's multi-year decline from 2021 highs (~$83) to ~$6

## Conference Call Highlights

**Participants:** Evan Spiegel (CEO), Derek Andersen (CFO)

---

### CEO Evan Spiegel — Key Statements

- On the strategic pivot: "We will seek to strike a better balance between the pace of community growth and the rate of top line growth in order to pivot our business to more profitable growth." Described 2025 as the year Snap began deliberately shifting from DAU maximization toward monetization efficiency and profitability.

- On AI in engineering: "40% of new code at Snap is AI generated." Framed AI-generated code as a structural efficiency gain that allows a flat or modest headcount growth rate to support more product development.

- On Spectacles (5th gen): Transitioned from R&D to developer focus; described developers building "really spectacular things" including utilities and educational AR experiences. The 2026 consumer launch is a top priority.

- On North America DAU declines: Characterized the reduction as intentional — Snap cut paid community growth marketing to improve unit economics. The company expects to restart "responsibly" once the economic profile of each acquired user justifies the spend.

- On Snapchat+: The 71% subscriber growth to 24M represents a "foundational shift" in how Snap thinks about its revenue mix — recurring, high-margin subscription revenue reduces dependence on advertising cycles.

---

### CFO Derek Andersen — Key Statements

- On Q4 EBITDA performance: "Adjusted EBITDA flow-through was 51% in Q4," with gross margin reaching 59%, up from 55% in Q3 2025 — one of the largest sequential margin improvements the company has seen.

- On infrastructure costs: Infrastructure costs per DAU of $0.86 came in **below guidance** as Snap shifted to calibrating infrastructure spend to monetization potential by geography — investing more per user in high-ARPU North America while moderating costs in lower-ARPU Rest of World.

- On 2026 opex structure: Total adjusted operating expenses guided at ~$3B with infrastructure at $1.6–1.65B (flat YoY at low end); this signals that the company believes the cost structure has largely right-sized.

- On share repurchases: A new $500M buyback authorization was approved — at current ~$6 stock prices, this represents approximately 8% of the fully diluted market cap. Management expressed confidence in the intrinsic value of the business at current prices.

- On FY2025 results: "Revenue of $5.93 billion and Adjusted EBITDA of $689 million represent meaningful evidence that our business model is working and improving."

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Specs commercialization strategy**
Multiple analysts asked about the path from the developer subscription program to consumer Spectacles. Spiegel said the developer program is revealing compelling use cases across utilities (navigation, task management) and education, and that the 2026 consumer launch remains on track. Analysts pressed on whether Snap would seek outside capital or partnerships to fund hardware commercialization; management said "there's flexibility" but did not commit to a specific structure.

**2. Advertising recovery in North America large accounts**
Analysts flagged that large-account North America advertising remained a headwind while SMBs grew 28% YoY. Spiegel acknowledged continued headwinds in the North America large customer business but highlighted bright spots in Financial Services and Autos, and noted new leadership in the North America LCS segment with new ad products (Sponsored Snaps, Promoted Places) to build on.

**3. Regulatory age verification risks**
Australia's platform-level age verification rules resulted in the removal of approximately 400,000 Snapchat accounts. Analysts asked about potential similar regulations in the U.S. and UK. Spiegel acknowledged the risk, noted Snap has been an industry leader in building age-appropriate experiences (My AI age filtering, Family Center parental controls), but acknowledged future regulation could affect user counts in key markets.

**4. Infrastructure efficiency vs. AI/ML investment**
Some analysts were concerned that infrastructure cost reductions could limit Snap's ability to invest in the ML models that drive ad performance improvements. CFO Andersen clarified that AI/ML infrastructure investment is continuing — the calibration is specifically around reducing overcapacity in low-ARPU geographies, not cutting ML investment.
