The resulting 49% single-day collapse (to \$14.74) triggered eight simultaneous analyst downgrades from BofA, JPMorgan, Barclays, Evercore ISI, Needham, Truist, Jefferies, and Citizens — the broadest single-session analyst exodus since the IPO.

Q1-2026 results (June 2, 2026) confirmed the damage: revenue -26.2% to \$197.9M (slightly above the \$189.8M consensus), net loss of -\$21.4M, Adjusted EBITDA of -\$7.0M, and full-year guidance withdrawn. One early positive signal embedded in the report: IL MAKIAGE CPA in May declined an estimated 28% sequentially from April, representing the first meaningful improvement since Q4 2025. The board also authorized a \$200M share repurchase; ODD bought back \$82.3M of Class A shares in Q1, reducing the float by ~10.6%.

Securities fraud class action suits have been filed (Levi & Korsinsky, Hagens Berman) on behalf of investors who purchased ODD between February 26, 2025 and February 24, 2026. The core allegation: management observed the advertising disruption in H2 2025 but continued issuing beat-and-raise guidance without disclosure. This litigation overhang will persist for years.

## Verdict

ODD has gone from one of the cleanest beat-and-raise growth stories in consumer tech to a company with negative FCF, net debt of \$307M, no FY guidance, and a securities fraud lawsuit — all within two reporting cycles. The price has bounced sharply off its post-earnings low as investors speculate on an H2 recovery, but the structural picture remains deeply negative. The key inflection point is Q2-2026 earnings: a revenue beat above the guided -25–30% decline range and any reinstatement of FY guidance would meaningfully change the thesis; a miss or further deterioration in the CPA trajectory would confirm this is a structural impairment, not a temporary dislocation. Until that data arrives, the risk/reward skews to patience over conviction.
