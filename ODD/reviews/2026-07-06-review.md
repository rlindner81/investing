ODDITY Tech's revenue trajectory tells two distinct stories: a strong FY2025 (+25.2% Y/Y to \$809.8M) capped by four consecutive quarters of ~25–27% growth, followed by a severe cliff in Q1-2026 (-26.2% Y/Y to \$197.9M) driven entirely by a CPA dislocation with its largest advertising partner. This is not competitive deterioration — first orders declined ~50% as acquisition costs surged 83% Y/Y through May — but the damage is real and the recovery timeline remains uncertain. Q2-2026 guidance of \$169–181M implies a further ~25–30% Y/Y decline, and full-year guidance was withdrawn entirely.

FCF has deteriorated in lockstep with revenue. FY2025 company FCF was \$83.6M (vs. \$134.5M in FY2024, -38%), and the TTM is now -\$24.8M. Strict ex-SBC FCF TTM is -\$73.9M. SBC is \$34.9M TTM — material relative to a \$965M market cap — and the gap between company FCF and strict ex-SBC widens further as capitalized software spend (\$14.2M TTM) accumulates. There is no visible FCF floor at current revenue run rates.

At \$17.04 and a \$965M market cap, the stock trades at 1.3x TTM P/S and 1.4x forward NQ guidance P/S. These multiples look optically cheap relative to the pre-disruption business, but cheap-on-revenue is only attractive if FCF recovers alongside it. Net debt is -\$307M and worsening — the balance sheet has swung from \$137M net cash a year ago, partly because the company deployed cash aggressively on buybacks.

The guidance trajectory reveals a sharp break: the raise-every-quarter cadence of 2024–early 2025 gave way to a withdrawn FY guide and two consecutive Y/Y decline quarters. Management cited May CPA improving 28% M/M and the ad partner estimating a 40–60% CPA recovery capability, but that range is wide and H2-2026 is a binary bet on normalization. The historical beat rate of 1.5–4% above NQ guidance suggests Q1's actual -26% vs. guided -30% was in character, but it offers little comfort on the magnitude of the decline. A class-action lawsuit alleging material omissions around the advertising disruption adds incremental overhang.

Verdict: Speculative recovery trade — the business model is intact if CPA normalizes, but current FCF is negative, the balance sheet has net debt, FY guidance is withdrawn, and the stock has already bounced ~73% from the post-earnings low, compressing the margin of safety.

ODD has staged a dramatic price recovery since reporting Q1-2026 results on June 2. From a post-earnings low near \$9.84, the stock surged +20.7% in the week of June 22–26 and an additional +25.3% in the week of June 29–July 2, landing at \$17.04 — a ~73% bounce off the earnings collapse low. This week (WTD) shows a slight -0.4% pullback, suggesting the immediate momentum is cooling after an extraordinary run. In contrast, benchmarks (SPY, XLY, ONLN) were largely flat-to-modestly-positive during the same June surge, confirming ODD's move was stock-specific rather than sector-driven.

The SMA picture is mixed. On a weekly basis (5/10/20-day), the prior week showed ODD's price above all short-term SMAs (15.8>14.3>12.6), consistent with strong near-term momentum. On a monthly basis, the 20/50/200-day SMAs read 12.9/13.2/29.6 — ODD at \$17.04 is now above the 20-day and 50-day, but remains deeply below the 200-day (\$29.6). The arrow notation ↓↓ reflects that the SMAs themselves are still in bearish alignment (20-day < 50-day < 200-day), meaning the structural downtrend is intact even as price temporarily outpaces the near-term averages. SPY's SMAs remain in a healthy bullish alignment.

Relative volume tells a telling story: after two weeks of elevated activity during the surge (rvol running 99–131% of the 20-day average in June), volume has faded sharply in July MTD to 68%/67%/79% vs. 20/50/200-day averages. The conviction behind the move is evaporating — buyers who wanted in on the recovery story have largely acted, and residual volume is thin.

Implied volatility is the most striking data point: ODD options are pricing ±13.3%/week and ±27.7%/month. That is roughly 10x the SPY weekly IV and nearly 3x XLY.
