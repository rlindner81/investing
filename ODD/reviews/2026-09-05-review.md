The last data point remains Q1-2026, and it is a break, not a wobble. Revenue fell -26.2% Y/Y to \$197.9M, snapping eight consecutive quarters of 23–28% growth and eight consecutive beats of next-quarter guidance (by +1.5% to +3.9%). FY-2025 closed at \$809.8M, +25.2%; the TTM figure has already rolled down to \$739.7M and will fall further as Q2's guided -25% to -30% replaces last year's \$241.1M.

The cash-flow reversal is the more serious damage. Company FCF was +\$83.6M in FY-2025 and is now -\$24.8M TTM, with Q1-2026 alone at -\$21.1M. The gap to the strict ex-SBC variant is wide and widening: -\$73.9M TTM against -\$24.8M company FCF, because \$34.9M of annual SBC and \$14.2M of capitalized software sit outside the company's own FCF definition. FCF margin has gone from +20.8% (FY-2024) to -10.0% strict on a TTM basis. Both P/FCF rows read n/m — there is no earnings-power anchor left.

The balance sheet flipped from +\$137M net cash (Q1-2025) to -\$306M net debt, driven by the June 2025 \$600M exchangeable notes issue plus \$82.3M of Q1 buybacks. One genuine improvement post-quarter: in June 2026 ODD retired \$50M of those notes for \$35M cash, a \$15M discount, leaving \$550M outstanding — accretive and not yet in these figures. Liquidity is still ample at ~\$667M cash and investments plus a \$350M undrawn facility.

At 1.1x TTM P/S and a \$779M market cap, the optics are cheap against \$810M of FY-2025 revenue. But FY guidance is withdrawn — there is no forward anchor at all, and FW P/S FY reads n/m. Only the next-quarter guide (\$169–181M) is live, at 1.1–1.2x forward.

Verdict: optically cheap, but the multiple is doing no work when the denominator has no floor and FCF is negative.

## 3. News

### The surface narrative

The tape has been quiet — roughly two articles a week — because everything is waiting on one date. ODDITY confirmed on July 22 that **Q2-2026 results land pre-market Wednesday, September 9**, with a call at 8:30 a.m. That is four days out and dominates everything below. Consensus sits at roughly \$178M revenue and ~\$0.12 EPS, against company guidance of -25% to -30% Y/Y (\$169–181M) and adjusted EBITDA of \$8–10M.

The one item that actually moved the stock in the last month was **Truist raising its target to \$16 from \$12 (Hold maintained)**, arguing Q2 will beat consensus and that quarter-to-date Q3 readings through August 26 also point to outperformance — driven mainly by accelerating SpoiledChild growth.

### The deeper read

**Truist's call is semi-substantiated but should be discounted.** It rests on a proprietary credit-card panel, which is real transaction data rather than narrative — but it is unverifiable by outsiders, US-centric against ODD's ~17.5% international mix, and the same firm kept a Hold while raising the target, which is a hedge, not a conviction. Notably, it also **contradicts this repo's own independent proxy work**: `ODD/notes/SimilarwebProxy.md` shows SpoiledChild traffic at +26.4% (May), **-32.2% (Jun), -26.6% (Jul)** — indexed off May, roughly **-50% in two months** — with Display as its #1 channel at 37.65% and its **US volume down ~49%**, faster than the total. Truist's "accelerating SpoiledChild growth" and that traffic series cannot both be right. Weight the disagreement accordingly.

**The most substantiated positive is the note repurchase.** On June 11 Oddity Finance retired **\$50M principal of the 0% 2030 exchangeables for \$35M cash** (~70 cents), closing June 17, leaving \$550M outstanding. Real, filed, ~\$15M accretive, and not yet in our Q1 net-debt figure. The notes strike at ~\$92 versus \$13.75 — deeply out of the money, hence the discount.

**Substantiated but two-sided:** the CPA data. Management's own index shows IL MAKIAGE CPA at 1.0–1.5 through H1-2025 spiking to **2.8 in H1-2026**, with **first orders -50% versus repeat orders -15%**, and **May CPA down ~28% sequentially**. The remediation — shifting 40% of acquisition revenue off "Try Before You Buy" — is concrete. But our own Similarweb series shows IL MAKIAGE at **-7.0%, -12.9%, -10.5%** M/M through July: the first derivative has not crossed zero in any observed month.

**Mostly attention:** the August rally itself. Consensus target is ~\$11.07 against a \$13.75 price — the Street models *downside*. Goldman is at Sell/\$8, Morgan Stanley Equal-weight/\$10.

### Positioning, catalysts, risks

**Short interest is the dominant technical fact: ~6.58M shares, 23% of a 28.6M float, ~9 days to cover.** That is squeeze fuel into a binary print, and it plausibly explains an August advance that happened on 48% of 200-day average volume.

**Insider selling is steady but benign in character** — CFO Lindsay Drucker Mann sold on Aug 5, Aug 27 (5,503 at \$15.00) and Sep 1 (7,717 at \$15.09), all disclosed as 10b5-1 RSU tax-withholding. Twenty-two Form 4/144 filings since June 1 is optically heavy but mechanically routine. Separately, David S. Cohen (32 years at Morgan Stanley IM) joined the board and audit committee on Aug 20 — governance-positive, financially immaterial.

**Risks:** the H2 cohort trap is structural — the Q1 acquisition collapse guarantees depressed repeat revenue even if CPA fully normalizes, and management concedes this. Platform dependency on a single unnamed ad partner is unresolved. Securities class actions covering Feb 2025–Feb 2026 remain outstanding. And \$550M of debt against negative TTM FCF narrows the margin for error.

**Rating:** -0.7 — SELL

## Verdict

ODD is four days from the single print that resolves this thesis, and nothing has been decided in the interim — Q1-2026 remains the last data point, with revenue -26.2% Y/Y, TTM FCF at -\$24.8M, net debt of ~\$306M, and full-year guidance withdrawn. The August bounce has already failed: the stock gave back -4.5% WTD on rising volume after advancing on roughly half of normal participation, and the Street's \$11.07 consensus target sits below the \$13.75 price. The bull case rests almost entirely on Truist's card-panel claim of accelerating SpoiledChild growth, which is directly contradicted by our own Similarweb series showing SpoiledChild traffic down ~50% across June and July — a disagreement that Q2 will settle. The main risk to a bearish stance is mechanical rather than fundamental: 23% of the float is short at ~9 days to cover, and a beat against the guided -25% to -30% could force a violent squeeze. What would change the rating is Q2 revenue above the \$181M high end combined with any reinstatement of FY guidance and evidence that IL MAKIAGE's monthly traffic decline has crossed zero; conversely, a miss on already-lowered guidance would confirm structural impairment rather than a technical dislocation.

**Rating:** -0.9 — SELL
