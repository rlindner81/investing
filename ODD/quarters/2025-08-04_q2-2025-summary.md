### CEO Oran Holtzman — Key Statements

- "ODDITY's momentum in 2025 continues, with another strong result this quarter and great progress on our long-term growth initiatives... This is more EBITDA and more free cash flow in the first half of the year than we delivered for the entire full year of 2023, the year of our IPO."

- On beating guidance: "In Q2, we once again beat our financial targets on revenue, profit, and earnings per share, as we have every quarter for the ninth quarter since our IPO."

- On Brand 3 (METHODIQ) go-to-market: "In terms of it's still B2C. It's still using our user base, still using our technology." Noted that the biggest differentiator is personalization — developed ~25 customer cohorts with unique treatment combinations, showing "material, material improvement in satisfaction — some numbers are above 50% compared to what exists in the market."

- On SpoiledChild soft launch learnings for Brand 3: "By the way, SpoiledChild was the same for the first almost three years. We did not even test international." Brand 3 will also be US-only at launch.

- On ODDITY Labs: ~70 scientists in Boston. Continuing to invest in new molecules for Brand 3 and Brand 4 near-term, plus longer-term molecules and delivery systems.

- On managing growth vs. profitability: "We want long-term, predictable, very strong growth." Confirmed ability to grow faster than delivered; constraint is deliberate to compound at 20% for many years.

---

### CFO Lindsay Drucker Mann — Key Statements

- On gross margin (+10 bps YoY): "We had some higher supply chain expenses that were offset by lower supply chain expenses to end up with a pretty flattish outcome." Guided conservatively at 70.5%; delivered 72.3%. Reiterated teams manage to DC margin (contribution margin after media spend), not gross margin.

- On tariffs: Continued to expect less than 100 bps gross margin headwind in 2025 and similarly manageable in 2026. "Based on the information we have today" with initial flow-through already seen in Q2.

- On international (H1 detail): International sales grew over 40% to ~\$85M. \$75M from established markets (UK, Australia, Canada, Germany, Israel). ~\$10M from test markets (France, Italy, Spain). "This is a business that could easily be as large as our U.S. business."

- On repeat rates: "Repeat continues to increase as a percentage of the business year over year... our 12-month repeat cohorts... remain very strong, over 100%, and performing well for both brands."

- On the exchangeable note: Zero coupon, five-year maturity, upsized to \$600M. Capped call purchased at ~10.5% of offering size limits dilution until stock approximately doubles. Ended Q2 with \$815M cash, equivalents, and investments.

- On capital allocation: Priorities are (1) reinvest in business, (2) M&A, (3) opportunistic buybacks. \$103M remaining on buyback authorization; no repurchases year to date.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Q3 gross margin guidance below consensus (Youssef Squali, Truist)**
CFO explained slight sequential compression is seasonal: H2 is repeat-heavy (lower AOV products) and fixed COG leverage is lower with smaller revenue dollars. No structural issues flagged. Guided 71% full year.

**2. Brand 3 not needed for 2025 or 2026 targets (Youssef Squali, Truist)**
CEO clarified that even if Brand 3 replicated SpoiledChild's first-year performance (~$25M), it would not be material relative to 2026's revenue base. Brand 3 upside is incremental to the algorithm.

**3. Growth management — will ODDITY constrain IL MAKIAGE/SpoiledChild to offset Brand 3 upside (Lauren Lieberman, Barclays)**
CFO: Yes, the company actively constrains growth to sustain 20% compounding. "We have the ability to grow faster than the actual numbers that we deliver. Our approach is to make sure that every single year we can compound at 20%."

**4. Brand 3 soft launch timing — shift from Q3 to Q4 (Lauren Lieberman, Barclays)**
CEO: Soft launch is essentially trial runs at small acquisition spend to identify issues before full launch. Will begin soft launch tests in Q3; formal launch with real acquisition spending begins Q4. "Official launch is where we begin spending real dollars both on brand and user acquisition."

**5. Return on brand investments (Anna Lizzul, BofA)**
CEO cited SpoiledChild as a benchmark: invested ~$20–25M, now generating ~$200M of revenue with very healthy margins after three years. ODDITY Labs has ~70 scientists; believes that investment will pay off at scale.

**6. International drivers — new markets vs. deeper penetration (Andrew Boone, Citizens JMP)**
International growth driven by both deeper penetration in established markets and initial spending in prospective markets (France, Italy, Spain). CEO noted that in new markets there are no existing users and no repeat yet, so acquisition costs are higher at the start.

**7. Repeat rate trends (Andrew Boone, Citizens JMP)**
Repeat rates remain very strong. 12-month cohort repeat rates >100% for both brands. Repeat increasing as a percentage of total revenue year over year.

**8. Brand 3 long-term revenue streams (Dara Mohsenian, Morgan Stanley)**
CEO: Revenue comes primarily from products (OTC + prescription). Mobile app for compliance monitoring. Telehealth platform with planned expansion into additional medical domains beyond dermatology. First-ever launch of ODDITY Labs proprietary molecules. About 50% of ODDITY's 60M+ users report skin issues, making them a natural initial target market.

**9. Brand 3 progress since Q1 (Dara Mohsenian, Morgan Stanley)**
CEO: "Three months is a short cycle." Key recent progress: unlocking both the diagnosis capability and ensuring the right customized product is matched to the right user. "We made a big progress in matching the right patient with the right treatment."

**10. Brand 3 focus areas and patient populations (Scott Schoenhaus, KeyBanc)**
CEO: Main initial push around acne and hyperpigmentation. Eczema products are ready but prevalence is smaller. Body products planned for Q1 2026. Expects new users from outside ODDITY's existing base to diversify the platform.
