## Conference Call Transcript Highlights

**Participants:** Oran Holtzman (Co-Founder & CEO), Lindsay Drucker Mann (Global CFO), Niv Preis (CTO, available for Q&A), Maria Lycouris (IR)

---

### CEO Oran Holtzman — Key Statements

- "Our Q1 results exceeded our expectations across all metrics and allow us to raise our full-year outlook. Once again, in Q1 2025, we beat revenue and EBITDA like we did every single quarter since going public — eight quarters in a row."

- On growth vectors: "The beauty industry is transforming, just like we said it would more than five years ago. ODDITY is a winner in this transformation." Highlighted two key vectors: the shift to online D2C and the shift toward high-efficacy products.

- On IL MAKIAGE SKIN: "It's on track to approach 40% of the brand revenue this year. Skin is a huge growth driver with multiple attractive product categories, with big TAMs, high AOVs, and great replenishment cycles."

- On international: Both US and international grew double digits in Q1. International is still less than 20% of the business. Called it "a massive engine for ODDITY" given that competitors derive less than 30% of their business from the US.

- On Brand 3 (METHODIQ): "I am more excited than ever before to see it live." Highlighted computer vision tools with 94% agreement with dermatologist tagging for acne grading and 93% recall for acne lesion localization. Predictive view algorithm trained on user data to visualize expected skin improvements.

- On the macro environment: "We see this moment as a massive opportunity for ODDITY. Our DNA and business model allow us to play full offense in times like today."

---

### CFO Lindsay Drucker Mann — Key Statements

- On gross margin of 74.9%: Expanded 116 bps; driven by cost efficiencies and product mix (more skin). Noted gross margin is not a KPI the teams are benchmarked to — they manage DC margin (contribution margin after media spend). Stated long-term gross margin run-rate is "more in the high 60s kind of range" and not committed to sustaining 71%+ levels.

- On repeat revenue: "Repeat revenue is the largest part of our business, exceeding 60% of total revenue in 2024 and increasing as a percent of our business again so far in 2025."

- On tariffs: Expected 50–100 bps gross margin impact in 2025. Current tariff assumption includes fully loaded China tariffs plus 10% on Europe. Stated the range remains accurate even if European tariffs doubled after the 90-day pause. Mitigated by limited China exposure (most costs come from Europe).

- On capital allocation: Exited Q1 with \$257M cash and zero debt, plus new \$200M credit facility. "We expect the current volatile market backdrop will create new opportunities for us to put our strong balance sheet to work" — including brand/business acquisitions, technology, and team acquisitions. Described internal hurdle rates as "high."

- On adjusted EBITDA margin: "We do not see a reason to deliver higher than a 20% EBITDA margin, especially because we really believe these investments can be massive unlocks for our business performance."

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. International expansion (Youssef Squali, Truist)**
International did great in Q1. Both US and international grew double digits. International remains less than 20% of the business. IL MAKIAGE has existing operations in US, Canada, UK, Germany, Australia, and Israel. France, Italy, and Spain already tested at large scale. CEO characterized international as a "massive engine" given competitor revenue mix.

**2. FTC click-to-cancel rule (Youssef Squali, Truist)**
CFO: Assessed as a non-issue. ODDITY's subscription model is fully opt-in (not opt-out), cancellation is self-serve online, and internal testing of compliance interpretations showed minimal impact on the business.

**3. Gross margin drivers and long-term trajectory (Mark Mahaney, Evercore ISI)**
CFO explained that gross margin outperformance vs. guidance is intentional — teams are given flexibility to chase DC margin (not gross margin). Long-term, gross margins expected in the "high 60s" range; company is committed to 20%+ adjusted EBITDA margins, not gross margin preservation.

**4. Brand 3 readiness (Cory Carpenter, JPMorgan)**
CEO said vision technology is "in better shape than what I hoped to be for launch" after nearly four years of development. Product range is the widest ODDITY has ever launched. Testing showed strong matching between diagnosis and treatment. No specific revenue targets shared.

**5. Tariff exposure details (Cory Carpenter, JPMorgan)**
CFO reiterated 50–100 bps gross margin impact for 2025. Guidance incorporates current tariff levels: full China plus 10% on Europe. Range holds even if European tariffs double after 90-day pause. Limited China exposure cited as key advantage.

**6. International strategy — scaling vs. new markets (Dara Mohsenian, Morgan Stanley)**
CEO explained that existing markets (US, Canada, UK, Germany, Australia, Israel) are active; France, Italy, Spain have been tested at large scale; some large developing markets recently began testing with "very strong" results. Strategy is to expand only where unit economics support it.

**7. M&A priorities (Dara Mohsenian, Morgan Stanley)**
CEO: Looking to acquire things ODDITY doesn't have. Focused on biotech and AI. Would consider a brand acquisition only if it has strong products or capabilities that would take years to build internally.

**8. SG&A investment breakdown / future brand investment (Andrew Boone, Citizens JMP)**
CFO outlined three main SG&A investment pillars: (1) new brand development (Brand 3 and Brand 4 already have teams); (2) ODDITY Labs (~60+ scientists in Boston); (3) technology (largest team in the company). Also highlighted the Phionic acqui-hire earlier in 2025.

**9. Telehealth infrastructure as platform (Andrew Boone, Citizens JMP)**
CEO: Once the prescription/compounding infrastructure is built for Brand 3, it can be reused across additional medical domains. "The potential is massive for us. It took us years to build it."
