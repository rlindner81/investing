## The CPA Crisis — Q1 Update

The Q1 2026 results reflect the continued fallout from the algorithm dislocation at Oddity's largest advertising partner (widely interpreted as Meta) that was disclosed in February 2026.

**Progress and remediation:**
- CPA trended worse each month through April (Jan < Feb < Mar < Apr in severity)
- May was the **first month of sequential CPA improvement** — approximately 28% decline in IL MAKIAGE CPA from April, breaking a multi-month negative trend
- The ad partner itself estimates it can recover **40–60% of CPA** through changes in its own systems alone
- Oddity successfully shifted **40% of acquisition revenue out of Try-Before-You-Buy** (TBYB) into standard buy model by end of Q1 with no impact on unit economics
- TBYB is now a "tiny percentage" of total orders; management plans to maintain it but at a lower proportion
- Management brought in an outside team to review marketing for the first time, alongside continued in-house remediation

**Why Q2 guidance still shows -25 to -30% despite May improvement:**
- Q2 guidance assumes CPA remains similarly difficult (May improvement not assumed to be durable)
- Q1 first-order shortfall (~50% decline) directly reduces Q2 repeat revenue since fewer Q1 customers exist to reorder
- Recovery sequencing: CPA improvement → first orders recover → repeat orders follow → profitability returns

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. EBITDA trajectory and recovery cadence**
Management emphasized sequencing: first-order volume must recover before repeat revenue can accelerate, and Q1's first-order shortfall creates an overhang through at least H1. The H2 improvement depends on CPA normalization coming early enough to generate meaningful first orders. No quarterly EBITDA guidance was given for H2.

**2. Why Q2 is still -25 to -30% despite May improvement**
May's improvement is real but not yet material enough to shift the overall Q2 CPA average. Additionally, the loss of Q1 first orders directly suppresses Q2 repeat revenue. The sequential positive trend in May is the leading indicator management is watching.

**3. Try-Before-You-Buy structural risk**
Analysts continued to probe whether TBYB is permanently impaired. Management pushed back: TBYB is "great for the consumer" and will be retained, now balanced at a lower proportion. The 40% shift to standard buy with no unit economics impact was presented as validating the model's flexibility, not its abandonment.

**4. Ad platform concentration and diversification**
The primary partner remains "by far the largest ad partner in beauty in the U.S., way more than 50% of the market." Management said diversification is being considered but no timeline was given. They pointed out this platform is dominant for D2C beauty broadly, and competitors face less impact only because they are omni-channel.

**5. SpoiledChild vs. IL MAKIAGE**
SpoiledChild also sees elevated CPA but with less severity, and the brand continued to grow in Q1. Management plans to apply IL MAKIAGE remediation learnings to SpoiledChild once the fixes are validated.

**6. Methodiq traction and Oddity Labs pipeline**
Methodiq is acquiring customers organically (not cross-selling from IL MAKIAGE base), tracking toward $25M year-one revenue. Two new LABS-derived products added to the Methodiq lineup in Q1 — DuraxSynd (eczema treatment, ODD-L1669 molecule) and XariLac (scalp prevention, ODD-L103 molecule). Labs pipeline continues in anti-aging, hyperpigmentation, and acne prevention.

**7. Long-term 2027 impact**
Analysts pushed on whether 2026 cohort loss creates a multi-year drag. Management acknowledged the 2027 headwind from reduced 2026 new customer acquisition, but said the plan is to return to "full power" growth spending as soon as CPA normalizes to compensate.
