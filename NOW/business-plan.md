# ServiceNow, Inc. (NYSE: NOW) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

ServiceNow is an enterprise software company built on a single, extensible cloud
platform (the **Now Platform**, now marketed as the **ServiceNow AI Platform**) that
digitizes and automates workflows across an organization — IT, employee (HR),
customer, and, increasingly, AI-agent governance. Its founding thesis: enterprises run
on fragmented systems and manual processes, and a single system of action that sits
across those silos can orchestrate how work actually gets done. By 2026 the company had
repositioned itself as "the AI control tower for business reinvention," selling AI
agents and an AI governance layer (Now Assist, AI Control Tower, Otto) on top of its
established workflow franchises.

- **Founded:** 2004 (as Glidesoft) by Fred Luddy; renamed Service-now.com in 2006
- **IPO:** June 2012 (NYSE: NOW), priced at $18/share
- **Headquarters:** Santa Clara, California
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year)
- **FY2025 Revenue:** $13,278M total (+21% YoY); subscription revenue $12,883M
- **Stock split:** 5-for-1, effective December 17, 2025 (first split in company history)

## Revenue Model

ServiceNow sells multi-year **subscriptions** to its cloud platform, priced primarily
by number of users/agents and by the mix of products/SKUs deployed. Revenue is
overwhelmingly recurring and ratable, which produces high visibility.

**Subscription revenues (~97% of total):**
- SaaS access to the Now Platform and its workflow products, recognized ratably over
  the contract term. This is the number ServiceNow guides on every quarter (it does not
  guide total revenue).
- Very high gross margin (non-GAAP subscription gross margin ~81%).

**Professional services and other (~3% of total):**
- Implementation, configuration, and training. Intentionally kept small and roughly
  break-even to gross-loss; ServiceNow steers implementation work to its partner
  ecosystem (Accenture, Deloitte, EY, KPMG) rather than doing it in-house.

**Key durable-revenue metrics ServiceNow reports:**
- **cRPO** (current remaining performance obligations) — contracted revenue to be
  recognized in the next 12 months; the market's favorite forward indicator. ~$13.2B,
  +21% YoY as of Q2 2026.
- **RPO** (total remaining performance obligations) — ~$29.0B as of Q2 2026, +21% YoY.
- **Customers with >$1M ACV** and **>$5M ACV** cohorts, and the count of **$1M+ net-new
  ACV** deals per quarter — the large-enterprise land-and-expand engine.

## Products

The platform is organized into a handful of workflow franchises plus a fast-growing AI
layer:

### Technology / IT Workflows
- **IT Service Management (ITSM):** the founding franchise — incident, problem, change,
  and request management. Still the anchor product in most land deals.
- **IT Operations Management (ITOM):** discovery, event management, and the **CMDB**
  (configuration management database) — increasingly pitched as the governed "data
  foundation" for enterprise AI.
- **Security & Risk (SecOps / GRC / IRM):** security incident response, vulnerability
  response, and integrated risk/compliance — a strategic growth area, supercharged by
  the 2026 Armis and Veza acquisitions and the AI Control Tower.

### Employee (HR) Workflows
- HR service delivery, employee onboarding/offboarding, and workplace service delivery.

### Customer Workflows
- Customer Service Management (CSM) and Field Service Management.

### Creator / App Engine
- Low-code/pro-code app development (**ServiceNow Studio**, App Engine) that lets
  customers and partners build custom workflows on the platform.

### AI Layer (the 2024–2026 growth story)
- **Now Assist** — generative-AI assistants embedded across ITSM, CSM, HR, and dev,
  sold as premium "Pro Plus" SKUs.
- **AI Control Tower** — a governance/observability plane for every AI agent, model, and
  workflow running in the enterprise (regardless of vendor); the centerpiece of the "AI
  control tower" positioning.
- **Otto** — a unified AI experience (2026) combining Now Assist, Moveworks, and AI
  Experience to route and complete work across departments.
- **ServiceNow AI ("Agentic AI"):** crossed **$1B in annual contract value** in Q2 2026;
  agentic deployments grew ninefold in nine months.

## Technology & Competitive Position

### Single-platform / single-data-model moat
- Every product runs on one platform, one data model, and one code base. This is
  ServiceNow's core structural advantage over best-of-breed point tools: customers land
  in one workflow and expand into adjacent ones without integration friction. It is also
  why the CMDB matters strategically — it is the governed system of record that AI agents
  need to act safely.

### Large-enterprise land-and-expand
- ServiceNow sells top-down to the Global 2000 and government. Net expansion (measured by
  renewal rates ~98% and growing $1M+/$5M+ ACV cohorts) is the engine; the average
  customer buys more SKUs and more users over time.

### AI positioning
- Rather than compete as a foundation-model provider, ServiceNow positions itself as the
  **orchestration and governance layer** that works with any model (partnerships with
  NVIDIA, Microsoft, AWS, Anthropic) — selling AI as premium SKUs on an existing,
  sticky install base with the enterprise data and workflow context that models lack.

### Partner ecosystem
- Global SIs (Accenture, Deloitte, EY, KPMG) drive implementation and demand; hyperscaler
  marketplaces (AWS Marketplace crossed $1B in transactions) are a growing distribution
  channel.

Go-to-market is a direct enterprise sales force augmented by the SI/hyperscaler partner
ecosystem; there is no meaningful self-serve motion.

## Growth Strategy

1. **Monetize AI as premium SKUs** — convert the installed base to Now Assist "Pro Plus,"
   grow agentic AI ACV (target: 30% of ACV from AI by 2030), and sell the AI Control
   Tower as the enterprise governance standard.
2. **Expand Security & Risk** — leverage Armis (asset/exposure) and Veza (identity) plus
   AI Control Tower to build an autonomous security-and-risk franchise.
3. **Deepen large-enterprise expansion** — grow the $5M+ ACV cohort and $1M+ net-new-ACV
   deal count; lengthen contract durations (driving RPO growth).
4. **Land the CMDB as the AI data foundation** — position ITOM/CMDB as the governed data
   layer autonomous AI needs.
5. **Long-term financial targets (2026 Financial Analyst Day):** $30B+ subscription
   revenues, 30% of ACV from AI, and a **Rule of 60+** (constant-currency subscription
   growth + FCF margin) by 2030, plus a commitment to cut SBC to <10% of revenue by 2029.

## Key Risks

### Valuation / growth-deceleration
- ServiceNow has historically traded at a premium multiple; the stock de-rated sharply
  through 2025–2026 (P/S compressed from ~19x to ~6–7x as subscription growth decelerated
  from the high-20s toward ~20%). Any further deceleration or a cRPO miss is punished hard.

### AI-monetization execution
- The entire bull case now leans on AI SKUs converting the base and agentic ACV
  compounding. If enterprise AI ROI disappoints or competitors (Microsoft, Salesforce,
  point AI vendors) win the governance layer, the premium unwinds.

### Balance-sheet shift / M&A
- Historically nearly debt-free, ServiceNow raised **~$8B in debt** in Q2 2026 (senior
  notes + term loan + commercial paper) to fund the Armis/Veza acquisitions, moving from a
  large net-cash to a modest net-debt position. Integration and capital-allocation risk
  rises with the acquisition cadence.

### Stock-based compensation dilution
- SBC runs ~15% of revenue (~$2B/yr), materially reducing FCF on a strict basis; the
  company has committed to <10% by 2029 but has not yet demonstrated the reduction.

### FX and macro
- International revenue creates recurring FX headwinds/tailwinds in guidance; enterprise
  IT budgets are macro-sensitive on complex, long sales cycles.
