ServiceNow's fundamentals are still compounding hard even as the multiple has collapsed. Revenue **re-accelerated to +24.0% Y/Y in q2-2026** (\$3,987M total), up from the ~20–21% band of the prior four quarters and the FY-2025 pace of +20.9% — a rare inflection *up* at this scale. The company has beaten the high end of its own next-quarter guidance every quarter shown (+3% to +6% actual-vs-guide) and raised the full-year subscription guide repeatedly, a spotless raise track record. (Note: NOW guides *subscription* revenue only, so the guidance rows measure against the subscription base, ~97% of total.)

FCF quality is the one real blemish. TTM company FCF is \$4,580M at a healthy **31.1% margin**, but that figure adds back \$2,185M of stock-based comp; the **strict ex-SBC margin is only 16.3%**, roughly half. SBC runs ~15% of revenue — management has committed to sub-10% by 2029 but hasn't yet delivered it, so the gap between "company" and "real" FCF stays wide. FCF is also lumpy quarter to quarter (q2 company FCF fell -10.1% Y/Y and strict-ex-SBC went negative at -\$179M on a heavy-SBC quarter).

Valuation is where the story turns compelling. TTM **P/S has de-rated to 7.6x from ~19x** in early 2024; **P/FCF is 24.3x** (company) — cheap for a 20%+ grower with 98–99% renewal rates and 80%+ subscription gross margins. Forward P/S on FY guidance is ~7.1x. On strict ex-SBC, P/FCF is a still-rich 46.5x, so the "cheapness" leans on the company FCF definition.

Balance sheet shifted meaningfully: NOW took on **~\$7.5B of debt in q2-2026** (from \$1.5B) to fund the Armis/Veza security acquisitions, moving from large net-cash toward modest net-debt (\$2.5B cash vs \$7.5B debt). Verdict: growth re-accelerating into a heavily de-rated multiple — attractive on company FCF, with SBC and the new leverage as the caveats.

**Rating:** 1.3 — BUY

## 3. News

**The surface narrative.** NOW reported Q2-2026 after-close on Jul 22 and the story is entirely "beat-and-raise into an AI-disruption panic." The stock fell -3.7% the next session to \$91.94, extending a ~38% YTD decline, before bouncing to ~\$107 by Jul 27. Two forces dominate: (1) a genuine strong quarter, and (2) a sector-wide fear that AI agents will gut per-seat SaaS pricing — the same fear that produced NOW's **-17.75% single-day crash after the April Q1 print, its worst day ever as a public company**.

**The deeper read — substantiated.** The Q2 beat rests on hard, disclosed numbers, not narrative: **subscription revenue \$3,877M (+24.5% Y/Y)**, 150 bps above guidance; **total revenue \$3,987M**; adjusted EPS \$0.90 vs ~\$0.86 est; **AI ACV crossed \$1B**; RPO \$29B / cRPO \$13.2B (+21% cc); FY subscription guide raised to \$15.76–15.78B. These are in the company's own release ([servicenow.com](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)) and corroborated across outlets. Crucially, the two margin guides *held* this time — the twin margin cuts on the April call were what actually crashed the stock, so their absence is the real "tell."

**The deeper read — two legitimate cautions.** CFO Mastantuono attributed **~half the Q2 beat to US-Federal on-prem revenue pulled forward from Q3**, and a new **July 1 pricing change triggered early renewals** that may have borrowed cRPO strength from Q3 — Jefferies and KeyBanc (Underweight, \$85) both flagged this. Both are substantiated by management commentary and are real quality-of-beat concerns, not rumor.

**The disruption thesis — partly narrative.** The bear case (IBM's Jul warning that clients shifted budgets from software to AI *hardware*; OpenAI's "Presence" enterprise-agent launch; the "why pay \$100/seat when an agent does it for pennies" framing) is a real, verifiable macro shift, but its *impact on NOW specifically* is still speculative — NOW's \$1B AI ACV and 98% renewal rate are the concrete counter-evidence. NVIDIA's Jensen Huang publicly called NOW "the operating system of enterprise AI agents."

**Sentiment & catalysts.** Analyst consensus is **Strong Buy, avg target ~\$140** (≈35–45% upside), though targets were cut ~24% over three months (DA Davidson, KeyBanc trims). Motley Fool (Jul 26) calls it a long-term buy at ~5x forward P/S. Next catalyst is Q3 earnings (~late Oct), where the pull-forward and pricing-change concerns get tested. Key risks: the disruption narrative re-taking the tape, a Q3 cRPO air-pocket from borrowed renewals, SBC/dilution, and integration of the debt-funded Armis/Veza deals.

**Rating:** 1.0 — BUY

## Verdict

ServiceNow is a rare case of fundamentals *re-accelerating* (+24% Y/Y subscription growth, AI ACV past \$1B, spotless beat-and-raise, 98% renewals) straight into a ~40% YTD de-rate that took the multiple from ~19x P/S to ~7.6x — the whole move driven by a sector-wide "AI eats SaaS" fear rather than any crack in the business. The main risk is that this fear is right at the margin: watch Q3, where ~half the Q2 beat was pulled-forward federal on-prem revenue and a July-1 pricing change may have borrowed cRPO, so a Q3 air-pocket is a live threat, alongside heavy SBC (~15% of revenue) and the new ~\$7.5B of acquisition debt. The tape confirms the tension — a violent downtrend, but with an early reflex bounce off a washed-out ~\$92 base and support (POC) building below. A clean Q3 that dispels the pull-forward worry and shows AI consumption inflecting would re-rate the stock toward the ~\$140 analyst consensus; a cRPO stumble or renewed disruption panic sends it back to retest the lows.

**Rating:** 0.9 — BUY
