Growth is intact and the multiple has collapsed anyway — that gap is the whole story here.

**Revenue trend.** No deceleration worth the name. Q2-2026 total revenue of \$3,987M grew +24.0% Y/Y, an *acceleration* over Q1-2026's +22.1% and FY-2025's +20.9%. The four most recent quarters run +18.6%, +22.4%, +21.8%, +20.7%, +22.1%, +24.0% — a remarkably flat band around low-20s for three straight years. Nothing in the reported top line shows the AI-driven erosion the market has been pricing.

**FCF profile.** Strongly positive but violently seasonal, and the SBC gap is the real issue. TTM company FCF is \$4,580M (31.1% margin). Strict ex-SBC FCF is only \$2,395M (16.3%) — SBC of \$2,185M TTM consumes **48% of company FCF**. That is a heavy, persistent dilution charge, not a one-off. Q2-2026 was the weakest quarter in the series: company FCF \$473M (−10.1% Y/Y, the first FCF decline since q2-2024) and strict FCF actually *negative* at −\$179M, because Q2 SBC jumped to \$652M — well above the \~\$490M quarterly run-rate. Q1/Q4 are the cash-heavy quarters (collections seasonality), so read FCF on a TTM basis only.

**Valuation.** The de-rating is severe. P/S fell 19.0x (FY-2024) → 9.2x (FY-2025) → **9.9x TTM**, bottoming at 6.3x in April 2026. P/FCF company is 31.9x TTM, down from 61.2x. On strict ex-SBC FCF it's still 61.0x — the honest number, and it isn't cheap. Forward P/S on FY guidance is 9.3x. For a business compounding low-20s with 31% FCF margins, high-single-digit P/S is genuinely undemanding versus its own history; the strict-FCF multiple is what keeps this from being an obvious bargain.

**Guidance track record.** Immaculate and mechanical: every quarter beats its own next-quarter guide by +2.8% to +6.0%, and FY guides beat by +3.1% to +4.9%. Management sandbags consistently, so the Q3 guide of \$3,975–3,980M should be read as a floor.

**Balance sheet.** Now a net debt story: debt rose \$1.49B → \$7.52B against \$2.50B cash (≈\$5.0B net debt), funding the Armis and Veza deals. Investment-grade and \~1.1x TTM company FCF, so serviceable, but the free-optionality cushion is gone.

**Verdict:** growth and guidance discipline are unimpaired while the multiple has halved — attractive, with SBC-adjusted cash generation the one thing preventing an unqualified call.

**Rating:** 1.3 — BUY

## 3. News

**The surface narrative.** NOW ripped \~30% in August, and the coverage credits three things: the Q2 beat-and-raise, a wave of analyst target hikes (BofA \$130→\$150 on Aug 19, sending the stock +7% that day; Wells Fargo \$160→\$175 on Aug 17), and a sector-wide sympathy move on Aug 27 (+9.2%) after Salesforce's report suggested the "SaaSpocalypse" was overdone. The framing across most outlets is that the market is unwinding an AI-disruption discount it applied earlier in 2026.

**The deeper read.** The distinction that matters here is between the Q2 numbers and the rally that followed.

*Substantiated.* Q2-2026 is hard data, confirmed directly in the company's 8-K: subscription revenue \$3,877M (+24.5%), total \$3,987M (+24%), cRPO \$13.20B (+21%), RPO \$29.0B (+21%), 123 deals over \$1M net-new ACV (+\~40% Y/Y), 658 customers above \$5M ACV. **ServiceNow AI crossed \$1B in ACV** — that one is in the release, not just commentary, and it's the single most important datapoint against the disruption thesis: the AI transition is showing up as revenue, not attrition. The guidance raise is real and committed (FY subscription \$15,760–15,780M). Also substantiated and negative: the Armis (\$7.6B, closed Apr 20) and Veza (\$1.2B, Mar 2) acquisitions are in the 10-Q, and they took debt from \$1.49B to \$7.52B and goodwill to \$9,837M.

*Mostly attention.* The August rally itself. BofA explicitly **did not change earnings estimates** — the target hike was a re-rating call on sector sentiment, not new information about NOW. The Aug 27 +9.2% move had no company-specific news at all; it was a read-through from a competitor's print. The widely repeated "50% of net new business is non-seat-based" and "98% renewal rate" figures are **not in the 8-K** — they come from call commentary and secondary write-ups, so they're management characterization rather than audited disclosure. Treat them accordingly. The move from \~\$99 (June) to \~\$148 (Aug 31) is roughly a 50% re-rating on one quarter of data plus sentiment; that is running ahead of the fundamentals, which improved but did not improve 50%.

**The one real yellow flag in the numbers.** Q2 subscription revenue beat partly on a *timing* shift — strong U.S. Federal demand pulled on-premise revenue forward from Q3 into Q2. Management calls it pure timing, but it means Q2's +24.5% borrowed from Q3, and the Q3 guide of 20% growth (below the \~\$4B consensus) reflects that. GAAP subscription gross margin also fell to \~73.5% from \~80% on Armis/Veza intangible amortization — a real, ongoing drag from the M&A.

**Catalysts.** Q3 earnings expected **Oct 28, 2026** (after close; not yet officially confirmed by the company — Market Chameleon models Oct 23–29). That print is the test of whether the federal pull-forward was timing or borrowed growth. Watch the \$1.5B FY AI ACV target, now viewed as credible.

**Sentiment/positioning.** Institutional ownership \~87%, \~2,300 filers, with net accumulation. Short interest is low and falling — under \~2% of float, \~2 days to cover — so there's minimal bearish positioning and no squeeze dynamic; the August move was real buying, not covering. (Caveat: much indexed short/13F data predates the Dec 2025 5-for-1 split and is stale.) CEO Bill McDermott bought \$3M personally in February.

**Risks.** The AI-disruption thesis is unproven either way — Gartner's estimate that agents divert \~20% of enterprise software spend by 2030 is a forecast, not evidence, but ServiceNow's seat-based base sits in its path and terminal value is what the market is actually arguing about. Beyond that: integration risk on the largest acquisition in company history, the new net-debt position, margin dilution from purchased intangibles, and federal exposure that just demonstrated it can move revenue between quarters.

**Rating:** 0.9 — BUY

## Verdict

ServiceNow is a high-quality compounder that the market de-rated on a thesis its own numbers keep contradicting: revenue grew +24% in Q2-2026 while the P/S multiple went from 19x to under 10x, and ServiceNow AI crossing \$1B in ACV is concrete evidence the AI transition is additive rather than cannibalistic. The main risk is that the bears are early rather than wrong — the terminal-value question on seat-based software is genuinely unresolved, and it is compounded by three things visible in the filings: SBC consuming \~48% of company FCF, a new \~\$5B net debt position from Armis and Veza, and a Q2 beat that borrowed federal on-premise revenue from Q3. The August rally has already recovered \~50% off the June low largely on sentiment and analyst re-rating rather than new fundamental information, so the easy re-rating is done and the entry is no longer exceptional. The Oct 28 Q3 print is the decision point: a clean number above the sandbagged 20% guide with AI ACV tracking toward \$1.5B would confirm the pull-forward was timing and justify a higher rating, while a Q3 miss, evidence of cRPO deceleration, or SBC staying at Q2's elevated \$652M level would suggest the discount was warranted.

**Rating:** 1.0 — BUY
