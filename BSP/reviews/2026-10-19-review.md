**Revenue.** Q2-2026 revenue of \$704.2M was up 126% Y/Y, following +132% in Q1, so headline growth is holding up. Nearly all of it is bought: organic growth was only 3% in Q2, down from 6% in Q1. TTM revenue is \$2.04B. Q3 guidance of \$733–745M (+113% at the midpoint) and FY-2026 guidance of \$2.78–2.82B include only businesses owned at the report date. That means Airtable (closed in September) and Miro (\~\$600M ARR, closing in Q4) sit on top of the guide. Q2 was the first report since the IPO, so there is no raise-or-cut track record yet.

**FCF.** Cash generation is real and growing. TTM company FCF is \$453M (22.2% margin), and Q2 alone delivered \$178M (25.3%). Capex is negligible. The strict ex-SBC figure is \$382M (18.7%), so SBC of \$71M takes off about 15% of FCF. That's a moderate burden, not an alarming one. Two caveats. First, acquisitions (\$2.29B in H1-2026) are the real capital spend and aren't counted as capex. Second, the Q3/Q4-2025 split is a placeholder, although the TTM figures are unaffected.

**Valuation.** At \$46.83, the market cap is \$28.1B. That's 13.8x TTM sales, 62x company FCF and 74x strict FCF. On guidance, forward P/S is 11.5x (NQ) and 10.0x (FY). Adding Airtable and Miro to the run-rate probably pushes forward sales toward \$3.8B+, which would bring P/S down to the high single digits. Even so, these are premium software multiples for a business whose organic growth is in the low single digits. You're paying for the acquisition machine, not for the assets it owns.

**Balance sheet.** Cash is \$793M against \$4.88B of debt, so net debt is \~\$4.1B, before the \$1.25B + €395M TLB add-ons that fund Miro. Enterprise value is therefore meaningfully above the market cap. The credit upgrades (Ba3/BB-) and Berkshire's \$330M help, but leverage is the core of the model.

**Verdict:** The cash-flow quality is real and the M&A engine is working, but at \~60x+ FCF with 3% organic growth and \~\$4B+ of net debt, the stock already prices in years of flawless deal execution.

**Rating:** -0.3 — HOLD

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** The story right now is Berkshire Hathaway. On Oct 14 Berkshire agreed to buy 10M newly issued shares at \$33.00 (\$330M), lifting its stake to \~4% on top of the \$600M it put in at the IPO. The stock closed +41% at \$53.46, peaked at \$59.50 intraday on Oct 15, then fell 15.6% to \$47.07 on Oct 16. CNBC and the Motley Fool ran "should you follow Berkshire?" features over the weekend. A week earlier, on Oct 7, the stock jumped \~24% intraday (closing +8 from the open) with no announced catalyst.

**Deeper read, item by item:**

- **Berkshire placement: substantiated.** The company's 6-K/PR confirms it, and it is committed cash. The fine print matters, though. Berkshire bought at \$33, \~12% *below* the prior close and \~30% below today's price. It got a discount, and the market then paid a 40%+ premium over Berkshire's price. The Berkshire endorsement is real, but the price move far outran the economics of the deal itself. Friday's fade is the market correcting that.
- **The Oct 7 move: mostly attention.** No filing or release landed that day. The after-the-fact explanations (Miro financing, credit upgrades) point to events from Sep 22–Oct 2 that were already public. It looks like momentum and short-covering in a name with 8.8% short interest.
- **Debt financing and credit upgrades: substantiated.** The TLB add-ons closed Oct 2 (\$1.25B + €395M, upsized from \$750M + \$250M targets). Moody's moved to Ba3 and S&P to BB-. That's real evidence of credit-market appetite, but it also adds \~\$1.7B of debt.
- **Miro (\$1.355B EV, \~\$600M ARR): substantiated** by a definitive agreement. It is still pending regulatory approval, with closing guided for Q4. Sellers reinvesting \$295M in BSP equity is a vote of confidence, though it also means some dilution.

## Verdict

Bending Spoons is a high-quality cash compounder built on acquisitions. FCF margins are above 20%, credit is upgrading, deals are flowing (Airtable closed, Miro pending), and Berkshire has now invested twice. But the October rally ran far ahead of anything concrete: Berkshire bought at \$33, and the stock still trades \~40% above that at \~60x TTM FCF with only 3% organic growth and \~\$4B+ of net debt that is still rising. The tape shows distribution below the \~\$54 volume node, and an IPO lock-up expiry is approaching. The main risk is that the market starts valuing BSP on organic growth and leverage rather than on deal velocity. A clean Q3 print (\~Nov 12) with organic growth back toward mid-single digits and a fast Miro close, or a pullback toward the mid-to-high \$30s, would turn this into a buy. A guide miss or another debt-funded deal at a richer price would push it lower.

**Rating:** -0.1 — HOLD
