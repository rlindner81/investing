# Bending Spoons S.p.A. (Nasdaq: BSP) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

Bending Spoons is a Milan-based serial acquirer of digital businesses. It buys software and internet companies with large, predictable revenue bases (mostly subscription), puts them through a deep transformation (team reorganization, a technology rebuild, product acceleration, and monetization optimization), and reinvests the resulting cash flow, plus generous amounts of debt, into the next acquisition. It has completed 50+ acquisitions and has never sold a material business. Its portfolio includes AOL, Brightcove, Eventbrite, Evernote, Harvest, komoot, Remini, StreamYard, Tractive, Vimeo, WeTransfer, and, as of September 2026, Airtable.

- **Founded:** June 2013 in Milan by Luca Ferrari, Francesco Patarnello, Matteo Danieli, Luca Querella (and Tomasz Greber), on \$40K left over from their failed startup Evertale
- **IPO:** July 1, 2026 (Nasdaq Global Select: BSP) at \$29.00, above the \$26–28 range. 57.97M shares offered (34.4M primary, 23.6M secondary) plus a fully exercised 8.7M greenshoe. Primary net proceeds were \$1.10B. Berkshire Hathaway bought \$600M in the IPO and added \$330M on Oct 14, 2026 (\~4% stake).
- **Headquarters:** Milan, Italy (offices in London, Madrid, Warsaw, and New York)
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year)
- **Filer status:** Foreign private issuer, US GAAP, USD reporting. Quarterly results come on 6-K; the first 20-F covers FY2026.
- **FY2025 Revenue:** \$1,306.4M (+95% YoY); 2023–25 CAGR of 84%

## Revenue Model

Revenue is the sum of the portfolio businesses' revenue. Growth is overwhelmingly **inorganic**: Q2-2026 revenue rose 126% YoY on only 3% organic growth.

**By source (Q1-2026):**
- **Subscriptions (84%):** recurring consumer, prosumer, and SMB/enterprise subscriptions (Evernote, WeTransfer, StreamYard, Vimeo, Remini, komoot, Harvest, Brightcove, Tractive). Subscriptions were 93–95% of revenue in 2023–25.
- **Advertising (12%):** mostly AOL (webmail and news portal) and WeTransfer, sold largely through programmatic networks. The share rose sharply with the AOL deal (Jan 2026).
- **Other (4%):** Eventbrite ticketing fees and transactional revenue.

**Retention quality:**
- Net revenue retention: 93% (2023), 91% (2024), 95% (2025), 94% (Q1-2026). By business, Evernote is 99%, AOL 95%, StreamYard 91%, and Remini 87%.
- 48% of subscription revenue comes from customers with 5+ years' tenure (28% with 10+). Revenue-weighted average tenure is 8.0 years.
- Management says the mix is \~50% consumer / \~50% professional and enterprise, and it is tilting toward enterprise (Brightcove, Vimeo, Airtable, Miro).

**Geography (Q1-2026):** US \$374M of \$601M (\~62%), UK \$36M, Germany \$20M, Canada \$16M, Japan \$14M.

**Non-GAAP lens:** Management runs the business on **Adjusted Operating Income**, which strips out amortization of acquired intangibles, transaction costs, and reorganization costs, but keeps SBC as an expense. Adj. OI margin was 36% (2023), 45% (2024), 47% (2025), 51% (Q1-2026), and 54% (Q2-2026). GAAP operating margin is \~20–34%; the gap is mostly amortization and severance from serial reorganizations.

## The Playbook & Competitive Position

### Three-Step Playbook
1. **Acquire:** target large, forecastable digital revenue bases with significant room for improvement. Underwriting hurdles are **65% levered / 25% unlevered IRR**, with acquisition debt modelled at the lower of 85% of EV or what the target's FCF can repay in 5 years. Acquisition EV deployed: \$194M (2023), \$876M (2024), \$1.92B (2025), \$2.01B (Q1-2026 alone).
2. **Transform and optimize:** reorganize (most acquired staff are let go; of 1,830 FTEs added with AOL, Eventbrite, and Vimeo, only "a few hundred" are expected to remain), rebuild the tech stack on BSP's platform, redesign the UI, and run heavy monetization experiments (paywalls, pricing, plan segmentation).
3. **Reinvest:** recycle OCF plus incremental debt into new deals, raising equity opportunistically.

### The Platform (claimed moat)
- **People ("Spooners"):** \~800K applications in 2025, 286 hires (<0.04%). Staff are redeployed across businesses as task forces. Revenue per FTE Spooner was \$2.57M in 2025, with a run rate above \$4M in Q2-2026.
- **Proprietary technology:** shared data infrastructure, a lifetime value (LTV) predictor, and an experimentation toolkit (3,000 experiments in 2025). Most acquired businesses adopt nearly all of it.
- **AI leverage:** the AI-authored share of pull requests went from <10% (Q1-2025) to >90% (end of Q1-2026). "Alt-Spooner", an in-house agent running on self-hosted open-weight models, processed 100B+ tokens in its first three weeks.

### Competition
On the buy side, BSP competes mostly with PE sponsors and occasionally with strategic acquirers. Its pitch is that it can improve assets more than other bidders, so it can pay more and still clear its hurdles. Each business also faces its own product competitors (AOL vs Gmail/Outlook, Vimeo vs Cloudflare Stream, WeTransfer vs Google Drive, and so on).

## Markets & Channel Mix

- **US-centric revenue** (\~62%) with a European operating base. Hiring is diversifying away from Italy (>50% of Q2-2026 hires were outside Italy).
- Distribution: direct web and app-store subscriptions (app-store fees appear in cost of revenue as "distribution and payment processing") plus programmatic advertising.
- Paid user acquisition is cut back sharply after each acquisition (e.g. Eventbrite paid UA down 30%).

---

## Growth Strategy

- **Bigger deals, faster:** BSP estimates its addressable universe at 1,000+ businesses with \~\$400B of 2025 revenue. Enterprise SaaS has re-rated down ("prices are now more reasonable"), which opened the Airtable and Miro deals.
- **Funding stack:** term loan B add-ons (\$1.25B + €395M, Sep 2026), euro term loan A and RCF facilities (€590M + €30M post-Q2), a €500M SACE-backed loan (Jul 2026), IPO proceeds, the Berkshire placement, and seller roll-over equity (Miro).
- **Organic upside from transformations in progress:** AOL (new CMS, rebuilt ad-tech stack, webmail migration, with short-term ad disruption baked into guidance), Eventbrite, Vimeo, and Tractive.
- **Guidance** (as of Q2-2026, owned businesses only, no pending deals): Q3-2026 revenue \$733–745M; FY2026 revenue \$2.78–2.82B (+114% at midpoint); FY adj. OI \$1.46–1.51B.

## Key Risks

### Acquisition-Dependence and Return Decay
Organic growth is \~flat (+3% in Q2-2026) and Remini and Splice are declining. Headline growth requires an ever-larger flow of deals. Management itself tells investors to "assume some deterioration" in returns as deal size grows. Operational capacity (enough Spooners to transform several large businesses at once) is an acknowledged bottleneck.

### Leverage and Interest Burden
Net debt was \$4.09B at Q2-2026 (2.4× covenant leverage, which uses *pro-forma, cost-savings-adjusted* EBITDA; the covenant ceiling is 4.0×). Q2 interest expense was \$109M (+205% YoY), and \$794M of debt matures within 12 months. Debt has grown further since (TLB add-ons, Airtable). Rising rates or a failed refinancing would squeeze the reinvestment loop.

### Earnings Quality
- A large and growing gap between GAAP and adjusted figures: amortization of acquired intangibles (\$82M in Q2) plus recurring "one-off" reorganization charges (\$51M in Q2), which recur because acquisitions are continuous.
- A Q1-2026 cash flow **restatement**: \$48M of acquired Eventbrite restricted cash had been misclassified into OCF.
- No standalone Q3/Q4-2025 figures have been published, so the history is thin.

### Governance and Control
Four co-founders hold all class A shares (5 votes each), giving them \~83% of voting power post-IPO. BSP is an Italian S.p.A. under the Italian Civil Code (board plus audit-committee system) and a foreign private issuer with lighter disclosure duties. The 180-day IPO lock-up expires around late December 2026.

### Product/Platform Risks
AOL ad revenue depends on programmatic markets and an in-flight ad-tech migration. Consumer apps face app-store fee and policy risk. AI could disrupt incumbent tools (Evernote, Remini) as easily as it helps BSP run them. Aggressive monetization changes risk churn and brand damage (e.g. user backlash at Evernote and WeTransfer).

### FX
The cost base is in euros while revenue is mostly USD. Euro-denominated debt creates large FX swings in "other income" (a \$20M gain in Q2-2026).
