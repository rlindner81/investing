# PayPal Holdings, Inc. (NASDAQ: PYPL) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

PayPal Holdings, Inc. operates a two-sided global payments platform that connects
merchants and consumers, enabling digital and mobile payments across online, in-app,
and increasingly in-store commerce. PayPal's core thesis: as commerce continues to
move online and across borders, a trusted, ubiquitous payments network that sits at
the center of the checkout — for both the merchant accepting the payment and the
consumer funding it — captures a durable toll on global digital commerce. The company
monetizes primarily by taking a fee on total payment volume (TPV) that flows across
its network.

- **Founded:** December 1998 (as Confinity); merged with X.com (March 2000); renamed PayPal (June 2001)
- **eBay era:** Acquired by eBay in 2002; operated as eBay's payments arm for 13 years
- **Spin-off / relisting:** Spun off from eBay July 18, 2015; relisted on Nasdaq July 20, 2015 (PYPL)
- **Headquarters:** San Jose, California
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year)
- **FY2025 Revenue:** \$33,172M (+4.3% YoY)
- **Scale:** ~200+ markets, 25 currencies; ~24M merchant accounts and ~400M+ consumer accounts historically; ~24,000 employees

## Revenue Model

PayPal reports a single "Net revenues" line, but the economics come from two buckets:

**Transaction revenues (~90% of revenue):**
- Fees charged to merchants (and some consumers) as a percentage of, and/or fixed
  fee per, transaction processed across PayPal, Venmo, and Braintree.
- The primary volume metric is **Total Payment Volume (TPV)** — the dollar value of
  payments processed. Revenue is a "take rate" on TPV. Because a large and growing
  share of volume is lower-take-rate unbranded processing (Braintree), reported
  revenue grows slower than TPV, and management increasingly steers investors toward
  **transaction margin dollars** (revenue net of transaction expense and credit
  losses) as the key profit metric rather than headline revenue.

**Other value-added services (~10% of revenue):**
- Revenue from partnerships, referral fees, subscription fees, gateway fees,
  consumer credit products, and — importantly — **interest earned on customer
  balances and on the float** of funds held on behalf of customers. Interest on
  customer balances became a meaningful profit contributor as rates rose in 2023–2024
  and is now a headwind as rates decline.

**Key economic nuance — branded vs. unbranded:**
- **Branded checkout** (the yellow PayPal button, "Pay with PayPal/Venmo") is the
  high-margin core. Its growth (or lack thereof) is the single most-watched operating
  metric and the crux of the bear case.
- **Unbranded / PSP processing** (Braintree) is high-volume, low-margin merchant
  acquiring that competes directly with Stripe and Adyen. It has driven much of TPV
  growth but dilutes take rate and margin.

## Products & Brand Portfolio

### PayPal (core wallet & checkout)
The flagship two-sided network: a consumer digital wallet and merchant checkout
button. Funds transactions from bank accounts, cards, PayPal balance, or credit.
The branded checkout experience is PayPal's highest-margin product and strategic
priority.

### Venmo
US peer-to-peer payments app with strong Gen Z / Millennial adoption and a social
feed. Monetization levers: Venmo debit/credit cards, "Pay with Venmo" at merchant
checkout, instant transfer fees, and business profiles. A major growth focus under
recent management as PayPal works to monetize Venmo's large, engaged user base.

### Braintree (unbranded PSP)
Full-stack payment processor / gateway for large enterprises (used by many marketplaces
and apps). Drives large TPV at low take rates; the primary battleground vs. Stripe and
Adyen.

### Other brands
- **Xoom** — international remittances / cross-border money transfer
- **Honey** — browser shopping/deals extension acquired 2020 (offers, rewards, cart)
- **Hyperwallet** — mass-payout platform for marketplaces and gig platforms
- **Zettle** (formerly iZettle) — in-person / point-of-sale card readers (SMB, in-store)
- **PayPal Credit / Pay Later (BNPL)** — consumer credit and buy-now-pay-later
- **PayPal USD (PYUSD)** — a USD-backed stablecoin, PayPal's crypto/blockchain bet

## Technology & Competitive Position

### Two-sided network & trust
PayPal's core moat is its two-sided network: hundreds of millions of consumers with
stored credentials on one side and tens of millions of merchants on the other. Its
brand carries consumer trust ("buyer protection") that can lift merchant conversion
at checkout — the crux of branded checkout's value proposition.

### Data & risk management
Decades of transaction data underpin fraud detection, risk scoring, and credit
underwriting at scale across 200+ markets — a genuine advantage in loss rates and
regulatory compliance that is hard for newer entrants to replicate.

### Where the moat is contested
- **Checkout:** Apple Pay, Shop Pay (Shopify), and native card wallets increasingly
  compete for the branded checkout slot that has been PayPal's crown jewel.
- **Unbranded processing:** Stripe and Adyen out-innovated PayPal/Braintree on
  developer experience and enterprise features during the 2020s.
- **P2P:** Venmo competes with Zelle (bank-owned), Cash App (Block), and Apple Cash.

### AI / agentic commerce (2025–2026 bet)
Management is positioning PayPal for "agentic commerce" — enabling AI agents to
transact — via partnerships announced with Google, OpenAI, and Perplexity, plus
PayPal's stablecoin (PYUSD) rails.

## Markets & Channel Mix

| Channel | Notes |
|---|---|
| Online branded checkout | Core, highest-margin; growth is the key debate |
| Unbranded / Braintree PSP | Large TPV, low take rate; competes w/ Stripe & Adyen |
| Venmo (US P2P + commerce) | Under-monetized asset; key growth lever |
| In-store / omnichannel | Smaller; Zettle, contactless, debit cards |
| Cross-border | Xoom remittances, cross-border merchant volume |

- The US is the largest and most profitable market; interest on customer balances
  is a US-rate-sensitive contributor.
- Volume mix has shifted toward lower-take-rate unbranded processing, compressing
  overall take rate even as TPV grows.

## Growth Strategy

1. **Reaccelerate branded checkout** — modernize the checkout experience (one-click,
   Fastlane guest checkout, higher conversion) to defend and regrow the high-margin core.
2. **Monetize Venmo** — convert Venmo's large engaged base into commerce and card
   revenue (Pay with Venmo, debit/credit cards, business profiles).
3. **Improve unbranded economics** — grow Braintree PSP volume while raising its
   margin through value-added services rather than pure price competition.
4. **Transaction-margin-dollar growth** — management's north-star profitability metric;
   guidance and incentives are framed around it, not headline revenue.
5. **Advertising & data** — build a commerce media / ads business leveraging PayPal's
   transaction data (a newer, higher-margin optionality).
6. **Agentic commerce & PYUSD** — position the network for AI-driven purchasing and
   stablecoin settlement.
7. **Capital return** — large, consistent share buybacks (~\$6B/yr) plus a newly
   **initiated quarterly dividend (announced Q3 2025)**, steadily shrinking the share
   count.

## Key Risks

### Branded checkout stagnation (the core bear case)
Branded checkout growth has been sluggish while competition (Apple Pay, Shop Pay,
native wallets) intensifies. If PayPal loses checkout share, the high-margin core
erodes even as low-margin unbranded volume grows — a mix shift that pressures overall
margins and the multiple.

### Take-rate compression
The shift toward unbranded/Braintree volume structurally lowers the blended take rate,
so revenue grows slower than TPV. Investors increasingly discount headline TPV growth.

### Interest-rate sensitivity
A meaningful slice of profit comes from interest on customer balances; falling rates
in 2025–2026 are an explicit guidance headwind.

### Competitive intensity
Stripe and Adyen in processing; Apple, Shopify, and banks (Zelle) in consumer-facing
payments; Block's Cash App in P2P. PayPal competes on multiple fronts simultaneously.

### Execution / leadership transition
Two CEO changes in ~2.5 years (Dan Schulman → Alex Chriss Sept 2023 → Enrique Lores
March 2026). The Board's Feb 2026 statement that "the pace of change and execution was
not in line with expectations" underscores execution risk. Q4 2025 revenue missed and
FY2026 EPS guidance implied a decline, prompting the CEO change.

### Regulation
Global payments, lending, consumer-protection, privacy, and crypto/stablecoin
regulation across 200+ jurisdictions create ongoing compliance and enforcement risk.
