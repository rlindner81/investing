PayPal's top line is stable and modestly reaccelerating. TTM revenue is \$34.1B, with the newly reported q2-2026 up +4.8% Y/Y and q1-2026 up +7.2% — versus the +1.2% trough in q1-2025 and +4.3% for FY-2025. This is a mature payments franchise growing mid-single digits, not a growth story: branded checkout is running only \~2% currency-neutral, with the composite carried by Venmo, Braintree and BNPL.

FCF is the real asset and it just repaired. TTM company FCF is \$6.59B (19.3% margin), up sharply from \$5.50B at the July review, because q2-2026 delivered \$1.78B against a distorted \$0.69B year-ago quarter (+156.5% Y/Y) — a working-capital normalization in the receivables book rather than an operating step-change. FY-2025 FCF of \$5.56B was still -17.8% below the \$6.77B FY-2024 peak, so the trend is a recovery off a weak year, not a new high. SBC is disciplined at \~\$1.0B/yr — roughly 15% of FCF, low for large-cap tech — so strict ex-SBC FCF of \$5.59B (16.4% margin) sits close to the company figure. Earnings quality is clean.

Valuation is outright cheap and got cheaper. At \$52.17 the TTM P/S is 1.3x and P/FCF is 6.8x (8.1x strict ex-SBC) — near the q4-2025 trough of 1.1x / 6.9x set when the stock traded at \$41, and roughly half the 2.1x / 12.2x prints of a year ago. Market cap is \$44.97B against \$6.6B of TTM FCF. The share count is the quiet compounder: 862M vs. 1,053M two years ago, a \~18% reduction, with \~\$6B of FY-2026 buybacks guided against a \$45B cap.

The balance sheet deteriorated: debt rose to \$13.40B (a \$2.5B short-term line appeared at Jun'26) against \$8.31B cash — \$5.1B net debt versus \$2.4B a quarter earlier, funding the buyback. Easily serviced, but the buyback is now partly levered. Guidance is EPS-only (FY-2026 non-GAAP EPS raised to \~\$5.38), so forward P/S rows are n/m. Verdict: a cheap, FCF-rich, aggressively shrinking share base — the multiple already discounts stagnation.

**Rating:** +1.3 — BUY

## 3. News

**Both open questions from the July 23 review have now resolved — in opposite directions.**

**Q2 FY26 (July 28) was a genuine beat-and-raise — substantiated.** Against management's own pre-guided weak quarter, PayPal delivered non-GAAP EPS of \$1.38 vs. \$1.28 consensus and revenue of \$8.68B vs. \$8.47B expected (+5% Y/Y), with currency-neutral TPV of \$486.4B (+9%). Transaction margin dollars grew +1% to \$3.9B (+3% ex-interest-on-balances). Management *raised* FY-2026 non-GAAP EPS to \~\$5.38 from a prior "low-single-digit decline to slightly positive," lifted FY transaction margin dollars to \~\$15.6B — reversing a guided decline — and raised branded-checkout guidance to low-single-digit growth. Branded checkout stabilized at +2% currency-neutral for a second straight quarter; Venmo debit MAUs +50%, BNPL +26%. This is all in the 8-K and the 10-Q now in this repo — hard, committed numbers. The catch is equally real: non-GAAP operating margin contracted 248bp to 17.4% as non-transaction opex rose +9%, and GAAP EPS fell to \$1.25 from \$1.29. The stock rose only \~+3.8% on the print (day 0 of the reaction table).

**The Stripe/Advent bid is dead — substantiated, and it is the dominant fact.** On Aug 27 the consortium walked away from its \$60.50/share (\~\$53B) offer. PYPL fell -12.7% on Aug 28 (intraday -16%), from \$61.32 to \$53.66, on 36.3M shares. The proximate cause per Axios was a circular standoff: Stripe priced off the \$47.37 undisturbed price and refused to pay for appreciation it believed its own bid had created, while the board — which had pushed for \~\$70 — credited its turnaround. The board also cited regulatory and financing concerns. Target cuts followed immediately (Truist \$62→\$53, Mizuho \$60→\$51, Loop \$62→\$50; RBC was the outlier, raising to \$70). Consensus is Hold with targets clustered \$56–\$60 — i.e. analysts see roughly zero to +15% from \$52.17.

**September has been a drip of negatives, all substantiated.** A WARN filing confirms 251 layoffs at San Jose HQ (part of a disclosed \~20% / 4,500-role, \$1.5B-savings program under CEO Enrique Lores, with \$400M of actions due by year-end 2026). On Sep 3 PayPal *paused* the sale of its \~\$900M–\$1B venture portfolio after top bids came in near 60 cents on the dollar — a real, if small, mark-to-market on fintech asset values and a lost cash source. A securities class action (Goodman v. PayPal, N.D. Cal.) covering Feb 2024–Feb 2026 over branded-checkout disclosures is proceeding past its lead-plaintiff deadline. PYUSD circulation has contracted from a \$4.3B peak to \~\$2.8B — the asset Stripe was reportedly chasing is shrinking.

**Sentiment vs. substance.** Institutional Q2 13F flow was heavy accumulation (Norges \~\$922M new, CPPIB +694%, Lazard +1,066%, Primecap +9.5%), but that was filed *into* the deal window and is now stale as a signal. Short interest is only 4.4% at 2.0 dtc and down -38% — the merger-arb book has unwound, removing a mechanical bid. Retail narrative has flipped from "takeover target" to "value trap," and the \$0.14 quarterly dividend (record Sep 4) is too small to anchor anything. **Key risks:** the stock now trades purely on standalone execution with no bid underneath; branded checkout at +2% is stabilization, not growth, against Apple Pay, Shop Pay, Klarna and Braintree-rival Stripe; opex is running ahead of the savings program with most benefit promised in Q4; and net debt rose to \$5.1B funding buybacks. **Next catalyst: Q3 FY26 earnings, Tuesday Oct 27, 2026 (pre-open)**, where Lores must show the \$400M of cost actions and Q4 margin inflection are real.

## Verdict

The two binary questions from July both answered, and they cancel: PayPal delivered a real beat-and-raise on July 28 (EPS \$1.38 vs. \$1.28, FY guide lifted to \~\$5.38, transaction-margin decline reversed to growth), but on August 27 Stripe and Advent walked from their \$60.50 bid and the stock gave back the entire deal premium in one -12.7% session. What remains is a genuinely cheap, FCF-rich asset — \$6.6B TTM FCF at 6.8x, 1.3x sales, an 18% smaller share count in two years — with no acquirer underneath it and a broken chart where every nearby volume shelf (POC 53.7–55.5) is overhead supply on fading, uncapitulated volume. The main risk is that branded checkout at +2% is stabilization rather than growth while opex runs ahead of the \$1.5B savings program, so the cheapness persists as a value trap rather than resolving. Evidence at the Oct 27 Q3 print that the \$400M of cost actions and the promised Q4 margin inflection are landing — or branded checkout accelerating past low-single digits — would push this to BUY; a renewed bid would do it faster. Another guide-down, or checkout share loss resuming, takes it to SELL.

**Rating:** +0.0 — HOLD
