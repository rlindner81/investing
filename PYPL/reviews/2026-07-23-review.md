## 3. News

PayPal is the center of an active, high-stakes takeover battle — the dominant story. On July 21, 2026, PayPal's board formally rejected a \$60.50/share (~\$53B) all-cash joint bid from **Stripe and Advent International**, calling it inadequate and pushing for a price closer to \$70. The consortium had lined up ~\$50B of debt (JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley) plus ~\$17B of equity (Block and others); the \$60.50 offer was a 28% premium to the undisturbed \$47.37 price. The strategic prize is reportedly PYUSD, PayPal's stablecoin network across ~70 markets, and its consumer-trust layer for AI-agent commerce, more than its 439M accounts. PayPal has hired Goldman Sachs and Evercore to weigh options (sale vs. independence), and analysts broadly expect the consortium to return with a sweetened bid. This explains the +18.7% single-week and +26.4% MTD price surge documented in the price table — the rally is deal-driven, not fundamentals-driven.

**Next catalyst is imminent and pivotal: Q2 FY26 earnings on July 28, 2026 (before open).** Management has pre-guided a deliberately weak quarter — low-single-digit currency-neutral revenue growth, a low-single-digit decline in transaction margin dollars, and a high-single-digit non-GAAP EPS fall (consensus EPS ~\$1.28 vs. \$1.40 a year ago). The tough comp reflects last year's partner renewal, stronger credit, and a favorable tax rate. A convincing beat strengthens the board's negotiating hand and could force a higher Stripe bid; a miss or further branded-checkout share loss makes rejecting a 28% premium look costly.
