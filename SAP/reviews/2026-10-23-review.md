**Revenue is re-accelerating.** Y/Y growth has climbed for three straight quarters — +3.3% (q4-2025) → +6.0% → +9.4% → +13.2% in q3-2026 (in USD; \~12% at constant currency per SAP). Part of the USD figure is EUR strength, but the underlying driver is real: cloud revenue +26% and current cloud backlog +28% to €24.1B. TTM revenue is \$44.2B.

**FCF is strong and much cleaner than a year ago.** TTM company FCF is \$10.1B (22.9% margin), up from \$4.7B in FY-2024 when restructuring cash out depressed it. q3-2026 FCF was +35.8% Y/Y. SBC has fallen from \$2.7B (FY-2024) to \$1.7B TTM, so the strict ex-SBC view (\$8.4B, 18.9% margin) is now only \~17% below company FCF — no longer a material quality gap. Quarterly FCF is heavily front-loaded (Q1 collections), so read it on a TTM basis.

**Valuation is fair, no longer cheap.** At \$240.59 the stock trades at 6.3x TTM sales, 27.3x company FCF and 33.0x strict FCF. That's up sharply from the July trough (4.0x / 17.7x) but well below the 2025 peak (8.2x / 47–66x). The `FW P/S FY guidance` row (6.7–6.8x) is misleading: the FY-2026 guide in `FINANCIALS.yml` is cloud+software revenue only (€36.3–36.8B), not total revenue — so it sits *below* TTM total revenue and overstates the forward multiple. On total revenue growing \~12%, forward P/S is roughly 5.6–5.8x.

**Guidance:** FY-2026 cloud+software guidance was held flat through Q1/Q2 and at Q3 was nudged "toward the upper end" with the cloud revenue outlook updated; profit and FCF guidance reaffirmed. That is a steady record with a mild upward tilt, not an aggressive raise. FY-2025 came in on its guide.

**Balance sheet:** roughly neutral — \$10.5B cash vs \$11.9B debt (\~\$1.4B net debt), with debt rising as SAP funds its €10B buyback (shares out down from 1,166M to 1,147M this year). Short interest is negligible (0.4%).

**Verdict:** a high-quality, accelerating, FCF-rich compounder at a fair \~27x FCF — upside now depends on backlog conversion rather than multiple re-rating.

**Rating:** +0.6 — BUY

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** The story is SAP's Q3 2026 beat (Oct 21, after the close) and the rally that followed. Headlines lead with "first €10B quarter," "cloud growth accelerates," and a round of price-target raises (Deutsche Bank, Berenberg, Oddo BHF, plus bullish Jefferies/BofA notes). The ADR rose \~1.6% into the print and \~3.5% the next day, to \~\$241.

**Deeper read — substantiated.** The core of the rally rests on reported numbers from SAP's own release and quarterly statement:
- Total revenue €10.27B (+13%, +12% cc) vs \~€10.10B consensus.
- Cloud revenue €6.67B (+26%); Cloud ERP Suite +29%.
- Current cloud backlog €24.1B (+28%, +26% cc). This beat UBS's 24–25% bar and directly rebuts UBS's August downgrade thesis that CCB would slow to \~23% in H2.
- Non-IFRS operating profit \~€3.03B (+18%) and FCF \~€1.72B (+36%).
- Non-IFRS EPS €1.89 vs €1.83 consensus.

Guidance was nudged toward the upper end of the cloud and cloud+software ranges, with operating profit and FCF reaffirmed. This is a modest, real upgrade, not a reset. The buyback is concrete: 25.2M shares repurchased at an average €164.79 through Sep 30 under the €10B program. *(Note: sources disagree on USD consensus figures, and the 6-K is not yet on EDGAR.)*

**Mostly attention.** The price-target raises are opinion following the numbers, not new information. The "Autonomous Enterprise" / Joule / AI-agent framing is narrative. "Nine of the ten largest deals included AI" is a qualitative claim, and SAP still discloses no AI revenue. UBS's count of \~17 live agents vs a 200 year-end target is a fair yellow flag. One metric cuts the other way: cloud gross margin slipped \~0.7pp.

**Price moves since last report.** From the July 23 print (\~\$146, near the \~€131–134 June lows), the stock has risen \~64%. The driver was the fading "SaaSpocalypse" fear: the AI-agent-driven software sell-off that began with the January 29 backlog miss (−16%). The bottom was narrative-driven and so was much of the July–August rebound (IGV +12.9% in August). This week's leg, by contrast, is backed by hard backlog data.

**Catalysts.** Q4/FY-2026 results and the FY-2027 outlook in late January 2027 will be the key test of whether CCB converts. The ongoing €10B buyback runs through end-2027.

**Sentiment.** Short interest is negligible (0.4%, 2.0 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic. Sell-side is split on valuation after the rerate.

**Risks.** The AI-disruption thesis for seat-based SaaS could re-ignite on any agentic-AI headline. EUR/USD swings move the ADR. Cloud gross-margin dilution from AI and hyperscaler costs. A January guide that disappoints, as in 2026.

## Verdict

SAP has turned the corner from its January 2026 backlog miss. Revenue growth has re-accelerated for three straight quarters to \~13%, current cloud backlog is +28%, and TTM FCF of \$10.1B now carries a much smaller SBC drag. At \~27x FCF the stock is fairly rather than cheaply valued, and the chart shows a volume-confirmed breakout. The main risk is a renewed AI-disruption scare in seat-based software. After a \~64% run from the July lows, the stock is also short-term overextended, so the January 2027 FY-2027 guide becomes a high-stakes event. A CCB deceleration below \~24% or a soft 2027 cloud outlook would cut the rating to HOLD. Sustained 25%+ backlog growth with cloud margins stabilizing would support a higher one.

**Rating:** +0.8 — BUY
