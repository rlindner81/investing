# BARK, Inc. (NYSE: BARK) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

BARK, Inc. is a New York-based, NYSE-listed omnichannel brand whose mission is to "make all dogs happy." The company designs and sells proprietary BARK-branded toys, accessories, and consumables through direct-to-consumer subscriptions and a retail network of 50,000+ doors. BARK does not describe itself primarily as a subscription box company — it describes itself as a dog-first brand platform building multiple product and service verticals around the human-dog relationship.

- **Founded:** 2012
- **IPO:** June 2, 2021 (NYSE: BARK), via SPAC merger with Northern Star Acquisition Corp. at ~$1.6B enterprise value
- **Headquarters:** New York City, NY
- **Fiscal Year End:** March 31
- **FY2025 Revenue:** $484.2M (down 1.2% YoY; FY ended March 31, 2025)

---

## Revenue Model

BARK generates revenue through two reported segments:

**1. Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) — 85.9% of FY2025 revenue ($415.8M)**
- Subscription boxes delivered monthly (BarkBox, Super Chewer, BARK Bright)
- Consumables sold through bark.co (BARK Food, treats, dental products)
- BARK Air premium dog-first charter flights
- DTC revenue declined 4.7% YoY in FY2025 as subscriber counts have trended lower

**2. Commerce — 14.1% of FY2025 revenue ($68.3M)**
- Proprietary BARK-branded products sold through retail partners: Target, Walmart, Petco, PetSmart, Chewy, Amazon
- Commerce grew 27.2% YoY in FY2025 — the company's main growth vector

**Key financial characteristics:**
- Gross margin: ~62–64%, improving as product mix shifts toward consumables and DTC margin recovers
- Subscription model generates predictable recurring revenue but is subject to churn
- Marketing is the primary lever for DTC subscriber acquisition; elevated CAC has pressured unit economics
- Tariff exposure concentrated in toys & accessories category (largely China-sourced); consumables largely domestic

## Product Portfolio

### BarkBox (Flagship Subscription)
The original product: a monthly themed box containing 2 toys and 2–3 bags of treats, built around a rotating monthly theme.

- **Price:** ~$23–$35/month depending on plan length
- **Audience:** Standard chewer dogs
- **Add-ons:** Customizable for size, allergies, and chew intensity

### Super Chewer
A premium subscription for aggressive chewer dogs, featuring heavy-duty rubber and rope toys that resist destruction.

- **Price:** ~$35–$46/month depending on plan length
- **Differentiation:** Targets a customer segment poorly served by standard toys

### BARK Bright
A dental health subscription focused on teeth-cleaning chews and oral hygiene products for dogs.

### BARK Food & Consumables
Dog food and treat line sold via direct-to-consumer subscription and on bark.co. Domestically sourced, giving BARK a tariff-resilient revenue stream. Consumables have grown to approximately one-third of total company revenue and represent the company's primary strategic growth priority for FY2026 and beyond.

- **BARK in the Belly:** Sub-brand that donates 100% of food profits to fight canine hunger

### BARK Air (Launched FY2025)
A dog-first charter flight service — the first air travel experience designed specifically around dogs rather than accommodating them as an afterthought.

- **Revenue:** $5.8M in inaugural year (FY2025); $1.8M in Q4 FY2025
- **Passengers:** ~1,000 passengers over 100+ flights in first year
- **Strategic significance:** Anchors a broader push into premium dog-lifestyle services; differentiates BARK as a lifestyle brand rather than a subscription box company

### Subscriber Perks (Launched October 2025)
A loyalty program offering subscribers over $1,500 in annual value through exclusive offers and discounts from travel, wellness, dog care, and lifestyle partners. Designed to improve subscriber retention and broaden the platform relationship beyond monthly boxes.

## Technology & Platform

- Migrated subscriber base to **Ordergroove** (subscription platform) and **Shopify** (eCommerce) in FY2026 — intended to reduce cost, improve personalization, and enable new subscription models
- Proprietary customer data from millions of dog profiles (breed, size, age, chew intensity, allergies) used to personalize product selection and theme curation
- Leverages scale of 50,000+ retail doors for brand awareness that drives DTC acquisition

## Growth Strategy

1. **Consumables shift:** Reallocate resources from toys & accessories toward consumables and services — consumables are tariff-resilient, domestically sourced, and drive higher-frequency repeat purchases
2. **Commerce expansion:** Continue growing retail presence across existing and new partners; Commerce is the fastest-growing segment
3. **Subscriber quality over quantity:** Deliberately reduce low-quality subscriber acquisition; accept near-term DTC revenue pressure in exchange for better retention, LTV, and profitability
4. **BARK Air & services:** Scale premium dog-lifestyle services as a brand differentiator and high-margin revenue opportunity
5. **Profitability focus:** Prioritize Adjusted EBITDA generation and free cash flow over top-line growth; reached debt-free status in Q3 FY2026 after repaying 2025 Convertible Notes in full
6. **Platform modernization:** Complete migration to Ordergroove + Shopify infrastructure to reduce operational costs and enable personalized subscriber experiences

## Key Risks

### Near-Term: DTC Revenue Decline
DTC revenue has declined steadily as the subscriber base has contracted — down 4.7% in FY2025 and accelerating to -19.9% in Q2 FY2026. Management has characterized this as a deliberate strategic choice (favoring quality over quantity), but continued subscriber attrition without a clear reacceleration path is a structural concern.

### Structural Risks
- **Churn concentration in year-one subscribers:** Retention is weakest in the first year; if a cohort disappoints, it creates downstream revenue drag that compounds over multiple years
- **Tariff exposure:** A significant portion of toys & accessories are sourced from China; escalating tariffs (~$4M additional H1 FY2026 headwind) compress margins and require pricing or mix adjustments
- **Platform concentration:** Significant reliance on Meta/Instagram for subscriber acquisition; any algorithmic change (similar to what impacted ODD) would have an outsized effect on new subscriber economics
- **Retail execution risk:** Commerce growth is encouraging but depends on maintaining shelf space and promotional support at major retail partners; losing a key account would create a step-down in revenue
- **Macro sensitivity:** Dog toys and subscription boxes are discretionary; pet humanization tailwinds are long-term positive, but near-term consumer confidence headwinds could accelerate subscriber churn
- **Scale economics:** BARK remains subscale relative to its fixed cost base — operating leverage is limited until revenue stabilizes and grows
