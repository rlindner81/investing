## Stock Reaction

Shares were mixed following the announcement. Revenue beat guidance and the Zacks consensus estimate by ~3.2%, but EPS of $(0.04) missed the consensus estimate of approximately $(0.03) by ~38%. The net loss improvement ($(7.0)M vs. $(10.0)M prior year) and positive Adjusted EBITDA of $0.1M were constructive, but the Commerce gross margin compression and negative free cash flow (driven by a $10M inventory build) tempered enthusiasm. The stock had been under pressure during 2025 given the sustained DTC revenue decline.

## Conference Call Transcript Highlights

**Participants:** Michael K. Mougias (VP, Investor Relations), Matt Meeker (Co-Founder, CEO & Executive Chairman), Zahir Ibrahim (CFO)

**Analysts:** Maria Ripps (Canaccord Genuity), Ryan Robert Meyers (Lake Street Capital Markets)

---

### CEO Matt Meeker — Key Statements

- "Revenue came in ahead of guidance, we closely managed our marketing spend to ensure we delivered positive adjusted EBITDA, and we achieved our strongest DTC gross margin quarter to date."

- On tariffs and strategy: "Tariffs and policy shifts under the current administration create a lot of uncertainty, but we have a plan and are committed to achieving adjusted EBITDA profitability." Outlined four-part response: manufacturing diversification outside China, operational productivity gains, potential modest price increases, and supply chain realignment.

- On DTC gross margin: The 250 bps improvement to 67.0% was driven by the shift toward higher-value Super Chewer subscriptions, improved product mix toward consumables, and disciplined promotional activity. Management characterized this as the best DTC gross margin quarter in the company's history.

- On Commerce growth: The 49.5% revenue gain was driven by strong performance at Costco, Amazon, Chewy, and TJX. Management acknowledged the Commerce gross margin (~31.7%) was temporarily depressed by two factors: opportunistic liquidation of surplus/legacy inventory through discount retailers, and elevated tariff costs on seasonal toy products that came in at the 145% tariff rate. Guided the Commerce margin to return to the low-to-mid 40% range.

- On BARK Air: Surpassed $2M in quarterly revenue — management characterized the product as validating that there is real premium demand for dog-first travel experiences.

- On full-year guidance: Declined to provide full-year revenue or EBITDA guidance, citing the inability to forecast tariff policy trajectory and its second-order effects on consumer demand.

---

### CFO Zahir Ibrahim — Key Statements

- On consolidated gross margin: The 70 bps decline to 62.3% was driven entirely by Commerce mix, as Commerce (with depressed margins) represented a larger share of revenue. DTC gross margin improved 250 bps to 67.0%.

- On marketing discipline: The $5.2M reduction in marketing spend (from $20.4M to $15.2M) was intentional. At current subscriber acquisition costs, the company is focused on quality of new subscribers acquired rather than maximizing volume.

- On inventory: The $10M inventory increase was planned ahead of anticipated demand — not a demand shortfall signal. It consumed cash in the quarter, driving the $(6.1)M free cash flow result.

- On the tariff response: H1 FY2026 tariff headwind was approximately $4M on inventory costs, concentrated in China-sourced toys. The company is actively expanding manufacturing outside of China and considering price adjustments as partial offsets.

- On share repurchases: $1.8M repurchased in Q1 at prevailing prices, consistent with the board's ongoing buyback authorization.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Commerce gross margin compression**
Analysts asked about the dramatic drop in Commerce margins. Management explained the two drivers (surplus inventory liquidation + 145% tariff rate on seasonal products) and emphasized the Q1 result is not representative of steady-state Commerce profitability. Guided return to low-to-mid 40% range.

**2. Tariff exposure and mitigation path**
Analysts pressed on the breadth of tariff exposure. Management described toys (China-sourced) as the primary exposed category; consumables (largely domestic) are resilient. Mitigation levers: geographic manufacturing diversification, vendor renegotiations, productivity gains, and price increases. Full-year tariff headwind expected to be approximately $4M in H1, with ongoing uncertainty for H2.

**3. DTC revenue decline sustainability**
Analysts asked when the DTC decline would find a floor. Management attributed the 16.7% decline to their own marketing pullback decisions rather than structural demand erosion — framing it as self-imposed. The company is acquiring fewer but higher-quality subscribers (measured by LTV and retention), with the subscriber base intentionally shrinking as lower-quality cohorts churn out.

**4. Subscriber quality vs. quantity**
Analysts explored the shift toward Super Chewer and higher-value plans. Management noted a favorable mix shift: more new subscribers opting for Super Chewer (higher AOV) versus the standard BarkBox, which contributed to the DTC gross margin improvement.

**5. Free cash flow and working capital**
Analysts asked about the $(6.1)M FCF result given the positive Adjusted EBITDA. Management explained the $10M inventory build as deliberate seasonal pre-positioning, not a demand issue. Expects working capital to normalize in subsequent quarters.

**6. BARK Air trajectory**
Analysts asked about the path to meaningful scale. Management reiterated $2M+ in Q1 as a positive step, characterizing BARK Air as a proof-of-concept that validates the premium dog-lifestyle brand thesis. Declined to provide specific forward revenue targets.

**7. Full-year guidance withdrawal**
Analysts pushed on the decision to withhold full-year guidance. Management cited the speed at which tariff policy was changing (rates moving dramatically within days) as making any full-year financial forecast unreliable. Committed to providing quarterly guidance until the external environment stabilized.
