## Conference Call Transcript Highlights

**Participants:** Michael Mougias (VP, Investor Relations), Matt Meeker (Co-Founder, CEO & Executive Chairman), Zahir Ibrahim (CFO)

---

### CEO Matt Meeker — Key Statements

- "Our priorities remain — running the business with discipline, protecting profitability, and continuing to diversify the ways we serve dog parents."

- On revenue decline: Characterized the 22.1% decline as primarily self-inflicted — a "deliberate" and "measured" pullback in marketing spend to shift the subscriber acquisition mix toward higher-quality customers. The revenue miss was not market-driven or demand-driven.

- On subscriber strategy: Management is "comfortable" with the subscriber base shrinking over time. The frame: LTV of a retained, high-quality subscriber is worth more than the revenue from an acquired, low-quality subscriber who churns in year one.

- On debt repayment: Highlighted the full repayment of the 2025 Convertible Notes in cash as a landmark for balance sheet health — BARK is now fully debt-free.

- On consumables and Commerce: Reiterated that the strategic shift toward consumables (domestic, tariff-resilient, higher repeat frequency) and Commerce expansion is the right long-term direction even if it creates near-term revenue optics pressure.

---

### CFO Zahir Ibrahim — Key Statements

- On gross margins: Both DTC (+10 bps) and Commerce (+230 bps) gross margins improved despite the revenue decline — demonstrating that profitability discipline is holding even as the top line contracts.

- On marketing reduction: The $11M reduction in marketing spend was intentional. At current subscriber acquisition costs, the marginal subscriber acquired through high-spend channels does not meet the company's LTV hurdle.

- On free cash flow: Positive $1.6M FCF in Q3 is a tangible outcome of the profitability-first strategy — working capital improved as inventory was reduced by ~$10M.

- On the convertible notes: Full repayment eliminates interest expense and any dilution risk from the convertible structure, simplifying the capital structure significantly.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Revenue trajectory and bottom**
Analysts pressed on when the DTC decline would stabilize. Management declined to give a specific inflection point but emphasized that the decline is driven by their own marketing decisions, not structural demand erosion — implying they could re-accelerate if unit economics improve.

**2. Subscriber count and quality metrics**
Analysts asked for subscriber count data. Management framed the conversation around order volume and LTV rather than raw subscribers, consistent with the quality-over-quantity messaging.

**3. Marketing efficiency improvements**
Analysts asked whether the pullback in marketing spend was permanent or temporary. Management indicated it is strategic — they will reinvest in marketing when CAC/LTV math supports profitable acquisition, but will not chase volume for its own sake.

**4. Tariff impact in H2 FY2026**
Analysts asked about tariff exposure in Q3 and Q4. Management indicated the $4M H1 headwind (flagged in Q4 FY2025 call) had largely flowed through; ongoing exposure depends on tariff policy trajectory, with consumables providing a natural hedge.

**5. Commerce growth vs. prior quarter**
Commerce revenue of $18.8M was ~$1.5M below Q3 FY2025. Management noted timing shifts in retail partner orders as an explanation — the full-year trend (strong retail expansion) remains intact.

**6. BARK Air update**
Management indicated BARK Air is continuing to operate and build its customer base but did not disclose updated revenue figures. It remains a small but strategically meaningful contributor to the brand's premium positioning.

**7. Path to consistent profitability**
Analysts asked when BARK expects to reach sustainable positive Adjusted EBITDA. Management reiterated commitment to profitability as a priority but did not provide a specific timeline, stating results would be driven by the pace of consumables growth and Commerce expansion.
