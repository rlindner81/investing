# BARK, Inc. (NYSE: BARK) — Q1 FY2027 Earnings Results

**Reported:** August 6, 2026 (after close, 4:30 p.m. ET call)
**Period:** Three months ended June 30, 2026
**Note:** BARK's fiscal year runs April 1 – March 31. Q1 FY2027 = April–June 2026.

---

## Reported Financial Metrics

### Income Statement (Q1 FY2027 vs. Q1 FY2026)

| Metric | Q1 FY2027 | Q1 FY2026 | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net Revenue | \$78.8M | \$102.9M | -23.4% |
| DTC Revenue | \$66.7M | ~\$89.2M | -25.2% |
| — of which BARK Air | \$3.2M | ~\$2.3M | +37% |
| — DTC ex-Air | \$63.5M | \$86.8M | -26.8% |
| Commerce Revenue | \$12.1M | ~\$13.7M | -11.4% |
| Gross Profit | \$57.3M | \$64.1M | -\$6.8M |
| Reported Gross Margin | 72.7% | 62.3% | +1,040 bps |
| **Normalized Gross Margin** (ex tariff refund) | **63.4%** | 63.8% | -40 bps |
| Advertising & Marketing | \$9.5M | \$15.2M | -37% |
| G&A | \$47.8M | \$57.3M | -\$9.5M |
| Net Income (Loss) | +\$0.75M | \$(7.0)M | Turned positive |
| Adjusted EBITDA | +\$0.6M | +\$0.1M | Improved |
| Net cash used in operations | \$(3.5)M | \$(5.4)M | Improved |

*Reported gross margin and net income include a one-time \$7.4M IEEPA tariff refund
allocable to FY2026 costs, recorded entirely this quarter. It is **excluded** from
Adjusted EBITDA and is **not** recurring. Revenue of \$78.8M landed at the high end
of the \$77–79M guidance range; Adjusted EBITDA was within the \$0.0–1.0M range.*

### CEO Matt Meeker — Key Statements

- "We are off to a good start in fiscal 2027… the plan we described in June is working."
  Revenue of \$78.8M at the high end of guidance was "powered by strong subscriber
  retention, better-than-expected sales in the retail channel, and BARK Air flights
  filling up."

- On DTC: The decline is a **volume story on a smaller entry base**, not demand erosion.
  Retention +170 bps, AOV +\$0.45, and BarkBox subscriber LTV "near its highest level
  for us as a public company."

- On the tariff refund: Reported 72.7% gross margin includes a one-time FY2026 IEEPA
  tariff refund; **normalized gross margin was 63.4%**, in line with last year's record
  63.8%. The refund is excluded from Adjusted EBITDA and does not recur.

- On the product pipeline (fall FY2027 catalysts):
  - **Licksters** — a refillable enrichment toy + treat "razor/razor-blade" platform
    (recurring treat refills); a three-year innovation roadmap. Seeded into subscriber
    boxes now; retail rollout this fall at Target, PetSmart, Walmart, Amazon, Chewy.
  - **Crocs for Dogs** — partnership expands in October 2026 (toys, beds, accessories,
    new colorways); the dog shoes were BARK's most successful TikTok launch to date.
  - **Liquid Death** — Liquid Death's first-ever pet collaboration, a co-designed line
    of toys and accessories launching this fall.
  - **Girl Scout Cookie** program launching this winter (Commerce catalyst).

### Interim CFO Brian Dostie — Key Statements

- DTC ex-Air was \$63.5M vs. \$86.8M; orders down ~28% YoY while AOV rose \$0.45 —
  "the exact trade we said we were making."

- The \$7.4M IEEPA tariff recovery relates to FY2026 costs, was recorded as a
  **receivable** (no cash impact this period), and is excluded from Adjusted EBITDA.
  \$3.2M received as of quarter-end; majority of the remainder expected over coming
  quarters.

- Marketing \$9.5M, down 37% (>\$5.6M) YoY — discipline held, "prepared to reinvest
  when efficient customer acquisition opportunities present themselves."

- Shipping & fulfillment \$23.8M (30.2% of revenue, vs. 30.9%); other G&A \$23.9M,
  down ~6%. Inventory \$72.4M, down >\$25M YoY. "Priority is driving consistent cash
  generation over the balance of the year."

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. DTC return to growth in H2 (Ryan Meyers, Lake Street)**
Meeker: "Same level of confidence" as entering the year. The plan (new subscriber
adds × retention × AOV) is tracking; Q1 retention (+170 bps) and AOV performance were
"really great." Inflection expected on the original timeline; strengthening Commerce
lets DTC "stick to the plan" without unnatural actions.

**2. Commerce decline and H2 confidence (Meyers)**
Meeker: Q1 is seasonally the slowest, lumpy, and this year some orders slipped into
Q4 FY2026 (out of Q1). Long lead times give "great visibility" to the big order lumps
building through Q2–Q4. Consistent Nielsen toy-category share gains and new partner
placements (Licksters at Walmart/Target/Chewy/Amazon; Girl Scout Cookies this winter)
underpin confidence.
