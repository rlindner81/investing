Not materially disclosed in available sources. The quarter marked the second consecutive period of year-over-year revenue growth following eight consecutive quarters of decline. The new Shopify platform migration was progressing ahead of plan.

## Conference Call Transcript Highlights

**Participants:** Mike Mougias (VP, Investor Relations), Matt Meeker (Co-Founder, CEO & Executive Chairman), Zahir Ibrahim (CFO)

**Analysts:** Maria Ripps (Canaccord Genuity), Ryan Meyers (Lake Street Capital Markets), Kaumil Gajrawala (Jefferies), Ygal Arounian (Citi Group)

---

### CEO Matt Meeker — Key Statements

- On Q3 performance: Revenue surpassed the high end of expectations; the quarter delivered a $4.9M year-over-year improvement in Adjusted EBITDA — the 10th consecutive such improvement. Management characterized this as a critical inflection point.

- On Shopify migration: In late October, BARK moved all paid media traffic to its new Shopify platform. New subscriptions grew 11% year-over-year at lower acquisition cost. 43% of checkouts occurred via ShopPay — a feature unavailable on the legacy platform. Migration of remaining active subscriber cohorts to Shopify planned for Q4.

- On Commerce: Segment delivered $20.3M (+43% YoY). Amazon revenue expected to grow over 70% for the full year. Launched 30 Chewy SKUs in June, expanded to 150+ SKUs by Q3. International expansion underway: Amazon Europe launched, Pets at Home (UK), FreshNap distribution in Europe.

- On BARK Air: $2.0M in revenue with positive gross margin only seven months after launch. Asset-light model (leased planes, no capex planned). Next step: experimenting with lower price points (~$1,000/ticket vs. current $6,000–$8,000) to expand addressable demand.

- On FY2026 outlook: Anticipated top-line returning to growth with mid to high single-digit gains, built on a more profitable foundation.

- On share repurchases: ~$17M invested to repurchase over 11 million shares; will continue to repurchase shares as long as the company believes it is significantly undervalued.

---

### CFO Zahir Ibrahim — Key Statements

- On gross margin: Consolidated gross margin of 62.7% (+90 bps YoY); grown over 400 bps over two years. As Commerce grows as a share of revenue, it will create natural headwind to consolidated gross margin, but contribution margins across both segments are comparable.

- On operating expenses: Shipping and fulfillment was $36.7M (+3.4% YoY), but included $2.4M of one-time warehouse restructuring costs. Excluding that, shipping was down ~$1M YoY. Other G&A of $27.5M was down $3.2M YoY from headcount reductions.

- On marketing: $27.4M (+$2.3M YoY), reflecting strategic investments to acquire new customers at attractive unit economics. Described as a flexible line that can be dialed up or down based on returns.

- On Adjusted EBITDA: -$1.6M for Q3 represented a $4.9M improvement over the prior year.

- On free cash flow: -$2M in Q3, -$1.2M YTD. Expected to be near break-even for the full year.

- On balance sheet: $115M in cash, consistent with prior quarter; $42.5M convertible note classified as current (due to come due). Share repurchase program has ~$6M remaining under existing authorization.

- On tariffs (early commentary): Approximately one-third of volumes come from domestically sourced consumables (unaffected). For the remaining two-thirds (China-sourced), proactive supplier work is underway to absorb the 10% tariff in place at the time. No material gross margin impact anticipated.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. D2C trajectory and Shopify contribution**
Analysts asked how to parse the D2C recovery between market improvement and company-specific execution. Matt Meeker attributed nearly all the improvement to internal execution — platform changes, creative testing, channel mix shifts, and investment in mid/upper-funnel marketing. The Shopify migration drove the 11% subscriber growth, with further upside as the company continues to iterate.

**2. BARK Air strategic vision**
Analysts asked whether BARK Air could become a material financial contributor and whether it would require significant capital. Management indicated it is too early to call it material, but noted extremely positive unit economics (full planes, positive gross margin). The asset-light model (leasing planes/infrastructure) means no significant capex planned. Near-term experimentation: new routes and lower price points to broaden demand.

**3. Q4 revenue guidance range width**
Analysts asked why the Q4 guidance range was wide ($121.2M–$131.2M) with less than two months left in the quarter. Management explained that Commerce shelf resets happen in the March–April timeframe; shipment timing by retail partners can meaningfully shift revenue by a week or more. Given Commerce's growing contribution, this timing variability amplifies the uncertainty.

**4. Chewy and retail channel momentum**
Analysts asked about Chewy's revenue contribution and trajectory. Zahir confirmed week-on-week growth with Chewy, strong sentiment from buyer conversations, and BARK already ranking as one of Chewy's biggest pet toy suppliers. Amazon (+70% YoY) and other major retail customers also performing strongly.

**5. Marketing strategy — upper funnel shift**
Analysts asked whether the move further up the funnel (away from promotional/direct response) would create a longer payback period and whether Shopify conversion gains offset that. Management confirmed Shopify efficiency gains are being reinvested into higher-funnel brand-building rather than pocketed as cost savings. The framing: deliberately accepting a longer payback on brand investment in exchange for higher-quality, longer-retaining subscribers.

**6. FY2026 EBITDA outlook**
Analysts asked for EBITDA direction for FY2026. Zahir indicated a good year-over-year EBITDA improvement is expected, with formal guidance to be provided on the Q4 call in June. Longer term, the expectation is steady EBITDA margin improvement driven by scale and operating leverage.

**7. International Commerce opportunity**
Analysts asked about international potential and which channels will drive FY2026 Commerce growth faster than FY2025. Key drivers identified: continued Amazon expansion (domestic and Europe), new Chewy SKUs and product categories, TJ Maxx and Costco growth, Girl Scouts national program launching in FY2027, and new UK bricks-and-mortar distribution (Pets at Home, seasonal programs with major UK retailers).
