## Stock Reaction

Shares **dipped** following the announcement. The stock had rebounded ~18.8% in the prior month but remained down approximately **30% year-to-date** heading into earnings. The revenue miss (approximately $11M below consensus) offset the EBITDA beat.

## Conference Call Transcript Highlights

**Participants:** Matt Meeker (Co-Founder, CEO & Executive Chairman), Zahir Ibrahim (CFO)

---

### CEO Matt Meeker — Key Statements

- On Commerce: Highlighted 27% growth in Commerce as evidence that the BARK brand resonates beyond subscriptions.

- On BARK Air: Called it a meaningful proof-of-concept for the premium dog-lifestyle services category — nearly $6M in revenue in its first year with no established playbook to follow.

- On subscriber strategy: Signaled an intentional shift toward fewer, higher-quality subscribers rather than maximizing subscriber count — accepting near-term DTC revenue pressure in exchange for better LTV and profitability metrics.

- On tariffs: Acknowledged the $4M incremental headwind in H1 FY2026 from China-sourced toy imports; noted ongoing supply chain optimization and the strategic importance of growing the domestically sourced consumables business.

---

### CFO Zahir Ibrahim — Key Statements

- On gross margin: Q4's 63.6% gross margin was the company's best ever — driven by improved product mix (more consumables), disciplined promotional activity, and manufacturing efficiencies.

- On operating expenses: Sustained reductions in both marketing (-$1.5M) and G&A (-$1.6M) relative to Q4 FY2024 reflect structural cost discipline, not one-time savings.

- On capital allocation: The company repurchased 6M shares for $10.5M in Q4. With $94M in cash and no near-term capex requirements, continued buybacks are on the table.

- On the Q1 FY2026 guidance range of $99M–$101M: The implied ~14% decline reflects the combination of continued DTC subscriber attrition, reduced marketing spend, and the $4M tariff headwind absorbed in H1.

---

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Revenue miss magnitude**
Analysts pressed on the ~$11M revenue shortfall versus consensus. Management attributed the gap primarily to a deliberate reduction in subscriber acquisition marketing — accepting lower new subscriber volume in exchange for better unit economics on those acquired.

**2. BARK Air path to scale**
Analysts asked about BARK Air's profitability and expansion plans. Management indicated it is not yet a major financial contributor but serves the strategic purpose of establishing BARK as a comprehensive dog-lifestyle brand beyond monthly boxes.

**3. Tariff exposure and supply chain**
Analysts asked about the specifics of the $4M tariff headwind. Management explained that toys & accessories (primarily China-sourced) are the exposed category; consumables (largely domestic) are resilient. Long-term, the shift toward consumables reduces structural tariff risk.

**4. Commerce growth sustainability**
Analysts asked whether 27% Commerce growth would moderate. Management said the momentum is real — driven by new partnerships and expanded shelf space — but did not commit to sustaining 27% growth long-term.

**5. Subscriber count trends**
Management declined to provide a specific subscriber count but confirmed the subscriber base is smaller than prior periods. The frame offered: focus on quality of subscribers (LTV, churn rates) rather than raw count.

**6. Platform modernization**
Management highlighted the Ordergroove + Shopify migration as critical infrastructure work that will reduce cost per transaction and enable more personalized subscriber experiences in FY2026.
