## Stock Reaction

Not materially disclosed in available sources. Revenue of $107.0M came in above the high end of the prior-quarter guidance range of $102M–$105M, driven by stronger-than-expected D2C performance and a modest timing benefit in Commerce. The Adjusted EBITDA result of -$1.4M was within the guidance range. The major headline was the repayment of the $45M convertible note in cash after quarter end, making BARK debt-free for the first time as a public company. Gross margin compression (-250 bps) and the net loss deterioration to $(10.7)M from $(5.3)M were headwinds.

## Conference Call Transcript Highlights

**Participants:** Mike Mougias (VP, Investor Relations), Matt Meeker (Co-Founder, CEO & Executive Chairman), Zahir Ibrahim (CFO)

**Analysts:** Ryan Meyers (Lake Street Capital Markets), Maria Ripps (Canaccord Genuity), Kaumil Gajrawala (Jefferies)

---

### CEO Matt Meeker — Key Statements

- On debt repayment: BARK paid off its $45M convertible note using cash from the balance sheet the week before the call. Management emphasized the decision to pay it off in cash rather than refinance or issue equity as a statement of long-term confidence. BARK extended its $35M Western Alliance Bank credit facility on competitive terms.

- On revenue beat: Total revenue of $107M came in above the high end of guidance. Adjusted EBITDA of -$1.4M was within range; management noted they chose to deploy an incremental ~$1M in marketing during the quarter at highly efficient acquisition costs, which pulled EBITDA slightly lower than it otherwise would have been.

- On Commerce: $24.8M in Commerce revenue (+6% YoY), now representing 24% of total revenue (all-time high mix). Walmart footprint expanded during the quarter; strong traction at Chewy, Amazon, and Costco. BARK in the Belly kibble launched on Chewy and Amazon in August.

- On BARK Air: $3.6M revenue — up 138% YoY and up 54% from Q1. Highest gross margin quarter to date, driven by 93% seat fill rate. 99% five-star review rate maintained.

- On subscriber quality: Lowest CAC since FY2023. Two-thirds of new subscribers chose premium plans (Super Chewer or Combo Box) for the second consecutive quarter. Six consecutive months of improving subscriber retention. Last-mile delivery transitioned to Amazon (packages arrive on Amazon's blue trucks), reducing delivery time by ~one day.

- On Girl Scouts: Received the green light to participate in the annual Girl Scouts cookie program; will begin shipping products next summer. Described as a huge awareness and revenue opportunity alongside one of America's most iconic brands.

---

### CFO Zahir Ibrahim — Key Statements

- On gross margin: 57.9% consolidated gross margin, down 250 bps YoY. Two primary drivers: (1) revenue mix — Commerce and BARK Air represented 26.5% of total revenue vs. 20% prior year; (2) elevated tariff-related costs (~$7M in H1, expected $12M–$13M for the full year). Mitigation measures in H2 include sourcing from geographies outside China and implementing a Commerce price increase.

- On marketing: $15.4M, down 18% YoY — even with the incremental $1M deployment, total H2 marketing is expected to decline at a greater pace YoY.

- On cash: $63M at quarter end, down $22M sequentially, primarily due to working capital timing — higher Commerce receivables and inventory build ahead of the holiday season. The subsequent $45M debt repayment (not on this balance sheet) means Q3 opening cash is approximately $18M, with the $35M credit facility available.

- On balance sheet: No long-term debt following convertible note repayment. $35M revolving credit facility with Western Alliance Bank extended on competitive terms.

- On FY2026 EBITDA: Goal remains full-year Adjusted EBITDA break-even or better; management expects to be in that zip code. Significant external uncertainty (tariffs, macro, consumer sentiment) acknowledged.

### Analyst Q&A — Key Themes

**1. Implications of being debt-free**
Analysts asked how the debt repayment changes capital allocation flexibility. Management emphasized continuing to execute the existing plan — no immediate large swings or acquisitions given ongoing tariff uncertainty. Board meeting scheduled to discuss long-range plan and capital priorities. Share repurchases remain on the table.

**2. Full-year EBITDA confidence**
Analysts asked about comfort level with achieving full-year EBITDA break-even given the -$1.4M Q2 result and the wide Q3 guidance range (down to -$5M). Management confirmed break-even remains the goal but acknowledged volatility in the tariff environment and broader consumer sentiment.

**3. Commerce growth drivers**
Analysts asked what drove Commerce growth and whether it was durable. Zahir cited expanded toy distribution at Walmart and continued momentum at Amazon and Chewy (driven by higher SKU review counts and growing digital presence). Noted a modest timing benefit in Q2 from orders that shifted from Q3 into Q2.

**4. Subscriber acquisition efficiency and retention**
Maria Ripps asked what was driving the CAC improvement and whether the retention gains in newer cohorts would persist. Matt Meeker attributed CAC improvement to a more favorable channel mix (organic and brand-driven vs. paid Meta/Google), which is more sustainable and growing. On retention: six months of improvement across the subscriber base, driven by Shopify platform improvements, returning value to customers, and the favorable mix of organic vs. promotional new subscriber acquisition.

**5. Long-term capital allocation and buybacks**
Kaumil Gajrawala asked about investment priorities now that the balance sheet is clean. Management indicated priorities are: (1) continue executing the plan for EBITDA break-even, (2) continue diversification investments in Commerce, BARK Air, and new services/products, (3) assess long-range plan with the board. Buybacks remain on the table but were not committed to specifically during the call.
