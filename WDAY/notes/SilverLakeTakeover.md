# Silver Lake Takeover Approach (2026-08-13)

**Reuters exclusive, 2026-08-13** — Silver Lake in talks to take Workday private,
reportedly prepared to pay **up to \$43B**. Talks "ongoing," no deal guaranteed;
neither side commented. WDAY closed **\$206.45 (+18%, best day since 2016)**, halted
repeatedly, intraday high \$227.49 — market cap **~\$51.1B** vs ~\$43B unaffected.

## Verdict — ~25–35% chance of a signed deal

**The reported price is already below the market.** "Up to \$43B" reads as a
financing-constrained ceiling, not an opening bid. The stock trades ~19% above it and
~25% above the \$169 analyst consensus (range \$92–275). Any real bid must clear a bar
the leak itself raised.

## Why it's credible

- **Cheap on the numbers.** At the 2026-05-21 reference: **3.3x P/S, 10.2x P/FCF
  company**, on subscription revenue **+14.3% y/y** and a **32.6% FCF margin**. Classic
  "de-rated by narrative, not fundamentals" — WDAY fell ~51% from its \$311 peak on
  AI-disruption fear.
- **Relationship-sourced.** Bhusri and Silver Lake's Egon Durban know each other well.
- **Window open.** EA's \$55B LBO *closed* 2026-08-04, ten days before the leak;
  Thoma Bravo/Dayforce ~\$16B earlier in the year.

## Why it probably doesn't happen

**1. The equity gap is the deal.** WDAY supports less leverage than EA — company FCF is
\$2.97B TTM but **FCF ex-SBC is only \$1.40B** (15.3% margin); a take-private must
replace ~\$1.6B/yr of SBC with cash comp. Say ~\$20B debt (EA's JPMorgan commitment as
ceiling) against ~\$50B+ equity value → **~\$30–35B of equity needed**. SLP VII is
\$20.5B *total*; no GP puts >15–20% in one name → Silver Lake itself is good for
**\$3–4B**. **~\$27–31B has to come from outside.**

**2. Silver Lake doesn't carry deals this size — sovereigns do, and none is willing.**
In EA it ended up with **5.5%**; PIF holds 93.4%. So the real question is "who writes the
anchor check," and as of 2026-08-14 all four candidates are constrained:

| Fund | Check | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Silver Lake | \$3–4B | SLP VII is \$20.5B *total*; no GP puts >15–20% in one name |
| ADIA | \$3–6B | Only ever a **minority alongside a lead sponsor** — Qlik (~\$1B at \$10B val), Sapiens (Jun 2026). Won't anchor |
| CPPIB | \$2–4B | Qualtrics partner, but FY2026 **7.8% vs 13.2% benchmark**; sold 33 LP stakes for ~C\$4.0B; 2026 tech tickets run \$36–150M |
| GIC | ~0 | **"Very valuation sensitive"** (Wilmes); CEO warning on "valuation overshoot"; *currently selling* ~\$1B of PE stakes via PJT |
| **PIF — residual** | **\$18–25B alone** | See below |

**PIF is the whole question.** Its April 2026 board-approved 2026–2030 strategy cuts
foreign allocation to **~20%** (from 30%) and reframes the fund from "rapid growth" to
"sustained value creation." The **Aramco dividend is the binding constraint** — \$81.2B
regular for 2026 with the **performance-linked top-up discontinued**, against a fiscal
breakeven >\$90/bbl. It approved a **minimum 20% spending cut across 100+ portfolio
companies**, and **closed EA on 2026-08-04** (\$33B+ equity, ten days before this leak).
A second ~\$30B foreign software check in the same fiscal year is a very large ask.
⚠️ *One open door*: AI/digital infrastructure sits in the top tier of national priorities
(HUMAIN) — Workday framed as an AI/enterprise-data platform could squeeze through, but
needs MBS-level sponsorship the way Kushner engineered EA.

Sector backdrop reinforces it: PE platform buyouts in software hit a **decade low** in
2026 on tighter credit, AI revenue-model risk and the valuation reset.

**3. Founder control cuts both ways.** Bhusri + Duffield control **~68% of voting power**
via Class B (10 votes/share; structure sunsets Oct 2032). No deal without them — but
equally, no activist or arb pressure forcing one. Bhusri returned as CEO 2026-02-09 with
a **\$138.8M package, \$75M of it tied to five-year stock-price targets** — he may prefer
to disprove the AI thesis publicly. A leak that surfaces value without a transaction is
a live possibility.

## Silver Lake's software record — real, but financial not operational

| Deal | Size | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Dell (2013) | ~\$25B | Signature win; re-levered, bought EMC, re-IPO'd |
| VMware | via EMC/Dell | Sold to Broadcom; anchored ~\$20B distributions since 2023 |
| Qualtrics (2023, w/ CPPIB) | \$12.5B | \$18.15/sh, 62% premium; SAP carve-out |
| Software AG (2023) | ~\$2.6B | German tender offer |
| Electronic Arts (2025–26) | \$55B | Largest LBO ever; **SL only 5.5%** |

"Buffing up" here means **leverage plus cost discipline**, not product renaissance. Dell
is the one genuine operational success. EA's post-close playbook — **~\$700M/yr of cuts
against ~\$18B debt and ~\$1.8B annual interest** — is the likelier Workday template.
Relevant if holding for growth.

## Watch items

- **A named sovereign anchor.** PIF surfacing → odds back above 50%. Reporting that
  describes a *consortium* of five-plus funds → read as difficulty, not momentum.
- **Absent a named anchor within weeks**, base case is talks fade and WDAY retraces
  toward ~\$169 consensus.
- Payoff is roughly symmetric on sub-50% odds: ~15–20% downside on collapse vs ~\$225–235
  on a 30% premium to unaffected. Unattractive arb.
