Workday remains a mid-teens grower decelerating on a predictable glide path. Q2 FY2027 subscription revenue of \$2,471M was +13.9% y/y, the fourth straight quarter in a narrow +13.4% to +14.5% band, down from +16.9% for FY2025. The guide points lower still: Q3 at \$2,515M is +12%, and Rowe's pre-guide for FY2028 is \~11% — consistent with the \~11% second-half FY2027 run rate. Growth is not breaking, it is grinding down about 150bps a year, and the total subscription backlog line is the leading tell: +8.0% y/y against subscription revenue +13.9%. That gap leads the revenue line by roughly a year and it is not yet closing.

Guidance execution is immaculate but unexciting. Every next-quarter guide since q1-2025 has been beaten by +0.2% to +0.8% — a sandbagged, metronomic cadence that tells you almost nothing about upside. The FY guide has ratcheted \$9,925–9,950M to \$9,940–9,950M, a token trim of the low end, not a raise.

FCF is where the story is genuinely good and genuinely complicated. Company FCF is \$2,844M TTM at a 30.2% margin, with FY2026 at \$2,777M (+26.7%), and management holds the FY2027 guide at \$3.18B (+15%) on \$3.45B operating cash flow. But SBC runs \$1,646M TTM — 58% of company FCF — so strict ex-SBC FCF is only \$1,198M, a 12.7% margin. Q2 itself printed −\$2M ex-SBC, flattered downward by an extra payroll run. That gap is the single most important number for anyone underwriting a take-private: it is the cash comp a buyer must fund.

At \$186.05 the stock is 4.8x TTM sales, 15.8x company FCF, 4.5x forward FY guidance sales — and 37.5x strict ex-SBC FCF. Against 8.6–10.0x P/S through FY2025 that is a violent de-rating, but the old multiple was priced on 17–19% growth that no longer exists. Balance sheet is modest: \$3.40B cash against \$2.99B debt, roughly net-flat, with cash down from \$8.19B a year ago as \$5B of buyback completed six months early. Share count has fallen 266.9M to 241.2M in eight quarters, −9.6%. Cheap on cash earnings, fair on cash earnings that actually belong to shareholders.

**Rating:** +1.0 — BUY

## 3. News

**The surface narrative** is still the Silver Lake take-private. Reuters reported on 2026-08-13 that Silver Lake was in talks to acquire Workday at up to \$43B; the stock rose \~18% to \$206.45, its best day since 2016, halted repeatedly. Four weeks later there is still no 8-K, no merger agreement, no named financing partner and no denial. That silence is the single most important fact in this review.

**The depth pass says the deal story is now mostly attention.** The \$43B figure was Workday's *unaffected market cap*, not a bid — the reporting never described terms, only that talks had occurred "in recent months." Nothing concrete has been added since: no consortium member named, no bank commitment reported, no exclusivity. Meanwhile the stock at \$186.05 (\$44.9B) trades *above* the reported ceiling, so a deal at the leaked number is now a loss. Against that, [SilverLakeTakeover.md](../notes/SilverLakeTakeover.md) laid out the binding constraint and it has not moved: the equity check is \~\$30–35B, Silver Lake itself is good for \$3–4B, and no sovereign anchor has surfaced. PIF closed the \$55B EA LBO on 2026-08-04, ten days before this leak, under a 2026–2030 strategy that cuts foreign allocation to \~20%. The note's own test — "absent a named anchor within weeks, base case is talks fade" — has now run its clock. The four-session round-trip from \$208 to \$186 on falling volume is the market quietly marking that down.

**The substantiated items are the company's own.** Q2 FY2027 (2026-08-27) is hard data: subscription revenue \$2,471M +13.9%, cRPO \$9.034B +14.2%, non-GAAP operating margin 31.1%, gross retention 97%, FY2027 margin guide raised 30.5% to 31.0%. The \$5B buyback completed six months early and the board approved a **new \$4B open-ended program** — Cramer framed this as fighting off the takeover, which is interpretation, but the authorization itself is a disclosed, committed number and reads as a board that either believes the stock is cheap or is building an alternative to selling. AI disclosure is partially substantiated: >\$100M of new AI ACV, >25% of all new ACV, 5,500 customers on organic agents (+35% q/q), over half of net-new wins taking an AI SKU. The **unsubstantiated half is monetization** — only \~200 customers are on Flex Credits, the consumption meter, and when Zukin and Borges asked directly how many credits an agent consumes, Kazmaier answered "right now is difficult to answer." That is the gap between adoption and dollars, and it is explicitly *not* in the FY2028 pre-guide.

**Catalysts:** Financial Analyst Day **Oct 13, 2026**, 2pm PT, inside Rising (Las Vegas, Oct 12–15) — confirmed by press release 2026-09-01. Rowe pre-guided FY2028 at \~11% subscription growth with at least +200bps margin, framed as "early context." Q3 FY2027 prints late November, the first quarter with real Flex Credits conversion data. **Sentiment:** short interest 11.8%, 4.0 dtc, down 26% — deal shorts covering or arbs leaving. Goldman raised its target to \$164 on Aug 31, *below* the market. Consensus \~\$189.86 sits essentially at spot. **Risks:** deal fade without a floor bid; AI substituting rather than adding to seat-based pricing (the Materne question, unanswered); backlog +8.0% vs revenue +13.9% signalling FY2028 deceleration below 11%; Oracle and SAP pressure; founder control (\~68% voting) meaning no activist can force an outcome.

**Rating:** +0.2 — HOLD

## Verdict

Workday is a well-run compounder whose share price is currently a referendum on a deal that shows no sign of happening. The business is fine — 13–14% subscription growth, 31% margins rising to 33%, \$3.18B of FCF guided, and a share count down 9.6% in eight quarters — and at 4.8x sales and 15.8x company FCF it is cheap against its own history. But two things sit against paying up here: growth is pre-guided down to \~11% for FY2028 with total backlog already growing only 8.0%, and strict ex-SBC FCF is just \$1,198M, so the headline 15.8x becomes 37.5x once \$1.6B of annual stock comp is treated as the real cost it is. The main risk is that the Silver Lake premium bleeds out — four weeks with no named sovereign anchor, no terms and no 8-K, while the stock already trades above the leaked \$43B ceiling — leaving a stock that has to re-rate on \~11% growth alone. What would change the rating: a named anchor investor or actual bid terms (upgrade toward the deal case), or, more durably, Rowe putting a dollar figure on Flex Credits monetization at the Oct 13 analyst day and guiding FY2028 above 11% — the pre-planned scenario A in [AnalystDay2026.md](../notes/AnalystDay2026.md). Absent either, the existing tranche plan stands and this is a hold, not an add.

**Rating:** +0.3 — HOLD
