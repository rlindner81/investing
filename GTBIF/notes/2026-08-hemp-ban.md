# Intoxicating Hemp Ban — Federal & State

**Date:** August 2026

---

## The Law

**Section 781 of P.L. 119-37** (the Nov 2025 continuing appropriations act that
ended the shutdown) rewrote the federal definition of hemp with a one-year fuse:

- Hemp measured by **total THC** (incl. THCA), not delta-9 alone
- Finished products capped at **0.4 mg total THC per container**
- Cannabinoids made by **chemical synthesis or conversion** (delta-8, THC-O, HHC)
  excluded from the definition entirely

Estimated to render ~95% of the hemp-derived cannabinoid market federally
unlawful. The affected industry is commonly quoted at \$30B retail, though that
figure includes non-intoxicating CBD/wellness; intoxicating hemp specifically is
~\$18–28B.

## Decision Points

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2026-02-10 | FDA statutory deadline to publish permitted-cannabinoid lists — **missed**; compliance definitions still unpublished |
| 2026-04-30 | House passed 2026 Farm Bill 224–200 with the ban **untouched** |
| 2026-08-03 | Senate advanced stopgap 89–4 with a **partial delay** to Dec 11 |
| **2026-09** | **House vote on the Senate CR — the live variable** |
| **2026-11-12** | Ban effective. Synthetics banned this date **regardless** of any delay |
| 2026-12-11 | Natural-hemp total-THC piece + 0.4 mg cap, *if* the House delay passes |

The delay is partial: only the new definition and the 0.4 mg cap slide. The White
House reportedly told Senate Republicans there would be **no further extensions**.

## State Bans Already in Force

- **Ohio** — SB 56, effective 2026-03-20. Same 0.4 mg threshold as federal; the
  cleanest preview available. DeWine vetoed the 5 mg beverage carve-out.
- **Texas** — DSHS total-THC rule effective 2026-03-31; delta-8 enforcement from
  2026-07-31 with felony exposure. No adult-use channel to catch spill.
- **Tennessee** — HB 1376, effective 2026-01-01, but phased: "Legacy Licensees"
  run under the old framework until licenses expire. No adult-use channel.
- **Virginia** — HB 30, 2 mg cap effective 2026-08-15; see
  [2026-08-virginia-hemp.md](2026-08-virginia-hemp.md).
- **Categorical bans:** ID, HI, AR, KS, RI, SD. **Dispensary-channel only:** CT,
  NY, CO, OR, MN, NJ, CA.

## Estimated GTBIF Impact

**~\$84M incremental revenue, +7% on the \$1,209M TTM base**, phased over 2–4
years. At 40–50% contribution margin on existing cultivation and stores: \$34–42M
EBITDA, or **+17–21% to FCF** against \$198M TTM.

```
Intoxicating hemp base           \$22B
× regulated capture      ~18%  → \$4.0B    (Ohio-implied, see below)
× GTI-market geography    40%  → \$1.6B
× GTI share in markets    ~7%  → \$112M
× wholesale/retail mix   0.75  → \$84M
```

**Mix factor:** GTI revenue is ~50/50 retail/wholesale. Wholesale is recognized at
~50% of the retail ticket, so \$1 of new retail demand converts to ~\$0.75 of GTI
revenue.

**Geography is the binding constraint.** Hemp's largest markets — TX, TN, FL, the
Southeast — are precisely where GTI has no adult-use channel. PA is the standout
overlap: large GTI wholesale market, no adult-use yet.

## Model Weaknesses

**The capture rate is the whole model and it rests on one state, three months.**
Ohio post-ban sales ran +32% / +22.5% / +22% Y/Y (Apr/May/Jun 2026). Attributing
the excess over an assumed 20% baseline ramp implies **13–22% capture** — below
the 30% first assumed. But Ohio launched adult-use Aug 2024 and a market at that
stage grows 15–25% on ramp alone, so there is **no counterfactual**. If the true
baseline was 25%, capture goes to ~0; if 15%, capture is ~30%. Published data
cannot distinguish these. BDSA, who hold the transaction data, **declined to
publish a capture estimate** — a meaningful abstention.

**Sales declined sequentially** (\$112.5M → \$110M → \$107M), consistent with a
pantry-load plus partial retention rather than durable share transfer.

**Tennessee is not a usable comp** — phased via legacy licensees, and with no
adult-use channel it measures demand destruction, not capture.

**The largest effect is unmodelled.** Removing a cheap, untaxed substitute should
relieve price compression across GTI's *existing* \$1.2B base. That may exceed the
incremental volume entirely, and there is no empirical support for it whatsoever.

**Displacement is not capture.** Best guess at where the volume goes: 30–40%
illicit/gray, 20–25% demand destruction, 15–20% non-legal states, 25–35%
regulated — and that last figure is the contested one.

**Not GTI-specific.** Every MSO gets this simultaneously; it is a sector re-rating
input, not a competitive edge.
