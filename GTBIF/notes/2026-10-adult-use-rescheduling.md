# Adult-Use Rescheduling — Schedule III Timeline

**Date:** October 2026 (status as of 2026-10-19)

---

## The Story

Rescheduling has split into two tracks. **Medical is done:** the DOJ's April 2026
order put FDA-approved products and state-licensed medical marijuana in Schedule
III, and it is already showing up in GTI's P&L as partial 280E relief (cited in the
Q2-2026 net income bridge). **Adult-use is not:** everything else stays in
Schedule I pending a DEA formal hearing, and that proceeding has been **stayed
since 2026-09-29** while the ALJ decides whether to reopen the record for a GAO
report. The motion fight makes a 2026 effective date effectively dead and pushes
the realistic window into 2027 — the "next year, not this year" repricing that hit
the MSO group in early October.

The GAO report itself is not adverse on the merits. It faults DEA and HHS for
lacking written scheduling procedures, notes DEA agreed with HHS in **all 84** cases
reaching a final decision, and makes **no finding on cannabis**. Its value to
opponents is procedural: a hook to delay.

**The 55-day floor.** After the ALJ serves the recommendation, the record can't be
certified for **25 days** (21 CFR § 1316.65(c), which includes the 20-day exceptions
window), and a final order typically takes effect **30 days** after publication
(§ 1316.67 / 5 U.S.C. § 553(d)). 25 + 30 = 55 days, so Dec 31 minus 55 → \~Nov 6.
If the final rule is designated **major** under the Congressional Review Act, a
60-day effective-date delay is added on top.

## Scenarios

- **Stay lifted, no reopening** (DEA's position): report admitted on the papers,
  recommendation issued within weeks. Final order plausibly **Q1–Q2 2027**.
- **Supplemental briefing only:** adds a briefing cycle; recommendation after the
  midterms, final order **mid-2027**.
- **Hearing reopened for discovery/testimony:** reverses the DOJ's hard 07-15 stop;
  months more. **Late 2027** is the planning baseline cited by industry counsel, with
  2028+ possible if appeals follow.

Note the ALJ recommendation is **non-binding** — the final call rests with DEA
Administrator Terry Cole, and DEA has consistently backed Schedule III. Delay risk
is procedural, not a reversal of the agency's position.

## Why It Matters for GTBIF

- **280E on the adult-use book.** Medical sales already get relief; adult-use
  revenue stays under 280E until the broad rule takes effect. Each quarter of slippage is a quarter of full 280E on
  that base.
- **Uplisting.** Kovler calls adult-use rescheduling "the next big tell" for an
  NYSE/Nasdaq listing. On the Q2 call he said GTI would list **directly, in full**
  — rejecting the RYM-acquires-GTI route as tax-complicated — with RYM absorbed
  as a consolidated subsidiary (see [2026-07-RYM-note.md](2026-07-RYM-note.md)).
  Control of RYM is already done; only the GTI listing waits on this rule.
- **Institutional capital.** Q1 call: the biggest benefit is "a lot of new
  institutional investors." Management frames current multiples as a function of
  having essentially one large institutional buyer in the space.
- **Stock.** \~\$6.65 on 10-07 → \~\$6.40 on 10-18 as the stay dragged on. GTI was
  buying back at \~\$6 in Q2, so the dip sits at management's own buyback price.
