GTBIF grows slowly but converts well. Q2-2026 revenue of \$306.7M rose **+4.6% Y/Y**, continuing a low-single-digit pattern (+1.4%, +4.7%, +1.6%, +5.7%, +7.4%, +4.6% across the last six quarters). FY-2025 grew +3.4% on FY-2024's +7.8% — this is a mature, price-compressed market where growth comes from new state launches (Minnesota, Ohio, Connecticut, Florida) rather than same-store gains; comparable store sales actually fell 1.1%.

Cash generation is the real story and it is strong, if lumpy. TTM FCF is **\$197.8M on \$1,209M revenue — a +16.4% margin**, and FY-2025 FCF of \$213.9M was +86.0% over FY-2024's \$115.0M. That is a company throwing off serious cash relative to its size. The caveat is Q2-2026 itself: FCF of just **\$8.8M (-76.5% Y/Y)** on operating cash flow of \$29.0M against \$20.2M of capex. One weak quarter inside a good TTM, but worth watching — strict ex-SBC FCF for the quarter was **-\$1.8M**, the first negative print in two years. SBC is modest and well-behaved at \$43.8M TTM (~3.6% of revenue), so the company/strict gap is narrow — unusual and creditable in this sector.

Valuation is genuinely cheap on cash flow. TTM P/S **1.2x**, P/FCF **7.6x**, and even strict ex-SBC P/FCF is only **9.8x**. The multiple has compressed steadily from 45.3x P/FCF in q1-2024 to 7.6x today, driven as much by improving FCF as by the falling price. Net cash is roughly breakeven (\$284M cash vs \$283M debt).

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and verifiable: share count has fallen **236.1M → 218.6M (-7.4%)** over two years, with \$48.3M repurchased in Q2 alone at \$6.11 — below the current \$6.89. Guidance is qualitative only (the CFO's sequential hint), so there is no beat/miss track record to score; the Q3 hint is "flat sequentially."

Verdict: cheap, cash-generative, buying back stock — but growth is stalling and Q2 cash conversion was weak.

**Rating:** +0.8 — BUY

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** GTBIF reported Q2-2026 on Aug 4 (after close) into a sector that is fixated on one thing: federal rescheduling. Company headlines were mixed — revenue +4.6%, GAAP net income positive at \$4.9M, aggressive buybacks — but the stock fell while MSOS rallied.

**Depth pass — the tax tailwind is real and already banked.** The most substantiated development is regulatory and it has already hit the financials. The **DOJ's April 2026 final order reclassifying state-legal medical cannabis to Schedule III** is a completed action published in the Federal Register, not a proposal. Its effect is visible in GTBIF's own numbers: net income of \$4.9M versus a -\$0.6M loss a year earlier, driven by lower tax expense as **Section 280E ceased applying to portions of the business**. That is about as hard as evidence gets.

**What is still pending — and this is where sentiment runs ahead.** Adult-use cannabis remains Schedule I, and 280E still applies to the adult-use business, which is the bulk of GTBIF's revenue. The DEA's expedited hearing concluded July 15; **post-hearing briefs are due Aug 17**, after which the ALJ recommends and the DEA Administrator decides on an unspecified timeline. The hearing structure drew criticism for admitting mainly opposing testimony, and the April order is expected to face legal challenge. So the large remaining prize — full 280E relief and an eventual major-exchange uplisting — is genuinely unresolved. MSOS's +103.7% one-year NAV return through May reflects positioning ahead of a decision that has not been made; that is the detached-from-concrete part of the sector story.

**Why the stock fell anyway.** The quarter had real blemishes underneath the headline. Gross margin dropped to **45.0% from 49.9%**, hit by price compression and — self-inflicted — **\$17.5M of RYTHM brand licensing fees**, the first full quarter under the new fixed-fee structure, which the CFO said continues at that level in Q3. Comparable store sales fell **-1.1%**. Q3 was guided "flat sequentially." Reported EBITDA fell to \$53.1M from \$69.1M. The market marked down margin quality despite the tax benefit — a defensible reaction.

**Structure worth tracking.** Per this repo's [RYM note](../notes/2026-07-RYM-note.md), those licensing fees are not value leakage so much as value transfer into RYTHM Inc. (Nasdaq: RYM), where GTI holds ~33% plus \$72M of convertible notes positioning it for majority control and a reverse merger post-legalization. That framing is well-documented in filings, but it does mean reported gross margin understates consolidated economics.

**Catalysts / risks.** Aug 17 briefing deadline, then the ALJ recommendation; Virginia adult-use is **July 1, 2027** (not 2026) where GTBIF holds one of five vertical licenses; \$62.3M of buyback authorization remains through Sept 22, 2026. Risks: continued price compression, OTC illiquidity, an adverse or indefinitely delayed DEA outcome, and legal challenge to the April order.

**Rating:** +0.4 — HOLD

## Verdict

GTBIF is the cheapest cash flow in this group — 7.6x TTM P/FCF, 1.2x sales, a +16.4% FCF margin, and a share count down 7.4% in two years on buybacks executed below the current price. The April Schedule III order for medical cannabis is a banked, verifiable tax tailwind that turned GAAP net income positive. Against that, the operating business is barely growing (+4.6%, with comps at -1.1%), gross margin fell nearly 5pp on price compression plus \$17.5M of RYTHM licensing fees that recur in Q3, Q2 free cash flow collapsed to \$8.8M, and the stock underperformed its own sector by ~7pp in earnings week on a heavy-volume distribution day. The main risk is that the remaining upside is regulatory rather than operational: full 280E relief for adult-use is still undecided at the DEA and faces likely litigation, so the equity is partly a policy option. Confirmation of adult-use rescheduling — or a return to double-digit FCF conversion — would make this compelling; another quarter of flat revenue with compressing margins and no DEA progress would leave it a value trap.

**Rating:** +0.1 — HOLD
