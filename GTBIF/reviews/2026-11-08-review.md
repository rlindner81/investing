**Revenue is re-accelerating.** Q3-2026 revenue of \$324.4M grew +11.3% Y/Y, the fastest print in the display window. The trend runs +1.4% / +4.7% / +1.6% / +5.7% through 2025, then +7.4% / +4.6% / +11.3% this year. TTM revenue is now \$1.24B. Most of the Q3 jump came from Minnesota adult-use, and it beat management's own "flat sequentially" hint by \~6%. Comparable-store sales turned positive (+3.0%) for the first time since Q3-2024.

**FCF is solidly positive but softer than 2025.** TTM company FCF is \$179M (14.4% margin), against \$214M for FY-2025. Q2 (\$8.8M) and Q3 (\$38.0M) were both down Y/Y because inventory build (\$32M YTD) and higher capex ate into operating cash flow. SBC runs \~\$42M a year, so strict FCF ex-SBC is \$137M (11.0% margin). That gap is real but not punitive.

**Valuation is cheap.** At \$6.78 the market cap is \~\$1.48B: 1.2x TTM sales, 8.3x company FCF, 10.8x strict FCF. Both multiples sit at the bottom of the two-year range; Q1-2024 traded at 2.7x sales and 45x FCF. For a business growing double digits with positive FCF, this prices in either a permanent cannabis discount or a decline in earnings. GTBIF gives no dollar guidance; the CFO's qualitative hints have proved conservative, and Q3 beat its own guide clearly. The Q4 hint is "flat to down slightly," and Q4 will include RYTHM on a consolidated basis, so the numbers won't compare cleanly.

**Balance sheet.** Cash is \$316M against \$277M of debt (\$177M senior), so net cash is \~\$39M. Buybacks have cut the share count from 236M to 218.7M over two years, and \$81.6M of that was repurchased YTD in 2026. A new \$50M authorization is in place. The 280E tax relief on the medical business is already showing up in a lower tax line.

**Verdict:** the stock trades at a low single-digit multiple of FCF while revenue growth is accelerating, it holds net cash and it shrinks its share count every year. The main dents are falling Y/Y FCF and margin pressure from pricing.

**Rating:** +1.2 — BUY

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** The past two weeks brought three stories: GTI's Q3 print on Nov 4, the Nov 3 midterms, and the procedural fight over adult-use rescheduling. The headline coverage split. Some called it a "beat" (revenue \$324.4M vs. \~\$313M consensus); GuruFocus led with an "EPS miss" because the GAAP result was a loss of \$0.02.

**Q3 results — substantiated.** The 8-K and 10-Q back the beat: revenue +11.3% Y/Y, normalized EBITDA \$89.4M (27.6%, flat Y/Y), comps +3.0% (first positive since Q3-2024), CPG revenue +16%, and OCF of \$58.3M. The GAAP loss comes from \$15.8M of RYTHM brand-licensing fees in COGS and the lapping of a \$42M IP gain a year ago. Neither is an operating deterioration. The real negatives are also in the filing: gross margin fell to 45.2% from 49.4%, and the CFO expects "continued compression" on price.

**RYTHM consolidation — substantiated, accounting only.** Starting Oct 10, GTI consolidates RYM after an amendment removed the 49.99% conversion cap. No cash changed hands and ownership didn't change, but Q4 revenue and EBITDA comparisons will be distorted. Expect headline noise in February that says nothing about the core business.

**Rescheduling — substantiated progress, slow timeline.** The ALJ's Oct 22 order refused opponents' bid to reopen the hearing over the GAO report, which removes the main delay tactic. Initial and reply briefs were due Oct 29 and Nov 5. No recommended decision has been issued, and the DEA Administrator still decides after exceptions, so the 2027 timing in the notes still stands. Medical Schedule III is real: there is partial 280E relief in the tax line, and GTI holds DEA registrations in FL and VA.

**Election — substantiated, long-dated.** Massachusetts rejected repeal 76/24. Results moved Pennsylvania and Wisconsin closer to adult-use legalization in 2027, and both would be meaningful new markets for an MSO with PA exposure. These are real votes but uncommitted policy, and any revenue is 2027+ at best.

**Hemp ban — substantiated date, live uncertainty.** Synthetics lost hemp status Nov 12. The broad 0.4 mg cap lands Dec 11 unless the lame-duck Congress (starting Nov 9) delays it again or passes the Barr/Craig 1%-THC alternative. A ban that sticks is a tailwind for licensed operators, so this is the nearest binary catalyst.

**Price move.** The −4.7% / +8.5% two-step around the print matches MSOS's move toward the election, plus a delayed recognition of the revenue beat. That is supported by the filings rather than running ahead of them.

**Catalysts:** the hemp vote by Dec 11, the ALJ recommendation (any time), 280E guidance from Treasury/IRS (FY2027 priority), Q4 results in late February, and continued buybacks.

**Sentiment.** Short interest is negligible: 99K shares, \~0.05% of shares out. Retail sentiment still swings on rescheduling headlines. Institutional access remains limited by OTC listing and custody rules, and Kovler conceded that uplisting alone "does not change how investors value the business."

**Risks:** state-level price compression (MA, IL, NJ), a hemp delay that extends competition from untaxed products, an unfavorable or Schedule II ALJ recommendation, continued 280E tax liability on adult-use, and RYTHM related-party complexity.

**Rating:** +0.8 — BUY

## Verdict

GTBIF trades at \~1.2x sales and \~8x FCF while revenue growth has re-accelerated to +11%, comps have turned positive, the balance sheet is net cash and buybacks steadily cut the share count. Few cannabis names have fundamentals this good. The main risk is margin: price compression is shaving gross margin and pushing FCF down Y/Y. Federal relief (adult-use rescheduling, 280E clarity) keeps slipping into 2027, and the stock sits under its 50/200-day trend lines. A clean break above \~\$7.00, a hemp ban that sticks on Dec 11, or a favorable ALJ recommendation would argue for an upgrade. Further gross-margin erosion or another Q2-style FCF air pocket in Q4 would argue for a downgrade.

**Rating:** +0.8 — BUY
