# Netflix, Inc. (NASDAQ: NFLX) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

Netflix is the world's largest subscription streaming entertainment service, delivering TV series, films, games, live events, and — more recently — video podcasts to an audience approaching one billion people across 190+ countries. The company's thesis: it is still "incredibly small" relative to its opportunity, accounting for an estimated ~5% of global TV view share and penetrating less than 45% of its total addressable market of broadband households. Netflix generates revenue almost entirely from monthly membership fees, increasingly supplemented by a fast-growing advertising business.

- **Founded:** 1997 (Los Gatos, California) as a DVD-by-mail rental service; launched streaming in 2007
- **IPO:** May 2002 (NASDAQ: NFLX)
- **Headquarters:** Los Gatos, California
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year)
- **FY2025 Revenue:** \$45,183M (+16% YoY); operating margin 29.5%
- **Stock split:** 10-for-1 forward split effected November 14, 2025 (all historical per-share and share-count figures retroactively adjusted)

---

## Revenue Model

Netflix earns revenue in two ways:

**Subscription memberships (the large majority of revenue):**
- Recurring monthly fees across a tiered plan structure — from the ad-supported plan (\$8.99/month in the US) up through Standard and Premium tiers
- Ended 2025 with 325M paid memberships; growth now driven by a combination of net adds, price increases, and the paid-sharing crackdown that converted borrowers into paying members
- As of Q1 2025 Netflix stopped reporting quarterly membership and ARM figures, shifting investor focus to revenue and operating margin as the headline metrics

**Advertising (small but rapidly scaling):**
- Ad-supported plan launched November 2022; the ads plan now represents over 60% of new sign-ups in countries where it is offered
- Netflix built its own in-house ad-tech platform and works with 4,000+ advertising clients (+70% YoY as of Q1 2026)
- Ad revenue is projected to roughly double in 2026 to ~\$3B

**Regional revenue segments** (UCAN, EMEA, LATAM, APAC): UCAN (US & Canada) is the largest and highest-ARM region; APAC and LATAM are the fastest-growing on an F/X-neutral basis.

## Products

### Core streaming service
Series and films remain the vast majority of the content budget. Netflix programs at unprecedented scale across tastes, cultures, and languages, publishing weekly "Top 10" view rankings. Franchise hits (Bridgerton, One Piece, Wednesday, Squid Game) drive fandom, word-of-mouth, and retention.

### Live events
A growing programming category: sports (NFL Christmas games, WWE Raw, the World Baseball Classic in Japan), boxing (Tyson–Paul, Fury–Joshua), comedy specials, and one-off cultural moments (BTS live). Live drives sign-up spikes and advertiser interest.

### Games
Four focus categories — narrative, party & puzzle, mainstream, and kids. Netflix launched "Playground," a standalone kids gaming app, in 2026, and is testing cloud TV games. Games are positioned as a retention and engagement lever rather than a direct revenue line.

### Video podcasts
Launched 2025 — a mix of Netflix originals and licensed podcasts, over-indexing on daytime and mobile viewing, extending Netflix into new incremental engagement occasions.

### Technology / Open Connect
Netflix operates its own content-delivery network (Open Connect) and a best-in-class personalized recommendation engine. It is increasingly applying GenAI to improve recommendations, conversational discovery, promotional assets, and (via the 2026 InterPositive acquisition) filmmaking tools for creative partners.

## Technology & Competitive Position

### Scale and global programming moat
No other entertainment company programs at Netflix's scale across languages and cultures. Its global footprint and deep engagement give it a superior monetization model for content, making it the most attractive partner for creators and licensors — a self-reinforcing advantage.

### Engagement and the "must-have" positioning
Netflix strives to be the "first place people go for entertainment and the last they cancel." Its primary internal quality-engagement metric reached an all-time high in Q1 2026. Streaming continues taking view share from linear TV, expanding the runway.

### Advertising build-out
Owning the ad-tech stack (rather than relying on a third party) lets Netflix build incrementality-measurement products verified by first-party data — a differentiator as the ads business scales toward material contribution.

### Free cash flow generation
Unlike most streaming peers, Netflix is now a large, growing FCF generator (\$9.5B in FY2025; ~\$12.5B projected for 2026), funding buybacks and selective M&A while maintaining a ~1.1x cash-content-spend-to-amortization ratio.

## Growth Strategy

Netflix frames its strategy around three pillars:

1. **Delivering more entertainment value** — concentrate spend on core series and films, broaden into live, games, and video podcasts, and win the "most valuable moments of truth" that drive retention and word-of-mouth.
2. **Leveraging technology** — use GenAI and ML to improve recommendations, the member experience (new TV UI, vertical-video mobile feed), and creative tooling for partners.
3. **Improving monetization** — sophisticated plans and pricing, continued price increases, and scaling the ads business — reinvesting the returns into content and product to drive a virtuous cycle.

## Key Risks

### Competition for engagement
Netflix's competitive set now spans Alphabet (YouTube), Amazon, Apple, Comcast/NBCU, Disney, Meta, Roblox, TikTok, and local media companies worldwide — all competing for the same finite viewing time. Netflix's stated defense is simply to "get better faster than the competition."

### Content cost and timing
Content amortization growth is front-half-weighted in 2026; mis-timed or underperforming slates pressure margins. Content is by far the single largest area of spend.

### Membership growth maturity
With paid-sharing largely lapped and developed markets mature, incremental growth increasingly depends on pricing and ads rather than raw member adds — and Netflix no longer discloses quarterly membership figures.

### Macro / FX
A large share of revenue is non-USD; F/X swings and hedging materially affect reported growth and margins. Consumer discretionary spending is sensitive to macro conditions.

### Capital allocation / M&A
Netflix declined to raise its offer for Warner Bros. in 2026, receiving a \$2.8B termination fee instead — a reminder that large M&A is on the table but only "at the right price." Execution risk on future deals remains.
