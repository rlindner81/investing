Netflix continues to post steady, high-teens revenue growth: TTM revenue of \$46.9B, with Y/Y prints ranging +12.5% to +17.6% across the last eight quarters and holding +16.2% in the latest (q1-2026). Growth is remarkably consistent — neither accelerating dramatically nor decelerating — reflecting a mature but still-expanding subscriber and ad base.

The FCF story is the standout. TTM company FCF is \$11.9B (25.4% margin), up sharply from the FY-2024 run-rate, with the latest quarter showing a 41.6% FCF margin. FCF Y/Y growth has been explosive in spots (+91.4% in q1-2026, +87.0% in q2-2025), driven by operating-cash conversion outpacing revenue and disciplined capex (only ~\$756M TTM PP&E). SBC is immaterial for a company this size — \$437M TTM against \$11.9B FCF — so the gap between company FCF and strict-ex-SBC FCF is tiny (\$11.9B vs \$11.5B). This is genuine, high-quality cash generation, not accounting-flattered.

On valuation, the TTM column screams cheap versus Netflix's own history: 6.2x P/S and 24.4x P/FCF, well below the 8–12x P/S and 34–60x P/FCF the stock carried at each report date over the past two years. Forward P/S on FY guidance is just 5.6–5.7x. The compression is entirely price-driven — the stock has fallen from ~\$120 (mid-2025) to \$68.95 while fundamentals kept growing.

Guidance is a clean beat-and-raise machine: FY revenue guidance has stepped up each report (43.5→45.1→50.7–51.7B), and actual-vs-guide on next-quarter revenue has been positive in nearly every period (+0.4% to +1.2%). Balance sheet carries modest net debt (\$12.3B cash vs \$14.4B debt), trivial against FCF.

Verdict: high-quality compounder trading at a multi-year-low multiple after a sharp de-rating — fundamentals do not justify the price action.

## 3. News

**Q2 2026 earnings (reported July 16, after close) drove a sharp selloff despite solid results.** Revenue was \$12.56B (+13% Y/Y, a record) with EPS of \$0.80 (vs \$0.79 est.) and operating margin of 33.4%, ahead of the company's own forecast. Net income rose to \$3.40B from \$3.13B a year earlier. Yet the stock fell as much as -12.2% intraday Friday July 17, settling around -9% to a 52-week low (~\$68.95), levels last seen September 2024. This marks the fifth straight post-earnings decline — a persistent "beat-and-fade" pattern.

**Why it dropped:** (1) Soft Q3 guidance — Netflix guided Q3 revenue to ~\$12.86B and EPS \$0.82, both below Street consensus of ~\$13.0B and \$0.84, a rare double-miss on the outlook. (2) Reduced transparency — the company will cut its "What We Watched" engagement report to annual (Q1) starting 2027; Evercore's Mahaney noted "anytime a company cuts back on disclosure, it's never a good thing." (3) Deceleration fears — growth slowing faster than models assumed, compounding a >21% YTD decline into the print.

**Positives buried in the report:** the ad business remains on track for ~\$3B in 2026 (roughly doubling, advertiser count +70% Y/Y to 4,000+ clients); FY2026 revenue narrowed to \$51.0–51.4B at 31.5% operating margin; and the company executed a record \$4.7B of buybacks in the quarter (largest ever), with \$27.1B remaining authorization. A prior overhang — a terminated Warner Bros. acquisition carrying a \$2.8B break fee — has also cleared, with FCF guidance raised as a result.

**Analyst posture:** targets are being trimmed but ratings stay constructive. Bernstein cut to \$100 (Outperform), UBS to \$115, Seaport to \$102; KGI downgraded to Neutral. Consensus remains Moderate Buy/Buy (22 buys, 9 holds, 0 sells) with an average target near \$98 — implying material upside from \$69.

**Catalysts/risks ahead:** next earnings ~October 2026 (Q3); the reduced-disclosure shift takes effect 2027. Key risks: continued growth deceleration, rising streaming competition, and the market's demonstrated willingness to sell every print regardless of the beat.

**Rating:** +0.3

## Verdict

Netflix is a rare case where the fundamentals and the tape point in opposite directions. The business is a high-quality cash compounder — steady mid-teens revenue growth, \$11.9B TTM FCF at a 25% margin, negligible SBC, and a record-setting buyback — now trading at a multi-year-low 6.2x P/S and 24x P/FCF after a punishing four-month slide to a 52-week low. The main risk is that the market is repricing decelerating growth: five straight post-earnings drops, soft Q3 guidance, and reduced engagement disclosure suggest sentiment, not just valuation, is broken, and the chart shows a clean downtrend on neutral volume with no reversal signal. This nets to a HOLD — the value case is compelling but there is no technical evidence the selling is done. A stabilization above the ~\$73 broken-support/POC shelf, or a Q3 print (October) that re-accelerates revenue and restores the beat-and-raise narrative, would flip this decisively bullish; a break below \$66 into the low-volume air pocket would confirm further downside.

**Rating:** +0.2
