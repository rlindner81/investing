For a business still compounding revenue mid-teens with 20%+ FCF margins and light SBC, this is the cheapest NFLX has looked in years — arguably attractive rather than merely fair.

Guidance has a clean beat-and-raise track record: every quarter's actual has landed within +0.2% to +1.2% of the prior NQ guide, and FY revenue guidance has been raised steadily (FY-2026 now \$51.0-51.4B). Balance sheet carries \$9.1B cash against \$14.3B debt — a modest \$5.2B net-debt position, easily serviced by \$11B+ annual FCF.

Verdict: fundamentals are intact and the multiple has reset to a compelling level — the valuation setup is now a buy.

The quarter itself was roughly in line — revenue \$12.56B (+13.4% Y/Y, a record) and EPS \$0.80 vs. \$0.79 consensus — but the stock fell ~8% on soft forward guidance and dropped a further 7.3% on Friday July 18 (142M shares, >3x the 65-day average). The Q3 revenue guide of \$12.86B (+11.7% Y/Y, the slowest growth since late 2023) undershot the ~\$13B consensus, and Q3 EPS guidance of \$0.82 missed the \$0.84 forecast. Operating margin slipped to 33.4% from 34.1% a year ago, and FCF fell 33% to \$1.53B (partly a deal-termination-fee tax). The guidance miss triggered at least 11 analyst price-target cuts. Management did narrow FY-2026 revenue guidance up-and-in to \$51.0-51.4B.

**Unusual price action.** NFLX is down ~45% from its record high on June 30, 2025 — roughly \$259B of market cap erased — landing it among the 20 worst S&P 500 performers over that span. It has now fallen after each of the last four earnings reports. The stock is also down ~31% since mid-April, when a weak forecast coincided with the announcement that co-founder/chairman Reed Hastings would step down.

**Upcoming catalysts.** Next earnings ~October 20, 2026 (Q3, consensus EPS ~\$0.84). The key watch item is advertising: management reaffirmed ad revenue is on track to roughly double to ~\$3B in 2026 (from ~\$1.5B in 2025). A structural negative for transparency: Netflix is cutting its viewing-hours report from twice- to once-yearly starting January 2027, and Q1 was its last subscriber-metric disclosure — future quarters lean on regional revenue, making the business more opaque just as the growth story is questioned.

**Sentiment & risks.** The bear case is decelerating growth as password-sharing tailwinds fade, margin pressure, and reduced disclosure. The bull case rests on ad monetization and valuation: NFLX now trades ~20x forward earnings vs. a 10-year average of ~51x and at a slight discount to the S&P 500 for the first time since 2022. Analysts remain broadly constructive (TD Cowen buy \$112, Bernstein outperform \$100) but the debate hinges on whether ads and live/short-form content offset slowing subscriber and engagement growth amid intensifying competition. Net: momentum and narrative are clearly negative right now, only partly offset by an increasingly cheap multiple.

**Rating:** -0.6 — SELL

## Verdict

NFLX presents a genuine bull-bear standoff. The fundamentals are still excellent — mid-teens revenue growth, \$11B+ TTM FCF at a 23% margin, negligible SBC dilution, and a multiple that has reset from 10-12x sales to under 6x, its cheapest in years. Against that, the tape and narrative are firmly negative: the stock is down ~45% from its 2025 peak, sits below every falling moving average with no reversal signal, has fallen after four straight earnings reports, and just guided Q3 growth to its slowest pace since 2023 while cutting disclosure. The single most important driver from here is the advertising ramp (targeting ~\$3B in 2026) proving it can offset fading subscriber tailwinds. What would upgrade the rating: a decisive trend break above the falling 50/200-day averages on volume, or a Q3 print/guide that re-accelerates revenue and validates the ad story; what would downgrade it: another post-earnings drop or evidence ad growth is stalling. For now the compelling valuation and the ugly trend roughly cancel — a HOLD, with the bias toward accumulating on further weakness for patient capital.

**Rating:** -0.2 — HOLD
