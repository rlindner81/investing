**Revenue trend.** Growth is decelerating but still solidly mid-teens. TTM revenue is \$48.4B, with Y/Y prints stepping down from +17.6% (q4-2025) → +16.2% (q1-2026) → +13.4% (q2-2026). That q2 deceleration is the single most important number in the table: it is the first sub-14% print in the displayed window and it is what the market has been repricing. FY-2025 closed at \$45.2B (+15.9%), and FY-2026 guidance of \$51.0–51.4B implies \~+13% — management itself is now guiding to a low-teens company.

**FCF profile.** Strongly positive and structurally so. TTM FCF (company) is \$11.15B on a 23.1% margin; FY-2025 was \$9.46B (+36.7%) at 20.9%. The quarterly FCF line is noisy — q1-2026 +91.4% against q2-2026 ‑32.7% — but that is content-spend *timing*, not deterioration; the pair nets out to a clean full-year trend. SBC is remarkably light for a mega-cap tech: \$487M TTM against \$11.2B of FCF, so `strict ex SBC` FCF (\$10.66B) is only \~4% below the company figure. There is essentially no hidden dilution gap here, which is unusual and a genuine quality marker.

**Valuation.** This is the headline. At \$76.03 the stock trades at 6.5x TTM sales and **28.4x TTM FCF** (29.7x strict). Twelve months ago at q2-2025 those were 12.3x and 60.4x. The multiple has more than halved while fundamentals never broke — a compression, not a break. Forward P/S on FY-2026 guidance is 6.2x. For a \~13% grower with a 21–23% FCF margin, minimal SBC, and demonstrated pricing power, \~28x FCF is **fair, arguably modestly cheap — but not a screaming bargain.** The de-rate corrected an overvaluation rather than creating an obvious undervaluation.

**Guidance record.** Excellent and consistent. Next-quarter revenue has landed within ‑0.1% to +1.2% of the guide in every one of the last eight quarters — Netflix guides tightly and hits. FY guidance has been raised or narrowed upward each year (FY-2026 narrowed to \$51.0–51.4B at q2). No credibility problem.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt of \~\$5.2B (\$9.10B cash vs \$14.31B debt) against \$11B+ annual FCF — under half a turn of leverage, immaterial. Share count is shrinking meaningfully: 4,277.6M → 4,163.9M over eight quarters (‑2.7%), funded by a record \$4.7B q2 buyback with \~\$27B authorized.

**Verdict:** a high-quality, low-SBC, cash-generative franchise now trading at a fair-to-slightly-attractive multiple after a brutal de-rate — the risk has shifted from valuation to whether mid-teens growth holds.

**Rating:** +1.0 — BUY

## 3. News

**The surface narrative.** Two stories dominate the last four weeks: Bill Ackman's re-entry into Netflix (mid-August, a positive shock) and a wave of European price increases (early September, taken *negatively*). Underneath both sits the same unresolved question the stock has been trading on all year — whether monetization can carry growth now that subscriber levers are spent.

**Ackman / Pershing Square — substantiated, but a sentiment event.** The Q2 13F and Pershing's interim report disclosed a new \~\$934M Netflix position (\~3.15M post-split-equivalent shares, \~4.9% of the portfolio), bought after the stock fell roughly 50% from its June 2025 high; Alphabet was exited to fund it. The filing itself is hard data — a regulatory disclosure of a real transaction — and NFLX jumped \~3.4% on Aug 13. But the *thesis* attached to it ("Netflix has effectively won the streaming wars," \~20% earnings compounding, \~90% of earnings converting to FCF) is Ackman's opinion, not new company data. Worth noting the reflexive risk: Pershing sold its prior Netflix stake in April 2022 at a \~\$400M loss. Treat the stake as validation of the *valuation* argument, not as new information about the business.

**European price hikes — substantiated, and the more important item.** On Sept 3–4 Netflix raised UK prices (Standard-with-Ads +33.4% to £7.99; Standard +7.7% to £13.99; Premium +10.5% to £20.99, above £20 for the first time), and separately raised prices in Germany and Austria. These are committed, published, company-confirmed numbers — the hardest kind of data. Citi quantified the reach: the three markets cover \~36% of EMEA subscribers and \~12% of the global base, with an estimated +24% German ARPU, +15% Austrian, \~+4% EMEA and **\~+1.5% at the company level**.

**The paradoxical reaction, and what actually drove it.** NFLX fell \~4–5% on Sept 4 (40.2M shares) on what is, mechanically, a revenue *increase*. Two real drivers: (1) Citi flagged that German and Austrian consumer-protection law requires affirmative opt-in, so the ARPU benefit is **delayed and leakier than the headline**; (2) the market read a third price rise in a year — the second in the UK in seven months — as evidence Netflix is leaning on price precisely because volume growth has stalled. That second read is narrative, not data, but it is the live debate. Notably Citi *reiterated* Buy with a \$100 target through the selloff, and consensus sits at "Buy"/"Moderate Buy" with targets clustering \$94–97 — the sell-side did not follow the tape down. The gap between a \~\$95 consensus target and a \$76 price is itself a signal that the selling is positioning-driven, not estimate-driven.

**TF1 partnership — substantiated and underrated.** The France-only TF1+ integration (launched June 19) hit its 18-month audience targets within three weeks; TF1+ averaged 42M monthly streamers in H1 (+20% Y/Y), peaking at 44M in June. This is third-party verified operating data, and it establishes Netflix as an OTT *carrier* — a genuinely new business model with replication optionality across Europe. Reports that Netflix may host further third-party services are so far unconfirmed.

**Catalysts and sentiment.** Next earnings \~Oct 20, 2026 (after close; consensus \~\$12.87B revenue, \$0.82 EPS) — the decisive event, since it is the first read on whether the q2 deceleration to +13.4% was a floor. Netflix will also begin reporting theatrical box office for select films. Short interest is benign and falling: 2.2% of float, ‑9%, 2.1 days to cover — no squeeze setup and no organized bear campaign.

**Risks.** Engagement is the soft spot: view-hours growth ran \~2% in 1H-2026 against mid-teens revenue, and management deflects to an undisclosed "member quality" metric that cannot be audited. Price-led growth has a ceiling and rising regulatory friction (EU opt-in rules; South Africa's ICASA opening a pricing inquiry). Competitively, Paramount Skydance now owns Warner Bros. at \~\$111B, creating a large, levered, content-rich rival. And the medium-term watch-item: if GenAI genuinely halves production cost sector-wide, the scarcity value of Netflix's \~\$20B slate erodes for everyone.

**Rating:** +0.5 — BUY

## Verdict

NFLX is a fairly-to-attractively valued great business caught in a bad tape. The fundamental case is intact — 28.4x TTM FCF and 6.5x sales after a de-rate from 60x/12.3x, a 23% FCF margin, negligible SBC, an eight-quarter streak of hitting guidance within ±1.2%, and \$4.7B/quarter of buyback — and the September European price hikes plus the TF1 carrier model are real, committed monetization levers that Ackman's \$934M stake and a \~\$95 sell-side consensus both endorse. The tape says otherwise: price is below all three weekly SMAs and \~12% under its 200-day, ‑5.9% MTD against a flat market, with an \$82 volume shelf overhead and buyers absent on fading volume. The main risk is that q2-2026's deceleration to +13.4% revenue growth — with view-hours up only \~2% and price doing the heavy lifting — marks a mid-teens ceiling rather than a trough, in which case 28x FCF is merely correct and the stock is dead money until it grows in. The Oct 20 print is the swing factor: a Q3 that holds \~13% growth and shows the ads S-curve bending up turns this into a clear BUY, while another step-down in growth or the first sign that European price hikes are lifting churn would push it to SELL.

**Rating:** +0.2 — HOLD
