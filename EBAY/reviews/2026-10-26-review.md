**Revenue trend.** eBay has gone from a no-growth marketplace to a growth story. Reported revenue grew just +0.7% to +3.0% Y/Y through 2024, then picked up through 2025 (+1.1%, +6.1%, +9.5%, +15.0%) and peaked at +19.5% in Q1-2026 before easing to +14.8% in Q2-2026. Part of this is FX tailwind and tuck-in M&A (Goldin, Tise, and now Depop, which closed July 30). The Q3 guide of \$3,070–3,120M implies only \~9–11% reported growth even with two months of Depop. So the peak quarters are probably behind us, although the October 20 8-K already says Q3 came in above that range.

**FCF profile.** eBay is solidly FCF-positive. TTM company FCF is \$2,505M (20.9% margin), and the strict ex-SBC figure is \$1,861M (15.5%). The FY-2025 dip to \$1,484M is not structural: Q2-2025 carried \~\$935M of one-off tax outflows (taxes on equity-investment sales plus the final repatriation payment), and that quarter has now dropped out of the TTM. SBC runs \~\$644M a year, about 26% of company FCF. That's real dilution cost, but buybacks more than absorb it: shares fell from 509M to 445M (\~-13%) in two years. One caveat: 2025 cash flows are as originally reported, while 2026 is on the ASU 2025-06 basis. OCF and capex both shift under that change, but FCF does not.

**Valuation.** At \$119.48 the stock trades at 4.4x TTM sales, 21.2x company FCF and 28.6x strict FCF. That P/S is the highest multiple in the displayed window (2.5x in Q1-2024, 3.3x as recently as February). Forward P/S on the derived FY guide (\$12.3–12.4B) is 4.3x. For a business growing at a high-single to low-double-digit FX-neutral rate with \~21% FCF margins, \~21x FCF is fair but no longer cheap. The rerating has already priced in most of the acceleration.

**Guidance track record.** eBay beat its next-quarter revenue guide in all nine readable quarters, and the beats have been widening: +0.7% to +2.3% in 2024, then +2.1% to +4.5% since. It beat the FY ranges it issued at Q3-2024 and Q3-2025 as well. One caveat: the q2-2026 FY range is derived from the CFO's 11–12% FX-neutral comment, not printed by the company.

**Balance sheet.** Reported cash of \$2.31B against \$6.74B of debt gives \~\$4.4B of net debt in the table. Counting non-equity investments, liquidity is closer to the \~\$5B range. Q3 cash will also reflect the \~\$1.4B Depop payment. Leverage is modest against \~\$2.5B of FCF.

**Verdict:** this is a high-quality, consistently beating cash generator, but the multiple has re-rated to the top of its range just as growth is set to moderate.

**Rating:** 0.3 — HOLD

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** Three stories have driven EBAY over the last four weeks:

1. **Restructuring plus pre-announced beat (Oct 20–22).** eBay is cutting about 9% of its workforce (\~1,000 roles per a spokesperson, its second round this year). In the same filing it said Q3 will come in above its August guidance.
2. **Analyst upgrades.** Morgan Stanley named EBAY a "Top Pick" on Oct 12 (Buy, \$132 target), citing live commerce and recommerce.
3. **GameStop rumors.** A Oct 15 spike to an all-time-high close of \$122.91 was attributed to a "recycled" GameStop takeover rumor.

**Deeper read.**

- **Restructuring: substantiated.** The 8-K (items 2.02/2.05, accepted Oct 22) puts pre-tax charges at \$130–150M, mostly severance, booked largely in Q4-2026, with the plan done by end of Q1-2027. Savings are to be *reinvested*, not dropped to margin, so this is a reallocation story rather than a margin-expansion one.
- **Q3 "above guidance": substantiated but unquantified.** The 8-K is a formal item-2.02 disclosure, so it is real. It gives no number, though. Given nine straight NQ beats of +0.7% to +4.5%, a beat of the \$3.07–3.12B range was the base case anyway. What the pre-announcement really adds is a lower risk of a miss, not upside to estimates.
- **Morgan Stanley top pick: mostly attention.** It is opinion, not new data. The Street is split: StoneX Buy \$136, Bernstein Hold \$115, Wells Fargo Sell \$91. The average target is near \$117, *below* the current price.
- **GameStop: real stake, narrative-driven bid.** The stake is hard data. GameStop's 13D/As show \~43.4M shares (\~9.8%), making it eBay's second-largest holder. The \$125 cash-and-stock proposal from May was rejected by eBay's board as "neither credible nor attractive." In August Bloomberg reported that Cohen was weighing withdrawal in favor of a partnership. No new filing backs the Oct 15 rumor. That +5% day ran ahead of anything concrete, and Etsy (+7%) and ONLN (+3%) rallied the same day, so part of it was sector beta. Still, a 10% holder with a stated \$125 price puts a soft anchor near the current price.
- **Depop: substantiated.** The deal closed July 30 for \~\$1.4B cash. Management guided \~2.5pts of GMV contribution in the partial Q3, which flatters reported growth for the next three quarters.

**Price moves since the August print.** EBAY fell 3% in August, then rose 1% in September and 12.8% in October. October's gains came from a real driver (the pre-announced beat, Oct 22) and two attention drivers (the MS note and the GME rumor). Today's dip shows some profit-taking.

**Catalysts.** Q3 earnings on **Oct 28** after the close (consensus \~\$1.41 EPS on \~\$3.11B revenue). The key items are the Q4 guide and the first formal FY-2026 range, Depop integration, and any restructuring savings target. Beyond that: the holiday quarter (Q4 print in late February), any 13D move or 2027 proxy action by GameStop, and the Q1-2027 restructuring completion.

**Sentiment.** Short interest is low at 3.3–3.9% (3.4 days to cover) and has fallen from \~5% in 2024, so there is no squeeze fuel. Institutions are split, and the consensus is Hold. CEO Iannone sold 22K shares at \~\$108 in early October under a Form 144. That is routine, but it isn't a vote of confidence at these levels.

**Key risks:**
- Growth decelerating once Depop and FX tailwinds lap
- A trading-card/collectibles boom that may be cyclical
- Cross-border tariff and de minimis exposure
- Reinvestment eating restructuring savings
- An unpredictable 10% holder that could push a proxy fight or exit the stake

**Rating:** 0.5 — HOLD

## Verdict

eBay is executing well. Revenue growth has gone from \~1% to the mid-teens, it has beaten next-quarter guidance nine times in a row, it produces \~\$2.5B of TTM FCF, and steady buybacks have retired \~13% of the share count in two years. The Q3 beat is already confirmed by 8-K. The problem is price. At \~21x FCF and 4.4x sales, the multiple is at the top of its range just as growth is set to ease toward high single digits FX-neutral. The stock also trades above the Street's average target and right under \~\$121 volume resistance, with options pricing a \~±11% earnings-week move. The main risk is a "beat but decelerate" Q4 guide, especially if the restructuring savings get reinvested rather than showing up in margins. The rating would move to BUY on a pullback toward the \$108–110 POC zone, or on a Q4/FY guide implying double-digit organic growth into 2027. It would move to SELL if organic growth falls back toward mid-single digits or the GameStop stake turns into a forced exit overhang.

**Rating:** 0.4 — HOLD
