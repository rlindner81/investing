# KGC — All-In Sustaining Cost (AISC) Reference

**What AISC is:** the total cash cost to produce one ounce of gold *and* keep the
operation running at its current level, expressed as \$/oz. Per the World Gold
Council standard it rolls in mine-site cash costs (mining, processing, labor,
fuel, reagents, site G&A), **royalties & production taxes**, **sustaining
capital** (rebuilds, mine development, tailings/heap-leach maintenance),
sustaining exploration, and corporate G&A. It **excludes growth capital**
(e.g. Great Bear construction, Phase X development) — that is a choice to grow,
not a cost of running today's business.

**Why it matters:** the gap between realized gold price and AISC is roughly the
true economic margin per ounce, so AISC is the single best one-number proxy for
how defensible a miner is in a downturn. Caveat: it is a company-defined
non-GAAP measure (some latitude on "sustaining" vs "growth" capex), so it is
most reliable comparing a company to itself over time.

**Source note:** these figures come from the CFO's (Andrea Freeborough)
prepared remarks on each earnings call — see `quarters/*-transcript.md`. AISC is
**not** in the filed SEC statements (`quarters/*-report.md`); it lives in the
MD&A / press release.

## What the table tells you about cost management

- **The gross rise is misleading.** Full-year AISC went \$1,388 (2024) → \$1,571
  (2025) → \$1,730 guided (2026), ~+25% over two years — but that is *not* mostly
  operational slippage. Management decomposes the 2026 step (+10% over 2025) as
  ~4% royalties (a function of the higher gold price — rises because they're
  winning), ~5% inflation (macro, industry-wide), and ~1% mine sequencing
  (genuinely operational, and small). **The controllable portion of the increase
  is ~1%** — the tell that they manage cost well.

- **The margin column is the real story.** AISC rose ~25%, but margin/oz went
  from ~\$955 to ~\$3,476. In a gold bull market, disciplined cost management is
  not about making AISC *fall* (royalties make that nearly impossible) — it is
  about keeping AISC rising *slower* than the price. KGC has done that decisively.

- **Where to stay skeptical.** The ~\$1,730 guide is pinned to a \$4,500 gold
  assumption; royalties scale with price, so a higher gold price mechanically
  lifts reported AISC (not a red flag). The number to actually watch is the
  **inflation + sequencing** piece — the part within their control, and what
  would expose slipping discipline. So far it's ~5–6%/yr, in line with peers.

- **The stated offset** is the grade-enhancement strategy (Phase X, Curlew,
  Tasiast returning to higher grade in 2028, Great Bear/Lobo-Marte in the
  2030s): feeding progressively higher-grade ore to hold cost/oz roughly flat
  against inflation without needing gold to keep rising. That is the part of the
  cost story that would survive a gold reversion.

**Bottom line:** the AISC trend confirms KGC is a well-run, disciplined operator
— controllable cost creep is minimal — but it also makes plain that the *margin*
explosion is a gold-price phenomenon, not a cost-reduction one.
