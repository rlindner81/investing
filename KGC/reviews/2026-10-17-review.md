**Revenue** is still growing fast but slowing. Q2-2026 revenue was \$2,238M, +29.5% Y/Y, after +60.8% in Q1. TTM revenue is \$8.47B against \$7.05B for FY-2025. Q2 was also down 7% sequentially from Q1's \$2,408M peak. That is the gold-price whipsaw at work, not volume growth: Kinross guides production, not revenue (`prod-guided` across every column). Q3 will be the first quarter with the lower 1.84–1.86 Moz run-rate baked in (\~425k oz guided). Gold has also fallen from \~\$4,483 realized in Q2 to \~\$4,150 spot, so the Y/Y comparison against Q3-2025 (\$1,802M) gets tighter.

**FCF** is excellent. TTM company FCF is \$3.08B on a 36.4% margin. Strict FCF (\$3.07B) is almost identical: capitalized interest is negligible and SBC isn't broken out, so there is no hidden-cost gap. The trend is rolling over, though. Q2 FCF grew only +7.1% Y/Y because capex jumped to \$411M (+45% Q/Q) as the [Capex Ramp](../notes/CapexRamp.md) starts. H2 capex should hold at \~\$400M/qtr, while the gold price and ounce count both fall. A rough mark-to-spot (\~\$330/oz lower on \~1.85 Moz) takes \~\$0.5–0.6B off forward FCF.

**Valuation** is cheap. At \$24.02 the market cap is \$28.5B, which gives P/S of 3.4x and P/FCF of 9.2x on trailing numbers. On a spot-adjusted FCF of \~\$2.5B, P/FCF is still only \~11x. That is below where the stock traded through most of 2025 (11.7–16.1x) and close to the cycle lows. There are no forward P/S rows (n/m), because Kinross guides ounces and AISC, not revenue. The guidance record has just broken: the 2026-09-23 update cut 2026–27 production below the low end of the range and raised AISC from \$1,730 to \$1,850–1,900.

**Balance sheet**: cash is \$2.66B against \$739M of debt, so net cash is \~\$1.9B, up from net debt of \~\$1.8B in mid-2024. Shares outstanding fell from 1,230M to 1,186M (-3.6%) through buybacks. The capital return target is now 50% of FCF.

**Verdict:** a high-margin, net-cash, shrinking-share-count producer at \~9–11x FCF. It is cheap even after marking to spot gold, but the earnings momentum and execution story have both cracked.

**Rating:** +1.2 — BUY

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** The story right now is the 2026-09-23 "operational and return of capital update" and what it did to the stock. KGC fell \~21% in September, which put it among the worst names in MINING.COM's Top-50 miner ranking. Analysts cut targets afterwards: CIBC to \$34 from \$50, UBS to \$28 from \$30. BofA nudged up to \$33 and RBC reiterated Buy. On Oct 15 the stock jumped 7.2% (TSX: +6.9%) with no verified catalyst.

**Depth pass. Item by item:**

- **Production/cost cut: substantiated.** The 6-K exhibit 99.1 gives the numbers. 2026 and 2027 attributable production is now 1.84–1.86 Moz, 2–3% below the old low end and \~7–8% below the original \~2.0 Moz midpoint. Q3 production is guided at \~425k oz. The 2026 AISC guide goes from \$1,730 to \$1,850–1,900. The shortfall is limited to La Coipa (winter weather, operating problems) and Round Mountain (mining rate, grade and recovery). Paracatu and Tasiast are still on track for \~1.1 Moz combined, and Great Bear and Lobo-Marte are on schedule. This is real, hard data. The cut also extends into 2027, so it is not a one-off.
- **Capital return lift to 50% of FCF: substantiated.** Kinross has returned \~\$800M year-to-date in 2026 (\~\$655M of that in buybacks), and the share count drop to 1,186M in the filings confirms it. It reads partly as a sweetener released alongside bad news, but the cash behind it is real.
- **S&P upgrade to BBB (August): substantiated.** It fits the \~\$1.9B net-cash balance sheet.
- **Analyst price-target cuts: mostly attention.** They reprice the stock after the fact on a disclosed cut and add no new information. The \$37.70 consensus target (MarketBeat) looks stale against a \$24 stock.
- **Oct 15 +7.2%: mostly attention.** GDX was down on the week and no filing or release backs the move. Flow data shows a heavy final hour, which is consistent with short covering or a bottom-fishing rebalance rather than news.

**Macro driver: substantiated.** Gold is down \~21% from its \~\$5,595 January record to \~\$4,150. Behind that: the Fed's Sep 16 rate hike, US 10-year yields above 5%, and Brent near \$108 after Hormuz talks collapsed. The gold-miner unwind wiped \$79B (-12.7%) off the 15 largest producers in September. A weak September jobs report has since cut the odds of an October hike to the mid-teens, which has steadied gold. Morgan Stanley calls \$4,000 a floor, but that is opinion.

**Catalysts:**
- Q3-2026 results on **Oct 28 after close**, call Oct 29 at 8:00 ET. This is the first print against the new guide. Watch AISC against the \$1,850–1,900 range and buyback pace.
- The Great Bear capex re-cut in early 2027. Management has already warned it will land above the PEA's \$1.4B.

**Key risks:**

- Further misses at the smaller mines.

- Great Bear cost inflation.

## Verdict

On the numbers, Kinross is cheap: 9.2x trailing FCF and \~11x marked to spot gold, a 36% FCF margin, \~\$1.9B net cash, and a shrinking share count now backed by a 50%-of-FCF return policy. Two things are working against it. First, the September production/AISC cut damaged management's execution credibility and extends into 2027. Second, the macro backdrop (a Fed hike, yields above 5%, gold \~21% off its high) is pulling the whole sector down, and the chart is in a confirmed long-term downtrend with only tentative support at \~\$23.7. The main risk is margin compression from three directions at once: gold below \$4,000, the cut ounce base, and capex ramping toward Great Bear's construction peak. A clean Q3 on Oct 28 would move the rating toward BUY: AISC inside the new range, H2 buybacks on pace, no further mine-level slippage. So would gold holding \~\$4,000 or better. Another guidance trim or a break below \$22 would move it toward SELL.

**Rating:** +0.2 — HOLD
