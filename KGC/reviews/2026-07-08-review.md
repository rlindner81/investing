Kinross Gold's fundamentals are firing on all cylinders as a leveraged play on record gold prices. Revenue growth is accelerating: the latest reported quarter (q1-2026) grew +60.8% Y/Y to \$2.41B, on top of +36.9% for full-year 2025 (\$7.05B). TTM revenue stands at \$7.96B, a clean uptrend with no sign of deceleration — each recent quarter has posted 25–60% Y/Y gains as the realized gold price roughly doubled.

The FCF profile is the standout. TTM company FCF is \$3.03B (+119.9% Y/Y in the latest quarter), up from \$1.37B in FY-2024 — a near-doubling driven by operating leverage on higher gold prices while capex held roughly flat. FCF is strongly positive and compounding. There is essentially no gap between company FCF (\$3.03B) and strict ex-SBC FCF (\$3.02B): stock comp and other capitalized spend are immaterial, so FCF quality is high and un-diluted.

Valuation is cheap for this growth and cash generation. TTM P/S is 3.5x and P/FCF is just 9.1x — both well below the peaks hit when the stock traded near \$34 in early 2026 (5.9x P/S, 16.1x P/FCF at the q4-2025 column). The recent pullback to \$23 has re-rated the multiple down materially even as FCF kept climbing. The balance sheet is a fortress: \$2.19B cash against \$738M debt for ~\$1.45B net cash, having steadily paid down debt from \$2.23B in early 2024.

Gold miners give production, not revenue, guidance ("prod-guided"), so forward P/S rows are n/m; management reaffirmed 2026 production and cost targets. The obvious caveat: these multiples are only as durable as the gold price — a sustained gold decline would compress both revenue and FCF fast. But on trailing figures, KGC looks inexpensive with pristine cash generation.

## 3. News

The dominant story is macro, not company-specific. Gold hit a record ~\$4,961/oz on January 23, 2026, then rolled into a ~28% correction, trading near \$4,050 on July 8 — its lowest since early July — after President Trump declared the interim Iran peace deal "over," lifting oil >5% and reviving inflation/higher-rate fears. The VanEck Gold Miners ETF (GDX) is off roughly 20% from its March 2 high near \$117 to ~\$94. This bullion pullback is what has dragged KGC from ~\$34 to \$23; the weakness is sector-wide, not a Kinross execution failure.

**Upcoming catalyst.** Kinross reports Q2 2026 results after the close on **July 29, 2026**, with the call on July 30 — the key near-term event. Q1 2026 was strong: record attributable FCF of \$837.5M (more than doubled Y/Y), supporting \$250M of buybacks in the quarter and a stated policy to return ~40% of FCF to shareholders in 2026. Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance — ~2.0M attributable Au-eq oz (±5%), AISC ~\$1,730/oz, capex ~\$1.5B — and remains on track. The realized gold price in Q1 was \$4,873/oz vs. \$2,857 a year earlier, the engine behind the FCF surge.

**Sentiment.** Miner-focused commentary frames the June sell-off as healthy consolidation rather than a new bear market, citing record miner profits, central-bank accumulation, and stagflation signals; several outlets see a late-July-through-August rebound and project bullion at \$4,750–5,500 over 6–9 months. That is constructive but far from consensus.

**Key risks.** KGC's earnings are a direct leveraged bet on the gold price — the same operating leverage that doubled FCF cuts the other way if gold keeps falling toward miners' AISC. Additional risks: a firmer dollar / higher real rates if inflation reaccelerates, geopolitical whipsaw, and jurisdictional/operational exposure across its mine portfolio. Near-term momentum is negative into an earnings print that should nonetheless show strong cash generation.

## Verdict

Kinross is a cheap, cash-gushing gold miner (TTM P/FCF ~9x, ~\$3B FCF, ~\$1.45B net cash, 40% of FCF returned to shareholders) caught in a sharp sector-wide gold correction that has taken the stock from ~\$34 to \$23 in a clean, still-intact downtrend. The bull case is the fundamentals and re-rated multiple; the main risk is that KGC's earnings are a leveraged bet on the gold price, so continued bullion weakness compresses both revenue and FCF quickly. The July 29 Q2 print is the near-term swing factor — strong FCF is likely, but the tape needs to base first. A stabilization/reversal in gold and the SMA structure (or a decisive beat-and-buyback update) would tilt this from HOLD toward BUY; a break of gold toward miner AISC would push it to SELL.

**Rating:** +0.1
