**Revenue has rolled over.** Q3-2026 metal sales were \$1,774.7M, -1.5% Y/Y. That ends a run of +26–61% quarterly growth and is down sharply from \$2,238M in Q2 and \$2,408M in Q1. Price did not cause it: realized gold rose 23% Y/Y to \$4,249/oz. Volume did. Attributable production fell to \~425 koz from \~504 koz a year ago (the September La Coipa/Round Mountain cut), and Q3 sales were only 410 koz because ounces were left unsold over the September 30 bank holiday. TTM revenue is still \$8.44B, but the trajectory is now flat to down unless gold rises.

**FCF is still strong on a TTM basis, but Q3 was weak.** TTM company FCF is \$2.64B (31% margin). Q3 alone fell to \$274.8M (15.5% margin, -61% Y/Y), squeezed from three directions:
- Operating cash flow fell to \$714M, with \$151M of cash taxes and a \~\$128M working-capital drain.
- Capex rose to \$439.6M, above the \~\$400M/qtr run-rate the [Capex Ramp](../notes/CapexRamp.md) note expected. Management now steers to the mid-to-upper end of the \$1.5B guide.
- AISC was \$1,947/oz. The 9-month figure is \$1,827, so landing inside the \$1,850–1,900 guide implies a Q4 near \$1,950–2,100.

Adjusted for working capital, attributable FCF was \$391M. SBC is negligible (\$3.9M), and capitalized interest is small, so strict FCF ≈ company FCF.

**Valuation is cheap.** At \$21.79:
- P/S is 3.0x and P/FCF 9.7x (strict 9.8x). That is the lowest P/FCF in the display window apart from Q2's 8.9x, and it compares with 16.1x in February.
- Market cap is \$25.7B against \~\$1.9B net cash (\$2.64B cash, \$739M debt, no maturities before 2033).
- The share count fell to 1,178M (-2.6% Y/Y). The 50%-of-FCF return commitment is being met: \$230M of buybacks in Q3 plus another \$100M in October.

**Guidance.** Kinross guides production and costs, not revenue, so there are no forward P/S rows. The track record has turned negative: the September cut lowered 2026 and 2027 production and raised AISC. Q3 landed inside the new ranges, but 2027 is guided flat at \~1.86 Moz with capex "subject to inflation." Margin growth now depends entirely on gold.

**Verdict:** Optically cheap with an excellent balance sheet and real capital returns. But revenue and FCF are now shrinking on a lower ounce base, rising AISC and a capex ramp, so the low multiple partly reflects lower earnings power.

**Rating:** +0.9 — BUY

## 3. News

**Surface narrative.** Kinross reported Q3-2026 after the close on October 28. Headlines split between "adjusted EPS beat" and "revenue miss, costs surge 20%":
- Adjusted EPS was \$0.50, against consensus of \~\$0.45–0.47 (one outlet had \$0.54).
- Revenue was \$1.77B, against \$1.84–1.94B depending on the source.
- The stock fell \~5% the next morning.

The backdrop is a falling gold tape. Gold has slipped to \~\$4,000–4,050, down \~6% in a month, and the Fed held rates ahead of the US mid-terms. Standard Chartered flags \$3,886 as the technical line to watch.

**Deeper read, item by item:**
- **Q3 execution: substantiated.** Every key figure comes from the filed statements and the call:
  - Production was 425 koz at \$1,947 AISC; the 9-month AISC is \$1,827.
  - Q4 is guided at 430–450 koz to deliver \~1.86 Moz for 2026.
  - La Coipa and Round Mountain met their reduced plans. Round Mountain recovery rose to 60% from 44%.

  This is the "clean Q3" the prior review asked for: AISC inside the range, no new slippage, buybacks on pace. The market sold anyway. The weak quarter is real (revenue -1.5% Y/Y, attributable FCF \$281M vs \$687M a year ago). Part of it is timing: unsold ounces were sold in early October, and \$110M of the working-capital drain should reverse.
- **"Costs surge 20%": substantiated but already known.** AISC rose because the ounce base was cut, the denominator effect described in [AISC](../notes/AISC.md). It was guided in September, not newly revealed.
- **Capital returns: substantiated.** The return target is now 50% of FCF. Kinross has returned \$950M+ year to date, including \$100M of buybacks after quarter end, and has repurchased \~5% of the share count since April 2025. These are hard cash numbers.
- **Great Bear: a new risk, substantiated.** Grassy Narrows First Nation is seeking leave to appeal the advanced-exploration (AEX) permits, with no decision likely before H1-2027. Separately, the federal impact-statement decision is still needed by late spring 2027 to keep the summer-2027 construction start. Management repeated that initial capital will exceed the \$1.4B PEA estimate. That number comes in early 2027 (see [Great Bear](../notes/GreatBear.md)).
- **Price-target cuts: mostly attention.** Since September, targets have come down at CIBC (to \$34), BofA (to \$32.75), UBS (to \$28) and RBC (to \$39). The average is still \~\$36–38, well above the price. These follow the September cut and the gold move; they add no information of their own.

**Price moves since the last report.**
- The 12.5% drop on September 23 rested on real data: the production cut and the AISC raise.
- The October drift (-9%) mostly tracks gold and GDX.
- The post-Q3 gap is partly a revenue/FCF headline reaction to timing effects, so it looks somewhat ahead of the fundamentals.

**Catalysts:**
- Q4/FY results and the 2027 production, cost and capex guidance, due \~mid-February 2027.
- The Great Bear capital re-cut, early 2027.
- A Fort Knox Phase 11 update, H1 2027.
- The Grassy Narrows appeal and the federal Great Bear decision, by spring 2027.
- Gold's \~\$3,900–4,000 support.

**Sentiment and risks.** Short interest is low (1.5%), and institutions are trimming targets rather than exiting. The key risks:
- Gold losing \$4,000.
- 2027 capex guided above \$1.5B because of inflation and the added projects.
- Flat 2027 production meeting inflation, which squeezes margins.
- Permitting or First Nations delays at Great Bear.

**Rating:** -0.2 — HOLD

## Verdict

Kinross delivered the Q3 the last review asked for: AISC on track for the revised \$1,850–1,900 range, no new mine-level slippage, and buybacks running at the higher 50%-of-FCF pace. The stock still trades at under 10x trailing FCF with \~\$1.9B net cash. That support is offset by shrinking quarterly revenue and FCF, flat production into 2027, a capex ramp landing at the upper end and heading higher, and a new Grassy Narrows appeal on the Great Bear permits. The chart has broken \~\$23.7 support into a downtrend that is weaker than GDX's. The main risk is gold losing the \~\$3,900–4,000 zone while costs and capex are rising. The rating would move toward BUY on two conditions together: gold holds above \$4,000 and KGC reclaims \~\$23.7 on volume, or the February 2027 guidance shows contained capex and unit costs. A break of gold below \$3,886 or a large Great Bear capex re-cut would move it toward SELL.

**Rating:** -0.2 — HOLD
