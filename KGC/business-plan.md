# Kinross Gold Corporation (NYSE: KGC / TSX: K) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

Kinross Gold is a senior gold-mining company that explores for, acquires, develops and operates gold properties across the Americas and West Africa. It is a pure-play precious-metals producer: essentially all revenue comes from selling gold (with silver as a minor by-product), so the business is a commodity price-taker whose profitability is driven by production volume, cost discipline (all-in sustaining cost) and the prevailing gold price. Kinross reports production and sales on a **gold-equivalent ounce (Au eq. oz)** basis, converting silver to gold at a market ratio.

- **Founded:** 1993 (Toronto), formed from the combination of three junior producers; grown by acquisition into a global senior producer
- **Listings:** NYSE: KGC and TSX: K
- **Headquarters:** 25 York Street, Toronto, Ontario, Canada
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year); reporting currency **USD**
- **Regulatory status:** Canadian MJDS filer — files a **40-F** annually and furnishes quarterly results on **Form 6-K** (no 10-Q/10-K)
- **FY2025 Revenue:** $7,051.1M (record, +37% YoY)
- **FY2025 Production:** 2,128,052 Au eq. oz

## Revenue Model

Kinross generates revenue almost entirely from **metal sales** — gold doré and gold-equivalent ounces sold into the market at spot/realized prices, plus a small amount of by-product silver. There is no meaningful pricing power: realized price tracks the global gold price. The economic levers are therefore:

1. **Volume** — gold-equivalent ounces produced and sold (~2.0–2.1M oz/yr)
2. **Realized gold price** — set by the market; the single largest swing factor in revenue (FY2025 revenue rose ~37% on higher gold prices with roughly flat volume)
3. **Cost per ounce** — production cost of sales and all-in sustaining cost (AISC) per Au eq. oz; the margin between AISC and the gold price is the core profitability metric

Because revenue = ounces × gold price, **Kinross does not guide revenue.** It guides annual **production**, **production cost of sales per ounce**, **all-in sustaining cost per ounce**, and **capital expenditures** — never a revenue figure. (This is why the FINANCIALS guidance rows carry hints rather than numbers.)

## Competitive Position & Strategy

Kinross competes among the senior gold producers (Newmont, Barrick, Agnico Eagle, AngloGold). Its strategy centers on:

1. **Operational consistency** — Kinross has met or beaten its production/cost guidance for several consecutive years, rebuilding credibility with the market
2. **Free cash flow and deleveraging** — the higher gold price plus cost discipline drove **record attributable free cash flow of $2,473.5M in FY2025**; the company repaid term debt, reached a **net cash position in Q3 2025**, and cut total debt to ~$738M by year-end 2025
3. **Capital returns** — a quarterly dividend plus an active share buyback (shares outstanding fell from ~1,229M at end-2024 to ~1,194M by Q1 2026)
4. **Organic growth** — advancing Great Bear and brownfield expansions (Round Mountain Phase X, Manh Choh) to sustain ~2.0M oz/yr with a maintained three-year outlook
5. **Portfolio quality over scale** — following the 2022 divestitures of its Russian (Kupol/Dvoinoye) and Ghanaian (Chirano) assets, the portfolio is concentrated in lower-risk mining jurisdictions plus Mauritania

## Key Risks

### Gold Price (Primary)
Revenue and cash flow are almost entirely a function of the gold price, which is volatile and driven by macro forces (real rates, the dollar, central-bank demand, geopolitical risk) wholly outside the company's control. The strong FY2024–FY2025 results were substantially a gold-price story; a sharp price decline would compress margins quickly.

### Jurisdiction / Geopolitical
- **Mauritania (Tasiast)** is the single most important asset and sits in a frontier jurisdiction — fiscal, permitting and security risk is concentrated there
- History demonstrates the tail risk: Kinross lost its Russian operations following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine and exited Ghana the same year
- Operations in Brazil and Chile carry royalty, tax and permitting risk

### Cost Inflation & Operating Execution
Labor, energy, fuel and consumable inflation pressure AISC; mining is capital- and reserve-intensive, and grade/throughput variability can move unit costs materially.

### Reserve Replacement & Permitting
Mines deplete; sustaining the ~2.0M oz/yr profile depends on reserve replacement and on permitting/developing Great Bear and other projects on schedule.

### Currency & Environmental/Reclamation
Costs are incurred in local currencies against USD revenue; long-dated reclamation and closure obligations are significant balance-sheet liabilities.
