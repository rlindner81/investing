Revenue re-accelerated meaningfully in q2-2026: \$1,843.5M, +16.1% Y/Y — the strongest growth rate since q4-2024 and a clear step up from q1-2026's +13.8% and the +2.7% trough of q4-2025. That reverses the 2025 deceleration narrative (FY-2025 grew just +9.2% against FY-2024's +31.4%) and shows two consecutive quarters of accelerating growth. The revenue line understates the underlying momentum — gross bookings grew +23% Y/Y to \$5.5B, well ahead of revenue, and Active Riders hit a record 30.5M (+17%).

Cash generation stays the core of the thesis but has flattened. TTM operating CF is \$1,195M and TTM company FCF \$1,112M — a 16.4% FCF margin — but q2-2026 FCF of \$319.6M was down -3.0% Y/Y and q1-2026 up only +2.3%, so FCF growth has stalled after FY-2025's +45.6% surge. The culprit is rising capex: TTM capex PP&E jumped to \$82.8M from \$52.8M in FY-2025, reflecting the Nashville AV depot and fleet build. The strict ex-SBC measure is \$801.9M TTM (11.8% margin), and SBC at \$310.4M TTM is actually declining modestly — a genuine positive, since the company-vs-strict gap is narrowing rather than widening.

Valuation remains very cheap on cash flow: TTM P/S of 0.9x and P/FCF of 5.7x company (7.9x strict ex-SBC) at \$16.72. That is higher than the 4.8x trough at the July review's \$14.20 but still below the 7.8x–11.4x range of late 2025. The balance sheet is roughly net-cash-neutral (\$1.14B cash vs \$991M debt), and management is aggressively shrinking the share count — 379.2M shares, down from 420.3M a year ago (-9.8%), funded by \$400M of buybacks in H1 alone. Lyft guides gross bookings only, so forward P/S rows are n/m.

A mid-teens grower at ~6x FCF with a shrinking share count is inexpensive; the discount is the market pricing legal and AV risk, not deteriorating fundamentals.

## 3. News

**The surface narrative** is that Lyft's operating turnaround is working while its legal and AV overhangs refuse to clear. Q2-2026 (Aug 6, after close) was a genuine beat on the metrics that matter: gross bookings \$5.50B vs \$5.37B consensus (+23% Y/Y), revenue \$1.84B vs \$1.81B, adjusted EBITDA \$177.2M vs \$171.6M (+37% Y/Y), and record Active Riders of 30.5M. Q3 EBITDA guidance of \$183–203M also came in above the \$190.4M consensus midpoint. This is **substantiated** — every figure is in the 8-K and 10-Q, and the market's near-flat reaction on the day (-1.1% regular, +0.4% after hours) suggests the beat was largely priced. GAAP EPS of \$0.13 missed the \$0.14 estimate, the one blemish.

**The dominant risk is now concretely dated.** The Bleecker Street short report of July 23 — alleging \$1.3B–\$2.7B of undisclosed rideshare sexual-assault liabilities against only \$533M of combined legal/tax accruals — was the driver of July's -8% week. Its *evidentiary* core is substantiated: MDL No. 3171 was genuinely centralized in the N.D. Cal. on February 5, 2026 (17 actions, grown to ~72 by August), JCCP No. 5061 in California state court holds roughly 2,000 cases, and Lyft self-reported 6,809 serious sexual assaults over 2017–2022. The *damages estimate* is not — it extrapolates from outlier verdicts, and Lyft's rebuttal that insurance coverage applies is untested. The critical near-term catalyst: **the first California state bellwether trial is set for September 30, 2026**, roughly three weeks out. No Lyft case has ever been tried to a jury. The Uber comparables cut both ways — an \$8.5M federal verdict in February, but only \$5,000 in May. This is the single event most likely to re-rate the stock in either direction.

**AV pressure is real and getting worse at the margin.** Waymo opened direct-to-consumer service in Denver, San Diego and Tampa on September 1, and Rosenblatt initiated at Neutral on August 28 citing a "structural competitive gap." Lyft's own Waymo tie-up is narrower than Uber's — Flexdrive runs depot operations in Nashville (the 80,000 sq ft facility opens in October, ~50 vehicles serviced twice daily), which makes Lyft a fleet-ops vendor rather than the demand aggregator. That distinction is substantiated by the deal structure itself and is the strongest part of the bear case.

**Substantiated positives:** the Gett UK acquisition closed (~\$55M, immaterial to Q2 but makes Lyft the leading London black-cab app), the Curb partnership expanded to NYC, and the \$1.0B buyback authorized in February is being executed — \$400M in H1 2026, cutting shares -9.8% Y/Y. That is capital return backed by actual cash, not a promise.

**Sentiment is genuinely divided.** Analyst targets span \$14 to \$23 with the post-earnings cluster around \$17–20 — essentially no consensus edge at spot. Short interest is heavy at roughly 17.6% of float (~70.9M shares; lender-based estimates run higher), well above the ~12.7% peer average, so the trial outcome has squeeze potential in either direction. A cluster of insider sales in early September (CLO, CFO, CAO) is mildly negative optics, though the CLO sale was under a 10b5-1 plan adopted in June.

**Key risks:** the September 30 verdict and any resulting settlement pressure against a \$1.7B unrestricted-cash position; AV disintermediation; a persistent ~24% share against Uber's ~76%; and gig-classification exposure.

**Rating:** -0.4 — HOLD

## Verdict

Lyft's fundamentals improved materially since the July review: revenue growth accelerated to +16.1% Y/Y, gross bookings grew +23%, Active Riders hit a record 30.5M, and the company is retiring stock aggressively (-9.8% Y/Y) with \$400M of buybacks in H1 — all at 0.9x sales and 5.7x FCF on a net-cash-neutral balance sheet. That is a cheap, cash-generative, re-accelerating business, and the primary trend has repaired (price now above a rising 20/50/200 stack, +6.9% in July and +3.2% in August against a flat-to-down XLY). The offsetting reality is that the discount is not irrational: the September 30 California bellwether trial is three weeks away, no Lyft assault case has ever reached a jury, and against a \$1.7B unrestricted cash position the Bleecker Street liability range — even discounted heavily — is financially material. Meanwhile FCF growth has stalled (-3.0% Y/Y in Q2) as AV capex ramps, and Waymo's September 1 direct-to-consumer expansion into three new metros sharpens the question of whether Lyft ends up a fleet-ops vendor rather than a demand aggregator. The rating moves up on a defense verdict or modest damages on September 30 plus reclaimed 17.4 POC resistance; it moves down on a large plaintiff verdict, evidence of settlement reserves requiring an equity raise, or FCF margin compressing further as AV spend builds.

**Rating:** 0.2 — HOLD
