# Lyft, Inc. (NASDAQ: LYFT) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

Lyft is a transportation-network company that operates one of the two dominant
ridesharing marketplaces in North America (alongside Uber). Its platform matches
riders requesting transportation with a network of independent drivers, and it
has expanded over time into adjacent mobility modes — bikes, scooters, and, via
its 2025 acquisition of FreeNow, European taxi and private-hire dispatch. Lyft's
core thesis: a purpose-built, rider-first mobility marketplace can compound
Active Riders, Gross Bookings, and — increasingly — free cash flow, while
autonomous vehicles (AV) reshape the long-term cost structure of the network.

- **Founded:** 2012 (as Zimride's Lyft product; Zimride founded 2007 by Logan
  Green and John Zimmer)
- **IPO:** March 29, 2019 (NASDAQ: LYFT), priced at $72/share (~$24B valuation);
  the first of the major US ridesharing companies to go public, ahead of Uber
- **Headquarters:** San Francisco, California
- **FY End:** December 31 (calendar year)
- **FY2025 Revenue:** $6,316.3M (+9% YoY); FY2025 Gross Bookings ~$17.2B
- **FY2025 Free Cash Flow:** $1,115.6M (all-time high)

## Revenue Model

Lyft's GAAP **revenue** is the net amount it retains from each transaction after
paying drivers — not the total transaction value. The total transaction value is
reported separately as **Gross Bookings** (the total dollar value of rides and
other offerings invoiced to riders, including taxes/tolls/fees, excluding driver
tips). Revenue as a percentage of Gross Bookings ("take rate") is the key bridge
between the two, and has trended higher as Lyft grew higher-margin revenue
streams (media/ads, and insurance-related revenue mechanics).

**Primary streams:**
- **Rideshare (majority of Gross Bookings):** The core marketplace. Lyft
  collects the rider fare, remits the driver's earnings, and retains the balance
  (service fee + commission) as revenue, net of incentives.
- **Bikes & scooters:** Lyft owns and operates bikeshare systems in several major
  US cities (e.g. Citi Bike in New York, Bay Wheels in the Bay Area, Divvy in
  Chicago) plus a scooter fleet. Capitalized as PP&E ("scooter fleet").
- **Lyft Media (advertising):** A fast-growing, high-margin ad business — in-app
  ads, in-car tablets (Lyft Tablets), bikeshare-station and e-bike surfaces, and
  partner-integrated placements. Strategically important as an incremental,
  capital-light margin lever.
- **FreeNow (from mid-2025):** European multi-mobility "super app" (taxi, private
  hire, car sharing) operating across ~9 countries and 150+ cities. Acquired to
  give Lyft international exposure for the first time and a foothold in European
  taxi dispatch.
- **Partnerships & subscriptions:** Lyft Pink (rider subscription), and deep
  partner integrations (Chase, DoorDash, United Airlines, Disney) that channel
  demand into the marketplace. By Q1 2026, nearly 27% of North American rides
  were partnership-linked.

## Products & Platform

### Rideshare (Core)
The flagship marketplace: standard Lyft, shared rides (Wait & Save), premium
tiers (Lyft XL, Lux, Black), scheduled rides, and airport pickups. Two-sided
marketplace dynamics — driver supply and rider demand — govern pricing, ETAs,
and marketplace health (primetime/surge, incentives).

### Driver Experience
Lyft positions itself as a driver-friendly alternative to Uber: earnings
commitments (e.g. a guarantee that drivers keep at least 70% of rider payments
each week after external fees), upfront pay/destination transparency, and Express
Drive vehicle rentals for drivers without a qualifying car.

### Bikes & Scooters (Micromobility)
Owned-and-operated bikeshare systems under municipal contracts in major US
metros, plus e-bikes and scooters. Capital-intensive (fleet is PP&E) but deepens
the multimodal habit and urban footprint.

### Lyft Media
The advertising arm — the clearest high-margin growth vector. Monetizes the app,
in-car tablets, e-bikes, and bikeshare stations, and sells first-party audience
data to advertisers. Grows Gross-Bookings-adjacent revenue without incremental
driver cost.

### FreeNow (Europe)
Acquired 2025; a taxi/PHV/car-share aggregator across major European markets.
Lyft's first international operations, driving the FY2025 goodwill/intangibles
step-up and the "six continents" positioning.

### Autonomous Vehicles (AV)
Lyft's long-term platform bet. Rather than build its own AV stack (it sold its
Level 5 self-driving unit to Toyota's Woven Planet in 2021), Lyft partners with
AV developers to deploy autonomous vehicles onto its marketplace — positioning
the network as the demand and fleet-management layer for third-party AVs.

## Technology & Competitive Position

### Duopoly Marketplace
US rideshare is effectively a two-player market (Uber and Lyft). Scale drives a
liquidity flywheel: more drivers → shorter ETAs / lower prices → more riders →
more driver earnings. Lyft is the #2 player by share, which constrains pricing
power versus Uber but leaves meaningful room to grow Active Riders and take rate.

### Free Cash Flow Inflection
The defining recent development: Lyft turned structurally free-cash-flow
positive, generating $766M in FY2024 and $1.1B in FY2025. FCF materially exceeds
GAAP net income because of large non-cash SBC, favorable working-capital dynamics
in insurance reserves, and low maintenance capex. This reframes Lyft from a
cash-burning growth story into a cash-generative compounder.

### Insurance
Rideshare insurance is one of Lyft's largest and most volatile cost lines.
Insurance reserves are a major balance-sheet item and a swing factor in both cost
of revenue and operating cash flow (reserve build/release moves OCF). Managing
insurance cost per mile is a core operational focus.

### Lyft Media & Data
First-party rider/route data powers a differentiated ad product. Media is
capital-light and margin-accretive, and a rare area where Lyft can grow revenue
faster than Gross Bookings.

## Growth Strategy

1. **Grow Active Riders & frequency:** Active Riders is management's "leading
   indicator of growth"; six-plus consecutive quarters of double-digit YoY growth
   into 2026. Partnerships (Chase, DoorDash, United, Disney) are a major funnel.
2. **Expand take rate & Lyft Media:** Grow revenue faster than Gross Bookings via
   higher-margin advertising and better marketplace monetization.
3. **Sustain and grow free cash flow:** Convert scale into cash; a $500M+ share
   repurchase program returns capital.
4. **Internationalize via FreeNow:** Integrate European operations; extend the
   platform beyond North America for the first time.
5. **Autonomous vehicles:** Partner to onboard third-party AV fleets onto the
   marketplace, positioning Lyft as the demand/fleet layer of an AV future.
6. **Driver-first differentiation:** Earnings commitments and transparency to
   attract and retain supply against Uber.

## Key Risks

### Competitive (Uber)
Uber is larger, globally diversified, and better-capitalized, with Uber Eats
cross-subsidizing mobility. Uber sets much of the market's pricing and driver-pay
environment; Lyft is the price-taker #2.

### Autonomous Vehicles — Double-Edged
AVs could remove driver cost from the network long-term, but they also threaten
disintermediation: AV operators (e.g. Waymo) can run their own consumer apps and
bypass Lyft entirely. Whether Lyft becomes the AV demand layer or is bypassed is
an existential strategic question.

### Insurance Cost Volatility
Rising insurance costs per mile directly compress margins; reserve development
swings operating cash flow. A key, hard-to-forecast cost line.

### Regulatory / Driver Classification
Driver classification (independent contractor vs. employee) remains a live legal
and legislative risk across US states and internationally; reclassification would
materially raise costs. Local rideshare regulation and minimum-pay rules add
uncertainty.

### Stock-Based Compensation & Dilution
SBC remains a large non-cash expense (~$322M in FY2025) and a persistent source
of dilution, partly offset now by buybacks.

### Macro / Discretionary Demand
Rideshare demand is discretionary and cyclical; a consumer downturn pressures
ride frequency and Gross Bookings.
