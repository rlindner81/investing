# Oddity Tech (NASDAQ: ODD) — Business Plan

## Company Overview

Oddity Tech is an Israeli-founded, Nasdaq-listed consumer technology company that builds DTC (direct-to-consumer) beauty and wellness brands powered by AI, machine learning, and computer vision. The company's core thesis: use technology to solve personalized product matching at scale, online, without physical retail. Oddity does not describe itself as a beauty company — it describes itself as a tech company that happens to make beauty and wellness products.

- **Founded:** 2018 (re-founding of IL MAKIAGE)
- **IPO:** July 2023 (NASDAQ: ODD), raised $423M at ~$1.97B valuation
- **Headquarters:** Tel Aviv, Israel (Oddity Labs in Boston, MA)
- **Incorporated:** Cayman Islands
- **FY2025 Revenue:** $809.8M (+25% YoY)

---

## Revenue Model

Oddity generates approximately 97% of revenue through direct-to-consumer online channels — no wholesale to retailers, eliminating the typical 50%+ margin haircut from retail distribution.

**Key revenue characteristics:**
- ~70% of 2025 revenue came from repeat customers
- 12-month net revenue repeat rates exceeding 100% (customers spend more over time, not less)
- Management claims profitable first-order economics under normal customer acquisition cost conditions
- Gross margins ~71–73%, reflecting the high-margin nature of DTC with no intermediaries
- IL MAKIAGE offers an "Encore Box" subscription ($72/month for 3 full-sized products)

## Brand Portfolio

### IL MAKIAGE (Flagship)
Originally a 1972 Israeli makeup brand purchased by the Holtzman siblings from receivership for ~$3.37M, relaunched in 2018 as a tech-first DTC brand.

- **Focus:** Complexion products (foundation, primer, concealer) and skincare
- **Key product:** PowerMatch AI — a quiz + smartphone camera scan that identifies a customer's perfect foundation shade. Claims 90% accuracy using computer vision that maps skin tones, detects facial blood flow, and builds melanin and hemoglobin maps from a standard phone camera. Reduces return rates by more than 10%.
- **Revenue:** Crossed $500M in 2024; ~$560M in FY2025 (low double-digit growth)
- **IL MAKIAGE Skin:** Skincare sub-line grew from ~30% to ~40% of brand revenue in 2025
- **Position:** Ranked #1 foundation and primer brand in U.S. prestige online dollar sales
- **Target:** $1 billion revenue by 2028

### SpoiledChild (Launched 2022)
Second brand focused on skin, hair, and body/wellness products.

- **Revenue:** Reached ~$100M in under a year from launch (one of the fastest DTC brand ramps on record); ~$250M in FY2025
- **Strategic significance:** Validates the "platform" thesis — same AI infrastructure, data assets, and customer acquisition playbook used to launch a second brand at scale

### METHODIQ (Launched November 2025)
Third brand; a medical telehealth dermatology platform competing in the $40B+ telehealth/prescription skincare market (direct competitor to Hims & Hers).

- **Focus:** Acne, hyperpigmentation, and eczema — targeting 50M Americans with acne and 30M with eczema
- **Products:** 28 Rx and non-Rx products; 100+ personalized treatment combinations
- **Technology:** AI-powered skin analysis, computer vision tracking, customized prescription + non-prescription treatment plans
- **Science:** Backed by 4 years of internal development; powered by proprietary Oddity Labs molecules
- **Early performance:** Growing faster than IL MAKIAGE did at a comparable launch stage
- **Revenue model:** Recurring medical revenue with higher regulatory barriers to competitive entry

### Brand 4 (In Development)
Management has confirmed a fourth brand is in development, leveraging the same AI platform infrastructure. No public details disclosed.

## Technology Differentiation & Competitive Moat

### AI / Skin Matching Platform
- Proprietary dataset of 50+ million customers and 10+ million unique facial images
- ML models trained to match dermatologist-level accuracy for skin analysis
- **Flywheel:** More customers → more data → better models → better recommendations → lower return rates → more customers
- Tech team constitutes ~40% of total headcount — unusual for a beauty company
- Computer vision extends beyond shade matching: skin condition assessment, hemoglobin/melanin mapping, real-time tracking for METHODIQ

### Oddity Labs (Science Moat)
- Boston-based R&D facility with 60+ scientists
- AI-driven molecular discovery engine (similar to pharma drug discovery workflows) to identify and develop novel proprietary molecules
- **Key acquisitions:**
  - Voyage81 ($40M, 2021) — computational imaging
  - Revela ($76M, 2023) — biotech molecule R&D
  - Fionix IP — enhanced AI research
- Plans to launch 8+ products featuring proprietary molecules in 2026 (4 for METHODIQ, 4 for existing brands)
- Creates product-level moat: competitors can copy packaging and marketing, but not proprietary molecules

### Data Moat
- Over 1 billion data points accumulated
- DTC model means Oddity owns the customer relationship entirely — no retailer sits between them and the data
- Traditional beauty giants (L'Oréal, Estée Lauder) sell primarily through retail and have limited direct customer data at this granularity

## Growth Strategy

1. **Existing brands:** IL MAKIAGE targeting $1B by 2028; SpoiledChild scaling toward $300M+; both in early stages of international penetration
2. **METHODIQ:** Scale telehealth dermatology using Oddity Labs proprietary molecules; expand from acne/hyperpigmentation/eczema into additional medical domains
3. **Brand 4:** Development ongoing; expected to use same platform infrastructure
4. **Oddity Labs:** Commercialize proprietary molecule pipeline across brands; at least 8 products in 2026
5. **Geographic expansion:** Systematic rollout into European markets; responsibly scale proven markets
6. **Capital flexibility:** $776M cash + $350M undrawn credit facility for M&A and new brand launches

## Key Risks

### Near-Term: Advertising Algorithm Disruption
In Q4 2025/early 2026, the company disclosed an "unprecedented dislocation" with its largest advertising platform (widely interpreted as Meta/Instagram). Algorithm changes pushed ad spend into lower-quality auctions at approximately 50–100%+ higher customer acquisition costs, turning first-order economics negative. Q1 2026 revenue guided to decline ~30% YoY. Full-year 2026 guidance suspended. Management expects meaningful progress in Q2 and normalization in Q3 or Q4 2026.

### Structural Risks
- **Platform concentration:** ~25% of revenue directly attributable to a single advertising platform
- **Try-Before-You-Buy model:** Depends on ad platforms correctly interpreting the TBYB conversion signal
- **Regulatory complexity:** METHODIQ must navigate healthcare regulations, state medical licensing, and prescription drug rules
- **Governance:** Dual-class share structure concentrates voting power with founders; limited independent board oversight
- **Macro:** Premium DTC beauty is discretionary spending, sensitive to consumer confidence
