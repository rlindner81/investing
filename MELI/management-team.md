# MercadoLibre, Inc. (Nasdaq: MELI) — Management Team

## Executive Leadership

---

### Marcos Galperín — Co-Founder, Chairman & Chief Executive Officer

**Background:**
- Born 1971 in Buenos Aires, Argentina, into a family associated with SADESA, one of the world's largest leather producers
- BA from the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania
- MBA from Stanford Graduate School of Business (1999), where the idea for MercadoLibre took shape

**Founding MercadoLibre:**
- Founded MercadoLibre in 1999 while at Stanford, famously pitching investors and recruiting his first backers with the help of a finance professor
- Built the company through the dot-com bust, an early partnership with (and investment from) eBay, and the buildout of Mercado Pago and Mercado Envios into a full commerce-plus-fintech ecosystem
- Led the company's Nasdaq IPO in August 2007 and its evolution into Latin America's most valuable internet company

**Leadership style and vision:**
- Long-tenured founder-CEO with a reputation for long-term, KPI-driven management and a deliberate refusal to issue short-term financial guidance
- Champions heavy reinvestment (logistics, credit, Mexico) over near-term margin optimization
- Relocated to Uruguay; has been an outspoken voice on Argentine economic policy
- One of the most respected operators in emerging-markets technology; his continuity is central to the investment thesis

**Governance:**
- Chairman and CEO; a significant long-term shareholder whose interests are closely aligned with public investors
- Unlike many founder-led tech companies, MELI has a **single class of common stock** — no dual-class super-voting structure — so control rests on economic ownership and board support rather than special voting rights

## Corporate Governance Notes

- **Founder-led with aligned incentives:** Marcos Galperín has led the company since inception (1999) and remains both Chairman and CEO. His long tenure and ownership stake align him with long-term shareholders.
- **Single share class:** Unlike Snap, Meta, Alphabet, and many other founder-led tech companies, MercadoLibre has **one class of common stock with no super-voting shares**. Voting power tracks economic ownership — a relatively shareholder-friendly structure.
- **Tightly held, high-priced stock:** Only ~50.7M shares outstanding at a nominal price in the ~\$1,600–2,400 range. MELI has historically avoided stock splits, keeping the share count small and the price high.
- **Deliberate no-guidance policy:** Management does not issue quarterly or annual revenue/EPS guidance. Investors rely on disclosed KPIs (GMV, TPV, MAU, credit book, take rate, margins, NIMAL) and qualitative commentary, which can lead to large post-earnings stock moves on expectations resets.
- **Reinvestment-first philosophy:** The board and management consistently prioritize long-term market share and ecosystem investment (logistics, credit, Mexico, advertising) over near-term margin, accepting margin volatility from credit provisioning and FX.
- **Emerging-markets exposure:** Governance and results are shaped by operating across Argentina, Brazil, and Mexico, with attendant currency, inflation, and regulatory considerations reflected in USD-reported financials.
