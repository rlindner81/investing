JD's revenue growth has decelerated sharply but may be finding a floor. FY-2025 delivered solid +13.0% Y/Y growth (CNY \$193B vs \$171B), but within that year the trajectory deteriorated: Q1 at +15.8%, Q2 at +22.4% (the peak), Q3 at +14.9%, and Q4 at just +1.5%. The most recent Q1-2026 print of +4.9% Y/Y suggests a modest reacceleration from the Q4 trough, though it's too early to call a durable recovery. The deceleration likely reflects intensifying domestic competition from Pinduoduo/Temu and Alibaba, as well as a challenging Chinese consumer environment.

FCF is positive and meaningful: TTM company FCF of \$4,589M at a 7.9x P/FCF multiple is genuinely inexpensive for a large-cap ecommerce company. However, FY-2025 annual company FCF of \$2,195M was a notable decline from FY-2024's \$7,490M — a -70.7% Y/Y drop driven by working capital headwinds and elevated capex. Q1-2026 standalone FCF was -\$434M (seasonally normal; same quarter was -\$2,827M in 2025, so a significant improvement). Strict ex-SBC FCF TTM of \$2,574M is about \$2B less than company FCF due to SBC (\$718M TTM) and additional capex spend (\$1,296M TTM), making the strict multiple 14.1x — still reasonable for the growth profile.

At a TTM P/S of 0.2x and P/FCF of 7.9x, JD is among the cheapest large-cap ecommerce names globally. The 0.2x P/S has been consistent across the history shown, suggesting the market has persistently discounted JD for China risk, competitive concerns, and governance. Net cash of \$4.05B provides a comfortable cushion. Shares outstanding have declined from 1,527M (Q1-2024) to 1,351M (Q1-2026) — a ~11.5% reduction over two years — reflecting an active and meaningful buyback program. JD provides no formal revenue or earnings guidance, which is standard for Chinese companies but limits forward visibility.

**Verdict:** JD is a deeply discounted large-cap ecommerce business with positive FCF, active buybacks, and a net cash balance sheet, weighed down by decelerating growth and persistent China-specific risk premium.

## 3. News & Catalysts

**Q1-2026 Earnings (reported May 12, 2026):** JD beat expectations with net revenues of RMB 315.7B (+4.9% Y/Y), driven by general merchandise acceleration (+14.9% Y/Y). JD Retail posted a record quarterly operating profit of RMB 15B, up +16.5% Y/Y, with operating margin expanding 70bps to 5.6%. Gross margin grew 90bps to 16.8%. Electronics/home appliances remained weak (-8.4% Y/Y), though showing sequential improvement. Advertising and commission revenues grew at strong double-digit rates. Non-GAAP net income of RMB 7.4B was down significantly from RMB 12.8B in Q1-2025, reflecting escalating investment losses in JD's food delivery expansion.

**June Selloff — Regulatory Double Hit:** JD's -12.4% June decline stemmed from two regulatory shocks. First, in mid-June Beijing's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) summoned JD (along with other ecommerce platforms) for misleading promotions during the annual "618" mid-year shopping festival — the stock fell ~6% in a single day. Second, SAMR published draft rules prohibiting prolonged large-scale subsidies in the food delivery industry, pressuring JD's nascent food delivery business. Combined, these events wiped out most of April's gains and pushed JD well below sector benchmarks in June.

**Daiwa Downgrade:** Daiwa Capital downgraded JD from Buy to Hold on June 24, 2026, citing concerns about deteriorating earnings momentum and food delivery drag on profitability. This was the most notable analyst action during the period; 34 of 37 LSEG analysts still rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, with a consensus 12-month price target of \$39.48 (~47% upside from current levels).

**Upcoming Catalysts:** Q2-2026 earnings likely in mid-August 2026 — the "618" festival performance and food delivery loss trajectory will be the key focus. Any additional SAMR regulatory action or guidance on food delivery subsidies is a near-term binary risk. U.S.–China trade policy and tariff developments remain a macro overhang for the ADR.

**Market Sentiment:** Institutional posture remains broadly constructive (95% buy-rated), but the Daiwa downgrade and regulatory sensitivity signal that the risk premium is elevated. Short interest on the ADR tends to move with U.S.–China geopolitical news flow. Retail sentiment on Chinese ADRs has been cautious since 2021 and has not fully recovered.

**Key Risks:** Regulatory unpredictability (SAMR can move the stock 5–6% in a single summons); food delivery losses deepening as JD subsidizes market share; intensifying competition from Pinduoduo/Temu and Alibaba on price; China consumer macro weakness; ADR governance and delisting risk for U.S.-listed shares; CNY/USD currency translation headwinds.

## Rating

BUY

JD combines record retail operating margins (Q1-2026 operating profit +16.5% Y/Y, a new high), a deeply discounted valuation at 0.2x P/S and 7.9x P/FCF, a net cash balance sheet (\$4B), and an aggressive buyback that has retired ~11.5% of shares over two years — a compelling combination for a large-cap ecommerce platform.
