**Revenue trend.** The headline is a break in a 12-year streak: Q2-2026 revenue of \$51.6B (¥346.4B) was **−2.9% Y/Y**, JD's first reported revenue decline since the 2014 IPO. That follows +4.9% in Q1-2026 and a FY-2025 that grew +13.0%, so the deceleration is severe and recent — the +22.4% Q2-2025 comp was inflated by China's trade-in subsidy program, and electronics/home appliances (−11.8% Y/Y) is where the air came out. General merchandise (+5.6%) and services (+6.8%) still grew, so this is a category-specific base effect layered on soft domestic consumption, not a whole-business collapse.

**FCF profile.** Cash generation is genuinely strong and improving. TTM company FCF of \$6.41B (+3.3% margin) is a real recovery from the \$2.22B FY-2025 trough, and Q2-2026's \$5.25B was **+50.9% Y/Y** on a 10.2% margin. The gap to strict-ex-SBC FCF (\$4.30B TTM) is the honest lens: SBC of \$638M TTM is still a material drag, and the [SBC ramp note](../notes/2026-07-SBC-ramp-note.md) shows it is concentrated in G&A executive grants rather than broad hiring — a corporate comp step-up that has not yet proven temporary. Note the seasonal whipsaw: Q1 is reliably FCF-negative for JD, so single-quarter reads mislead.

**Valuation.** This is the cheapest large-cap Chinese e-commerce name by a wide margin. **P/S 0.2x**, **P/FCF company 5.9x**, and even the conservative **strict ex-SBC P/FCF of 8.8x** are deeply discounted — and both FCF multiples have compressed sharply from FY-2025's 15.2x/147.9x as cash generation normalized. Balance sheet is a net cash position of roughly **\$2.9B** (\$13.27B cash vs \$10.41B debt), and the share count has fallen from 1,452M to 1,344M — a genuine **7.4% reduction** over two years, backed by \$1B of Class A repurchases in H1-2026.

**Guidance.** JD issues no formal revenue guidance, so the forward P/S rows are blank and the trajectory has to be read off management commentary — they guided JD Retail back to positive growth in Q3 with acceleration in Q4.

**Verdict.** A shrinking top line at 5.9x FCF with net cash and real buybacks is a value setup, not a growth one — the multiple already prices in far worse than what the cash flows show.

**Rating:** 1.2 — BUY

## 3. News

**The surface narrative.** Three stories dominate JD right now: the Q2 revenue decline and the 10% selloff it triggered, the EU's foreign-subsidies probe of the Ceconomy takeover, and a "Michael Burry is buying JD" retail narrative. They differ sharply in how much hard data sits underneath.

**Q2-2026 earnings (Aug 13) — substantiated, and better than the tape suggests.** Revenue of ¥346.4B was **−2.9% Y/Y**, the first decline since the 2014 IPO, and the stock fell **~8.5% in the US / >10% in Hong Kong**. But the underlying report was a beat: non-GAAP EPS of ¥6.29 topped the ¥5.63 consensus, revenue actually came in *above* the ¥342.7B street number, non-GAAP net income rose **+20.8%** to ¥8.9B, and net margin expanded 50bps to 2.6%. JD Retail posted its **17th consecutive quarter** of Y/Y gross margin expansion (18.5%, +1.3pp) at a record 4.6% operating margin. New Businesses' operating loss narrowed to ¥9.9B from ¥14.8B, with **food-delivery losses down more than 50% Y/Y** on lower subsidy-per-order and better delivery efficiency. Logistics revenue grew **+24.3%** to ¥64.1B. This is all disclosed, audited-track data — the selloff was expectation-driven (shares had run +25.5% in July into the print), not fundamentals-driven. CEO Sandy Xu's "definitive turning point for our profitability trajectory" is forward-looking narrative, but the margin lines behind it are real.

**Ceconomy / EU FSR — substantiated and genuinely binary.** JD **offered remedies** to the Commission, confirmed on the EC's own case page dated **Aug 18, 2026**; the terms are undisclosed. That offer pushed the decision deadline from the Oct 2 date in this repo's [Ceconomy note](../notes/2026-07-Ceconomy-EU-probe-note.md) to a provisional **Oct 23, 2026**. The EC issued a formal Statement of Grounds in July alleging preferential PRC financing, tax incentives and grants distorted the €2.23B (~\$2.6B) bid. Germany and Italy have both cleared it under national security / golden-power rules with data-protection conditions. The escalation worth flagging: **China has ordered entities not to assist the EU probe**, calling it "improper extraterritorial jurisdiction" — that raises the odds of a bad outcome by making cooperation harder, and turns a deal question into a geopolitical one. Precedent (ADNOC/Covestro, cleared with concessions) leans toward clearance-with-remedies.

**Michael Burry — mostly attention.** The claim circulating is not backed by any filing. Scion **deregistered with the SEC and filed its final 13F for Q3-2025**, in which the JD position had already been sold entirely. Any 2026 position is a Substack newsletter mention, unverifiable and unsized. Treat this as sentiment noise.

**Regulatory & competitive.** SAMR fined seven platforms including JD ¥3.6B (\$528M) in April 2026 over food-safety/"ghost delivery" violations — real and paid, but JD's share was modest and PDD absorbed the bulk. More important, the **subsidy war is visibly ending**: Alibaba said Taobao Flash investment will shrink significantly, Meituan disowned price warring, Beijing issued draft rules curbing delivery subsidies. That directly underwrites JD's loss-narrowing trajectory.

**Catalysts & sentiment.** Next: the **Oct 23 EU decision**, then Q3 earnings in mid-to-late November (the test of management's "JD Retail returns to growth in Q3" claim — uncommitted, no formal guidance behind it). Sentiment is bifurcated: 36-analyst consensus is Strong Buy at a **\$39.66 target (~40% upside)**, but Susquehanna cut to Hold with a \$30 target. Capital returns are concrete — **\$1B repurchased in H1-2026** with ~\$1B remaining, plus a 3.5% dividend yield.

**Risks.** A Ceconomy prohibition would kill the international thesis; consumer-electronics price inflation and weak Chinese discretionary spending could extend the revenue decline past the base effect; food-delivery competition could reignite; and the China-EU standoff adds ADR-level political risk on top.

**Rating:** 0.7 — BUY

## Verdict

JD is a cheap, cash-generative business whose stock is being priced for a top-line decline that the reported numbers suggest is a subsidy-comp artifact rather than a structural break. The bull case rests on hard data: 5.9x company FCF (8.8x strict ex-SBC), roughly \$2.9B net cash, a 7.4% two-year reduction in share count, 17 straight quarters of retail gross-margin expansion, and food-delivery losses halving as Beijing and the platforms themselves wind down the subsidy war. The main risk is that the two open questions are both binary and both land soon — the **Oct 23 EU decision** on Ceconomy, now complicated by Beijing ordering entities not to cooperate with the probe, and the **November Q3 print** that must validate management's uncommitted claim that JD Retail returns to growth. The technical picture argues against rushing: price is below a falling intermediate trend with the July volume shelf at 29.0–30.4 as overhead resistance, and the decline has drifted lower on fading volume without capitulation, which typically continues before it reverses. A Q3 print confirming the return to Y/Y retail growth, or a clean EU clearance, would move this decisively to BUY; a Ceconomy prohibition or a second consecutive revenue decline would break the "base effect" explanation and warrant a cut.

**Rating:** 0.5 — BUY
