# JD.com / CECONOMY — EU Foreign Subsidies Probe Note

**Date:** July 2026

---

## The Story

JD.com is trying to buy Germany's CECONOMY AG — the parent of MediaMarkt and
Saturn, the largest consumer-electronics retailer in the EU — as its beachhead
for expansion outside China. The offer, announced July 2025, is a voluntary
public takeover at **€4.60 per share in cash**, valuing CECONOMY at roughly
**€2.2 billion (\$2.6 billion)**. The bid vehicle is Jingdong Holding Germany
GmbH, a wholly-owned indirect JD.com subsidiary.

The deal is now the first Chinese acquisition ever pulled into an **in-depth
investigation under the EU's Foreign Subsidies Regulation (FSR)**. This is not
ordinary merger control — the Commission has said the transaction does not turn
on competition/market-share concerns, but on whether Chinese state support gave
JD.com an unfair edge in the bidding. The Commission's preliminary view is that
JD.com may have benefited from **preferential financing, tax incentives and
grants from entities attributable to the PRC**, and that those subsidies may have
let JD.com offer a higher price and distort the negotiation for CECONOMY.

JD.com rejects this outright. Its position: the acquisition **"will not be
financed by any foreign subsidies granted by China or any other non-EU Member
State,"** but by external private bank debt and cash from ordinary business
operations.

The state of play in mid-July 2026 is bifurcated: JD.com has **cleared the German
national-security review**, but the **EU FSR probe is the live gating item**, with
a hard decision deadline of **October 2, 2026**. The company still guides to
closing in **the second half of 2026**.

## The German Approval (already cleared)

Germany's Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy approved the deal after
an FDI-style review of its impact on public order and security. Conditions attached:

- JD.com must ensure the **personal data of CECONOMY's German customers remains
  protected**.
- Berlin retains **strong monitoring and control rights**, and the ability to
  **revoke the approval** if conditions are violated.

JD.com called the German approval "an important milestone." This clears the
national-security track but is **separate from and does not resolve** the EU FSR
probe.

---

## The EU FSR Probe (the live item)

**Legal basis:** Foreign Subsidies Regulation (FSR) — the EU's instrument for
policing non-EU state aid that distorts the internal market. This is the first
in-depth FSR probe of a Chinese M&A deal, so it is also a precedent-setting test
case for how Brussels treats Chinese acquisitions of EU assets.

**Commission's preliminary concerns:**
- JD.com may have received foreign subsidies (preferential financing, tax
  incentives, grants) from entities attributable to the PRC.
- Those subsidies may have **distorted the acquisition process** — enabling a
  higher offer price and supporting CECONOMY's activities/growth via JD.com's tech
  and logistics in a way that distorts the EU market.

**Possible outcomes at the Oct 2 deadline:**
1. **Clear unconditionally** — deal proceeds.
2. **Clear with commitments/remedies** — JD.com offers concessions to address
   distortion concerns.
3. **Prohibit** — deal blocked.

**JD.com's rebuttal:** funded by external private bank debt + ordinary-course
cash, no foreign subsidies involved.

---

## Why This Matters for the Position

- The **€4.60/share cash** offer is the anchor. FSR risk is binary-ish: clearance
  (with or without remedies) vs. prohibition. A prohibition kills the European
  expansion thesis and the deployment of capital behind it.
- **October 2, 2026** is the date to watch. Remedies, if required, could reshape
  the deal economics or the strategic value (e.g. data/logistics integration
  limits) even in a "clear-with-commitments" outcome.
- Politically loaded: first Chinese FSR case, amid broader EU–Beijing tensions —
  the decision carries precedent weight beyond this single deal, which cuts both
  ways on how predictable the outcome is.

---

## What We Don't Know / To Track

- Whether JD.com will **offer remedies**, and what they'd be (financing
  transparency, data-handling firewalls, logistics-integration limits).
- **Tender-offer acceptance level** and any acceptance-period extensions tied to
  the regulatory timeline.
- Whether the Commission signals its leaning before the Oct 2 statutory deadline.
