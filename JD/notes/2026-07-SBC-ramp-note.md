# JD.com — Share-Based Compensation Ramp Note

**Date:** July 2026

---

## The Observation

`FINANCIALS.yml` shows JD's stock-based comp climbing steeply: FY-2024 SBC of
\$442.9M rose to \$697.9M in FY-2025, and the TTM figure is \$719.5M — roughly a
**60% increase** off a business whose revenue grew only mid-single-digits. This
note records *why*, because the naive read ("dilution running ahead of growth")
misses where the money is actually going.

## Where It Lands — It's Concentrated in G&A, Not Broad-Based

The per-line SBC footnote in the earnings letters (note (2) in the reconciliation
tables) breaks SBC out by expense line. The jump is overwhelmingly in **General &
administrative**, i.e. corporate/executive-level grants — *not* spread across the
rank-and-file fulfillment/marketing workforce:

| Line (standalone quarter) | Q2-2024 | Q2-2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| G&A | \$304M | **\$1,212M** | **~4x** |
| R&D | \$164M | \$296M | +80% |
| Fulfillment | \$108M | \$75M | down |
| Marketing | \$80M | \$49M | down |
| **Total** | **\$666M** | **\$1,657M** | **+149%** |

JD itself attributes the G&A expense-ratio rise to SBC: the Q2-2025 letter says
G&A rose to 0.9% of revenue from 0.7% *"primarily due to the increase in
share-based compensation expenses."* The Q1-2026 split repeats the pattern (G&A
leads; fulfillment SBC ticks up only modestly, \$71M → \$116M, consistent with the
food-delivery workforce build-out — a small slice, not the driver).

So the SBC surge is a **leadership/retention grant story concentrated at the
corporate level**, not evidence of mass hiring or industry-wide job-hopping
churning through JD's cost base.

## The Industry Backdrop — Real, But Not JD's Main Cause

There *is* a genuine 2025–2026 Chinese-tech talent war, so the instinct isn't
wrong in spirit — it's just not what's moving JD's line:

- **AI talent war.** Maimai's new-AI-position index leapt +543% Y/Y (Jan–Oct
  2025). ByteDance expanded its bonus pool +35% and its pay-raise budget +150%,
  offering *special stock options to its AI team to prevent defections*; Tencent
  reportedly doubled salaries to poach researchers. Campus "Top Seed" packages
  roughly doubled Y/Y. Equity/options are the retention tool of choice.
- **But** JD's ramp shows up in G&A, not R&D — so it reads as **executive
  retention / corporate grants**, likely tied to the food-delivery push and the
  Ceconomy international expansion (see
  [Ceconomy EU probe note](2026-07-Ceconomy-EU-probe-note.md)), rather than JD
  bidding for AI researchers. If it were the AI war, the surge would sit in R&D.

## Why It Matters for Valuation

This is why `check-valuation`'s **strict-ex-SBC** FCF diverges so hard from
**company** FCF (TTM 15.8x vs 8.9x P/FCF). SBC is a real, growing economic cost
that JD's "company FCF" ignores. Because it's concentrated in G&A executive
grants during a loss-making expansion phase, watch whether it normalizes once the
food-delivery losses narrow and Ceconomy closes — or whether it's a permanent
step-up in the corporate comp base. The strict-ex-SBC row is the honest lens here.
